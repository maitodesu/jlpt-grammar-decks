に

Meaning · marks a point - where something ends up, where it sits, when it happens, or who receives it

に names a point, never a stretch or an area. The place a movement finishes, the spot a thing occupies, the moment on the clock, the person a thing lands with: all of those are single points, and に is how Japanese pins them down. Nearly every use below is a version of that one idea.

に · Formation

に is a particle. It attaches straight onto a noun, a time word, or a ます-stem, and nothing changes shape.

に · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - destination Noun + に + motion verb 学校がっこう → 学校がっこうに行いく
Noun - where something exists Noun + に + ある／いる 台所だいどころ → 台所だいどころにある
Noun - person who receives Person + に + giving or telling verb 母はは → 母ははに話はなす
Time word with a number or a name Time + に 六時ろくじ → 六時ろくじに起おきる
Verb ます-stem - purpose stem + に + 行いく／来くる／帰かえる 買かいます → 買かいに行いく
Noun - thing entered, boarded, sat on Noun + に + 入はいる／乗のる／座すわる いす → いすに座すわる
Period - rate or frequency Period + に + number 週しゅう → 週しゅうに三回さんかい
Noun or な-adjective - result of a change Noun + に + なる 先生せんせい → 先生せんせいになる

に · Formation (cont.)

Note - which time words take に? If the time word is a number or a fixed name on the calendar, it takes に: 七時しちじに, 三月さんがつに, 月曜日げつようびに, 二十日はつかに, 誕生日たんじょうびに. If it is positioned relative to now, or if it is a frequency word, it takes nothing at all: 今日きょう, 明日あした, 昨日きのう, 今朝けさ, 今晩こんばん, 先週せんしゅう, 来年らいねん, 毎日まいにち, いつ. Adding に to those is one of the most persistent N5 errors. 朝あさ, 夜よる and 夏なつ sit in between - both forms are heard, and the bare one is more common in speech.

ゆうべ母ははに電話でんわしました。
I phoned my mother last night.

I'm just popping out to buy some bread.

There's a meeting in Osaka next Wednesday.

I swim at the gym three times a week.

When I was a kid I often came to this park to play.

すみません、この書類しょるいを田中たなかさんに渡わたしてもらえますか。
Sorry - could you pass this document to Tanaka?

に · Nuance & register

に is completely register-neutral. It is the same in plain speech, in ます-form, in business email and in newspapers. Choosing に never makes you sound stiff or casual.

What varies is whether you say it. In quick casual speech the destination に is routinely dropped:

  • どこ行いくの? - "Where are you off to?"
  • ちょっとコンビニ行いってくる。 - "Just nipping to the convenience store."

That dropping is limited to destinations. The に of existence, the に of a recipient, and the に of a clock time are not dropped - remove them and the sentence stops parsing rather than sounding casual.

に · Nuance & register (cont.)

に is phonologically weightless. It never carries stress, and it attaches so tightly to the preceding noun that Japanese speakers hear 六時ろくじに as one word. Do not pause before it.

One more use worth knowing early, because it turns up constantly: に marks the result of a change, with なる and する. 姉あねは医者いしゃになりました。 部屋へやをきれいにしてください。 It is the same "landing point" idea - the thing ends up at that state.

に · に vs で

This is the single most common particle error at N5, and it is worth over-learning.

Rule of thumb - に marks where something is; で marks where something happens.

If the verb is ある, いる, 住すむ, 座すわる, 置おく, 泊とまる - verbs about a thing occupying a spot - use に. If the verb is an activity that could happen anywhere, use で.

公園こうえんにベンチがあります。
There's a bench in the park.

公園こうえんでお弁当べんとうを食たべました。
We ate our packed lunch in the park.

に · に vs で (cont.)

Same park; different job. The bench is located there, so に. The eating took place there, so で.

The trap that catches nearly everyone is 住すむ. In English "live in Tokyo" feels like an activity, but Japanese treats it as being located somewhere:

兄あには東京とうきょうに住すんでいます。
My older brother lives in Tokyo.

❌東京とうきょうで住すんでいます is wrong. Learn 住すむ as a に-verb the way you learn a verb's conjugation.

に · に vs へ

Rule of thumb - for a destination they are interchangeable. Everywhere else, only に works.

空港くうこうに着つきました。
I've arrived at the airport.

Swap that に for へ and the sentence is odd, because 着つく is about the arrival point, which is に territory. Where the verb is plain movement - 行いく, 来くる, 帰かえる - either particle is fine and the difference is a shade of emphasis: に on the endpoint, へ on the heading.

に · に vs へ (cont.)

The clean division is what へ cannot do. It cannot mark a time (❌六時ろくじへ), it cannot mark existence (❌部屋へやへいます), and it cannot mark the person you tell or ask something (❌先生せんせいへ聞ききました). All three are に.

に · に vs を with verbs of movement

Rule of thumb - go in, get on, sit down: に. Go out, get off, pass through: を.

English uses a preposition for both directions, so learners reach for one particle for all of them. Japanese splits them. Entering, boarding and attaching take に, because they end at a point. Leaving and traversing take を, because the noun is the thing being departed or crossed.

次つぎの駅えきで急行きゅうこうに乗のります。
I'll get on the express at the next station.

次つぎの駅えきで電車でんしゃを降おります。
I'll get off the train at the next station.

に · に vs を with verbs of movement (cont.)

The same pairing runs through 入はいる／出でる and 大学だいがくに入はいる／大学だいがくを出でる.

に · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日あしたに東京とうきょうへ行いきます。 → ✅ 明日あした東京とうきょうへ行いきます。
    Time words that float relative to now - 今日きょう, 明日あした, 毎日まいにち, 来週らいしゅう - take no particle at all.

  • ❌ 公園こうえんでベンチがあります。 → ✅ 公園こうえんにベンチがあります。
    ある and いる report where a thing exists, and existence is always に.

  • ❌ 先生せんせいを聞ききました。 → ✅ 先生せんせいに聞ききました。
    The person you ask is a recipient, not an object. 聞きく takes を for the question and に for the person.

  • ❌ パンを買かうに行いきます。 → ✅ パンを買かいに行いきます。
    Purpose に attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form. With a する-noun you can drop the verb entirely: 買かい物ものに行いく.

に · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 東京とうきょうで住すんでいます。 → ✅ 東京とうきょうに住すんでいます。
    住すむ is a location-of-existence verb in Japanese, however active "live" feels in English.

に · Practice 1

1. 来週らいしゅうの金曜日きんようび____友達ともだちに会あいます。

来週らいしゅうの金曜日きんようびに友達ともだちに会あいます。

金曜日きんようび is a named day on the calendar, so it takes に. Note that 来週らいしゅう on its own would take nothing - it is the 金曜日きんようび inside the phrase that licenses the particle.

に · Practice 2

2. 机つくえの上うえ（に・で）辞書じしょがあります。

机つくえの上うえに辞書じしょがあります。

The verb is ある, which reports where a thing exists, and existence takes に. で would need an action happening on the desk, which a dictionary is not doing.

に · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I go to the gym twice a week."

週しゅうに二回にかいジムに行いきます。

Two different jobs for the same particle. The first に is rate - a number of times per period. The second is destination. Saying 週しゅう二回にかい without に is possible in casual speech but the full form is safer.

に · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 毎朝まいあさに六時ろくじ起おきて、会社かいしゃに行いきます。

✅ 毎朝まいあさ六時ろくじに起おきて、会社かいしゃに行いきます。 "I get up at six every morning and go to work."

The two particles are swapped. 毎朝まいあさ is a frequency word and takes nothing; 六時ろくじ is a clock time and needs に.

に · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) パンを買かいに行いきます。 (b) パンを買かって行いきます。

(a) is purpose: buying bread is the reason for going. You set off, and the errand is what the trip is for. Nothing is said about where you go afterwards.

(b) is sequence: you buy bread and then continue on your way. The going is already planned, and the bread is picked up en route - this is what you would say about bringing something to a friend's house.

へ - direction, toward

Meaning · points movement in a direction - toward, off in the direction of

へ orients. It says which way something is heading, and unlike に it says nothing about arriving. That is why へ works for compass directions and for the address on an envelope, and why it is useless for times, for existence, and for people you talk to.

Note - へ is read e. When it is a particle, the hiragana へ is pronounced え, not he. 東京とうきょうへ is Tōkyō e. It is written へ for historical reasons only, exactly like the topic particle は being read wa.

へ - direction, toward · Formation

へ is a particle and attaches directly to a noun. The noun does not change.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - place you head for Place + へ + motion verb 京都きょうと → 京都きょうとへ行いく
Noun - compass or relative direction Direction + へ 右みぎ → 右みぎへ曲まがる
Noun - addressee of a letter or gift Person + へ 母はは → 母ははへ
Noun modifying another noun Noun + への + Noun 東京とうきょう → 東京とうきょうへの電車でんしゃ
Fixed greeting ようこそ + Place + へ 日本にほん → ようこそ日本にほんへ

へ - direction, toward · Formation (cont.)

Note - the fourth row is the one grammatical thing へ can do that に cannot. へ takes の and modifies a following noun; に does not. 友達ともだちへのプレゼント is natural, ❌友達ともだちにのプレゼント is not Japanese.

へ - direction, toward · Examples

駅えきへ行いきます。
I'm going to the station.

どこへ行いきますか。
Where are you going?

あの角かどを右みぎへ曲まがってください。
Please turn right at that corner.

来月らいげつ、国くにへ帰かえります。
I'm going back to my home country next month.

へ - direction, toward · Examples (cont.)

ようこそ日本にほんへ。
Welcome to Japan.

祖母そぼへ手紙てがみを書かきました。
I wrote a letter to my grandmother.

東京とうきょうへの電車でんしゃはもう出でてしまいました。
The train to Tokyo has already left.

台風たいふうが北きたへ進すすんでいるので、明日あしたの飛行機ひこうきは飛とばないかもしれません。
The typhoon is moving north, so tomorrow's flight may not take off.

へ - direction, toward · Nuance & register

へ is neutral in politeness - it is not more formal than に in the way learners are sometimes told. What is true is that へ is more common in writing and in slightly composed speech, and に dominates ordinary conversation. Announcements, timetables, travel writing and envelopes lean へ.

Two places where へ is essentially fixed and swapping in に would sound wrong:

  • Envelopes and dedications. 田中たなか様さまへ on the front of a card,
    両親りょうしんへ at the top of a letter. The へ here is "to, in the direction of you" rather than "handed to you".
  • ようこそ～へ. 大阪おおさかへようこそ, ようこそわが社しゃへ. A set frame.

へ - direction, toward · Nuance & register (cont.)

In fast casual speech へ is dropped just as に is - どこ行いくの? - so the choice disappears entirely at the most informal level.

One last register point: because へ emphasises the heading rather than the arrival, it carries a faint flavour of a journey being undertaken. 海うみへ is a touch more evocative than 海うみに. Song titles and book titles exploit this constantly. You do not need to reproduce the effect, but recognise it when you meet it.

へ - direction, toward · へ vs に

Rule of thumb - へ is the heading; に is the landing point. For plain 行いく／来くる／帰かえる they overlap almost completely, so pick either. Outside destinations, only に exists.

山やまの方ほうへ歩あるいていきました。
He walked off in the direction of the mountains.

山やまの上うえに着つきました。
We reached the top of the mountain.

The first says nothing about whether he got anywhere; へ only gives the bearing. The second is entirely about arrival, and that is a point, so に.

へ - direction, toward · へ vs に (cont.)

Because of this, verbs that focus on the endpoint prefer に: 着つく, 入はいる, 乗のる, 届とどく. Verbs of open-ended motion happily take へ: 向むかう, 進すすむ, 飛とぶ, 歩あるく.

And the hard boundary is worth restating. へ cannot mark a time (❌九時くじへ), cannot mark existence (❌部屋へやへ犬いぬがいます), and cannot mark the person you tell or ask (❌先生せんせいへ質問しつもんしました). Those are に jobs, all three.

へ - direction, toward · へ vs まで

Rule of thumb - へ gives the direction of travel; まで gives the far end of the stretch you cover.

駅えきへ走はしりました。
I ran to the station.

駅えきまで走はしりました。
I ran as far as the station.

へ - direction, toward · へ vs まで (cont.)

The first is about where you were headed. The second measures the running: the station is the point where it stopped, and the sentence implies the whole distance was covered on foot. That is why まで pairs with から and へ does not: 家いえから駅えきまで - a span with two ends.

へ - direction, toward · へ vs を

Rule of thumb - へ marks the place you are heading for; を marks the space you move through.

English "go along the river" and "go to the river" both look like one preposition slot, so learners over-use へ. When the noun is the route rather than the goal, Japanese uses を - with 通とおる, 渡わたる, 歩あるく, 散歩さんぽする, 曲まがる.

この橋はしを渡わたると、郵便局ゆうびんきょくがあります。
Cross this bridge and there's a post office.

へ - direction, toward · へ vs を (cont.)

あの橋はしへ行いきましょう。
Let's go over to that bridge.

Note that 曲まがる takes both, in different slots: 角かどを右みぎへ曲まがる - you turn at the corner, toward the right.

へ - direction, toward · Common mistakes

  • ❌ pronouncing 学校がっこうへ as "gakkō he" → ✅ "gakkō e"
    The particle へ is read え. Learners who first meet it in writing often carry the wrong pronunciation for years.

  • ❌ 七時しちじへ駅えきへ来きてください。 → ✅ 七時しちじに駅えきへ来きてください。
    へ cannot mark a time. Only the destination slot is available to it.

  • ❌ 教室きょうしつへ先生せんせいがいます。 → ✅ 教室きょうしつに先生せんせいがいます。
    Existence with ある／いる is always に. へ needs movement.

  • ❌ 東京とうきょうにの荷物にもつ → ✅ 東京とうきょうへの荷物にもつ
    Only へ combines with の to modify a following noun.

へ - direction, toward · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 先生せんせいへ聞ききました。 → ✅ 先生せんせいに聞ききました。
    A person you question is a recipient, not a direction. へ marks a person only when the thing sent is physical - a letter, a parcel, a present.

へ - direction, toward · Practice 1

1. 海外かいがい____の荷物にもつは三階さんがいで受うけ付つけています。

へ - direction, toward · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 会議かいぎが終おわってから、部長ぶちょうへ報告ほうこくします。

Reporting, telling, asking and teaching all mark the person with に. へ would only work if something physical were being sent to him.

へ - direction, toward · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 南みなみへ向むかっています。 (b) 南みなみの島しまに着つきました。

(a) reports a bearing. 向むかう is open-ended motion, and へ gives the direction without committing to a destination - you are somewhere on the way, pointing south.

(b) reports an arrival. 着つく is entirely about the endpoint, which is a point, so に is required; ❌島しまへ着つきました sounds wrong to a native ear.

で - location of an action

Meaning · at, in - the place where something is done

で marks the stage the action is played out on. Not where a thing sits, not where a movement ends: where the doing happens. If you can put "at" or "in" in front of an English verb phrase and it still describes an activity, Japanese almost certainly wants で.

で - location of an action · Formation

で is a particle and attaches straight onto a noun.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - place of an action Place + で + action verb 図書館としょかん → 図書館としょかんで勉強べんきょうする
Noun - organisation or institution Org + で + verb 会社かいしゃ → 会社かいしゃで働はたらく
Noun - event, as its venue Event は + Place + である パーティー → パーティーはホールである
Noun - the field a statement ranges over Scope + で（いちばん…） 日本にほん → 日本にほんでいちばん高たかい
Question word どこ + で どこ → どこで会あいますか

で - location of an action · Formation (cont.)

Note - one apparent exception that is really the rule. An event is something that takes place, so it uses で even with ある: 試験しけんは大学だいがくであります。 Compare a thing, which uses に: 大学だいがくに図書館としょかんがあります。 The particle tells you whether ある means "is held" or "is located".

で - location of an action · Examples

図書館としょかんで勉強べんきょうします。
I study at the library.

どこで買かいましたか。
Where did you buy it?

公園こうえんでサッカーをしました。
We played soccer in the park.

台所だいどころで母ははがご飯はんを作つくっています。
My mother is making dinner in the kitchen.

で - location of an action · Examples (cont.)

日本にほんでいちばん高たかい山やまは富士山ふじさんです。
The highest mountain in Japan is Mt Fuji.

来週らいしゅうのパーティーはこのホールであります。
Next week's party is being held in this hall.

会社かいしゃの近ちかくのカフェで昼ひるご飯はんを食たべることが多おおいです。
I usually have lunch at a café near the office.

先週せんしゅうの日曜日にちようび、駅前えきまえの映画館えいがかんで話題わだいの映画えいがを見みました。
Last Sunday I saw that much-talked-about film at the cinema by the station.

で - location of an action · Nuance & register

で is register-neutral. It behaves identically in casual speech, in ます-form and in writing, and it is never dropped the way a destination に can be - the sentence would become ambiguous, so speakers keep it.

Two habits of real speech are worth noting. First, で is very often followed by は for contrast: 家いえでは静しずかです - "at home (as opposed to elsewhere) he's quiet." Second, in a topic-first sentence the place can be lifted to the front with では: 日本にほんでは靴くつを脱ぬぎます。 That では is this same で, just wearing a topic marker.

で - location of an action · Nuance & register (cont.)

The scope use - 世界せかいでいちばん, クラスで一番いちばん - feels like a different word to learners, but it is the same idea stretched: the comparison is carried out within that field. It appears constantly in N5 superlative sentences, so learn it as part of this particle rather than as a separate item.

Finally, note that で is homophonous with the て-form of です／だ and with the て-form of verbs like 読よむ → 読よんで. Context separates them instantly, but a beginner reading aloud can be thrown. If a noun of place sits immediately before it, it is this particle.

で - location of an action · で vs に

Rule of thumb - で is where something happens; に is where something is.

Ask what the verb is doing. An activity - 食たべる, 働はたらく, 遊あそぶ, 会あう, 買かう, 泳およぐ - takes で. A verb of existence or position - ある, いる, 住すむ, 座すわる, 置おく - takes に.

駅前えきまえのカフェで友達ともだちを待まっています。
I'm waiting for my friend at the café by the station.

駅前えきまえに新あたらしいカフェができました。
A new café has opened up by the station.

Waiting is an activity; the café is its stage, so で. Coming into existence is not an activity in this sense - the café now is there - so に.

で - location of an action · で vs に (cont.)

The event/thing split under ある is where this rule earns its keep:

三階さんがいに会議室かいぎしつがあります。
There's a meeting room on the third floor.

会議かいぎは三階さんがいであります。
The meeting is on the third floor.

Same building, same verb, opposite particles - because a room is located and a meeting is held.

で - location of an action · で vs を

Rule of thumb - で is the place an action is set in; を is the space the action moves across.

A handful of motion verbs - 歩あるく, 走はしる, 飛とぶ, 渡わたる, 通とおる, 散歩さんぽする - can take the place as を, and the meaning shifts.

公園こうえんで走はしっています。
He's out running in the park.

公園こうえんを走はしっています。
He's running through the park.

で - location of an action · で vs を (cont.)

The first frames the park as the venue for a jog. The second traces a path across it - he entered one side and is heading for the other. With any verb that is not motion, を is simply unavailable for a place.

で - location of an action · で as place vs で as means

Rule of thumb - ask what kind of noun it is. A place gives you "at"; a tool, vehicle, language or material gives you "by / with".

The two で are the same particle historically, and Japanese speakers do not experience them as ambiguous, because the noun settles it instantly.

この部屋へやで作つくりました。
I made it in this room.

この機械きかいで作つくりました。
I made it with this machine.

で - location of an action · で as place vs で as means (cont.)

Identical grammar, and nobody is confused: a room is somewhere you can stand, a machine is something you can use. When both appear at once they simply stack, place first: 台所だいどころでナイフで切きる is grammatical though clumsy - a native speaker would rephrase.

で - location of an action · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 冷蔵庫れいぞうこでビールがあります。 → ✅ 冷蔵庫れいぞうこにビールがあります。
    ある reporting where a thing sits is に. Only an event takes であります.

  • ❌ 学校がっこうに勉強べんきょうします。 → ✅ 学校がっこうで勉強べんきょうします。
    Studying is an activity, and activities are staged with で. に here would try to make the school a destination or a location of existence.

  • ❌ 日本にほんにいちばん大おおきい町まち → ✅ 日本にほんでいちばん大おおきい町まち
    Superlatives set a field of comparison, and that field is marked で.

  • ❌ コンサートはこのホールにあります。 → ✅ コンサートはこのホールであります。
    A concert is held, not located. This is the one place ある takes で.

Meaning · with, by, in, out of - the tool, vehicle, language, material or cause that makes the action possible

で - means, instrument, cause · Nuance & register

で is register-neutral and is not dropped in casual speech - unlike a destination particle, it carries information nothing else supplies. はし食たべる is not casual Japanese, it is broken Japanese.

A few things worth knowing about how it behaves in real sentences:

で - means, instrument, cause · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 一人ひとりで, 二人ふたりで, みんなで. This is the standard way to say "alone",
    "the two of us", "all together". It is very frequent and learners reach for と instead, which does not work with a number.
  • Cause で is neutral and slightly detached. 事故じこで遅おくれました states
    the cause flatly and is perfect for an apology at work, because it does not argue. Compare the reason particle から, which sounds like you are making a case.
  • Amounts. 三日みっかで終おわります - "it'll be done in three days" - and
    五百円ごひゃくえんで買かいました - "I got it for five hundred yen." Here で marks the quantity the action was completed within or for.
  • で is often followed by contrastive は. 電車でんしゃでは行いけません

で - means, instrument, cause · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • "you can't get there by train (whatever else you might manage)."

Because the tool で and the place で look identical, Japanese avoids stacking two of them in one clause. If you need both, most speakers restructure the sentence rather than write two で in a row.

で - means, instrument, cause · で vs に with vehicles

Rule of thumb - 乗のる takes に; every other verb takes で.

This is the highest-frequency で error at N5, because English uses "on" for both.

東京駅とうきょうえきで新幹線しんかんせんに乗のります。
I'll board the shinkansen at Tokyo Station.

新幹線しんかんせんで大阪おおさかまで行いきます。
I'll go to Osaka by shinkansen.

乗のる is "get on", a movement that lands on a point, and points are に. 行いく, 来くる, 帰かえる, 通かよう all describe travel using the vehicle, so they take で. The opposite of 乗のる, 降おりる, takes を.

で - means, instrument, cause · で vs から for material

Rule of thumb - if you can still see what it was made of, use で. If the material was chemically transformed and has disappeared, use から.

このかばんは紙かみで作つくりました。
I made this bag out of paper.

ワインはぶどうから作つくります。
Wine is made from grapes.

で - means, instrument, cause · で vs から for material (cont.)

The bag is still visibly paper. The wine is not visibly grapes - the raw material went in and something else came out. The same split gives 木きで作つくった机つくえ (you can see the wood) but 米こめから作つくる日本酒にほんしゅ (you cannot see the rice). In practice で is far more common; から is reserved for genuine transformations, and native speakers will not object to で in borderline cases.

で - means, instrument, cause · で as means vs で as place

Rule of thumb - the noun decides. Somewhere you could stand is a place; something you could pick up, speak, or blame is a means.

Japanese does not experience these as two words, and neither should you after a little practice - the noun disambiguates before the verb arrives.

自転車じてんしゃで来きました。
I came by bicycle.

自転車じてんしゃ置おき場ばで待まっています。
I'm waiting at the bike park.

で - means, instrument, cause · で as means vs で as place (cont.)

The tricky residue is nouns that could be either. 電話でんわ is a means (電話でんわで話はなす, "talk on the phone"); 会社かいしゃ is a place. When a noun is genuinely ambiguous, Japanese relies on the verb: 新聞しんぶんで知しる is "find out from the newspaper" (means), because 知しる is not something you do at a newspaper.

で - means, instrument, cause · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 電車でんしゃで乗のります。 → ✅ 電車でんしゃに乗のります。
    乗のる is the one transport verb that takes に. Everything else in the travel vocabulary takes で.

  • ❌ 歩あるいてで帰かえりました。 → ✅ 歩あるいて帰かえりました。
    で attaches to nouns only. The て-form already carries the "by doing" sense.

  • ❌ この本ほんは英語えいごを書かいてあります。 → ✅ この本ほんは英語えいごで書かいてあります。
    A language used as a medium is a means. を would try to make English the thing that was written.

  • ❌ 木きから作つくった机つくえ → ✅ 木きで作つくった机つくえ
    The wood is still visible in the desk, so it is で. Save から for materials that have been transformed out of sight.

で - means, instrument, cause · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 友達ともだちと三人さんにんと行いきました。 → ✅ 友達ともだちと三人さんにんで行いきました。
    A number of people is a circumstance, not a companion. Counted groups take で.

から - because, since

Meaning · because - gives the reason for what is said in the other half of the sentence

から follows a whole clause and turns it into a reason. The reason comes first, the consequence second: 「reason + から、result」. It is the reason particle a speaker uses when they are willing to stand behind the reason - which is why it sits so naturally in front of a suggestion, an invitation or a command.

から - because, since · Nuance & register

から is neutral in politeness by itself - it appears in the most casual speech and in formal writing - but it is not neutral in attitude. から presents the reason as yours: something you assert, and are prepared to defend. That has three practical consequences.

It is the natural reason particle in front of a push. Invitations, suggestions, requests and commands almost always take から rather than ので: 時間じかんがないから急いそいで。 The reason exists to justify what you are asking for.

It can sound blunt in an apology. If you are explaining to a customer or a boss why something went wrong, から can read as defensive - "because X, that's why." ので is softer and is the safe default there.

から - because, since · Nuance & register (cont.)

It stands alone at the end of a sentence. ～から。 with nothing after it is extremely common in speech and leaves the consequence implied: 大丈夫だいじょうぶ、私わたしがやるから。 - "It's fine, I'll do it." This has a warm, reassuring feel, and is worth copying.

Two more forms you will meet immediately. ～からです answers a どうして question as a complete sentence: 「どうして休やすんだんですか。」「熱ねつがあったからです。」 And だから at the head of a sentence is this same から promoted to a conjunction: 明日あしたは試験しけんだ。だから、今夜こんやは勉強べんきょうする。 In polite speech that becomes ですから.

から - because, since · から vs ので

Rule of thumb - から is my reason and pushes; ので is the reason and explains. When in doubt with a superior, use ので.

Both mean "because". The difference is stance. から presents a judgement; ので presents a circumstance and lets the listener draw their own conclusion, which makes it sound more deferential.

明日あしたは休やすみだから、ゆっくりしよう。
Tomorrow's a day off, so let's take it easy.

電車でんしゃが遅おくれましたので、少すこし遅刻ちこくします。
My train was delayed, so I'll be a little late.

から - because, since · から vs ので (cont.)

There is a hard grammatical difference too, and it is the one exams test. After a noun or a な-adjective, から takes だ and ので takes な:

  • 病気びょうきだから ／ 病気びょうきなので
  • 静しずかだから ／ 静しずかなので

Finally, a command, a request or a volitional in the second half strongly prefers から. 危あぶないから下さがって! is natural; the ので version sounds oddly formal for a shout.

から - because, since · から vs the causal て-form

Rule of thumb - the て-form can give a cause, but only when the result is something that happened to you, never something you decide.

仕事しごとが忙いそがしくて、返事へんじが遅おくれました。
Work was hectic, and so my reply was late.

仕事しごとが忙いそがしいから、今日きょうは断ことわります。
Work is hectic, so I'm going to turn it down today.

から - because, since · から vs the causal て-form (cont.)

The first result is involuntary - the lateness simply followed. The second is a decision, and a decision needs から. This is why ❌忙いそがしくて、断ことわりましょう is wrong: you cannot hang a volitional, a request or a command off a causal て-form.

から - because, since · から as reason vs から as "from"

Rule of thumb - look at what is in front of it. A clause before から means "because"; a bare noun before から means "from".

The two are the same word historically - "starting from this fact" - and Japanese speakers never confuse them, because the test is mechanical.

日本にほんから来きました。
I came from Japan.

日本にほんが好すきだから来きました。
I came because I love Japan.

から - because, since · から as reason vs から as "from" (cont.)

日本にほん alone is a noun, so the first is a starting point. Add a predicate and the same から flips to a reason. The one place beginners genuinely trip is answering どうして with a bare noun - see the mistakes below.

から - because, since · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 学生がくせいから、安やすいです。 → ✅ 学生がくせいだから、安やすいです。
    A noun cannot sit directly in front of reason から; it needs だ or です. Without it the sentence reads "from a student".

  • ❌ 便利べんりなから、よく使つかいます。 → ✅ 便利べんりだから、よく使つかいます。
    な belongs to ので. から takes だ.

  • ❌ 「どうして休やすんだの?」「風邪かぜから。」 → ✅ 「風邪かぜだから。」
    The bare noun version says "from a cold" and sounds like a starting point. The same fix in polite speech: 風邪かぜだからです／風邪かぜでしたから.

から - because, since · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ (to a client) 担当たんとう者しゃが休やすんでいますから、明日あしたご連絡れんらくします。 → ✅ 担当たんとう者しゃが休やすんでおりますので、明日あしたご連絡れんらくします。
    Grammatically fine, socially blunt. In an apology or an explanation upward, ので removes the note of "and that's your answer".

  • ❌ 寒さむくて、窓まどを閉しめてください。 → ✅ 寒さむいから、窓まどを閉しめてください。
    A request cannot follow a causal て-form. Use から whenever the second half is something you want the listener to do.

から - from

から - from · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • から alone can be the predicate. 会議かいぎは三時さんじからです。 -
    "The meeting is from three." No verb is needed, and the question form works the same way: 何時なんじからですか。
  • からの modifies a noun, just as への does. 田中たなかさんからの手紙てがみ -
    "a letter from Tanaka." This is one of the very few particles that takes の directly.
  • Passage through, not just origin. 窓まどから入はいる,
    裏口うらぐちから出でる. から marks the opening something passes through, and English usually picks "through" rather than "from" here.
  • Range expressions. 一時いちじから三時さんじまで, 東京とうきょうから
    大阪おおさかまで. The pair is so tightly bound that hearing から makes a Japanese listener expect まで.

から - from · Nuance & register (cont.)

から also has an entirely separate life as the reason particle meaning "because". The test is what precedes it: a bare noun gives you "from", a finished clause gives you "because". There is no ambiguity in practice.

から - from · から vs まで

Rule of thumb - から is the near end, まで is the far end. They are a pair, and either can appear alone.

銀行ぎんこうは九時くじから三時さんじまで開あいています。
The bank is open from nine until three.

Use から alone when only the start matters (来月らいげつから働はたらきます), and まで alone when only the end does. What you cannot do is use one where the other belongs:

家いえから駅えきまで自転車じてんしゃで十五分じゅうごふんかかります。
It takes fifteen minutes from home to the station by bike.

から - from · から vs まで (cont.)

Note that まで, not へ, is what pairs with から. A から～へ sentence is not idiomatic Japanese.

から - from · から vs に for receiving

Rule of thumb - with もらう, 借かりる, 聞きく and 習ならう, both work for a person, but only から works for an organisation - and neither works with あげる, which is always に.

姉あねからまんがを借かりました。
I borrowed some manga from my sister.

姉あねにまんがを貸かしました。
I lent some manga to my sister.

から - from · から vs に for receiving (cont.)

When the verb is one of receiving, に and から are both possible for a person, with から making the source a little more explicit and に a little more personal. As soon as the source is a company, a school or a shop, から is the only natural choice: 会社かいしゃから連絡れんらくがありました. When the verb is one of giving - あげる, 貸かす, 送おくる, 教おしえる - the other party is a recipient and takes に only.

から - from · ～てから vs ～たあとで

Rule of thumb - ～てから says the first action is a precondition for the second. ～たあとで just puts them in order.

くすりを飲のんでから寝ねます。
I take my medicine and then go to bed.

映画えいがを見みたあとで、ご飯はんを食たべました。
After we saw the film, we had dinner.

から - from · ～てから vs ～たあとで (cont.)

The medicine has to come first for the sentence to make sense - that is てから. The film and the dinner are simply two events in sequence, which is あとで. Because てから implies dependency, it is what instructions and rules use: 手てを洗あらってから, 確認かくにんしてから. Also note てから cannot take past tense on the first verb, while あとで requires it.

から - from · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 先生せんせいからプレゼントをあげました。 → ✅ 先生せんせいにプレゼントをあげました。
    あげる is giving, so the other party is a recipient. Only receiving verbs allow から.

  • ❌ ご飯はんを食たべるから、くすりを飲のみます。 → ✅ ご飯はんを食たべてから、くすりを飲のみます。
    The wrong version is grammatical but means "because I'm going to eat". "After eating" needs the て-form.

  • ❌ 九時くじからに始はじまります。 → ✅ 九時くじから始はじまります。
    から already marks the time; stacking に on top of it is not possible. Choose one: 九時くじに始はじまります means "starts at nine" with no sense of a span.

から - from · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 「どうして遅おくれたの?」「電車でんしゃから。」 → ✅ 「電車でんしゃが遅おくれたから。」
    A bare noun before から gives the "from" reading - "from the train". A reason needs a full clause in front.

  • ❌ 三時さんじから五時ごじへ勉強べんきょうします。 → ✅ 三時さんじから五時ごじまで勉強べんきょうします。
    The far end of a span is まで. から never pairs with へ.

～てから because the call depends on the meeting being over. The て-form stays uninflected; the tense of the sentence sits on 電話でんわします at the end.

から - from · Practice 4

With the dictionary form, から reads as "because I'm going to open the window", which clashes with the past tense at the end. The て-form gives the intended "after".

から - from · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 田中たなかさんに聞ききました。 (b) 田中たなかさんから聞ききました。

(a) most naturally means "I asked Tanaka" - with に, Tanaka is the person you directed the question at, and the information may or may not have arrived.

(b) means "I heard it from Tanaka". から marks him as the source the information flowed out of, so the sentence is about what you now know, not about the asking. Both are possible with 聞きく, which is exactly why the particle carries the whole difference.

まで

Meaning · until, up to, as far as - and, in one special use, "even"

まで marks the far end. Something continues, and まで says where it stops: an hour on the clock, a station, the moment a condition changes. Whatever it attaches to is included - 五時ごじまで covers five o'clock itself. Its partner is から, which marks the near end.

まで · Formation

まで attaches directly to a noun or to a plain non-past verb.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - place Place + まで 駅えき → 駅えきまで歩あるく
Noun - time Time + まで 五時ごじ → 五時ごじまで働はたらく
Verb - plain non-past dictionary form + まで 終おわる → 終おわるまで待まつ
Paired with から A から B まで 月曜日げつようび → 月曜日げつようびから金曜日きんようびまで
Deadline Noun／Verb + までに 金曜日きんようび → 金曜日きんようびまでに出だす
Noun - "even" Noun + まで 子こども → 子こどもまで知しっている

まで · Formation (cont.)

Note - the verb before まで is always plain and non-past, no matter how the sentence ends. 終おわるまで待まちました is correct; ❌終おわったまで and ❌終おわりますまで are not. The final verb carries all the tense and all the politeness.

まで · Examples

駅えきまで歩あるきます。
I'll walk as far as the station.

五時ごじまで働はたらきます。
I work until five.

雨あめがやむまでここで待まちましょう。
Let's wait here until the rain stops.

この美術館びじゅつかんは月曜日げつようびまで休やすみです。
This museum is closed until Monday.

まで · Examples (cont.)

東京とうきょうから大阪おおさかまで新幹線しんかんせんで行いきました。
I took the shinkansen from Tokyo to Osaka.

そんなことは子こどもまで知しっていますよ。
Even children know that.

試合しあいが終おわるまでだれも席せきを立たたなかった。
Nobody left their seat until the match was over.

昨日きのうは仕事しごとが終おわらなくて、夜中よなかの二時にじまで会社かいしゃに残のこっていました。
I couldn't get finished yesterday and stayed at the office until two in the morning.

まで · Nuance & register

まで is register-neutral. It is identical in plain and polite speech and in writing, and it is never dropped.

Three things about how it feels in use:

まで · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • It is inclusive. 三日みっかまで means the third is still covered. If you need
    "before, not including", Japanese uses 前まえに rather than まで.
  • The "even" use is emphatic and slightly loaded. 先生せんせいまで笑わらった -
    "even the teacher laughed" - presents the teacher as the least likely member of the set. It always carries a note of surprise, and it can sound accusatory if the sentence is a complaint: 君きみまで反対はんたいするのか。 Use it deliberately.
  • までも and までは. 明日あしたまでには終おわります adds "by tomorrow at the
    latest"; そこまでしなくてもいい means "you don't have to go that far". These build on the same core idea of a limit and are worth recognising early.

まで · Nuance & register (cont.)

まで also survives on its own as a sentence-final marker in signs and notices: 受付うけつけは五時ごじまで。 Nothing is missing - the reader supplies です.

まで · まで vs までに

Rule of thumb - まで = "until", for something that continues. までに = "by", for something that happens once, at any point before the deadline.

This is the highest-value distinction on the page, and it is tested at every level.

五時ごじまで会社かいしゃにいます。
I'll be at the office until five.

五時ごじまでに会社かいしゃに戻もどります。
I'll be back at the office by five.

まで · まで vs までに (cont.)

The first stretches across the whole period; the second is a single event that lands somewhere inside it. So the test is the verb. Durative verbs - いる, 待まつ, 働はたらく, 続つづける, 寝ねている - take まで. Punctual verbs - 着つく, 出だす, 帰かえる, 終おわらせる - take までに.

明日あしたの朝あさまでにこの書類しょるいを出だしてください。
Please hand this document in by tomorrow morning.

Say まで there and you are asking someone to keep submitting the document all night.

まで · まで vs から

Rule of thumb - から opens the span, まで closes it. Either can stand alone; when both appear, から always comes first.

夏休なつやすみは七月しちがつから八月はちがつまでです。
The summer holiday runs from July to August.

Alone, each simply leaves the other end open. 来週らいしゅうから忙いそがしいです says nothing about when the busy period ends; 水曜日すいようびまで忙いそがしいです says nothing about when it started. The pairing is so strong that a から early in a sentence sets up an expectation of まで - leaving it unfilled is a stylistic choice, not an error.

まで · まで vs に and へ

Rule of thumb - に and へ give the goal of a movement. まで measures the stretch covered and says where it ended.

空港くうこうまで送おくります。
I'll take you as far as the airport.

空港くうこうに着つきました。
I've arrived at the airport.

まで · まで vs に and へ (cont.)

The first is about the journey - the whole distance is covered and the airport is where your involvement stops; it quietly implies you are not going further. The second is about the endpoint alone. This is why 送おくる, 歩あるく and 走はしる take まで so naturally, and why 着つく cannot: arriving has no stretch to measure.

まで · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 金曜日きんようびまでレポートを出だしてください。 → ✅ 金曜日きんようびまでにレポートを出だしてください。
    Handing something in happens once, so it needs the deadline form. The まで version asks for continuous submission.

  • ❌ 六時ろくじまでに勉強べんきょうしました。 → ✅ 六時ろくじまで勉強べんきょうしました。
    Studying is durative - it filled the period - so it takes plain まで.

  • ❌ 映画えいがが終おわりますまで席せきを立たたないでください。 → ✅ 映画えいがが終おわるまで席せきを立たたないでください。
    The verb in front of まで is always plain non-past. Politeness lives at the end of the sentence.

まで · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 駅えきまでに送おくります。 → ✅ 駅えきまで送おくります。
    With a place, までに would mean "by the time we reach the station". If you mean "as far as", it is plain まで.

  • ❌ 三日みっかまでに出だしてください（meaning "before the 3rd"） → ✅ 二日ふつかまでに出だしてください
    まで and までに include the point they name, so the first sentence allows the 3rd itself. To exclude it, name the day before, or say 三日みっかの前まえに.

まで · Practice 1

1. 宿題しゅくだいは水曜日すいようび____出だしてください。

宿題しゅくだいは水曜日すいようびまでに出だしてください。

Handing in homework is a one-off act, so Wednesday is a deadline, not a stretch. Plain まで would ask the student to keep handing it in continuously until Wednesday.

まで · Practice 2

2. 父ちちは毎晩まいばん遅おそく（まで・までに）仕事しごとをしています。

父ちちは毎晩まいばん遅おそくまで仕事しごとをしています。

仕事しごとをする here is durative - it fills the evening - so it takes まで. までに needs a verb that happens at a single moment.

まで · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'll wait here until you get back."

待まつ is the classic durative verb, so まで. The verb in front of まで stays in the plain non-past - 帰かえってくる, not 帰かえってきた - even though the return is in the future.

まで · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 会議かいぎが始はじまりますまで、ロビーでお待まちください。

✅ 会議かいぎが始はじまるまで、ロビーでお待まちください。 "Please wait in the lobby until the meeting begins."

まで takes the plain dictionary form. The sentence is still perfectly polite: お待まちください at the end carries that, and it is the only place politeness is marked.

まで · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 五時ごじまでにレポートを書かきます。 (b) 五時ごじまでレポートを書かきます。

(a) is a promise about a deadline: the report will be finished at some point before five. Nothing is said about when you actually work on it - you might write it in the last ten minutes.

(b) is a description of how you will spend the time: you will be writing the report right up to five o'clock. It says nothing about whether it gets finished. Native speakers hear these as completely different commitments, which is why the extra に matters so much.

と - and, joining a complete list

Meaning · and - joins nouns into a list, and claims the list is complete

と glues nouns together: A と B is "A and B". The part learners are not told is that と is exhaustive. A と B と C says those three are all of them. If you only want to name a couple of examples out of many, Japanese has a different particle, や, and using と where you meant や makes a claim you did not intend.

と - and, joining a complete list · Formation

と sits between the nouns. The last noun takes whatever particle the sentence needs; the と-group behaves as a single noun phrase.

Attaches to Form Example
Two nouns Noun と Noun パン → パンと卵たまご
Three or more Noun と Noun と Noun 犬いぬ → 犬いぬと猫ねこと鳥とり
The group takes a particle (N と N) + が／を／は 父ちち・母はは → 父ちちと母ははが来きます
Choosing between two N と N、どちらが… コーヒー → コーヒーと紅茶こうちゃ、どちらが

と - and, joining a complete list · Formation (cont.)

Note - と joins nouns only. It cannot join verbs, adjectives or clauses. For those, Japanese uses the て-form (安やすくて便利べんり) or そして (買かい物ものをしました。そして、帰かえりました). The と you meet at the end of a clause - 春はるになると暖あたたかくなる - is a different word entirely, a conditional.

と - and, joining a complete list · Examples

パンと牛乳ぎゅうにゅうを買かいました。
I bought bread and milk.

父ちちと母ははは大阪おおさかにいます。
My father and mother are in Osaka.

朝あさご飯はんは卵たまごとごはんです。
Breakfast is eggs and rice.

すみません、コーヒーとケーキをください。
Excuse me - a coffee and a cake, please.

と - and, joining a complete list · Examples (cont.)

英語えいごと中国語ちゅうごくごとフランス語ごが話はなせます。
I can speak English, Chinese and French.

会議かいぎに出でるのは部長ぶちょうと私わたしの二人ふたりだけです。
The only two attending the meeting are the manager and me.

この料理りょうりには塩しおとこしょうとオリーブオイルしか使つかっていません。
This dish uses nothing but salt, pepper and olive oil.

旅行りょこうに持もっていくのはパスポートとカメラと薬くすりぐらいです。
About all I'm taking on the trip is my passport, a camera and some medicine.

と - and, joining a complete list · Nuance & register

と is register-neutral and is never dropped. In a list of three or more it is repeated between every pair - Japanese has no equivalent of the English "A, B and C" where the first joins are commas. A comma-only list (犬いぬ、猫ねこ、鳥とり) does exist, but it reads as a written enumeration, like items on a form, rather than as speech.

A trailing と after the last item - 本ほんと、ノートと。 - is heard when someone is still counting things off out loud, and it signals "…and there may be more coming". Once the list is finished, drop it. In a written sentence a trailing と before a particle is simply wrong: ❌本ほんとノートとが.

と - and, joining a complete list · Nuance & register (cont.)

The exhaustiveness of と is what makes it useful in shops, orders and instructions, where you want to be precise. 材料ざいりょうは小麦粉こむぎこと水みずと塩しおです means those are the ingredients, full stop. In relaxed conversation about vaguer things, や is often the more natural choice, and over-using と can make you sound like you are reading from a list.

One more thing と does that looks unrelated but is the same idea: it sets up a two-way comparison. コーヒーと紅茶こうちゃ、どちらがいいですか。 The two items are being presented as a closed set to choose between - exhaustive again.

と - and, joining a complete list · と vs や

Rule of thumb - と says "these and no others". や says "these among others".

かばんの中なかには財布さいふと鍵かぎが入はいっています。
There's a wallet and keys in my bag.

かばんの中なかには財布さいふや鍵かぎが入はいっています。
There's a wallet, keys and so on in my bag.

The first is a complete inventory - that bag holds two things. The second offers a sample; there is clearly other stuff in there. Because や is a sample, it very often finishes with など: 財布さいふや鍵かぎなど. That など is only available to や; ❌財布さいふと鍵かぎなど is contradictory.

と - and, joining a complete list · と vs や (cont.)

Practical consequence: if someone asks what is in a place and you are not sure you have listed everything, や protects you. If you are giving an order or a specification, use と.

と - and, joining a complete list · と vs the て-form and そして

Rule of thumb - と is for nouns. To link adjectives use the て-form; to link actions use the て-form or そして.

This is the most frequent と error, because English "and" does all three jobs.

この部屋へやは広ひろくて明あかるいです。
This room is spacious and bright.

朝あさご飯はんを食たべて、会社かいしゃへ行いきました。
I had breakfast and went to work.

Both would be ungrammatical with と. The rule has no exceptions worth learning at N5: if what you are joining ends in い, な or a verb, と is not the tool.

と - and, joining a complete list · と as "and" vs と as "with"

Rule of thumb - if the と-phrase is one of the things the verb is about, it is "and". If it names a person the subject acts together with, it is "with".

The same two words can go either way, and only the sentence shape decides.

田中たなかさんと山田やまださんが来きました。
Tanaka and Yamada came.

田中たなかさんが山田やまださんと来きました。
Tanaka came with Yamada.

と - and, joining a complete list · と as "and" vs と as "with" (cont.)

In the first, が follows the whole group, so the group is one subject: two people arrived. In the second, が has already marked Tanaka as the subject, so 山田やまださん can only be a companion. Watch where the subject particle lands and the ambiguity disappears. Adding 一緒いっしょに forces the "with" reading outright.

と - and, joining a complete list · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この町まちは静しずかときれいです。 → ✅ この町まちは静しずかで、きれいです。
    と cannot join adjectives. な-adjectives link with で, い-adjectives with the て-form.

  • ❌ 朝あさご飯はんを食たべたと、出でかけました。 → ✅ 朝あさご飯はんを食たべて、出でかけました。
    Two actions in sequence are linked with the て-form. と after a clause makes a conditional, not a list.

  • ❌ りんごとバナナとなど好すきです。 → ✅ りんごやバナナなどが好すきです。
    など marks an open-ended sample, which contradicts the "complete list" meaning of と. など belongs with や.

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんと山田やまださんとが来きました。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんと山田やまださんが来きました。
    No と after the final item in a written sentence. The group takes its particle directly.

と - and, joining a complete list · Practice 1

1. 机つくえの上うえにノート____ペンがあります。

Two nouns joined into one subject group, and the claim is that those are the two things on the desk. If you were only naming examples of what was lying there, や would be the choice.

An order is exactly where と belongs - you are stating the complete list of what you want. Using や here would leave the waiter unsure whether more was coming.

と - and, joining a complete list · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この部屋へやは広ひろいときれいです。

✅ この部屋へやは広ひろくてきれいです。 "This room is spacious and clean."

と joins nouns, never adjectives. An い-adjective drops its final い and takes くて. As written, 広ひろいと would be read as a conditional - "if it's spacious".

と - and, joining a complete list · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) パーティーには田中たなかさんと山田やまださんが来きました。 (b) パーティーには田中たなかさんや山田やまださんが来きました。

(a) reports the full guest list: two people came, and naming a third would contradict the sentence. This is what you say when the party was small or when you are being precise.

(b) picks two names out of a crowd. や implies other guests you have not bothered to mention, so the party was bigger - and it protects you if you have forgotten someone.

と - with, accompaniment

Meaning · with - names the person you do something together with, or the other party in a two-sided action

と marks a partner. Sometimes the partner is optional - you could have gone to the film alone - and sometimes the verb cannot exist without one: you cannot marry, argue or shake hands by yourself. Both are the same と.

と - with, accompaniment · Formation

と attaches directly to a noun, almost always a person or an animal, and the phrase then modifies the verb.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - companion Person + と + verb 友達ともだち → 友達ともだちと行いく
Noun - companion, emphasised Person + と一緒いっしょに + verb 家族かぞく → 家族かぞくと一緒いっしょに食たべる
Noun - the other party in a mutual act Person + と + 結婚けっこんする／けんかする／会あう 彼かれ → 彼かれと結婚けっこんする
Question word だれ + と だれ → だれと来きましたか
Combined with a count Person + と + number + で 友達ともだち → 友達ともだちと二人ふたりで

と - with, accompaniment · Formation (cont.)

Note - 一緒いっしょに is optional but common. It adds nothing grammatically; it just makes the togetherness explicit and slightly warmer. The に is not optional: ❌友達ともだちと一緒いっしょ行いく.

友達ともだちと映画えいがを見みました。
I saw a film with a friend.

だれと来きましたか。
Who did you come with?

姉あねと一緒いっしょに買かい物ものに行いきます。
I'm going shopping with my older sister.

去年きょねん、大学だいがくの先輩せんぱいと結婚けっこんしました。
Last year I married a senior from my university.

I've arranged to speak with my teacher tomorrow.

At the weekend I'm going to the river with my family for a barbecue.

I'll get back to you once I've talked it over with my section chief.

と - with, accompaniment · Nuance & register

と is register-neutral and is not dropped, even in fast speech - remove it and the sentence loses the only thing marking the companion.

The set it belongs to is worth naming, because it decides the particle for a whole class of verbs. Mutual verbs need two parties, and the second one is always と: 結婚けっこんする, 離婚りこんする, けんかする, 相談そうだんする, 約束やくそくする, 話はなす, 似にる - with the exception noted below. Learn these as と-verbs and a large slice of N4 grammar comes for free.

と - with, accompaniment · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two frequency notes. だれと is the standard way to ask "who with?", and it comes up constantly in small talk about the weekend. And と一緒いっしょに is the polite, slightly fuller version you use when the companionship is the point: 部長ぶちょうと一緒いっしょに伺うかがいます。

Finally, keep this と separate from the quotation と, which follows a whole clause and precedes 言いう, 思おもう or 書かく: 明日あした行いくと言いいました。 If a full sentence sits in front of と, it is quotation, not accompaniment. And if a bare noun sits in front and the group is what the verb is about, it is the "and" と instead.

と - with, accompaniment · と vs で

Rule of thumb - と needs a named companion. A number of people takes で.

This pair trips up almost everyone, because English says "with two people" and "with a friend" the same way.

友達ともだちと行いきました。
I went with a friend.

三人さんにんで行いきました。
The three of us went.

You cannot say ❌三人さんにんと行いきました, because a bare number names nobody. The same logic gives 一人ひとりで for "alone" and みんなで for "all together".

と - with, accompaniment · と vs で (cont.)

The two combine happily when you want both facts at once:

同僚どうりょうと四人よにんで飲のみに行いきました。
I went out drinking with colleagues, four of us in all.

と - with, accompaniment · と vs に with mutual verbs

Rule of thumb - と presents the action as two-sided; に presents it as one-directional, aimed at the other person.

Several verbs take either particle, and the choice changes the picture.

駅えきで先生せんせいに会あいました。
I ran into my teacher at the station.

駅えきで先生せんせいと会あいました。
I met up with my teacher at the station.

と - with, accompaniment · と vs に with mutual verbs (cont.)

With に, you did the meeting - it can be an accident, and often is. With と, both parties met, which implies it was arranged. The same split runs through 相談そうだんする (上司じょうしに = you take the problem to them; 上司じょうしと = you thrash it out together) and 話はなす.

Some verbs allow only one. 結婚けっこんする and けんかする are と only, because they are unavoidably two-sided. 似にる is に only - 父ちちに似にています, "I take after my father" - even though resemblance sounds mutual in English.

と - with, accompaniment · と as "with" vs と as "and"

Rule of thumb - find the subject marker. If が or は follows the whole と-group, it is "and". If the subject has already been named elsewhere, the と-phrase is "with".

兄あにと姉あねは東京とうきょうに住すんでいます。
My brother and sister live in Tokyo.

兄あには姉あねと東京とうきょうに住すんでいます。
My brother lives in Tokyo with my sister.

In the first, は scopes over both people, so they are one subject: two residents. In the second, は has already claimed 兄あに, so 姉あねと can only be accompaniment.

と - with, accompaniment · と as "with" vs と as "and" (cont.)

When there is no subject particle at all - a bare 友達ともだちと行いきました - the reading is "with", because a list of things needs a slot in the sentence and this one has none. Adding 一緒いっしょに removes any doubt.

と - with, accompaniment · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 一人ひとりと行いきました。 → ✅ 一人ひとりで行いきました。
    A count of people is a circumstance, not a companion. Numbers take で.

  • ❌ 友達ともだちと三人さんにんと行いきました。 → ✅ 友達ともだちと三人さんにんで行いきました。
    The first と names the companions; the number that follows still needs で.

  • ❌ 山田やまださんに結婚けっこんしました。 → ✅ 山田やまださんと結婚けっこんしました。
    Marrying is unavoidably two-sided, so it is と only. The に version is a very common learner error and sounds strange to a native ear.

と - with, accompaniment · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんと一緒いっしょ行いきます。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんと一緒いっしょに行いきます。
    一緒いっしょに is a fixed adverbial phrase and cannot lose its に.

  • ❌ 父ちちと似にています。 → ✅ 父ちちに似にています。
    似にる is the mutual-looking verb that takes に. Resemblance in Japanese is modelled as one thing pointing at another.

と - with, accompaniment · Practice 1

1. 昨日きのう、同おなじクラスの人ひと____勉強べんきょうしました。

昨日きのう、同おなじクラスの人ひとと勉強べんきょうしました。

A named person doing the activity alongside you is a companion, which is と. で would be wrong here because 人ひと is not a count.

と - with, accompaniment · Practice 2

2. 旅行りょこうには一人ひとり（と・で）行いきました。

旅行りょこうには一人ひとりで行いきました。

一人ひとり is a number of people, not a person, so it takes で. There is no companion to mark - that is the whole point of the sentence.

と - with, accompaniment · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "There's something I'd like to discuss with the manager."

部長ぶちょうと相談そうだんしたいことがあります。

相談そうだんする with と frames it as a two-sided discussion, which is what "discuss with" means. 部長ぶちょうに相談そうだんする is also correct Japanese, but it means taking a problem to him for advice.

と - with, accompaniment · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 去年きょねん、高校こうこうの友達ともだちに結婚けっこんしました。

✅ 去年きょねん、高校こうこうの友達ともだちと結婚けっこんしました。 "Last year I married a friend from high school."

結婚けっこんする belongs to the mutual-verb set, where the other party must be と. に would try to make the friend a recipient, which makes no sense for a marriage.

と - with, accompaniment · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部長ぶちょうに話はなしました。 (b) 部長ぶちょうと話はなしました。

(a) is one-directional: you told him something. The information went from you to him, and the sentence says nothing about whether he replied - this is what you say after reporting a problem upward.

(b) is two-sided: the two of you had a conversation. Both people spoke, and the sentence implies some exchange took place. Choosing と with a superior can also sound a shade more equal, so に is the safer default when you are simply reporting.
