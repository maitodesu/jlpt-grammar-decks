に

Meaning · marks a point - where something ends up, where it sits, when it happens, or who receives it

に names a point, never a stretch or an area. The place a movement finishes, the spot a thing occupies, the moment on the clock, the person a thing lands with: all of those are single points, and に is how Japanese pins them down. Nearly every use below is a version of that one idea.

に · Formation

に is a particle. It attaches straight onto a noun, a time word, or a ます-stem, and nothing changes shape.

に · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - destination Noun + に + motion verb 学校(がっこう) → 学校(がっこう)に行(い)く
Noun - where something exists Noun + に + ある／いる 台所(だいどころ) → 台所(だいどころ)にある
Noun - person who receives Person + に + giving or telling verb 母(はは) → 母(はは)に話(はな)す
Time word with a number or a name Time + に 六時(ろくじ) → 六時(ろくじ)に起(お)きる
Verb ます-stem - purpose stem + に + 行(い)く／来(く)る／帰(かえ)る 買(か)います → 買(か)いに行(い)く

に · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - thing entered, boarded, sat on Noun + に + 入(はい)る／乗(の)る／座(すわ)る いす → いすに座(すわ)る
Period - rate or frequency Period + に + number 週(しゅう) → 週(しゅう)に三回(さんかい)

に · Formation (cont.)

Note - which time words take に? If the time word is a number or a fixed name on the calendar, it takes に: 七時(しちじ)に, 三月(さんがつ)に, 月曜日(げつようび)に, 二十日(はつか)に, 誕生日(たんじょうび)に. If it is positioned relative to now, or if it is a frequency word, it takes nothing at all: 今日(きょう), 明日(あした), 昨日(きのう), 今朝(けさ), 今晩(こんばん), 先週(せんしゅう), 来年(らいねん), 毎日(まいにち), いつ. Adding に to those is one of the most persistent N5 errors. 朝(あさ), 夜(よる) and 夏(なつ) sit in between - both forms are heard, and the bare one is more common in speech.

I get up at seven.

ゆうべ母(はは)に電話(でんわ)しました。
I phoned my mother last night.

ちょっとパンを買(か)いに行(い)ってきます。
I'm just popping out to buy some bread.

来週(らいしゅう)の水曜日(すいようび)に大阪(おおさか)で会議(かいぎ)があります。
There's a meeting in Osaka next Wednesday.

I swim at the gym three times a week.

すみません、この書類(しょるい)を田中(たなか)さんに渡(わた)してもらえますか。

に · Nuance & register

に is completely register-neutral. It is the same in plain speech, in ます-form, in business email and in newspapers. Choosing に never makes you sound stiff or casual.

What varies is whether you say it. In quick casual speech the destination に is routinely dropped:

  • どこ行(い)くの? - "Where are you off to?"
  • ちょっとコンビニ行(い)ってくる。 - "Just nipping to the convenience store."

That dropping is limited to destinations. The に of existence, the に of a recipient, and the に of a clock time are not dropped - remove them and the sentence stops parsing rather than sounding casual.

に is phonologically weightless. It never carries stress, and it attaches so tightly to the preceding noun that Japanese speakers hear 六時(ろくじ)に as one word. Do not pause before it.

に · Nuance & register (cont.)

One more use worth knowing early, because it turns up constantly: に marks the result of a change, with なる and する. 姉(あね)は医者(いしゃ)になりました。 部屋(へや)をきれいにしてください。 It is the same "landing point" idea - the thing ends up at that state.

に · に vs で

This is the single most common particle error at N5, and it is worth over-learning.

Rule of thumb - に marks where something is; で marks where something happens.

If the verb is ある, いる, 住(す)む, 座(すわ)る, 置(お)く, 泊(と)まる - verbs about a thing occupying a spot - use に. If the verb is an activity that could happen anywhere, use で.

公園(こうえん)にベンチがあります。
There's a bench in the park.

公園(こうえん)でお弁当(べんとう)を食(た)べました。
We ate our packed lunch in the park.

Same park; different job. The bench is located there, so に. The eating took place there, so で.

に · に vs で (cont.)

The trap that catches nearly everyone is 住(す)む. In English "live in Tokyo" feels like an activity, but Japanese treats it as being located somewhere:

兄(あに)は東京(とうきょう)に住(す)んでいます。
My older brother lives in Tokyo.

❌東京(とうきょう)で住(す)んでいます is wrong. Learn 住(す)む as a に-verb the way you learn a verb's conjugation.

に · に vs へ

Rule of thumb - for a destination they are interchangeable. Everywhere else, only に works.

空港(くうこう)に着(つ)きました。
I've arrived at the airport.

Swap that に for へ and the sentence is odd, because 着(つ)く is about the arrival point, which is に territory. Where the verb is plain movement - 行(い)く, 来(く)る, 帰(かえ)る - either particle is fine and the difference is a shade of emphasis: に on the endpoint, へ on the heading.

The clean division is what へ cannot do. It cannot mark a time (❌六時(ろくじ)へ), it cannot mark existence (❌部屋(へや)へいます), and it cannot mark the person you tell or ask something (❌先生(せんせい)へ聞(き)きました). All three are に.

に · に vs を with verbs of movement

Rule of thumb - go in, get on, sit down: に. Go out, get off, pass through: を.

English uses a preposition for both directions, so learners reach for one particle for all of them. Japanese splits them. Entering, boarding and attaching take に, because they end at a point. Leaving and traversing take を, because the noun is the thing being departed or crossed.

次(つぎ)の駅(えき)で急行(きゅうこう)に乗(の)ります。
I'll get on the express at the next station.

次(つぎ)の駅(えき)で電車(でんしゃ)を降(お)ります。
I'll get off the train at the next station.

に · に vs を with verbs of movement (cont.)

The same pairing runs through 入(はい)る／出(で)る and 大学(だいがく)に入(はい)る／大学(だいがく)を出(で)る.

に · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日(あした)に東京(とうきょう)へ行(い)きます。 → ✅ 明日(あした)東京(とうきょう)へ行(い)きます。
    Time words that float relative to now - 今日(きょう), 明日(あした), 毎日(まいにち), 来週(らいしゅう) - take no particle at all.

  • ❌ 公園(こうえん)でベンチがあります。 → ✅ 公園(こうえん)にベンチがあります。
    ある and いる report where a thing exists, and existence is always に.

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)を聞(き)きました。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)に聞(き)きました。
    The person you ask is a recipient, not an object. 聞(き)く takes を for the question and に for the person.

  • ❌ パンを買(か)うに行(い)きます。 → ✅ パンを買(か)いに行(い)きます。
    Purpose に attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form. With a する-noun you can drop the verb entirely: 買(か)い物(もの)に行(い)く.

に · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 東京(とうきょう)で住(す)んでいます。 → ✅ 東京(とうきょう)に住(す)んでいます。
    住(す)む is a location-of-existence verb in Japanese, however active "live" feels in English.

に · Practice 1

1. 来週(らいしゅう)の金曜日(きんようび)____友達(ともだち)に会(あ)います。

来週(らいしゅう)の金曜日(きんようび)に友達(ともだち)に会(あ)います。

金曜日(きんようび) is a named day on the calendar, so it takes に. Note that 来週(らいしゅう) on its own would take nothing - it is the 金曜日(きんようび) inside the phrase that licenses the particle.

2. 机(つくえ)の上(うえ)（に・で）辞書(じしょ)があります。

机(つくえ)の上(うえ)に辞書(じしょ)があります。

The verb is ある, which reports where a thing exists, and existence takes に. で would need an action happening on the desk, which a dictionary is not doing.

に · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I go to the gym twice a week."

週(しゅう)に二回(にかい)ジムに行(い)きます。

Two different jobs for the same particle. The first に is rate - a number of times per period. The second is destination. Saying 週(しゅう)二回(にかい) without に is possible in casual speech but the full form is safer.

に · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 毎朝(まいあさ)に六時(ろくじ)起(お)きて、会社(かいしゃ)に行(い)きます。

✅ 毎朝(まいあさ)六時(ろくじ)に起(お)きて、会社(かいしゃ)に行(い)きます。 "I get up at six every morning and go to work."

The two particles are swapped. 毎朝(まいあさ) is a frequency word and takes nothing; 六時(ろくじ) is a clock time and needs に.

に · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) パンを買(か)いに行(い)きます。 (b) パンを買(か)って行(い)きます。

(a) is purpose: buying bread is the reason for going. You set off, and the errand is what the trip is for. Nothing is said about where you go afterwards.

(b) is sequence: you buy bread and then continue on your way. The going is already planned, and the bread is picked up en route - this is what you would say about bringing something to a friend's house.

へ - direction, toward

Meaning · points movement in a direction - toward, off in the direction of

へ orients. It says which way something is heading, and unlike に it says nothing about arriving. That is why へ works for compass directions and for the address on an envelope, and why it is useless for times, for existence, and for people you talk to.

Note - へ is read e. When it is a particle, the hiragana へ is pronounced え, not he. 東京(とうきょう)へ is Tōkyō e. It is written へ for historical reasons only, exactly like the topic particle は being read wa.

へ - direction, toward · Formation

へ is a particle and attaches directly to a noun. The noun does not change.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - place you head for Place + へ + motion verb 京都(きょうと) → 京都(きょうと)へ行(い)く
Noun - compass or relative direction Direction + へ 右(みぎ) → 右(みぎ)へ曲(ま)がる
Noun - addressee of a letter or gift Person + へ 母(はは) → 母(はは)へ
Noun modifying another noun Noun + への + Noun 東京(とうきょう) → 東京(とうきょう)への電車(でんしゃ)
Fixed greeting ようこそ + Place + へ 日本(にほん) → ようこそ日本(にほん)へ

へ - direction, toward · Formation (cont.)

Note - the fourth row is the one grammatical thing へ can do that に cannot. へ takes の and modifies a following noun; に does not. 友達(ともだち)へのプレゼント is natural, ❌友達(ともだち)にのプレゼント is not Japanese.

I'm going to the station.

Where are you going?

あの角(かど)を右(みぎ)へ曲(ま)がってください。

I wrote a letter to my grandmother.

東京(とうきょう)への電車(でんしゃ)はもう出(で)てしまいました。
The train to Tokyo has already left.

台風(たいふう)が北(きた)へ進(すす)んでいるので、明日(あした)の飛行機(ひこうき)は飛(と)ばないかもしれません。
The typhoon is moving north, so tomorrow's flight may not take off.

へ - direction, toward · Nuance & register

へ is neutral in politeness - it is not more formal than に in the way learners are sometimes told. What is true is that へ is more common in writing and in slightly composed speech, and に dominates ordinary conversation. Announcements, timetables, travel writing and envelopes lean へ.

Two places where へ is essentially fixed and swapping in に would sound wrong:

  • Envelopes and dedications. 田中(たなか)様(さま)へ on the front of a card,
    両親(りょうしん)へ at the top of a letter. The へ here is "to, in the direction of you" rather than "handed to you".
  • ようこそ～へ. 大阪(おおさか)へようこそ, ようこそわが社(しゃ)へ. A set frame.

In fast casual speech へ is dropped just as に is - どこ行(い)くの? - so the choice disappears entirely at the most informal level.

へ - direction, toward · Nuance & register (cont.)

One last register point: because へ emphasises the heading rather than the arrival, it carries a faint flavour of a journey being undertaken. 海(うみ)へ is a touch more evocative than 海(うみ)に. Song titles and book titles exploit this constantly. You do not need to reproduce the effect, but recognise it when you meet it.

へ - direction, toward · へ vs に

Rule of thumb - へ is the heading; に is the landing point. For plain 行(い)く／来(く)る／帰(かえ)る they overlap almost completely, so pick either. Outside destinations, only に exists.

山(やま)の方(ほう)へ歩(ある)いていきました。
He walked off in the direction of the mountains.

山(やま)の上(うえ)に着(つ)きました。
We reached the top of the mountain.

The first says nothing about whether he got anywhere; へ only gives the bearing. The second is entirely about arrival, and that is a point, so に.

へ - direction, toward · へ vs に (cont.)

Because of this, verbs that focus on the endpoint prefer に: 着(つ)く, 入(はい)る, 乗(の)る, 届(とど)く. Verbs of open-ended motion happily take へ: 向(む)かう, 進(すす)む, 飛(と)ぶ, 歩(ある)く.

And the hard boundary is worth restating. へ cannot mark a time (❌九時(くじ)へ), cannot mark existence (❌部屋(へや)へ犬(いぬ)がいます), and cannot mark the person you tell or ask (❌先生(せんせい)へ質問(しつもん)しました). Those are に jobs, all three.

へ - direction, toward · へ vs まで

Rule of thumb - へ gives the direction of travel; まで gives the far end of the stretch you cover.

駅(えき)へ走(はし)りました。
I ran to the station.

駅(えき)まで走(はし)りました。
I ran as far as the station.

The first is about where you were headed. The second measures the running: the station is the point where it stopped, and the sentence implies the whole distance was covered on foot. That is why まで pairs with から and へ does not: 家(いえ)から駅(えき)まで - a span with two ends.

へ - direction, toward · へ vs を

Rule of thumb - へ marks the place you are heading for; を marks the space you move through.

English "go along the river" and "go to the river" both look like one preposition slot, so learners over-use へ. When the noun is the route rather than the goal, Japanese uses を - with 通(とお)る, 渡(わた)る, 歩(ある)く, 散歩(さんぽ)する, 曲(ま)がる.

この橋(はし)を渡(わた)ると、郵便局(ゆうびんきょく)があります。
Cross this bridge and there's a post office.

あの橋(はし)へ行(い)きましょう。
Let's go over to that bridge.

へ - direction, toward · へ vs を (cont.)

Note that 曲(ま)がる takes both, in different slots: 角(かど)を右(みぎ)へ曲(ま)がる - you turn at the corner, toward the right.

へ - direction, toward · Common mistakes

  • ❌ pronouncing 学校(がっこう)へ as "gakkō he" → ✅ "gakkō e"
    The particle へ is read え. Learners who first meet it in writing often carry the wrong pronunciation for years.

  • ❌ 七時(しちじ)へ駅(えき)へ来(き)てください。 → ✅ 七時(しちじ)に駅(えき)へ来(き)てください。
    へ cannot mark a time. Only the destination slot is available to it.

  • ❌ 教室(きょうしつ)へ先生(せんせい)がいます。 → ✅ 教室(きょうしつ)に先生(せんせい)がいます。
    Existence with ある／いる is always に. へ needs movement.

  • ❌ 東京(とうきょう)にの荷物(にもつ) → ✅ 東京(とうきょう)への荷物(にもつ)
    Only へ combines with の to modify a following noun.

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)へ聞(き)きました。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)に聞(き)きました。
    A person you question is a recipient, not a direction. へ marks a person only when the thing sent is physical - a letter, a parcel, a present.

へ - direction, toward · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 南(みなみ)へ向(む)かっています。 (b) 南(みなみ)の島(しま)に着(つ)きました。

(a) reports a bearing. 向(む)かう is open-ended motion, and へ gives the direction without committing to a destination - you are somewhere on the way, pointing south.

(b) reports an arrival. 着(つ)く is entirely about the endpoint, which is a point, so に is required; ❌島(しま)へ着(つ)きました sounds wrong to a native ear.

で - location of an action

Meaning · at, in - the place where something is done

で marks the stage the action is played out on. Not where a thing sits, not where a movement ends: where the doing happens. If you can put "at" or "in" in front of an English verb phrase and it still describes an activity, Japanese almost certainly wants で.

で - location of an action · Formation

で is a particle and attaches straight onto a noun.

で - location of an action · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - place of an action Place + で + action verb 図書館(としょかん) → 図書館(としょかん)で勉強(べんきょう)する
Noun - organisation or institution Org + で + verb 会社(かいしゃ) → 会社(かいしゃ)で働(はたら)く

Note - one apparent exception that is really the rule. An event is something that takes place, so it uses で even with ある: 試験(しけん)は大学(だいがく)であります。 Compare a thing, which uses に: 大学(だいがく)に図書館(としょかん)があります。 The particle tells you whether ある means "is held" or "is located".

We played soccer in the park.

My mother is making dinner in the kitchen.

日本(にほん)でいちばん高(たか)い山(やま)は富士山(ふじさん)です。

Next week's party is being held in this hall.

I usually have lunch at a café near the office.

先週(せんしゅう)の日曜日(にちようび)、駅前(えきまえ)の映画館(えいがかん)で話題(わだい)の映画(えいが)を見(み)ました。
Last Sunday I saw that much-talked-about film at the cinema by the station.

で - location of an action · Nuance & register

で is register-neutral. It behaves identically in casual speech, in ます-form and in writing, and it is never dropped the way a destination に can be - the sentence would become ambiguous, so speakers keep it.

Two habits of real speech are worth noting. First, で is very often followed by は for contrast: 家(いえ)では静(しず)かです - "at home (as opposed to elsewhere) he's quiet." Second, in a topic-first sentence the place can be lifted to the front with では: 日本(にほん)では靴(くつ)を脱(ぬ)ぎます。 That では is this same で, just wearing a topic marker.

The scope use - 世界(せかい)でいちばん, クラスで一番(いちばん) - feels like a different word to learners, but it is the same idea stretched: the comparison is carried out within that field. It appears constantly in N5 superlative sentences, so learn it as part of this particle rather than as a separate item.

で - location of an action · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, note that で is homophonous with the て-form of です／だ and with the て-form of verbs like 読(よ)む → 読(よ)んで. Context separates them instantly, but a beginner reading aloud can be thrown. If a noun of place sits immediately before it, it is this particle.

で - location of an action · で vs に

Rule of thumb - で is where something happens; に is where something is.

Ask what the verb is doing. An activity - 食(た)べる, 働(はたら)く, 遊(あそ)ぶ, 会(あ)う, 買(か)う, 泳(およ)ぐ - takes で. A verb of existence or position - ある, いる, 住(す)む, 座(すわ)る, 置(お)く - takes に.

駅前(えきまえ)のカフェで友達(ともだち)を待(ま)っています。
I'm waiting for my friend at the café by the station.

駅前(えきまえ)に新(あたら)しいカフェができました。
A new café has opened up by the station.

Waiting is an activity; the café is its stage, so で. Coming into existence is not an activity in this sense - the café now is there - so に.

で - location of an action · で vs に (cont.)

The event/thing split under ある is where this rule earns its keep:

三階(さんがい)に会議室(かいぎしつ)があります。
There's a meeting room on the third floor.

会議(かいぎ)は三階(さんがい)であります。
The meeting is on the third floor.

Same building, same verb, opposite particles - because a room is located and a meeting is held.

で - location of an action · で vs を

Rule of thumb - で is the place an action is set in; を is the space the action moves across.

A handful of motion verbs - 歩(ある)く, 走(はし)る, 飛(と)ぶ, 渡(わた)る, 通(とお)る, 散歩(さんぽ)する - can take the place as を, and the meaning shifts.

公園(こうえん)で走(はし)っています。
He's out running in the park.

公園(こうえん)を走(はし)っています。
He's running through the park.

で - location of an action · で vs を (cont.)

The first frames the park as the venue for a jog. The second traces a path across it - he entered one side and is heading for the other. With any verb that is not motion, を is simply unavailable for a place.

で - location of an action · で as place vs で as means

Rule of thumb - ask what kind of noun it is. A place gives you "at"; a tool, vehicle, language or material gives you "by / with".

The two で are the same particle historically, and Japanese speakers do not experience them as ambiguous, because the noun settles it instantly.

この部屋(へや)で作(つく)りました。
I made it in this room.

この機械(きかい)で作(つく)りました。
I made it with this machine.

で - location of an action · で as place vs で as means (cont.)

Identical grammar, and nobody is confused: a room is somewhere you can stand, a machine is something you can use. When both appear at once they simply stack, place first: 台所(だいどころ)でナイフで切(き)る is grammatical though clumsy - a native speaker would rephrase.

で - location of an action · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)でビールがあります。 → ✅ 冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)にビールがあります。
    ある reporting where a thing sits is に. Only an event takes であります.

  • ❌ 学校(がっこう)に勉強(べんきょう)します。 → ✅ 学校(がっこう)で勉強(べんきょう)します。
    Studying is an activity, and activities are staged with で. に here would try to make the school a destination or a location of existence.

  • ❌ 日本(にほん)にいちばん大(おお)きい町(まち) → ✅ 日本(にほん)でいちばん大(おお)きい町(まち)
    Superlatives set a field of comparison, and that field is marked で.

  • ❌ コンサートはこのホールにあります。 → ✅ コンサートはこのホールであります。
    A concert is held, not located. This is the one place ある takes で.

で - location of an action · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この川(かわ)を泳(およ)いではいけません。 → ✅ この川(かわ)で泳(およ)いではいけません。
    泳(およ)ぐ in the sense "go swimming" treats the river as a venue. を would mean swimming across it, which is not what a warning sign is saying.

で - means, instrument, cause

Meaning · with, by, in, out of - the tool, vehicle, language, material or cause that makes the action possible

で names what you used. A knife, a train, English, wood, a hundred yen, ten minutes, a typhoon - anything the action was carried out by means of, or because of. It is the same particle as the one that marks the place of an action, and it works the same way: it supplies the circumstance the verb depends on.

で - means, instrument, cause · Nuance & register

で is register-neutral and is not dropped in casual speech - unlike a destination particle, it carries information nothing else supplies. はし食(た)べる is not casual Japanese, it is broken Japanese.

A few things worth knowing about how it behaves in real sentences:

で - means, instrument, cause · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 一人(ひとり)で, 二人(ふたり)で, みんなで. This is the standard way to say "alone",
    "the two of us", "all together". It is very frequent and learners reach for と instead, which does not work with a number.

  • Cause で is neutral and slightly detached. 事故(じこ)で遅(おく)れました states
    the cause flatly and is perfect for an apology at work, because it does not argue. Compare the reason particle から, which sounds like you are making a case.

  • Amounts. 三日(みっか)で終(お)わります - "it'll be done in three days" - and
    五百円(ごひゃくえん)で買(か)いました - "I got it for five hundred yen." Here で marks the quantity the action was completed within or for.

  • で is often followed by contrastive は. 電車(でんしゃ)では行(い)けません

  • "you can't get there by train (whatever else you might manage)."

で - means, instrument, cause · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because the tool で and the place で look identical, Japanese avoids stacking two of them in one clause. If you need both, most speakers restructure the sentence rather than write two で in a row.

で - means, instrument, cause · で vs に with vehicles

Rule of thumb - 乗(の)る takes に; every other verb takes で.

This is the highest-frequency で error at N5, because English uses "on" for both.

東京駅(とうきょうえき)で新幹線(しんかんせん)に乗(の)ります。
I'll board the shinkansen at Tokyo Station.

新幹線(しんかんせん)で大阪(おおさか)まで行(い)きます。
I'll go to Osaka by shinkansen.

乗(の)る is "get on", a movement that lands on a point, and points are に. 行(い)く, 来(く)る, 帰(かえ)る, 通(かよ)う all describe travel using the vehicle, so they take で. The opposite of 乗(の)る, 降(お)りる, takes を.

で - means, instrument, cause · で vs から for material

Rule of thumb - if you can still see what it was made of, use で. If the material was chemically transformed and has disappeared, use から.

このかばんは紙(かみ)で作(つく)りました。
I made this bag out of paper.

ワインはぶどうから作(つく)ります。
Wine is made from grapes.

The bag is still visibly paper. The wine is not visibly grapes - the raw material went in and something else came out. The same split gives 木(き)で作(つく)った机(つくえ) (you can see the wood) but 米(こめ)から作(つく)る日本酒(にほんしゅ) (you cannot see the rice). In practice で is far more common; から is reserved for genuine transformations, and native speakers will not object to で in borderline cases.

で - means, instrument, cause · で as means vs で as place

Rule of thumb - the noun decides. Somewhere you could stand is a place; something you could pick up, speak, or blame is a means.

Japanese does not experience these as two words, and neither should you after a little practice - the noun disambiguates before the verb arrives.

自転車(じてんしゃ)で来(き)ました。
I came by bicycle.

自転車(じてんしゃ)置(お)き場(ば)で待(ま)っています。
I'm waiting at the bike park.

で - means, instrument, cause · で as means vs で as place (cont.)

The tricky residue is nouns that could be either. 電話(でんわ) is a means (電話(でんわ)で話(はな)す, "talk on the phone"); 会社(かいしゃ) is a place. When a noun is genuinely ambiguous, Japanese relies on the verb: 新聞(しんぶん)で知(し)る is "find out from the newspaper" (means), because 知(し)る is not something you do at a newspaper.

で - means, instrument, cause · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 電車(でんしゃ)で乗(の)ります。 → ✅ 電車(でんしゃ)に乗(の)ります。
    乗(の)る is the one transport verb that takes に. Everything else in the travel vocabulary takes で.

  • ❌ 歩(ある)いてで帰(かえ)りました。 → ✅ 歩(ある)いて帰(かえ)りました。
    で attaches to nouns only. The て-form already carries the "by doing" sense.

  • ❌ この本(ほん)は英語(えいご)を書(か)いてあります。 → ✅ この本(ほん)は英語(えいご)で書(か)いてあります。
    A language used as a medium is a means. を would try to make English the thing that was written.

  • ❌ 木(き)から作(つく)った机(つくえ) → ✅ 木(き)で作(つく)った机(つくえ)
    The wood is still visible in the desk, so it is で. Save から for materials that have been transformed out of sight.

で - means, instrument, cause · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 友達(ともだち)と三人(さんにん)と行(い)きました。 → ✅ 友達(ともだち)と三人(さんにん)で行(い)きました。
    A number of people is a circumstance, not a companion. Counted groups take で.

で - means, instrument, cause · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 一人(ひとり)で旅行(りょこう)しました。 (b) 友達(ともだち)と旅行(りょこう)しました。

(a) states the circumstance the trip was made under: alone. で here counts the party, which is why ❌一人(ひとり)と is impossible - there is no companion to name.

(b) names a companion, which is the job of と. The two particles are not competing for one slot: you can say 友達(ともだち)と二人(ふたり)で旅行(りょこう)しました and use both at once, "I travelled with a friend, just the two of us."

から - because, since

Meaning · because - gives the reason for what is said in the other half of the sentence

から follows a whole clause and turns it into a reason. The reason comes first, the consequence second: 「reason + から、result」. It is the reason particle a speaker uses when they are willing to stand behind the reason - which is why it sits so naturally in front of a suggestion, an invitation or a command.

から - because, since · Formation

から attaches to a finished clause. Whatever ends that clause has to be a complete predicate, so nouns and な-adjectives need だ or です first.

から - because, since · Nuance & register

から is neutral in politeness by itself - it appears in the most casual speech and in formal writing - but it is not neutral in attitude. から presents the reason as yours: something you assert, and are prepared to defend. That has three practical consequences.

It is the natural reason particle in front of a push. Invitations, suggestions, requests and commands almost always take から rather than ので: 時間(じかん)がないから急(いそ)いで。 The reason exists to justify what you are asking for.

It can sound blunt in an apology. If you are explaining to a customer or a boss why something went wrong, から can read as defensive - "because X, that's why." ので is softer and is the safe default there.

から - because, since · Nuance & register (cont.)

It stands alone at the end of a sentence. ～から。 with nothing after it is extremely common in speech and leaves the consequence implied: 大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)、私(わたし)がやるから。 - "It's fine, I'll do it." This has a warm, reassuring feel, and is worth copying.

Two more forms you will meet immediately. ～からです answers a どうして question as a complete sentence: 「どうして休(やす)んだんですか。」「熱(ねつ)があったからです。」 And だから at the head of a sentence is this same から promoted to a conjunction: 明日(あした)は試験(しけん)だ。だから、今夜(こんや)は勉強(べんきょう)する。 In polite speech that becomes ですから.

から - because, since · から vs ので

Rule of thumb - から is my reason and pushes; ので is the reason and explains. When in doubt with a superior, use ので.

Both mean "because". The difference is stance. から presents a judgement; ので presents a circumstance and lets the listener draw their own conclusion, which makes it sound more deferential.

明日(あした)は休(やす)みだから、ゆっくりしよう。
Tomorrow's a day off, so let's take it easy.

電車(でんしゃ)が遅(おく)れましたので、少(すこ)し遅刻(ちこく)します。
My train was delayed, so I'll be a little late.

There is a hard grammatical difference too, and it is the one exams test. After a noun or a な-adjective, から takes だ and ので takes な:

から - because, since · から vs ので (cont.)

  • 病気(びょうき)だから ／ 病気(びょうき)なので
  • 静(しず)かだから ／ 静(しず)かなので

Finally, a command, a request or a volitional in the second half strongly prefers から. 危(あぶ)ないから下(さ)がって! is natural; the ので version sounds oddly formal for a shout.

から - because, since · から vs the causal て-form

Rule of thumb - the て-form can give a cause, but only when the result is something that happened to you, never something you decide.

仕事(しごと)が忙(いそが)しくて、返事(へんじ)が遅(おく)れました。
Work was hectic, and so my reply was late.

仕事(しごと)が忙(いそが)しいから、今日(きょう)は断(ことわ)ります。
Work is hectic, so I'm going to turn it down today.

The first result is involuntary - the lateness simply followed. The second is a decision, and a decision needs から. This is why ❌忙(いそが)しくて、断(ことわ)りましょう is wrong: you cannot hang a volitional, a request or a command off a causal て-form.

から - because, since · から as reason vs から as "from"

Rule of thumb - look at what is in front of it. A clause before から means "because"; a bare noun before から means "from".

The two are the same word historically - "starting from this fact" - and Japanese speakers never confuse them, because the test is mechanical.

日本(にほん)から来(き)ました。
I came from Japan.

日本(にほん)が好(す)きだから来(き)ました。
I came because I love Japan.

から - because, since · から as reason vs から as "from" (cont.)

日本(にほん) alone is a noun, so the first is a starting point. Add a predicate and the same から flips to a reason. The one place beginners genuinely trip is answering どうして with a bare noun - see the mistakes below.

から - because, since · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 学生(がくせい)から、安(やす)いです。 → ✅ 学生(がくせい)だから、安(やす)いです。
    A noun cannot sit directly in front of reason から; it needs だ or です. Without it the sentence reads "from a student".

  • ❌ 便利(べんり)なから、よく使(つか)います。 → ✅ 便利(べんり)だから、よく使(つか)います。
    な belongs to ので. から takes だ.

  • ❌ 「どうして休(やす)んだの?」「風邪(かぜ)から。」 → ✅ 「風邪(かぜ)だから。」
    The bare noun version says "from a cold" and sounds like a starting point. The same fix in polite speech: 風邪(かぜ)だからです／風邪(かぜ)でしたから.

から - because, since · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ (to a client) 担当(たんとう)者(しゃ)が休(やす)んでいますから、明日(あした)ご連絡(れんらく)します。 → ✅ 担当(たんとう)者(しゃ)が休(やす)んでおりますので、明日(あした)ご連絡(れんらく)します。
    Grammatically fine, socially blunt. In an apology or an explanation upward, ので removes the note of "and that's your answer".

  • ❌ 寒(さむ)くて、窓(まど)を閉(し)めてください。 → ✅ 寒(さむ)いから、窓(まど)を閉(し)めてください。
    A request cannot follow a causal て-form. Use から whenever the second half is something you want the listener to do.

から - because, since · Practice 1

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っているから、中止(ちゅうし)にしよう。 (b) 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っていますので、中止(ちゅうし)といたします。

から - from

Meaning · from - the starting point of a distance, a stretch of time, or a flow of something

から marks where something begins. A place a journey starts from, a clock time an event starts at, the person a letter came from, the raw material a product started life as. Its natural partner is まで, which marks the other end.

から - from · Formation

から attaches directly to a noun, and it is the one place particle that also attaches to a て-form.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - place of origin Place + から 駅(えき) → 駅(えき)から歩(ある)く
Noun - starting time Time + から 九時(くじ) → 九時(くじ)から始(はじ)まる
Noun - person or source Source + から + もらう／借(か)りる／聞(き)く 先生(せんせい) → 先生(せんせい)から借(か)りる
Noun - raw material Material + から + 作(つく)る 米(こめ) → 米(こめ)から作(つく)る
Verb て-form - "after doing" て-form + から 食(た)べて → 食(た)べてから
Paired with まで A から B まで 東京(とうきょう) → 東京(とうきょう)から大阪(おおさか)まで

Note - ～てから is a fixed pattern meaning "after doing X". The verb must be in the て-form, and the tense of the whole sentence is carried by the final verb only: 帰(かえ)ってから電話(でんわ)しました. Never conjugate the て-form for past.

から - from · Nuance & register

から is register-neutral and is never dropped - it carries information no other part of the sentence supplies.

A few behaviours worth having ready:

から - from · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • から alone can be the predicate. 会議(かいぎ)は三時(さんじ)からです。 -
    "The meeting is from three." No verb is needed, and the question form works the same way: 何時(なんじ)からですか。
  • からの modifies a noun, just as への does. 田中(たなか)さんからの手紙(てがみ) -
    "a letter from Tanaka." This is one of the very few particles that takes の directly.
  • Passage through, not just origin. 窓(まど)から入(はい)る,
    裏口(うらぐち)から出(で)る. から marks the opening something passes through, and English usually picks "through" rather than "from" here.
  • Range expressions. 一時(いちじ)から三時(さんじ)まで, 東京(とうきょう)から
    大阪(おおさか)まで. The pair is so tightly bound that hearing から makes a Japanese listener expect まで.

から - from · Nuance & register (cont.)

から also has an entirely separate life as the reason particle meaning "because". The test is what precedes it: a bare noun gives you "from", a finished clause gives you "because". There is no ambiguity in practice.

から - from · から vs まで

Rule of thumb - から is the near end, まで is the far end. They are a pair, and either can appear alone.

銀行(ぎんこう)は九時(くじ)から三時(さんじ)まで開(あ)いています。
The bank is open from nine until three.

Use から alone when only the start matters (来月(らいげつ)から働(はたら)きます), and まで alone when only the end does. What you cannot do is use one where the other belongs:

家(いえ)から駅(えき)まで自転車(じてんしゃ)で十五分(じゅうごふん)かかります。
It takes fifteen minutes from home to the station by bike.

Note that まで, not へ, is what pairs with から. A から～へ sentence is not idiomatic Japanese.

から - from · から vs に for receiving

Rule of thumb - with もらう, 借(か)りる, 聞(き)く and 習(なら)う, both work for a person, but only から works for an organisation - and neither works with あげる, which is always に.

姉(あね)からまんがを借(か)りました。
I borrowed some manga from my sister.

姉(あね)にまんがを貸(か)しました。
I lent some manga to my sister.

When the verb is one of receiving, に and から are both possible for a person, with から making the source a little more explicit and に a little more personal. As soon as the source is a company, a school or a shop, から is the only natural choice: 会社(かいしゃ)から連絡(れんらく)がありました. When the verb is one of giving - あげる, 貸(か)す, 送(おく)る, 教(おし)える - the other party is a recipient and takes に only.

から - from · ～てから vs ～たあとで

Rule of thumb - ～てから says the first action is a precondition for the second. ～たあとで just puts them in order.

くすりを飲(の)んでから寝(ね)ます。
I take my medicine and then go to bed.

映画(えいが)を見(み)たあとで、ご飯(はん)を食(た)べました。
After we saw the film, we had dinner.

The medicine has to come first for the sentence to make sense - that is てから. The film and the dinner are simply two events in sequence, which is あとで. Because てから implies dependency, it is what instructions and rules use: 手(て)を洗(あら)ってから, 確認(かくにん)してから. Also note てから cannot take past tense on the first verb, while あとで requires it.

から - from · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)からプレゼントをあげました。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)にプレゼントをあげました。
    あげる is giving, so the other party is a recipient. Only receiving verbs allow から.

  • ❌ ご飯(はん)を食(た)べるから、くすりを飲(の)みます。 → ✅ ご飯(はん)を食(た)べてから、くすりを飲(の)みます。
    The wrong version is grammatical but means "because I'm going to eat". "After eating" needs the て-form.

  • ❌ 九時(くじ)からに始(はじ)まります。 → ✅ 九時(くじ)から始(はじ)まります。
    から already marks the time; stacking に on top of it is not possible. Choose one: 九時(くじ)に始(はじ)まります means "starts at nine" with no sense of a span.

  • ❌ 「どうして遅(おく)れたの?」「電車(でんしゃ)から。」 → ✅ 「電車(でんしゃ)が遅(おく)れたから。」
    A bare noun before から gives the "from" reading - "from the train". A reason needs a full clause in front.

から - from · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 三時(さんじ)から五時(ごじ)へ勉強(べんきょう)します。 → ✅ 三時(さんじ)から五時(ごじ)まで勉強(べんきょう)します。
    The far end of a span is まで. から never pairs with へ.

から - from · Practice 5

まで

Meaning · until, up to, as far as - and, in one special use, "even"

まで marks the far end. Something continues, and まで says where it stops: an hour on the clock, a station, the moment a condition changes. Whatever it attaches to is included - 五時(ごじ)まで covers five o'clock itself. Its partner is から, which marks the near end.

まで · Formation

まで attaches directly to a noun or to a plain non-past verb.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - place Place + まで 駅(えき) → 駅(えき)まで歩(ある)く
Noun - time Time + まで 五時(ごじ) → 五時(ごじ)まで働(はたら)く
Verb - plain non-past dictionary form + まで 終(お)わる → 終(お)わるまで待(ま)つ
Paired with から A から B まで 月曜日(げつようび) → 月曜日(げつようび)から金曜日(きんようび)まで
Deadline Noun／Verb + までに 金曜日(きんようび) → 金曜日(きんようび)までに出(だ)す
Noun - "even" Noun + まで 子(こ)ども → 子(こ)どもまで知(し)っている

まで · Formation (cont.)

Note - the verb before まで is always plain and non-past, no matter how the sentence ends. 終(お)わるまで待(ま)ちました is correct; ❌終(お)わったまで and ❌終(お)わりますまで are not. The final verb carries all the tense and all the politeness.

まで · Examples

駅(えき)まで歩(ある)きます。
I'll walk as far as the station.

五時(ごじ)まで働(はたら)きます。
I work until five.

雨(あめ)がやむまでここで待(ま)ちましょう。
Let's wait here until the rain stops.

この美術館(びじゅつかん)は月曜日(げつようび)まで休(やす)みです。
This museum is closed until Monday.

東京(とうきょう)から大阪(おおさか)まで新幹線(しんかんせん)で行(い)きました。
I took the shinkansen from Tokyo to Osaka.

まで · Examples (cont.)

そんなことは子(こ)どもまで知(し)っていますよ。
Even children know that.

試合(しあい)が終(お)わるまでだれも席(せき)を立(た)たなかった。
Nobody left their seat until the match was over.

昨日(きのう)は仕事(しごと)が終(お)わらなくて、夜中(よなか)の二時(にじ)まで会社(かいしゃ)に残(のこ)っていました。
I couldn't get finished yesterday and stayed at the office until two in the morning.

まで · Nuance & register

まで is register-neutral. It is identical in plain and polite speech and in writing, and it is never dropped.

Three things about how it feels in use:

まで · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • It is inclusive. 三日(みっか)まで means the third is still covered. If you need
    "before, not including", Japanese uses 前(まえ)に rather than まで.
  • The "even" use is emphatic and slightly loaded. 先生(せんせい)まで笑(わら)った -
    "even the teacher laughed" - presents the teacher as the least likely member of the set. It always carries a note of surprise, and it can sound accusatory if the sentence is a complaint: 君(きみ)まで反対(はんたい)するのか。 Use it deliberately.
  • までも and までは. 明日(あした)までには終(お)わります adds "by tomorrow at the
    latest"; そこまでしなくてもいい means "you don't have to go that far". These build on the same core idea of a limit and are worth recognising early.

まで also survives on its own as a sentence-final marker in signs and notices: 受付(うけつけ)は五時(ごじ)まで。 Nothing is missing - the reader supplies です.

まで · まで vs までに

Rule of thumb - まで = "until", for something that continues. までに = "by", for something that happens once, at any point before the deadline.

This is the highest-value distinction on the page, and it is tested at every level.

五時(ごじ)まで会社(かいしゃ)にいます。
I'll be at the office until five.

五時(ごじ)までに会社(かいしゃ)に戻(もど)ります。
I'll be back at the office by five.

まで · まで vs までに (cont.)

The first stretches across the whole period; the second is a single event that lands somewhere inside it. So the test is the verb. Durative verbs - いる, 待(ま)つ, 働(はたら)く, 続(つづ)ける, 寝(ね)ている - take まで. Punctual verbs - 着(つ)く, 出(だ)す, 帰(かえ)る, 終(お)わらせる - take までに.

明日(あした)の朝(あさ)までにこの書類(しょるい)を出(だ)してください。
Please hand this document in by tomorrow morning.

Say まで there and you are asking someone to keep submitting the document all night.

まで · まで vs から

Rule of thumb - から opens the span, まで closes it. Either can stand alone; when both appear, から always comes first.

夏休(なつやす)みは七月(しちがつ)から八月(はちがつ)までです。
The summer holiday runs from July to August.

Alone, each simply leaves the other end open. 来週(らいしゅう)から忙(いそが)しいです says nothing about when the busy period ends; 水曜日(すいようび)まで忙(いそが)しいです says nothing about when it started. The pairing is so strong that a から early in a sentence sets up an expectation of まで - leaving it unfilled is a stylistic choice, not an error.

まで · まで vs に and へ

Rule of thumb - に and へ give the goal of a movement. まで measures the stretch covered and says where it ended.

空港(くうこう)まで送(おく)ります。
I'll take you as far as the airport.

空港(くうこう)に着(つ)きました。
I've arrived at the airport.

The first is about the journey - the whole distance is covered and the airport is where your involvement stops; it quietly implies you are not going further. The second is about the endpoint alone. This is why 送(おく)る, 歩(ある)く and 走(はし)る take まで so naturally, and why 着(つ)く cannot: arriving has no stretch to measure.

まで · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 金曜日(きんようび)までレポートを出(だ)してください。 → ✅ 金曜日(きんようび)までにレポートを出(だ)してください。
    Handing something in happens once, so it needs the deadline form. The まで version asks for continuous submission.

  • ❌ 六時(ろくじ)までに勉強(べんきょう)しました。 → ✅ 六時(ろくじ)まで勉強(べんきょう)しました。
    Studying is durative - it filled the period - so it takes plain まで.

  • ❌ 映画(えいが)が終(お)わりますまで席(せき)を立(た)たないでください。 → ✅ 映画(えいが)が終(お)わるまで席(せき)を立(た)たないでください。
    The verb in front of まで is always plain non-past. Politeness lives at the end of the sentence.

  • ❌ 駅(えき)までに送(おく)ります。 → ✅ 駅(えき)まで送(おく)ります。
    With a place, までに would mean "by the time we reach the station". If you mean "as far as", it is plain まで.

まで · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 三日(みっか)までに出(だ)してください（meaning "before the 3rd"） → ✅ 二日(ふつか)までに出(だ)してください
    まで and までに include the point they name, so the first sentence allows the 3rd itself. To exclude it, name the day before, or say 三日(みっか)の前(まえ)に.

まで · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 五時(ごじ)までにレポートを書(か)きます。 (b) 五時(ごじ)までレポートを書(か)きます。

(b) is a description of how you will spend the time: you will be writing the report right up to five o'clock. It says nothing about whether it gets finished. Native speakers hear these as completely different commitments, which is why the extra に matters so much.

と - and, joining a complete list

Meaning · and - joins nouns into a list, and claims the list is complete

と glues nouns together: A と B is "A and B". The part learners are not told is that と is exhaustive. A と B と C says those three are all of them. If you only want to name a couple of examples out of many, Japanese has a different particle, や, and using と where you meant や makes a claim you did not intend.

と - and, joining a complete list · Formation

と sits between the nouns. The last noun takes whatever particle the sentence needs; the と-group behaves as a single noun phrase.

Attaches to Form Example
Two nouns Noun と Noun パン → パンと卵(たまご)
Three or more Noun と Noun と Noun 犬(いぬ) → 犬(いぬ)と猫(ねこ)と鳥(とり)
The group takes a particle (N と N) + が／を／は 父(ちち)・母(はは) → 父(ちち)と母(はは)が来(き)ます
Choosing between two N と N、どちらが… コーヒー → コーヒーと紅茶(こうちゃ)、どちらが

と - and, joining a complete list · Formation (cont.)

Note - と joins nouns only. It cannot join verbs, adjectives or clauses. For those, Japanese uses the て-form (安(やす)くて便利(べんり)) or そして (買(か)い物(もの)をしました。そして、帰(かえ)りました). The と you meet at the end of a clause - 春(はる)になると暖(あたた)かくなる - is a different word entirely, a conditional.

と - and, joining a complete list · Examples

パンと牛乳(ぎゅうにゅう)を買(か)いました。
I bought bread and milk.

父(ちち)と母(はは)は大阪(おおさか)にいます。
My father and mother are in Osaka.

朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)は卵(たまご)とごはんです。
Breakfast is eggs and rice.

すみません、コーヒーとケーキをください。
Excuse me - a coffee and a cake, please.

英語(えいご)と中国語(ちゅうごくご)とフランス語(ご)が話(はな)せます。
I can speak English, Chinese and French.

と - and, joining a complete list · Examples (cont.)

会議(かいぎ)に出(で)るのは部長(ぶちょう)と私(わたし)の二人(ふたり)だけです。
The only two attending the meeting are the manager and me.

この料理(りょうり)には塩(しお)とこしょうとオリーブオイルしか使(つか)っていません。
This dish uses nothing but salt, pepper and olive oil.

旅行(りょこう)に持(も)っていくのはパスポートとカメラと薬(くすり)ぐらいです。
About all I'm taking on the trip is my passport, a camera and some medicine.

と - and, joining a complete list · Nuance & register

と is register-neutral and is never dropped. In a list of three or more it is repeated between every pair - Japanese has no equivalent of the English "A, B and C" where the first joins are commas. A comma-only list (犬(いぬ)、猫(ねこ)、鳥(とり)) does exist, but it reads as a written enumeration, like items on a form, rather than as speech.

A trailing と after the last item - 本(ほん)と、ノートと。 - is heard when someone is still counting things off out loud, and it signals "…and there may be more coming". Once the list is finished, drop it. In a written sentence a trailing と before a particle is simply wrong: ❌本(ほん)とノートとが.

と - and, joining a complete list · Nuance & register (cont.)

The exhaustiveness of と is what makes it useful in shops, orders and instructions, where you want to be precise. 材料(ざいりょう)は小麦粉(こむぎこ)と水(みず)と塩(しお)です means those are the ingredients, full stop. In relaxed conversation about vaguer things, や is often the more natural choice, and over-using と can make you sound like you are reading from a list.

One more thing と does that looks unrelated but is the same idea: it sets up a two-way comparison. コーヒーと紅茶(こうちゃ)、どちらがいいですか。 The two items are being presented as a closed set to choose between - exhaustive again.

と - and, joining a complete list · と vs や

Rule of thumb - と says "these and no others". や says "these among others".

かばんの中(なか)には財布(さいふ)と鍵(かぎ)が入(はい)っています。
There's a wallet and keys in my bag.

かばんの中(なか)には財布(さいふ)や鍵(かぎ)が入(はい)っています。
There's a wallet, keys and so on in my bag.

The first is a complete inventory - that bag holds two things. The second offers a sample; there is clearly other stuff in there. Because や is a sample, it very often finishes with など: 財布(さいふ)や鍵(かぎ)など. That など is only available to や; ❌財布(さいふ)と鍵(かぎ)など is contradictory.

と - and, joining a complete list · と vs や (cont.)

Practical consequence: if someone asks what is in a place and you are not sure you have listed everything, や protects you. If you are giving an order or a specification, use と.

と - and, joining a complete list · と vs the て-form and そして

Rule of thumb - と is for nouns. To link adjectives use the て-form; to link actions use the て-form or そして.

This is the most frequent と error, because English "and" does all three jobs.

この部屋(へや)は広(ひろ)くて明(あか)るいです。
This room is spacious and bright.

朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べて、会社(かいしゃ)へ行(い)きました。
I had breakfast and went to work.

Both would be ungrammatical with と. The rule has no exceptions worth learning at N5: if what you are joining ends in い, な or a verb, と is not the tool.

と - and, joining a complete list · と as "and" vs と as "with"

Rule of thumb - if the と-phrase is one of the things the verb is about, it is "and". If it names a person the subject acts together with, it is "with".

The same two words can go either way, and only the sentence shape decides.

田中(たなか)さんと山田(やまだ)さんが来(き)ました。
Tanaka and Yamada came.

田中(たなか)さんが山田(やまだ)さんと来(き)ました。
Tanaka came with Yamada.

と - and, joining a complete list · と as "and" vs と as "with" (cont.)

In the first, が follows the whole group, so the group is one subject: two people arrived. In the second, が has already marked Tanaka as the subject, so 山田(やまだ)さん can only be a companion. Watch where the subject particle lands and the ambiguity disappears. Adding 一緒(いっしょ)に forces the "with" reading outright.

と - and, joining a complete list · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この町(まち)は静(しず)かときれいです。 → ✅ この町(まち)は静(しず)かで、きれいです。
    と cannot join adjectives. な-adjectives link with で, い-adjectives with the て-form.

  • ❌ 朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べたと、出(で)かけました。 → ✅ 朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べて、出(で)かけました。
    Two actions in sequence are linked with the て-form. と after a clause makes a conditional, not a list.

  • ❌ りんごとバナナとなど好(す)きです。 → ✅ りんごやバナナなどが好(す)きです。
    など marks an open-ended sample, which contradicts the "complete list" meaning of と. など belongs with や.

  • ❌ 田中(たなか)さんと山田(やまだ)さんとが来(き)ました。 → ✅ 田中(たなか)さんと山田(やまだ)さんが来(き)ました。
    No と after the final item in a written sentence. The group takes its particle directly.

と - and, joining a complete list · Practice 1

1. 机(つくえ)の上(うえ)にノート____ペンがあります。

机(つくえ)の上(うえ)にノートとペンがあります。

Two nouns joined into one subject group, and the claim is that those are the two things on the desk. If you were only naming examples of what was lying there, や would be the choice.

と - and, joining a complete list · Practice 2

2. 「冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)に何(なに)がありますか。」「たまご（と・や）チーズなどがあります。」

たまごやチーズなどがあります。

The など at the end gives it away: it means "and things like that", which only works with the sampling particle や. と would claim the fridge contains exactly two items, and then など would contradict it.

と - and, joining a complete list · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'd like a coffee and a sandwich, please."

コーヒーとサンドイッチをお願(ねが)いします。

An order is exactly where と belongs - you are stating the complete list of what you want. Using や here would leave the waiter unsure whether more was coming.

と - and, joining a complete list · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この部屋(へや)は広(ひろ)いときれいです。

✅ この部屋(へや)は広(ひろ)くてきれいです。 "This room is spacious and clean."

と joins nouns, never adjectives. An い-adjective drops its final い and takes くて. As written, 広(ひろ)いと would be read as a conditional - "if it's spacious".

と - and, joining a complete list · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) パーティーには田中(たなか)さんと山田(やまだ)さんが来(き)ました。 (b) パーティーには田中(たなか)さんや山田(やまだ)さんが来(き)ました。

(a) reports the full guest list: two people came, and naming a third would contradict the sentence. This is what you say when the party was small or when you are being precise.

(b) picks two names out of a crowd. や implies other guests you have not bothered to mention, so the party was bigger - and it protects you if you have forgotten someone.

と - with, accompaniment

Meaning · with - names the person you do something together with, or the other party in a two-sided action

と marks a partner. Sometimes the partner is optional - you could have gone to the film alone - and sometimes the verb cannot exist without one: you cannot marry, argue or shake hands by yourself. Both are the same と.

と - with, accompaniment · Formation

と attaches directly to a noun, almost always a person or an animal, and the phrase then modifies the verb.

と - with, accompaniment · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - companion Person + と + verb 友達(ともだち) → 友達(ともだち)と行(い)く
Noun - companion, emphasised Person + と一緒(いっしょ)に + verb 家族(かぞく) → 家族(かぞく)と一緒(いっしょ)に食(た)べる
Noun - the other party in a mutual act Person + と + 結婚(けっこん)する／けんかする／会(あ)う 彼(かれ) → 彼(かれ)と結婚(けっこん)する
Question word だれ + と だれ → だれと来(き)ましたか
Combined with a count Person + と + number + で 友達(ともだち) → 友達(ともだち)と二人(ふたり)で

と - with, accompaniment · Formation (cont.)

Note - 一緒(いっしょ)に is optional but common. It adds nothing grammatically; it just makes the togetherness explicit and slightly warmer. The に is not optional: ❌友達(ともだち)と一緒(いっしょ)行(い)く.

と - with, accompaniment · Examples

友達(ともだち)と映画(えいが)を見(み)ました。
I saw a film with a friend.

だれと来(き)ましたか。
Who did you come with?

姉(あね)と一緒(いっしょ)に買(か)い物(もの)に行(い)きます。
I'm going shopping with my older sister.

去年(きょねん)、大学(だいがく)の先輩(せんぱい)と結婚(けっこん)しました。
Last year I married a senior from my university.

明日(あした)、先生(せんせい)と話(はな)す約束(やくそく)をしました。
I've arranged to speak with my teacher tomorrow.

と - with, accompaniment · Examples (cont.)

弟(おとうと)とけんかして、母(はは)に怒(おこ)られました。
I had a fight with my little brother and got told off by my mother.

週末(しゅうまつ)は家族(かぞく)と川(かわ)へ行(い)って、バーベキューをする予定(よてい)です。
At the weekend I'm going to the river with my family for a barbecue.

課長(かちょう)と相談(そうだん)してから、お返事(へんじ)します。
I'll get back to you once I've talked it over with my section chief.

と - with, accompaniment · Nuance & register

と is register-neutral and is not dropped, even in fast speech - remove it and the sentence loses the only thing marking the companion.

The set it belongs to is worth naming, because it decides the particle for a whole class of verbs. Mutual verbs need two parties, and the second one is always と: 結婚(けっこん)する, 離婚(りこん)する, けんかする, 相談(そうだん)する, 約束(やくそく)する, 話(はな)す, 似(に)る - with the exception noted below. Learn these as と-verbs and a large slice of N4 grammar comes for free.

Two frequency notes. だれと is the standard way to ask "who with?", and it comes up constantly in small talk about the weekend. And と一緒(いっしょ)に is the polite, slightly fuller version you use when the companionship is the point: 部長(ぶちょう)と一緒(いっしょ)に伺(うかが)います。

と - with, accompaniment · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, keep this と separate from the quotation と, which follows a whole clause and precedes 言(い)う, 思(おも)う or 書(か)く: 明日(あした)行(い)くと言(い)いました。 If a full sentence sits in front of と, it is quotation, not accompaniment. And if a bare noun sits in front and the group is what the verb is about, it is the "and" と instead.

と - with, accompaniment · と vs で

Rule of thumb - と needs a named companion. A number of people takes で.

This pair trips up almost everyone, because English says "with two people" and "with a friend" the same way.

友達(ともだち)と行(い)きました。
I went with a friend.

三人(さんにん)で行(い)きました。
The three of us went.

You cannot say ❌三人(さんにん)と行(い)きました, because a bare number names nobody. The same logic gives 一人(ひとり)で for "alone" and みんなで for "all together".

The two combine happily when you want both facts at once:

と - with, accompaniment · と vs で (cont.)

同僚(どうりょう)と四人(よにん)で飲(の)みに行(い)きました。
I went out drinking with colleagues, four of us in all.

と - with, accompaniment · と vs に with mutual verbs

Rule of thumb - と presents the action as two-sided; に presents it as one-directional, aimed at the other person.

Several verbs take either particle, and the choice changes the picture.

駅(えき)で先生(せんせい)に会(あ)いました。
I ran into my teacher at the station.

駅(えき)で先生(せんせい)と会(あ)いました。
I met up with my teacher at the station.

With に, you did the meeting - it can be an accident, and often is. With と, both parties met, which implies it was arranged. The same split runs through 相談(そうだん)する (上司(じょうし)に = you take the problem to them; 上司(じょうし)と = you thrash it out together) and 話(はな)す.

と - with, accompaniment · と vs に with mutual verbs (cont.)

Some verbs allow only one. 結婚(けっこん)する and けんかする are と only, because they are unavoidably two-sided. 似(に)る is に only - 父(ちち)に似(に)ています, "I take after my father" - even though resemblance sounds mutual in English.

と - with, accompaniment · と as "with" vs と as "and"

Rule of thumb - find the subject marker. If が or は follows the whole と-group, it is "and". If the subject has already been named elsewhere, the と-phrase is "with".

兄(あに)と姉(あね)は東京(とうきょう)に住(す)んでいます。
My brother and sister live in Tokyo.

兄(あに)は姉(あね)と東京(とうきょう)に住(す)んでいます。
My brother lives in Tokyo with my sister.

In the first, は scopes over both people, so they are one subject: two residents. In the second, は has already claimed 兄(あに), so 姉(あね)と can only be accompaniment.

と - with, accompaniment · と as "with" vs と as "and" (cont.)

When there is no subject particle at all - a bare 友達(ともだち)と行(い)きました - the reading is "with", because a list of things needs a slot in the sentence and this one has none. Adding 一緒(いっしょ)に removes any doubt.

と - with, accompaniment · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 一人(ひとり)と行(い)きました。 → ✅ 一人(ひとり)で行(い)きました。
    A count of people is a circumstance, not a companion. Numbers take で.

  • ❌ 友達(ともだち)と三人(さんにん)と行(い)きました。 → ✅ 友達(ともだち)と三人(さんにん)で行(い)きました。
    The first と names the companions; the number that follows still needs で.

  • ❌ 山田(やまだ)さんに結婚(けっこん)しました。 → ✅ 山田(やまだ)さんと結婚(けっこん)しました。
    Marrying is unavoidably two-sided, so it is と only. The に version is a very common learner error and sounds strange to a native ear.

  • ❌ 田中(たなか)さんと一緒(いっしょ)行(い)きます。 → ✅ 田中(たなか)さんと一緒(いっしょ)に行(い)きます。
    一緒(いっしょ)に is a fixed adverbial phrase and cannot lose its に.

  • ❌ 父(ちち)と似(に)ています。 → ✅ 父(ちち)に似(に)ています。
    似(に)る is the mutual-looking verb that takes に. Resemblance in Japanese is modelled as one thing pointing at another.

と - with, accompaniment · Practice 1

1. 昨日(きのう)、同(おな)じクラスの人(ひと)____勉強(べんきょう)しました。

昨日(きのう)、同(おな)じクラスの人(ひと)と勉強(べんきょう)しました。

A named person doing the activity alongside you is a companion, which is と. で would be wrong here because 人(ひと) is not a count.

と - with, accompaniment · Practice 2

2. 旅行(りょこう)には一人(ひとり)（と・で）行(い)きました。

旅行(りょこう)には一人(ひとり)で行(い)きました。

一人(ひとり) is a number of people, not a person, so it takes で. There is no companion to mark - that is the whole point of the sentence.

と - with, accompaniment · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "There's something I'd like to discuss with the manager."

部長(ぶちょう)と相談(そうだん)したいことがあります。

相談(そうだん)する with と frames it as a two-sided discussion, which is what "discuss with" means. 部長(ぶちょう)に相談(そうだん)する is also correct Japanese, but it means taking a problem to him for advice.

と - with, accompaniment · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 去年(きょねん)、高校(こうこう)の友達(ともだち)に結婚(けっこん)しました。

✅ 去年(きょねん)、高校(こうこう)の友達(ともだち)と結婚(けっこん)しました。 "Last year I married a friend from high school."

結婚(けっこん)する belongs to the mutual-verb set, where the other party must be と. に would try to make the friend a recipient, which makes no sense for a marriage.

と - with, accompaniment · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部長(ぶちょう)に話(はな)しました。 (b) 部長(ぶちょう)と話(はな)しました。

(a) is one-directional: you told him something. The information went from you to him, and the sentence says nothing about whether he replied - this is what you say after reporting a problem upward.

(b) is two-sided: the two of you had a conversation. Both people spoke, and the sentence implies some exchange took place. Choosing と with a superior can also sound a shade more equal, so に is the safer default when you are simply reporting.
