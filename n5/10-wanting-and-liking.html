N5 - Wanting and liking

～たい

Meaning · want to do something - the speaker's own desire

～たい attaches to a verb and reports a wish felt from the inside. That "from the inside" is not decoration: it is a grammatical restriction. Plain ～たい is for your own desires, and for asking about the listener's. You cannot use it to state flatly what a third person wants.

～たい · Formation

Attach たい to the ます-stem, then conjugate it exactly like an い-adjective.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + たい 飲のみます → 飲のみたい
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + たい 食たべます → 食たべたい
Verb (する) し + たい する → したい／勉強べんきょうしたい
Verb (来くる) き + たい 来くる → 来きたい

Once たい is attached, 飲のみたい behaves like 高たかい in every respect:

～たい · Formation (cont.)

い-adjective form Rule Example
Polite + です 飲のみたいです
Negative たい → たくない 飲のみたくない
Past たい → たかった 飲のみたかった
Negative past たい → たくなかった 飲のみたくなかった
て-form たい → たくて 飲のみたくて
Adverbial たい → たく 飲のみたくなる
Before a noun たい + Noun 飲のみたいもの
Third person ます-stem + たがる 飲のみたがる／飲のみたがっている

～たい · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - たい is an い-adjective, not a verb. So it is 行いきたくない, never ❌行いきたいじゃない, and 行いきたかった, never ❌行いきたいでした.

～たい · Examples

水みずが**飲のみたい**。
I want some water.

日本にほんへ**行いきたい**です。
I want to go to Japan.

何なにが**食たべたい**ですか。
What do you want to eat?

今日きょうはどこにも**行いきたくない**。
I don't want to go anywhere today.

～たい · Examples (cont.)

子こどものとき、パイロットになりたかった。
When I was a kid, I wanted to be a pilot.

弟おとうとは新あたらしいゲームを**買かいたがっている**。
My little brother wants to buy the new game.

疲つかれたので今日きょうは早はやく**寝ねたい**んですが、まだ仕事しごとが終おわりません。
I'm exhausted and I want an early night, but I still haven't finished work.

来年らいねんは沖縄おきなわに行いって、海うみで**泳およぎたい**と思おもっています。
Next year I'm hoping to go to Okinawa and swim in the sea.

～たい · Nuance & register

～たい itself is neutral; ～たいです is polite in form. The trouble is that politeness of form does not fix bluntness of content. Stating a raw want at someone senior - 部長ぶちょう、早はやく帰かえりたいです - is grammatical and still lands badly, because you are pushing your preference at a superior. Softeners exist for exactly this:

～たい · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ～たいと思おもいます - "I'm thinking I'd like to…". The standard business
    hedge. 来週らいしゅうお休やすみを取とりたいと思おもいます。
  • ～たいんですが - sets up a request and leaves the ask unsaid.
    すみません、これを返かえしたいんですが…
  • ～たいのですが／～たく存ぞんじます - written and formal registers.

The same asymmetry runs the other way. Asking a superior 何なにが食たべたいですか is a bit bald; 何なんになさいますか or 何なにがよろしいですか is what you actually say. Among friends and to subordinates, plain ～たい? is perfectly normal.

～たい · Nuance & register (cont.)

In casual speech the negative ～たくない is the everyday form; ～たくありません is correct but stiff, and mostly turns up in writing or careful speech.

～たがる is a real verb (a う-verb: たがらない, たがります, たがって) and it carries a slight sense of watching someone from outside - you are describing observable behaviour, not reading a mind. That distance can shade into criticism, so たがる about a superior is rude: ❌社長しゃちょうは帰かえりたがっています.

～たい · ～たい vs ～たがる

Rule of thumb - ～たい for me and for you; ～たがる for him, her and them.

A flat statement about a third person's desire in plain ～たい sounds wrong to native ears, because you are claiming direct access to someone else's inner state.

妹いもうとはケーキを**食たべたがっています**。
My little sister wants cake.

～たい · ～たい vs ～たがる (cont.)

Note the particle: たがる is an ordinary verb, so its object takes を, never the が that たい allows. Note the ～ている too. For a desire someone is feeling right now, たがっている is the normal form; bare たがる reads as a habit - 弟おとうとはいつも新あたらしいものを買かいたがる - "my brother always wants to buy the newest thing."

たがる is not the only way out. These are often more natural, and politer:

  • 妹いもうとはケーキが食たべたいそうです。 (I hear that…)
  • 妹いもうとはケーキが食たべたいと言いっています。 (she says…)
  • 妹いもうとはケーキが食たべたいようです／みたいです。 (it seems…)

～たい · ～たい vs ～たがる (cont.)

Note - the third-person ban only applies to a plain main-clause statement. Inside a subordinate clause, plain たい about someone else is fine: 日本にほんに行いきたい人ひとは手てを挙あげてください。 「Anyone who wants to go to Japan, raise your hand.」 It is also fine under と思おもっている: 彼かれは行いきたいと思おもっています。

～たい · が vs を with ～たい

Rule of thumb - both are correct. が spotlights the thing you crave; を keeps the focus on the action. When in doubt at N5, が is the safer answer.

Because たい turns the verb into an adjective-like word, the old object can take が - and that option is genuinely alive in modern Japanese.

ビールが飲のみたい。
I want a beer. (it's beer specifically that I'm after)

ビールを飲のみたい。
I want to drink a beer. (the drinking is what I'm picturing)

Three places where を is the only option:

～たい · が vs を with ～たい (cont.)

  1. The を is not a direct object. Path and departure を never becomes が: 空そらを飛とびたい, 家いえを出でたい. 2. The clause already has a が. Two が in one clause collide: ❌私わたしがビールが飲のみたい. 3. The object is far from the verb. Once material intervenes, が degrades: ビールを冷ひやして、あとでゆっくり飲のみたい.

を is also the safer default in writing and in longer sentences; が is at its most natural in short, felt statements - the ones you say when you actually want the thing.

～たい · ～たい vs ほしい

Rule of thumb - ほしい wants a noun; ～たい wants an action.

新あたらしいパソコンがほしいです。
I want a new computer.

新あたらしいパソコンが**買かいたい**です。
I want to buy a new computer.

ほしい is also an い-adjective, and it carries the same first-person restriction as たい - third person needs ほしがっている. Its verb-taking cousin, ～てほしい, means you want someone else to do something: 手伝てつだってほしい - "I want you to help."

～たい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんは日本にほんに行いきたいです。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんは日本にほんに行いきたがっています。
    A flat statement about a third person's desire needs たがる, or a hedge like たいそうです／たいと言いっています.

  • ❌ 行いきたいじゃない／行いきたいでした → ✅ 行いきたくない／行いきたかった
    たい is an い-adjective. It never takes じゃない or でした.

  • ❌ 食たべるたい → ✅ 食たべたい
    たい attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form. 食たべます → 食たべ + たい.

  • ❌ コーヒーがほしいたい。 → ✅ コーヒーが**飲のみたい**。／コーヒーがほしい。
    ほしい and たい do the same job for different word classes. Never stack them.

～たい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 課長かちょう、何なにが食たべたいですか。 → ✅ 課長かちょう、何なにがよろしいですか。
    Grammatical, but too direct upward. Asking a superior about their wants calls for よろしいですか or なさいますか.

～たい · Practice 1

1. のどがかわきました。水みずが飲の____です。

水みずが飲のみたいです。

たい attaches to the ます-stem: 飲のみます → 飲のみ + たい. The polite form is just です on the end, because 飲のみたい is an い-adjective.

～たい · Practice 2

2. 妹いもうとは新あたらしい靴くつを（買かいたいです・買かいたがっています）。

妹いもうとは新あたらしい靴くつを**買かいたがっています**。

妹いもうと is a third person, so plain たい in a main clause is out - you cannot assert someone else's inner state directly. たがっている describes the desire as something you can observe from outside.

～たい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I didn't want to go to school yesterday."

きのうは学校がっこうに**行いきたくなかった**（です）。

Past negative of an い-adjective: たい → たく → たくない → たくなかった. There is no ❌行いきたいじゃなかった. Adding です makes it polite without changing the stem.

～たい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 今日きょうは映画えいがを見みに行いきたいじゃないです。

✅ 今日きょうは映画えいがを見みに行いきたくないです。

じゃない is the negative for nouns and な-adjectives. ～たい is an い-adjective, so the negative is built by swapping the final い for くない.

～たい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) ラーメンが食たべたい。 (b) ラーメンを食たべたい。

Both are correct and a speaker would accept either. (a) puts the spotlight on ramen as the object of the craving - "it's ramen I want", the thing itself. (b) keeps the sentence built around the action of eating, so it reads a touch more neutral and descriptive, and it's what you'd reach for in a longer or written sentence.

The choice narrows once the sentence grows: with material between the object and the verb, or with a が-marked subject already present, only を works.

ほしい

Meaning · want a thing - the speaker's own desire for an object

ほしい (kanji 欲ほしい, but usually written in kana at this level) is an い-adjective, not a verb. It does not take an object with を; the thing you want is marked with が. And like ～たい, it reports a want felt from the inside, so plain ほしい covers your own wants and questions about the listener's - never a flat statement about what a third person wants.

ほしい · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (the thing wanted) Noun + が + ほしい 車くるま → 車くるまがほしい
Verb (an action you want from someone) て-form + ほしい 手伝てつだう → 手伝てつだってほしい
Verb (an action you want to do) not possible - use ～たい 休やすむ → 休やすみたい
Third person's want Noun + を + ほしがる 犬いぬ → 犬いぬをほしがっている

Once ほしい is in place it conjugates exactly like 高たかい or 安やすい:

ほしい · Formation (cont.)

い-adjective form Rule Example
Polite + です ほしいです
Negative ほしい → ほしくない ほしくない
Polite negative + です／ありません ほしくないです／ほしくありません
Past ほしい → ほしかった ほしかった
Negative past ほしい → ほしくなかった ほしくなかった
て-form ほしい → ほしくて ほしくて
Adverbial ほしい → ほしく ほしくなる
Before a noun ほしい + Noun ほしいもの

ほしい · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ほしい is an い-adjective. So it is ほしくない, never ❌ほしいじゃない, and ほしかった, never ❌ほしいでした. And it never conjugates like a verb: there is no ❌ほしみます.

ほしい · Examples

お金かねがほしい。
I want money.

もっと時間じかんがほしいです。
I want more time.

誕生日たんじょうびに何なにがほしいですか。
What do you want for your birthday?

今いまは何なにもほしくない。
I don't want anything right now.

ほしい · Examples (cont.)

子こどものころ、自分じぶんの部屋へやがほしかった。
When I was little I wanted a room of my own.

弟おとうとは前まえから犬いぬをほしがっています。
My little brother has wanted a dog for ages.

返事へんじは急いそぎませんが、一度いちどゆっくり話はなす機会きかいがほしいです。
There's no rush on an answer, but I'd like a chance to talk it through properly.

この書類しょるい、明日あしたの朝あさまでに目めを通とおしてほしいんですが、お願ねがいできますか。
I'd like you to look these documents over by tomorrow morning - could I ask you to?

ほしい · Nuance & register

ほしいです is polite in form only. The content is still a bare statement of appetite, and pointing it at someone above you lands like a child asking for a present: 部長ぶちょう、新あたらしいパソコンがほしいです is grammatical and socially awkward. Upward, you reframe the want as a request or a hedge:

  • ～がほしいのですが／～がほしいんですが - trails off and lets the listener
    offer. 資料しりょうのコピーがほしいんですが…
  • ～をいただけますか／お願ねがいできますか - asks instead of wanting.
  • ～があると助たすかります - "it would help if I had…", the standard
    workplace softener.

ほしい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The same asymmetry runs upward in questions. 社長しゃちょう、何なにがほしいですか is far too direct; offering with いかがですか is what you actually say.

In a shop or a café, ほしい is simply the wrong tool. You do not say これがほしいです to a waiter - you say これをください or これをお願ねがいします. ほしい describes your inner state; ください performs the request.

ほしい · Nuance & register (cont.)

Casually, ほしい is very common and takes all the usual endings: ほしいなあ (wistful), ほしい！, ほしいんだけど…. Men and women use it alike. In writing the kanji 欲ほしい appears. What ほしい does not have is a humble register of its own - there is no polite conjugation that makes it suitable upward, which is why formal Japanese switches word entirely, to 頂いただきたい or お願ねがいしたい.

ほしがる is an ordinary う-verb (欲ほしがらない, ほしがります, ほしがって). It describes desire as something you can see in someone - the reaching, the asking, the looking. That outside-observer flavour makes it faintly belittling, so it is not used about superiors: ❌課長かちょうは新あたらしい車くるまをほしがっています.

ほしい · ほしい vs ～たい

Rule of thumb - ほしい wants a noun; ～たい wants an action. They divide the work; they never stack.

アイスクリームがほしい。
I want ice cream.

アイスクリームが**食たべたい**。
I want to eat ice cream.

ほしい · ほしい vs ～たい (cont.)

The two are otherwise twins: both are い-adjectives, both mark their target with が, both are barred from flat third-person statements, and both have a ～がる form for other people. What ほしい cannot do is attach to a verb - ❌休やすむがほしい is not a sentence. If the thing you want is an action you perform yourself, ～たい is the only route.

ほしい · ほしい vs ～てほしい

Rule of thumb - Noun + がほしい wants a thing; Verb-て + ほしい wants someone else to do something. The person you want it from takes に.

説明書せつめいしょがほしい。
I want the instruction manual.

使つかい方かたを**教おしえてほしい**。
I want you to show me how to use it.

母ははに来きてほしくない。
I don't want my mother to come.

ほしい · ほしい vs ～てほしい (cont.)

～てほしい is a wish, not a command, so it is gentler than a direct request - but it is also more personal, and to a superior it presumes. The polite ladder runs 待まってほしい → 待まっていただきたい → 待まっていただけないでしょうか. The negative is ～ないでほしい: 勝手かってに決きめないでほしい.

ほしい · ほしい vs ほしがる

Rule of thumb - ほしい for me and you; ほしがる for him, her and them. The particle flips too: が with ほしい, を with ほしがる.

娘むすめはずっとピアノをほしがっています。
My daughter has been wanting a piano for a long time.

Note the ～ている. A desire someone is feeling now is ほしがっている; bare ほしがる reads as a habit - あの子こは友ともだちのものを何なんでもほしがる.

ほしがる is not the only escape. These are often smoother, and safer upward:

ほしい · ほしい vs ほしがる (cont.)

  • 妹いもうとは新あたらしい辞書じしょがほしいそうです。 (I hear that…)
  • 妹いもうとは新あたらしい辞書じしょがほしいと言いっています。 (she says…)
  • 妹いもうとは新あたらしい辞書じしょがほしいようです。 (it seems…)

Note - the ban is on plain main-clause statements only. Inside a modifying clause, plain ほしい about other people is perfectly normal: 資料しりょうがほしい人ひとは、前まえの机つくえから取とってください。 「Anyone who wants a copy, take one from the desk at the front.」

ほしい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私わたしは車くるまをほしいです。 → ✅ 私わたしは車くるまがほしいです。
    ほしい is an adjective, so it has no direct object. を appears only with the verb ほしがる.

  • ❌ 山田やまださんは新あたらしい車くるまがほしいです。 → ✅ 山田やまださんは新あたらしい車くるまをほしがっています。
    A flat statement about a third person's want needs ほしがっている, or a hedge like ほしいそうです.

  • ❌ ほしいじゃない／ほしいでした → ✅ ほしくない／ほしかった
    い-adjective endings, not な-adjective ones.

  • ❌ 休やすむがほしい。 → ✅ 休やすみたい。
    ほしい cannot take a verb. Wanting to perform an action is ～たい territory.

ほしい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ （カフェで）これがほしいです。 → ✅ これをください。
    Naming your inner state at a server is odd. Ordering is done with ください or お願ねがいします.

ほしい · Practice 1

1. 新あたらしいカメラ____ほしいです。

新あたらしいカメラがほしいです。

ほしい is an い-adjective, and what it wants is marked with が, not を. The English "want a camera" has an object, but the Japanese does not.

ほしい · Practice 2

2. 兄あにはバイクを（ほしいです・ほしがっています）。

兄あにはバイクをほしがっています。

兄あに is a third person, so a plain ほしい statement would be claiming direct access to someone else's inner state. ほしがっている reports the desire as something you can observe - and note that it takes を, which is why the sentence was written with を to begin with.

ほしい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "When I was a student, I wanted a motorbike."

学生がくせいのとき、バイクがほしかった（です）。

The past of an い-adjective: ほしい → ほしかった. There is no ❌ほしいでした; adding です after ほしかった is what makes it polite.

ほしい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 辞書じしょをほしいんですが、いい店みせを知しりませんか。

✅ 辞書じしょがほしいんですが、いい店みせを知しりませんか。

The を is the interference: English treats "want" as a transitive verb, Japanese treats ほしい as an adjective whose target takes が.

ほしい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 静しずかにしてほしい。 (b) 静しずかにしてください。

(b) is a direct request aimed at the listener - it tells them to do something, and in the wrong context it can sound like an instruction. (a) reports the speaker's wish instead of issuing the request, so it is indirect, and among friends it comes across as softer, almost a complaint said out loud.

Indirectness is not the same as politeness upward, though. To a superior neither is ideal: 静しずかにしていただけませんか is the form that actually works.

～のが好き

Meaning · like doing something - a standing preference, not a present urge

好すき is a な-adjective meaning "pleasing, to my taste", and what pleases you is marked with が. English makes "like" a verb with an object; Japanese makes it an adjective with a subject. To like an action, you first turn the verb into a noun with the nominaliser の, then feed it to 好すき.

～のが好き · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary form) Dictionary + のが + 好すき 泳およぐ → 泳およぐのが好すき
Verb (alternative nominaliser) Dictionary + ことが + 好すき 読よむ → 読よむことが好すき
Noun Noun + が + 好すき 音楽おんがく → 音楽おんがくが好すき
Verb (する-noun) Noun + が + 好すき 料理りょうりする → 料理りょうりが好すき

好すき then behaves like 静しずか or 元気げんき - a な-adjective in every respect:

～のが好き · Formation (cont.)

な-adjective form Rule Example
Plain 好すきだ 泳およぐのが好すきだ
Polite 好すきです 泳およぐのが好すきです
Negative 好すきじゃない 泳およぐのが好すきじゃない
Polite negative 好すきじゃないです／ではありません 好すきじゃありません
Past 好すきだった／好すきでした 好すきでした
Negative past 好すきじゃなかった 好すきじゃなかったです
Before a noun 好すきな + Noun 好すきな歌うた
Intensified 大好だいすき 歌うたうのが大好だいすき

～のが好き · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - 好すき is an adjective, so there is no ❌好すきます, no ❌好すきません and no ❌好すきくない. And the verb needs の to become a noun first: ❌読よむが好すき → ✅読よむのが好すき.

～のが好き · Examples

歌うたを**歌うたうのが好すき**です。
I like singing.

私わたしは**走はしるのが好すき**じゃない。
I don't like running.

どんな音楽おんがくを**聞きくのが好すき**ですか。
What kind of music do you like listening to?

父ちちは朝あさ早はやく**起おきるのが好すき**です。
My father likes getting up early.

～のが好き · Examples (cont.)

子こどものころは絵えをかくのが大好だいすきでした。
As a child I absolutely loved drawing.

雨あめの日ひに家いえで映画えいがを**見みるのが好すき**です。
I like watching films at home on a rainy day.

妻つまは週末しゅうまつに市場いちばまで歩あるいて野菜やさいを**選えらぶのが好すき**です。
My wife likes walking to the market at the weekend and picking out the vegetables.

地図ちずを見みないで知しらない町まちを**歩あるき回まわるのが好すき**なんです。
The thing is, I love wandering around a town I don't know without looking at a map.

～のが好き · Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral. 好すきです is the everyday polite form, 好すきだ the plain one, and in casual speech the copula usually vanishes altogether - 甘あまいものが好すき, with a rising tone for a question. Upward, お好すきですか is the polite version: 部長ぶちょう、コーヒーはお好すきですか.

One important difference from ～たい: 好すき may be used flatly about a third person. A preference is a settled, observable trait, not a private impulse, so 姉あねは山やまに登のぼるのが好すきです needs no がる and no hedge. This is one of the few places where describing someone else is easier than describing yourself.

～のが好き · Nuance & register (cont.)

Be careful with people as the object. 田中たなかさんが好すきです is a confession of romantic feeling, not "I get on with Tanaka". If you mean the latter, say 田中たなかさんは、いい人ひとですね or 田中たなかさんと話はなすのが好すきです - attaching the liking to an activity defuses it.

Finally, negation carries weight. Japanese hedges dislike far more than English does: あまり好すきじゃないです is the polite way to decline food, music or a suggestion, and it is what a learner should reach for by default.

～のが好き · ～のが好き vs ～ことが好き

Rule of thumb - の for concrete, lived, experienced activity; こと for the activity in the abstract. With 好すき both are usually fine, and の is the more conversational of the two.

海うみで**泳およぐのが好すき**です。
I like swimming in the sea.

人ひとに**教おしえることが好すき**です。
I like teaching people.

The first is the feel of the water; the second is teaching as a category of thing. Push the difference and you can hear it, but for a plain statement of taste nobody will correct you either way.

～のが好き · ～のが好き vs ～ことが好き (cont.)

Where the choice stops being free is when the nominalised clause is not the object of an adjective but a noun in its own right - a は…です sentence, for instance. There only こと works:

趣味しゅみは古ふるい本ほんを**集あつめること**です。
My hobby is collecting old books.

❌集あつめるのです would be read as the explanatory のです, which means something else entirely.

～のが好き · ～のが好き vs ～たい

Rule of thumb - 好すき is what you like in general; ～たい is what you want now. Liking something does not mean you want it this minute.

コーヒーを**飲のむのが好すき**です。
I like drinking coffee. (a habit, a taste)

今いま、コーヒーが**飲のみたい**です。
I want a coffee right now. (an urge)

～のが好き · ～のが好き vs ～たい (cont.)

The two also behave differently across persons. ～たい cannot be stated flatly about someone else, but 好すき can - which is why a description of another person's tastes almost always comes out in 好すき: 夫おっとは料理りょうりするのが好すきです.

～のが好き · 好き vs 嫌い

Rule of thumb - 嫌きらい is much stronger than English "don't like". The everyday negative is あまり好すきじゃない.

運動うんどうするのはあまり好すきじゃないです。
I'm not that keen on exercising.

虫むしが**嫌きらい**です。
I hate insects.

～のが好き · 好き vs 嫌い (cont.)

Two traps sit here. First, 嫌きらい ends in い but is a な-adjective: 嫌きらいな食たべ物もの, 嫌きらいじゃない, never ❌嫌きらくない. Second, notice the は in the first sentence. Swapping the が for は when you negate is standard, and it softens further - you are marking this one thing as the exception rather than making a sweeping declaration.

～のが好き · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 本ほんを読よむが好すきです。 → ✅ 本ほんを読よむのが好すきです。
    A verb cannot be the subject of an adjective on its own. の nominalises it.

  • ❌ 音楽おんがくを好すきです。 → ✅ 音楽おんがくが好すきです。
    Interference from English. 好すき is not a verb and has no direct object.

  • ❌ ピアノを**好すきます**。 → ✅ ピアノが**好すきです**。
    好すき takes adjective endings. A verb 好すく does exist, but in everyday Japanese it survives mainly in the passive (人ひとに好すかれる).

  • ❌ 泳およぐのが**好すきくないです。 → ✅ 泳およぐのが好すきじゃない**です。
    な-adjective negation, despite the final き looking harmless.

～のが好き · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 好すきの食たべ物もの → ✅ 好すきな食たべ物もの
    Before a noun, a な-adjective takes な, not の.

～のが好き · Practice 1

1. 私わたしは自転車じてんしゃに乗のる____好すきです。

自転車じてんしゃに乗のるのが好すきです。

The verb 乗のる has to become a noun before it can be the subject of 好すき, and that is what の does. The particle after it is が, never を.

～のが好き · Practice 2

2. 弟おとうとは甘あまいものを食たべるのが（好すきです・食たべたいです）。

弟おとうとは甘あまいものを食たべるのが**好すきです**。

Two reasons. The sentence describes a standing taste rather than a present urge, and it is about a third person, where a flat ～たい is not allowed anyway. The second option would also leave the nominalised clause stranded.

～のが好き · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "My grandmother likes taking photographs of flowers."

祖母そぼは花はなの写真しゃしんを**撮とるのが好すき**です。

Note that no hedge is needed even though the subject is a third person: unlike ～たい, a preference can be asserted about other people directly.

～のが好き · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 兄あにはサッカーを好すきの人ひとです。

✅ 兄あにはサッカーが**好すきな人ひと**です。

Two problems in one short sentence. The object of 好すき takes が, not を; and before a noun a な-adjective uses な, not の.

～のが好き · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) パクチーはあまり好すきじゃないです。 (b) パクチーが**嫌きらい**です。

(b) is a flat declaration of dislike and sounds surprisingly strong in Japanese - closer to "I can't stand coriander" than to "I'm not a fan". (a) is the polite default: the softener あまり plus a negative, with は marking coriander as the one exception rather than condemning it outright.

If a host offers you something you would rather not eat, (a) is the sentence you want. (b) risks sounding like a complaint about the food.

～のが上手

Meaning · to be good at something - skill you can see in the result

上手じょうず is a な-adjective describing an ability that has visibly been trained. What someone is good at is marked with が, and to say they are good at an action you nominalise the verb with の first. The important thing to learn alongside the grammar is who you may say it about: 上手じょうず is a judgement handed down, so it is used about other people, and almost never about yourself.

～のが上手 · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary form) Dictionary + のが + 上手じょうず 話はなす → 話はなすのが上手じょうず
Noun Noun + が + 上手じょうず ピアノ → ピアノが上手じょうず
Person + thing Person + は + Thing + が + 上手じょうず 姉あねは絵えが上手じょうずです
Adverbially (modifying a verb) 上手じょうずに + Verb 上手じょうずに使つかう

The な-adjective forms behave as expected:

～のが上手 · Formation (cont.)

な-adjective form Rule Example
Plain 上手じょうずだ 歌うたうのが上手じょうずだ
Polite 上手じょうずです 歌うたうのが上手じょうずです
Negative 上手じょうずじゃない 上手じょうずじゃないです
Past 上手じょうずだった／上手じょうずでした 上手じょうずでした
Before a noun 上手じょうずな + Noun 上手じょうずな人ひと
Honorific お上手じょうず お上手じょうずですね

～のが上手 · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - 上手じょうず before a noun takes な; before a verb it takes に. 上手じょうずな字じ "neat handwriting" versus 上手じょうずに書かく "to write neatly".

～のが上手 · Examples

母ははは**料理りょうりを作つくるのが上手じょうず**です。
My mother is good at cooking.

彼かれは**字じを書かくのが上手じょうず**ですね。
He has lovely handwriting, doesn't he.

兄あには人ひとの名前なまえを**覚おぼえるのが上手じょうず**じゃないです。
My older brother isn't good at remembering people's names.

田中たなかさんは人ひとの話はなしを**聞きくのが上手じょうず**です。
Tanaka is a good listener.

～のが上手 · Examples (cont.)

息子むすこはまだ五ごさいですが、自転車じてんしゃに**乗のるのが上手じょうず**です。
My son is only five, but he's good at riding a bicycle.

娘むすめは小ちいさいころから絵えを**上手じょうずに**かきました。
My daughter drew well from the time she was small.

山田やまださんは会議かいぎで話はなしを**まとめるのが上手じょうず**なので、いつも司会しかいをお願ねがいしています。
Yamada is good at pulling a discussion together, so we always ask her to chair.

祖母そぼは昔むかしから着物きものを**着きるのが上手じょうず**で、近所きんじょの人ひとにもよく教おしえています。
My grandmother has always been good at putting on a kimono, and she often teaches the neighbours too.

～のが上手 · Nuance & register

上手じょうず is a verdict. You are standing outside someone's performance and rating it, and that is exactly why the word behaves the way it does socially.

First, you do not normally use it about yourself. ❌私わたしは英語えいごが上手じょうずです is grammatical and sounds boastful; the natural equivalents are 英語えいごが得意とくいです, 英語えいごが少すこし話はなせます, or the modest classic まだまだです.

～のが上手 · Nuance & register (cont.)

Second - and this catches learners going the other way - praising a superior with 上手じょうず is also awkward, because rating someone above you is not your place. 部長ぶちょう、英語えいごがお上手じょうずですね is softened by the honorific お, and is heard, but さすがですね or 勉強べんきょうになります is safer. Between equals and downward it is completely normal.

Third, when someone says 日本語にほんごがお上手じょうずですね to you, the expected reply is not ありがとうございます but a deflection: いいえ、まだまだです or そんなことないです. Accepting the compliment flatly is not wrong, just noticeably un-Japanese.

～のが上手 · Nuance & register (cont.)

お上手じょうずですね said with the wrong timing means "you flatterer" - お上手じょうずを言いって is a fixed phrase for exactly that. Context carries it, but be aware the word has that second life.

～のが上手 · ～のが上手 vs ～のが得意

Rule of thumb - 上手じょうず rates someone else; 得意とくい is how you talk about yourself. 得意とくい also carries confidence, not just skill.

林はやしさんはギターを**弾ひくのが上手じょうず**です。
Hayashi is good at playing the guitar.

私わたしは人ひとの前まえで**話はなすのが得意とくい**です。
I'm good at speaking in front of people.

～のが上手 · ～のが上手 vs ～のが得意 (cont.)

The difference is where the judgement comes from. 上手じょうず is an outside assessment of quality, so turning it on yourself is self-congratulation. 得意とくい describes your own sense of what you are strong at and can be asserted without immodesty - it is what you write on a job application.

Note that 得意とくい is not a full escape either: said too readily it still sounds confident, so many speakers reach for 少すこしできます or a plain potential form instead.

得意とくい about a third person is fine too, but it then reports their confidence: 弟おとうとは数学すうがくが得意とくいです suggests he would say so himself, whereas 弟おとうとは数学すうがくが上手じょうずです is your opinion of him - and for school subjects it sounds odd, because 上手じょうず wants a performed skill.

～のが上手 · 上手 vs できる

Rule of thumb - 上手じょうず is how well; できる is whether at all.

妹いもうとはスペイン語ごができます。
My little sister can speak Spanish.

妹いもうとはスペイン語ごを話はなすのが**上手じょうず**です。
My little sister speaks Spanish well.

The first says the ability exists; the second grades it. This is why できる and the potential form are the safe way to describe your own abilities - 少すこし泳およげます reports a fact, while 泳およぐのが上手じょうずです awards yourself a prize.

～のが上手 · 上手 vs できる (cont.)

They also differ in what they accept. できる takes nouns and する-nouns (運転うんてんができる) and, with ことが, verbs. 上手じょうず takes nouns and のが clauses, and refuses anything that is not a trainable skill: ❌背せが高たかいのが上手じょうずです is nonsense.

～のが上手 · 上手 vs うまい

Rule of thumb - same meaning, lower register. うまい is casual, slightly male-coded, and ambiguous with "tasty".

あいつ、歌うたうまいな。
Wow, he's a good singer.

先生せんせいはテニスが**お上手じょうず**ですね。
You're good at tennis, aren't you, sensei.

～のが上手 · 上手 vs うまい (cont.)

うまい is an い-adjective (上手うまい in kanji, though usually kana), so it takes うまくない, うまかった - a different paradigm from 上手じょうず entirely. It belongs to friends, sport and the internet, never to a business email.

Its other meaning is "delicious", which is where the ambiguity bites: この店みせ、うまい in a restaurant is about the food, not the chef's technique. And the negative 下手へた has no equivalent slot here - the casual opposite of うまい is へた or へたくそ, which is blunter still.

～のが上手 · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私わたしは日本語にほんごが**上手じょうずです**。 → ✅ 私わたしは日本語にほんごが**得意とくいです**。／少すこし話はなせます。
    Grammatical but boastful. 上手じょうず is a verdict on someone else.

  • ❌ ギターを上手じょうずです。 → ✅ ギターが上手じょうずです。
    上手じょうず is an adjective and has no direct object.

  • ❌ 泳およぐが上手じょうずです。 → ✅ 泳およぐのが上手じょうずです。
    A verb needs the nominaliser の before が.

  • ❌ 上手じょうずに人ひと → ✅ 上手じょうずな人ひと
    に modifies verbs, な modifies nouns.

～のが上手 · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 彼かれは上手じょうずします。 → ✅ 彼かれは上手じょうずです。／上手じょうずにします。
    上手じょうず is not a verb; it needs the copula, or に if it is describing how something is done.

～のが上手 · Practice 1

1. 姉あねはケーキを作つくる____上手じょうずです。

ケーキを作つくるのが上手じょうずです。

The verb 作つくる must be nominalised by の before it can take が and be graded by 上手じょうず.

～のが上手 · Practice 2

2. 面接めんせつで：「私わたしはコンピューターが（上手じょうずです・得意とくいです）。」

私わたしはコンピューターが**得意とくいです**。

Talking about your own strengths calls for 得意とくい. 上手じょうず is an evaluation you hand to someone else, so applying it to yourself in an interview would sound like bragging rather than reporting.

～のが上手 · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "My grandfather is good at growing vegetables."

祖父そふは野菜やさいを**育そだてるのが上手じょうず**です。

A third person, a trainable skill, and a verb that needs のが - the textbook shape for this pattern. 野菜やさいが上手じょうずです on its own would not work, because it is the growing, not the vegetables, that is skilled.

～のが上手 · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼女かのじょはピアノを上手じょうずに人ひとです。

✅ 彼女かのじょはピアノが上手じょうずな人ひとです。

Two errors. The thing you are good at takes が, not を; and before the noun 人ひと the な-adjective must be 上手じょうずな, since に is the form that attaches to verbs.

～のが上手 · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 山田やまださんは英語えいごが**上手じょうずです。 (b) 山田やまださんは英語えいごができます**。

(b) reports a plain fact: Yamada has English at her disposal. It says nothing about how good she is, and it is the neutral thing to write in an email about who can handle a foreign client.

(a) grades her, and grading is a social act. It is warm and complimentary between equals or from above, but if Yamada outranks you it can read as presumptuous - さすがですね avoids the problem.

～のが下手

Meaning · to be bad at something - a lack of skill, plainly stated

下手へた is a な-adjective, the mirror image of 上手じょうず. What you are bad at is marked with が, and an action has to be nominalised with の first. Because it is a blunt verdict, it is comfortable about yourself - where it reads as modesty - and uncomfortable about anyone else.

～のが下手 · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary form) Dictionary + のが + 下手へた 踊おどる → 踊おどるのが下手へた
Noun Noun + が + 下手へた テニス → テニスが下手へた
Person + thing Person + は + Thing + が + 下手へた 私わたしは絵えが下手へたです
Adverbially (modifying a verb) 下手へたに + Verb 下手へたに動うごく

～のが下手 · Formation (cont.)

な-adjective form Rule Example
Plain 下手へただ 踊おどるのが下手へただ
Polite 下手へたです 踊おどるのが下手へたです
Negative 下手へたじゃない 下手へたじゃないです
Past 下手へただった／下手へたでした 下手へたでした
Before a noun 下手へたな + Noun 下手へたな絵え
Softened あまり上手じょうずじゃない あまり上手じょうずじゃないです

～のが下手 · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - 下手へた ends in た, not い, so nothing about it is an い-adjective: ❌下手へたくない, ❌下手へたかった. It is 下手へたじゃない and 下手へただった.

～のが下手 · Examples

私わたしは**泳およぐのが下手へた**です。
I'm a bad swimmer.

うちの父ちちは**運転うんてんが下手へた**だ。
My dad is a bad driver.

部屋へやを**片付かたづけるのが下手へた**です。
I'm bad at tidying my room.

昔むかしは人ひとの前まえで**話はなすのが下手へた**でした。
I used to be bad at speaking in front of people.

～のが下手 · Examples (cont.)

弟おとうとはまだはしを**使つかうのが下手へた**です。
My little brother still isn't good with chopsticks.

兄あにはサッカーは上手じょうずですが、テニスは**下手へた**です。
My older brother is good at football but bad at tennis.

私わたしは**断ことわるのが下手へた**なので、いつも仕事しごとが多おおすぎます。
I'm bad at saying no, so I always end up with too much work.

字じが**下手へた**ですから、メールで送おくってもいいですか。
My handwriting is terrible, so would it be all right if I sent it by email?

～のが下手 · Nuance & register

Pointed at yourself, 下手へた is safe and even likeable. Japanese conversation runs on modesty, and 料理りょうりが下手へたなんです is a normal, friendly thing to say about your own cooking. There is no need to soften it.

Pointed at anyone else it is a judgement, and a harsh one. Telling a colleague 英語えいごが下手へたですね is close to an insult in English terms. The two standard repairs are あまり上手じょうずじゃない (a hedged negative) and 苦手にがて (a weakness rather than a failing) - and about a superior you simply do not go there at all.

～のが下手 · Nuance & register (cont.)

下手へたくそ exists and is much cruder; it is what one friend shouts at another over a video game, not something to try on strangers. Note also that unlike 上手じょうず, 下手へた has no polite お-form in ordinary use - there is nothing to be honorific about.

Note - 下手へたに before a verb has drifted away from "badly". It means carelessly, without thinking, rashly: 下手へたに動うごくと危あぶないです「Move carelessly and you'll get hurt」. The literal "does it badly" sense is usually carried by 下手へたな絵え or あまり上手じょうずじゃない instead.

～のが下手 · 下手 vs 苦手

Rule of thumb - 下手へた is about skill; 苦手にがて is about how you feel about it. 苦手にがて covers things you have no skill in at all - food, insects, particular people.

私わたしは歌うたを**歌うたうのが下手へた**です。
I'm a bad singer.

私わたしはカラオケが**苦手にがて**です。
I'm not keen on karaoke.

～のが下手 · 下手 vs 苦手 (cont.)

The first is a claim about your voice. The second says karaoke makes you uncomfortable - perhaps because you cannot sing, perhaps because you hate being looked at. That extra emotional layer is why 苦手にがて reaches places 下手へた cannot: なっとうが苦手にがてです and あの人ひとは少すこし苦手にがてです are both natural, while ❌なっとうが下手へたです is nonsense - you do not perform natto.

苦手にがて is also the kinder word about other people, because "he finds it hard" is gentler than "he is bad at it": 山田やまださんは数字すうじが苦手にがてみたいです.

～のが下手 · 下手 vs あまり上手じゃない

Rule of thumb - about yourself, 下手へた. About other people, the hedged negative あまり上手じょうずじゃない.

私わたしは**下手へた**ですが、やってみます。
I'm bad at this, but I'll give it a go.

彼かれは料理りょうりがあまり上手じょうずじゃないです。
He isn't a very good cook.

～のが下手 · 下手 vs あまり上手じゃない (cont.)

The hedge does two things at once. あまり plus a negative is the standard Japanese way of saying "not very", and phrasing the criticism as a shortfall of skill rather than presence of badness takes the sting out. Add みたいです or ようです and it softens again, because you are now reporting an impression rather than passing sentence.

The asymmetry is the same one that governs 上手じょうず, run in reverse: you praise other people and criticise yourself, never the other way round.

～のが下手 · Noun が下手 vs Verb のが下手

Rule of thumb - if a single noun names the skill, use it bare. Reach for のが when the skill only exists as an action.

姉あねはピアノが**下手へた**です。
My older sister is bad at the piano.

姉あねはピアノを**人ひとの前まえで弾ひくのが下手へた**です。
My older sister is bad at playing the piano in front of an audience.

～のが下手 · Noun が下手 vs Verb のが下手 (cont.)

Nouns that already name an activity - ピアノ, テニス, 料理りょうり, 運転うんてん - go straight in with が. Adding を弾ひくのが is not wrong, it just spells the activity out, which is what you want as soon as the description gets more specific than the noun can carry.

And some skills have no noun at all. There is no single word for "remembering people's names" or "saying no", so the verb clause is the only route: 人ひとの名前なまえを覚おぼえるのが下手へたです. What you cannot do is drop the の and hand the raw verb to the adjective.

～のが下手 · Common mistakes

  • ❌ なっとうが**下手へたです。 → ✅ なっとうが苦手にがて**です。
    Food is not a skill. 苦手にがて is the word for things you would rather avoid.

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんは英語えいごが**下手へたですね。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんは英語えいごがあまり上手じょうずじゃない**ですね。
    Direct 下手へた about another person is rude. Hedge it, or use 苦手にがて.

  • ❌ 踊おどるが下手へたです。 → ✅ 踊おどるのが下手へたです。
    The verb has to be nominalised by の before it can take が.

  • ❌ 下手へたくないです。 → ✅ 下手へたじゃないです。
    下手へた is a な-adjective. The final た is part of the reading, not an adjective ending.

～のが下手 · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 下手へたの絵え → ✅ 下手へたな絵え
    な-adjectives take な before a noun.

～のが下手 · Practice 1

1. 私わたしは人ひとに道みちを説明せつめいする____下手へたです。

道みちを説明せつめいするのが下手へたです。

説明せつめいする is a verb, so の turns it into something が can mark. You could also say 説明せつめいが下手へたです, using the noun on its own.

～のが下手 · Practice 2

2. 課長かちょうに：「部長ぶちょうはゴルフが（下手へたです・あまり上手じょうずじゃないです）ね。」

部長ぶちょうはゴルフがあまり**上手じょうずじゃないです**ね。

Even this is risky about a superior, but of the two it is the survivable one. A flat 下手へた about someone above you is a straight insult; the hedged negative reads as an observation rather than a verdict.

～のが下手 · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'm bad at getting up early."

私わたしは朝あさ早はやく**起おきるのが下手へた**です。

About yourself, 下手へた is the modest, natural choice. Many speakers would also say 苦手にがてです here, which adds "and I dislike it" to the meaning.

～のが下手 · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 私わたしは歌うたが下手へたくなかったです。

✅ 私わたしは歌うたが**下手へたじゃなかった**です。

下手へた only looks like an い-adjective. It is a な-adjective, so the negative past is じゃなかった, exactly as with 静しずかじゃなかった.

～のが下手 · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 私わたしは漢字かんじを書かくのが**下手へたです。 (b) 私わたしは漢字かんじが苦手にがて**です。

(a) is a statement about the output: your kanji come out badly formed. It says nothing about how you feel while writing them.

(b) is about your relationship with kanji - they are your weak point, you avoid them, tests involving them make you nervous. It may or may not imply that your handwriting is poor, and it is the sentence a learner is usually reaching for when they want to say "kanji are my nemesis".
