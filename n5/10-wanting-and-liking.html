N5 - Wanting and liking

  • ～のが好き
  • ～のが上手

～たい

Meaning · want to do something - the speaker's own desire

～たい attaches to a verb and reports a wish felt from the inside. That "from the inside" is not decoration: it is a grammatical restriction. Plain ～たい is for your own desires, and for asking about the listener's. You cannot use it to state flatly what a third person wants.

～たい · Formation

Attach たい to the ます-stem, then conjugate it exactly like an い-adjective.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + たい 飲(の)みます → 飲(の)みたい
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + たい 食(た)べます → 食(た)べたい
Verb (する) し + たい する → したい／勉強(べんきょう)したい
Verb (来(く)る) き + たい 来(く)る → 来(き)たい

Once たい is attached, 飲(の)みたい behaves like 高(たか)い in every respect:

～たい · Formation (cont.)

い-adjective form Rule Example
Polite + です 飲(の)みたいです
Negative たい → たくない 飲(の)みたくない
Past たい → たかった 飲(の)みたかった
Negative past たい → たくなかった 飲(の)みたくなかった
て-form たい → たくて 飲(の)みたくて
Adverbial たい → たく 飲(の)みたくなる
Before a noun たい + Noun 飲(の)みたいもの
Third person ます-stem + たがる 飲(の)みたがる／飲(の)みたがっている

～たい · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - たい is an い-adjective, not a verb. So it is 行(い)きたくない, never ❌行(い)きたいじゃない, and 行(い)きたかった, never ❌行(い)きたいでした.

～たい · Examples

水(みず)が飲(の)みたい。
I want some water.

日本(にほん)へ行(い)きたいです。
I want to go to Japan.

何(なに)が食(た)べたいですか。
What do you want to eat?

今日(きょう)はどこにも行(い)きたくない。
I don't want to go anywhere today.

子(こ)どものとき、パイロットになりたかった。
When I was a kid, I wanted to be a pilot.

～たい · Examples (cont.)

弟(おとうと)は新(あたら)しいゲームを買(か)いたがっている。
My little brother wants to buy the new game.

疲(つか)れたので今日(きょう)は早(はや)く寝(ね)たいんですが、まだ仕事(しごと)が終(お)わりません。
I'm exhausted and I want an early night, but I still haven't finished work.

来年(らいねん)は沖縄(おきなわ)に行(い)って、海(うみ)で泳(およ)ぎたいと思(おも)っています。
Next year I'm hoping to go to Okinawa and swim in the sea.

～たい · Nuance & register

～たい itself is neutral; ～たいです is polite in form. The trouble is that politeness of form does not fix bluntness of content. Stating a raw want at someone senior - 部長(ぶちょう)、早(はや)く帰(かえ)りたいです - is grammatical and still lands badly, because you are pushing your preference at a superior. Softeners exist for exactly this:

  • ～たいと思(おも)います - "I'm thinking I'd like to…". The standard business
    hedge. 来週(らいしゅう)お休(やす)みを取(と)りたいと思(おも)います。
  • ～たいんですが - sets up a request and leaves the ask unsaid.
    すみません、これを返(かえ)したいんですが…
  • ～たいのですが／～たく存(ぞん)じます - written and formal registers.

～たい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The same asymmetry runs the other way. Asking a superior 何(なに)が食(た)べたいですか is a bit bald; 何(なん)になさいますか or 何(なに)がよろしいですか is what you actually say. Among friends and to subordinates, plain ～たい? is perfectly normal.

In casual speech the negative ～たくない is the everyday form; ～たくありません is correct but stiff, and mostly turns up in writing or careful speech.

～たがる is a real verb (a う-verb: たがらない, たがります, たがって) and it carries a slight sense of watching someone from outside - you are describing observable behaviour, not reading a mind. That distance can shade into criticism, so たがる about a superior is rude: ❌社長(しゃちょう)は帰(かえ)りたがっています.

～たい · ～たい vs ～たがる

Rule of thumb - ～たい for me and for you; ～たがる for him, her and them.

A flat statement about a third person's desire in plain ～たい sounds wrong to native ears, because you are claiming direct access to someone else's inner state.

妹(いもうと)はケーキを食(た)べたがっています。
My little sister wants cake.

Note the particle: たがる is an ordinary verb, so its object takes を, never the が that たい allows. Note the ～ている too. For a desire someone is feeling right now, たがっている is the normal form; bare たがる reads as a habit - 弟(おとうと)はいつも新(あたら)しいものを買(か)いたがる - "my brother always wants to buy the newest thing."

たがる is not the only way out. These are often more natural, and politer:

～たい · ～たい vs ～たがる (cont.)

  • 妹(いもうと)はケーキが食(た)べたいそうです。 (I hear that…)
  • 妹(いもうと)はケーキが食(た)べたいと言(い)っています。 (she says…)
  • 妹(いもうと)はケーキが食(た)べたいようです／みたいです。 (it seems…)

Note - the third-person ban only applies to a plain main-clause statement. Inside a subordinate clause, plain たい about someone else is fine: 日本(にほん)に行(い)きたい人(ひと)は手(て)を挙(あ)げてください。 「Anyone who wants to go to Japan, raise your hand.」 It is also fine under と思(おも)っている: 彼(かれ)は行(い)きたいと思(おも)っています。

～たい · が vs を with ～たい

Rule of thumb - both are correct. が spotlights the thing you crave; を keeps the focus on the action. When in doubt at N5, が is the safer answer.

Because たい turns the verb into an adjective-like word, the old object can take が - and that option is genuinely alive in modern Japanese.

ビールが飲(の)みたい。
I want a beer. (it's beer specifically that I'm after)

ビールを飲(の)みたい。
I want to drink a beer. (the drinking is what I'm picturing)

Three places where を is the only option:

～たい · が vs を with ～たい (cont.)

  1. The を is not a direct object. Path and departure を never becomes が: 空(そら)を飛(と)びたい, 家(いえ)を出(で)たい. 2. The clause already has a が. Two が in one clause collide: ❌私(わたし)がビールが飲(の)みたい. 3. The object is far from the verb. Once material intervenes, が degrades: ビールを冷(ひ)やして、あとでゆっくり飲(の)みたい.

を is also the safer default in writing and in longer sentences; が is at its most natural in short, felt statements - the ones you say when you actually want the thing.

～たい · ～たい vs ほしい

Rule of thumb - ほしい wants a noun; ～たい wants an action.

新(あたら)しいパソコンがほしいです。
I want a new computer.

新(あたら)しいパソコンが買(か)いたいです。
I want to buy a new computer.

ほしい is also an い-adjective, and it carries the same first-person restriction as たい - third person needs ほしがっている. Its verb-taking cousin, ～てほしい, means you want someone else to do something: 手伝(てつだ)ってほしい - "I want you to help."

～たい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 田中(たなか)さんは日本(にほん)に行(い)きたいです。 → ✅ 田中(たなか)さんは日本(にほん)に行(い)きたがっています。
    A flat statement about a third person's desire needs たがる, or a hedge like たいそうです／たいと言(い)っています.

  • ❌ 行(い)きたいじゃない／行(い)きたいでした → ✅ 行(い)きたくない／行(い)きたかった
    たい is an い-adjective. It never takes じゃない or でした.

  • ❌ 食(た)べるたい → ✅ 食(た)べたい
    たい attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form. 食(た)べます → 食(た)べ + たい.

  • ❌ コーヒーがほしいたい。 → ✅ コーヒーが飲(の)みたい。／コーヒーがほしい。
    ほしい and たい do the same job for different word classes. Never stack them.

～たい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 課長(かちょう)、何(なに)が食(た)べたいですか。 → ✅ 課長(かちょう)、何(なに)がよろしいですか。
    Grammatical, but too direct upward. Asking a superior about their wants calls for よろしいですか or なさいますか.

～たい · Practice 1

1. のどがかわきました。水(みず)が飲(の)____です。

水(みず)が飲(の)みたいです。

たい attaches to the ます-stem: 飲(の)みます → 飲(の)み + たい. The polite form is just です on the end, because 飲(の)みたい is an い-adjective.

～たい · Practice 2

2. 妹(いもうと)は新(あたら)しい靴(くつ)を（買(か)いたいです・買(か)いたがっています）。

妹(いもうと)は新(あたら)しい靴(くつ)を買(か)いたがっています。

妹(いもうと) is a third person, so plain たい in a main clause is out - you cannot assert someone else's inner state directly. たがっている describes the desire as something you can observe from outside.

～たい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I didn't want to go to school yesterday."

きのうは学校(がっこう)に行(い)きたくなかった（です）。

Past negative of an い-adjective: たい → たく → たくない → たくなかった. There is no ❌行(い)きたいじゃなかった. Adding です makes it polite without changing the stem.

～たい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 今日(きょう)は映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)きたいじゃないです。

✅ 今日(きょう)は映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)きたくないです。

じゃない is the negative for nouns and な-adjectives. ～たい is an い-adjective, so the negative is built by swapping the final い for くない.

～たい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) ラーメンが食(た)べたい。 (b) ラーメンを食(た)べたい。

Both are correct and a speaker would accept either. (a) puts the spotlight on ramen as the object of the craving - "it's ramen I want", the thing itself. (b) keeps the sentence built around the action of eating, so it reads a touch more neutral and descriptive, and it's what you'd reach for in a longer or written sentence.

The choice narrows once the sentence grows: with material between the object and the verb, or with a が-marked subject already present, only を works.

ほしい

Meaning · want a thing - the speaker's own desire for an object

ほしい (kanji 欲(ほ)しい, but usually written in kana at this level) is an い-adjective, not a verb. It does not take an object with を; the thing you want is marked with が. And like ～たい, it reports a want felt from the inside, so plain ほしい covers your own wants and questions about the listener's - never a flat statement about what a third person wants.

ほしい · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (the thing wanted) Noun + が + ほしい 車(くるま) → 車(くるま)がほしい
Verb (an action you want from someone) て-form + ほしい 手伝(てつだ)う → 手伝(てつだ)ってほしい
Verb (an action you want to do) not possible - use ～たい 休(やす)む → 休(やす)みたい
Third person's want Noun + を + ほしがる 犬(いぬ) → 犬(いぬ)をほしがっている

Once ほしい is in place it conjugates exactly like 高(たか)い or 安(やす)い:

ほしい · Formation (cont.)

い-adjective form Rule Example
Polite + です ほしいです
Negative ほしい → ほしくない ほしくない
Polite negative + です／ありません ほしくないです／ほしくありません
Past ほしい → ほしかった ほしかった
Negative past ほしい → ほしくなかった ほしくなかった
て-form ほしい → ほしくて ほしくて
Adverbial ほしい → ほしく ほしくなる
Before a noun ほしい + Noun ほしいもの

ほしい · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ほしい is an い-adjective. So it is ほしくない, never ❌ほしいじゃない, and ほしかった, never ❌ほしいでした. And it never conjugates like a verb: there is no ❌ほしみます.

ほしい · Examples

お金(かね)がほしい。
I want money.

もっと時間(じかん)がほしいです。
I want more time.

誕生日(たんじょうび)に何(なに)がほしいですか。
What do you want for your birthday?

今(いま)は何(なに)もほしくない。
I don't want anything right now.

子(こ)どものころ、自分(じぶん)の部屋(へや)がほしかった。
When I was little I wanted a room of my own.

ほしい · Examples (cont.)

弟(おとうと)は前(まえ)から犬(いぬ)をほしがっています。
My little brother has wanted a dog for ages.

返事(へんじ)は急(いそ)ぎませんが、一度(いちど)ゆっくり話(はな)す機会(きかい)がほしいです。
There's no rush on an answer, but I'd like a chance to talk it through properly.

この書類(しょるい)、明日(あした)の朝(あさ)までに目(め)を通(とお)してほしいんですが、お願(ねが)いできますか。
I'd like you to look these documents over by tomorrow morning - could I ask you to?

ほしい · Nuance & register

ほしいです is polite in form only. The content is still a bare statement of appetite, and pointing it at someone above you lands like a child asking for a present: 部長(ぶちょう)、新(あたら)しいパソコンがほしいです is grammatical and socially awkward. Upward, you reframe the want as a request or a hedge:

  • ～がほしいのですが／～がほしいんですが - trails off and lets the listener
    offer. 資料(しりょう)のコピーがほしいんですが…
  • ～をいただけますか／お願(ねが)いできますか - asks instead of wanting.
  • ～があると助(たす)かります - "it would help if I had…", the standard
    workplace softener.

The same asymmetry runs upward in questions. 社長(しゃちょう)、何(なに)がほしいですか is far too direct; offering with いかがですか is what you actually say.

ほしい · Nuance & register (cont.)

In a shop or a café, ほしい is simply the wrong tool. You do not say これがほしいです to a waiter - you say これをください or これをお願(ねが)いします. ほしい describes your inner state; ください performs the request.

Casually, ほしい is very common and takes all the usual endings: ほしいなあ (wistful), ほしい！, ほしいんだけど…. Men and women use it alike. In writing the kanji 欲(ほ)しい appears. What ほしい does not have is a humble register of its own - there is no polite conjugation that makes it suitable upward, which is why formal Japanese switches word entirely, to 頂(いただ)きたい or お願(ねが)いしたい.

ほしい · Nuance & register (cont.)

ほしがる is an ordinary う-verb (欲(ほ)しがらない, ほしがります, ほしがって). It describes desire as something you can see in someone - the reaching, the asking, the looking. That outside-observer flavour makes it faintly belittling, so it is not used about superiors: ❌課長(かちょう)は新(あたら)しい車(くるま)をほしがっています.

ほしい · ほしい vs ～たい

Rule of thumb - ほしい wants a noun; ～たい wants an action. They divide the work; they never stack.

アイスクリームがほしい。
I want ice cream.

アイスクリームが食(た)べたい。
I want to eat ice cream.

The two are otherwise twins: both are い-adjectives, both mark their target with が, both are barred from flat third-person statements, and both have a ～がる form for other people. What ほしい cannot do is attach to a verb - ❌休(やす)むがほしい is not a sentence. If the thing you want is an action you perform yourself, ～たい is the only route.

ほしい · ほしい vs ～てほしい

Rule of thumb - Noun + がほしい wants a thing; Verb-て + ほしい wants someone else to do something. The person you want it from takes に.

説明書(せつめいしょ)がほしい。
I want the instruction manual.

使(つか)い方(かた)を教(おし)えてほしい。
I want you to show me how to use it.

母(はは)に来(き)てほしくない。
I don't want my mother to come.

ほしい · ほしい vs ～てほしい (cont.)

～てほしい is a wish, not a command, so it is gentler than a direct request - but it is also more personal, and to a superior it presumes. The polite ladder runs 待(ま)ってほしい → 待(ま)っていただきたい → 待(ま)っていただけないでしょうか. The negative is ～ないでほしい: 勝手(かって)に決(き)めないでほしい.

ほしい · ほしい vs ほしがる

Rule of thumb - ほしい for me and you; ほしがる for him, her and them. The particle flips too: が with ほしい, を with ほしがる.

娘(むすめ)はずっとピアノをほしがっています。
My daughter has been wanting a piano for a long time.

Note the ～ている. A desire someone is feeling now is ほしがっている; bare ほしがる reads as a habit - あの子(こ)は友(とも)だちのものを何(なん)でもほしがる.

ほしがる is not the only escape. These are often smoother, and safer upward:

  • 妹(いもうと)は新(あたら)しい辞書(じしょ)がほしいそうです。 (I hear that…)
  • 妹(いもうと)は新(あたら)しい辞書(じしょ)がほしいと言(い)っています。 (she says…)
  • 妹(いもうと)は新(あたら)しい辞書(じしょ)がほしいようです。 (it seems…)

ほしい · ほしい vs ほしがる (cont.)

Note - the ban is on plain main-clause statements only. Inside a modifying clause, plain ほしい about other people is perfectly normal: 資料(しりょう)がほしい人(ひと)は、前(まえ)の机(つくえ)から取(と)ってください。 「Anyone who wants a copy, take one from the desk at the front.」

ほしい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私(わたし)は車(くるま)をほしいです。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は車(くるま)がほしいです。
    ほしい is an adjective, so it has no direct object. を appears only with the verb ほしがる.

  • ❌ 山田(やまだ)さんは新(あたら)しい車(くるま)がほしいです。 → ✅ 山田(やまだ)さんは新(あたら)しい車(くるま)をほしがっています。
    A flat statement about a third person's want needs ほしがっている, or a hedge like ほしいそうです.

  • ❌ ほしいじゃない／ほしいでした → ✅ ほしくない／ほしかった
    い-adjective endings, not な-adjective ones.

  • ❌ 休(やす)むがほしい。 → ✅ 休(やす)みたい。
    ほしい cannot take a verb. Wanting to perform an action is ～たい territory.

  • ❌ （カフェで）これがほしいです。 → ✅ これをください。
    Naming your inner state at a server is odd. Ordering is done with ください or お願(ねが)いします.

ほしい · Practice 1

1. 新(あたら)しいカメラ____ほしいです。

新(あたら)しいカメラがほしいです。

ほしい is an い-adjective, and what it wants is marked with が, not を. The English "want a camera" has an object, but the Japanese does not.

ほしい · Practice 2

2. 兄(あに)はバイクを（ほしいです・ほしがっています）。

兄(あに)はバイクをほしがっています。

兄(あに) is a third person, so a plain ほしい statement would be claiming direct access to someone else's inner state. ほしがっている reports the desire as something you can observe - and note that it takes を, which is why the sentence was written with を to begin with.

ほしい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "When I was a student, I wanted a motorbike."

学生(がくせい)のとき、バイクがほしかった（です）。

The past of an い-adjective: ほしい → ほしかった. There is no ❌ほしいでした; adding です after ほしかった is what makes it polite.

ほしい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 辞書(じしょ)をほしいんですが、いい店(みせ)を知(し)りませんか。

✅ 辞書(じしょ)がほしいんですが、いい店(みせ)を知(し)りませんか。

The を is the interference: English treats "want" as a transitive verb, Japanese treats ほしい as an adjective whose target takes が.

ほしい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 静(しず)かにしてほしい。 (b) 静(しず)かにしてください。

(b) is a direct request aimed at the listener - it tells them to do something, and in the wrong context it can sound like an instruction. (a) reports the speaker's wish instead of issuing the request, so it is indirect, and among friends it comes across as softer, almost a complaint said out loud.

Indirectness is not the same as politeness upward, though. To a superior neither is ideal: 静(しず)かにしていただけませんか is the form that actually works.

～のが好き

Meaning · like doing something - a standing preference, not a present urge

好(す)き is a な-adjective meaning "pleasing, to my taste", and what pleases you is marked with が. English makes "like" a verb with an object; Japanese makes it an adjective with a subject. To like an action, you first turn the verb into a noun with the nominaliser の, then feed it to 好(す)き.

～のが好き · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary form) Dictionary + のが + 好(す)き 泳(およ)ぐ → 泳(およ)ぐのが好(す)き
Verb (alternative nominaliser) Dictionary + ことが + 好(す)き 読(よ)む → 読(よ)むことが好(す)き
Noun Noun + が + 好(す)き 音楽(おんがく) → 音楽(おんがく)が好(す)き
Verb (する-noun) Noun + が + 好(す)き 料理(りょうり)する → 料理(りょうり)が好(す)き

好(す)き then behaves like 静(しず)か or 元気(げんき) - a な-adjective in every respect:

～のが好き · Formation (cont.)

な-adjective form Rule Example
Plain 好(す)きだ 泳(およ)ぐのが好(す)きだ
Polite 好(す)きです 泳(およ)ぐのが好(す)きです
Negative 好(す)きじゃない 泳(およ)ぐのが好(す)きじゃない
Polite negative 好(す)きじゃないです／ではありません 好(す)きじゃありません
Past 好(す)きだった／好(す)きでした 好(す)きでした
Negative past 好(す)きじゃなかった 好(す)きじゃなかったです
Before a noun 好(す)きな + Noun 好(す)きな歌(うた)
Intensified 大好(だいす)き 歌(うた)うのが大好(だいす)き

～のが好き · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - 好(す)き is an adjective, so there is no ❌好(す)きます, no ❌好(す)きません and no ❌好(す)きくない. And the verb needs の to become a noun first: ❌読(よ)むが好(す)き → ✅読(よ)むのが好(す)き.

～のが好き · Examples

歌(うた)を歌(うた)うのが好(す)きです。
I like singing.

私(わたし)は走(はし)るのが好(す)きじゃない。
I don't like running.

どんな音楽(おんがく)を聞(き)くのが好(す)きですか。
What kind of music do you like listening to?

父(ちち)は朝(あさ)早(はや)く起(お)きるのが好(す)きです。
My father likes getting up early.

子(こ)どものころは絵(え)をかくのが大好(だいす)きでした。
As a child I absolutely loved drawing.

～のが好き · Examples (cont.)

雨(あめ)の日(ひ)に家(いえ)で映画(えいが)を見(み)るのが好(す)きです。
I like watching films at home on a rainy day.

妻(つま)は週末(しゅうまつ)に市場(いちば)まで歩(ある)いて野菜(やさい)を選(えら)ぶのが好(す)きです。
My wife likes walking to the market at the weekend and picking out the vegetables.

地図(ちず)を見(み)ないで知(し)らない町(まち)を歩(ある)き回(まわ)るのが好(す)きなんです。
The thing is, I love wandering around a town I don't know without looking at a map.

～のが好き · Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral. 好(す)きです is the everyday polite form, 好(す)きだ the plain one, and in casual speech the copula usually vanishes altogether - 甘(あま)いものが好(す)き, with a rising tone for a question. Upward, お好(す)きですか is the polite version: 部長(ぶちょう)、コーヒーはお好(す)きですか.

One important difference from ～たい: 好(す)き may be used flatly about a third person. A preference is a settled, observable trait, not a private impulse, so 姉(あね)は山(やま)に登(のぼ)るのが好(す)きです needs no がる and no hedge. This is one of the few places where describing someone else is easier than describing yourself.

Be careful with people as the object. 田中(たなか)さんが好(す)きです is a confession of romantic feeling, not "I get on with Tanaka". If you mean the latter, say 田中(たなか)さんは、いい人(ひと)ですね or 田中(たなか)さんと話(はな)すのが好(す)きです - attaching the liking to an activity defuses it.

～のが好き · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, negation carries weight. Japanese hedges dislike far more than English does: あまり好(す)きじゃないです is the polite way to decline food, music or a suggestion, and it is what a learner should reach for by default.

～のが好き · ～のが好(す)き vs ～ことが好(す)き

Rule of thumb - の for concrete, lived, experienced activity; こと for the activity in the abstract. With 好(す)き both are usually fine, and の is the more conversational of the two.

海(うみ)で泳(およ)ぐのが好(す)きです。
I like swimming in the sea.

人(ひと)に教(おし)えることが好(す)きです。
I like teaching people.

The first is the feel of the water; the second is teaching as a category of thing. Push the difference and you can hear it, but for a plain statement of taste nobody will correct you either way.

Where the choice stops being free is when the nominalised clause is not the object of an adjective but a noun in its own right - a は…です sentence, for instance. There only こと works:

～のが好き · ～のが好(す)き vs ～ことが好(す)き (cont.)

趣味(しゅみ)は古(ふる)い本(ほん)を集(あつ)めることです。
My hobby is collecting old books.

❌集(あつ)めるのです would be read as the explanatory のです, which means something else entirely.

～のが好き · ～のが好(す)き vs ～たい

Rule of thumb - 好(す)き is what you like in general; ～たい is what you want now. Liking something does not mean you want it this minute.

コーヒーを飲(の)むのが好(す)きです。
I like drinking coffee. (a habit, a taste)

今(いま)、コーヒーが飲(の)みたいです。
I want a coffee right now. (an urge)

The two also behave differently across persons. ～たい cannot be stated flatly about someone else, but 好(す)き can - which is why a description of another person's tastes almost always comes out in 好(す)き: 夫(おっと)は料理(りょうり)するのが好(す)きです.

～のが好き · 好(す)き vs 嫌(きら)い

Rule of thumb - 嫌(きら)い is much stronger than English "don't like". The everyday negative is あまり好(す)きじゃない.

運動(うんどう)するのはあまり好(す)きじゃないです。
I'm not that keen on exercising.

虫(むし)が嫌(きら)いです。
I hate insects.

Two traps sit here. First, 嫌(きら)い ends in い but is a な-adjective: 嫌(きら)いな食(た)べ物(もの), 嫌(きら)いじゃない, never ❌嫌(きら)くない. Second, notice the は in the first sentence. Swapping the が for は when you negate is standard, and it softens further - you are marking this one thing as the exception rather than making a sweeping declaration.

～のが好き · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 本(ほん)を読(よ)むが好(す)きです。 → ✅ 本(ほん)を読(よ)むのが好(す)きです。
    A verb cannot be the subject of an adjective on its own. の nominalises it.

  • ❌ 音楽(おんがく)を好(す)きです。 → ✅ 音楽(おんがく)が好(す)きです。
    Interference from English. 好(す)き is not a verb and has no direct object.

  • ❌ ピアノを好(す)きます。 → ✅ ピアノが好(す)きです。
    好(す)き takes adjective endings. A verb 好(す)く does exist, but in everyday Japanese it survives mainly in the passive (人(ひと)に好(す)かれる).

  • ❌ 泳(およ)ぐのが好(す)きくないです。 → ✅ 泳(およ)ぐのが好(す)きじゃないです。
    な-adjective negation, despite the final き looking harmless.

  • ❌ 好(す)きの食(た)べ物(もの) → ✅ 好(す)きな食(た)べ物(もの)
    Before a noun, a な-adjective takes な, not の.

～のが好き · Practice 1

1. 私(わたし)は自転車(じてんしゃ)に乗(の)る____好(す)きです。

自転車(じてんしゃ)に乗(の)るのが好(す)きです。

The verb 乗(の)る has to become a noun before it can be the subject of 好(す)き, and that is what の does. The particle after it is が, never を.

～のが好き · Practice 2

2. 弟(おとうと)は甘(あま)いものを食(た)べるのが（好(す)きです・食(た)べたいです）。

弟(おとうと)は甘(あま)いものを食(た)べるのが好(す)きです。

Two reasons. The sentence describes a standing taste rather than a present urge, and it is about a third person, where a flat ～たい is not allowed anyway. The second option would also leave the nominalised clause stranded.

～のが好き · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "My grandmother likes taking photographs of flowers."

祖母(そぼ)は花(はな)の写真(しゃしん)を撮(と)るのが好(す)きです。

Note that no hedge is needed even though the subject is a third person: unlike ～たい, a preference can be asserted about other people directly.

～のが好き · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 兄(あに)はサッカーを好(す)きの人(ひと)です。

Two problems in one short sentence. The object of 好(す)き takes が, not を; and before a noun a な-adjective uses な, not の.

～のが好き · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) パクチーはあまり好(す)きじゃないです。 (b) パクチーが嫌(きら)いです。

(b) is a flat declaration of dislike and sounds surprisingly strong in Japanese - closer to "I can't stand coriander" than to "I'm not a fan". (a) is the polite default: the softener あまり plus a negative, with は marking coriander as the one exception rather than condemning it outright.

If a host offers you something you would rather not eat, (a) is the sentence you want. (b) risks sounding like a complaint about the food.

～のが上手

Meaning · to be good at something - skill you can see in the result

上手(じょうず) is a な-adjective describing an ability that has visibly been trained. What someone is good at is marked with が, and to say they are good at an action you nominalise the verb with の first. The important thing to learn alongside the grammar is who you may say it about: 上手(じょうず) is a judgement handed down, so it is used about other people, and almost never about yourself.

～のが上手 · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary form) Dictionary + のが + 上手(じょうず) 話(はな)す → 話(はな)すのが上手(じょうず)
Noun Noun + が + 上手(じょうず) ピアノ → ピアノが上手(じょうず)
Person + thing Person + は + Thing + が + 上手(じょうず) 姉(あね)は絵(え)が上手(じょうず)です
Adverbially (modifying a verb) 上手(じょうず)に + Verb 上手(じょうず)に使(つか)う

The な-adjective forms behave as expected:

な-adjective form Rule Example
Plain 上手(じょうず)だ 歌(うた)うのが上手(じょうず)だ
Polite 上手(じょうず)です 歌(うた)うのが上手(じょうず)です

Rule of thumb - 上手(じょうず) before a noun takes な; before a verb it takes に. 上手(じょうず)な字(じ) "neat handwriting" versus 上手(じょうず)に書(か)く "to write neatly".

～のが上手 · Examples

母(はは)は**料理(りょうり)を作(つく)るのが上手(じょうず)**です。
My mother is good at cooking.

彼(かれ)は**字(じ)を書(か)くのが上手(じょうず)**ですね。
He has lovely handwriting, doesn't he.

兄(あに)は人(ひと)の名前(なまえ)を**覚(おぼ)えるのが上手(じょうず)**じゃないです。
My older brother isn't good at remembering people's names.

田中(たなか)さんは人(ひと)の話(はなし)を**聞(き)くのが上手(じょうず)**です。
Tanaka is a good listener.

息子(むすこ)はまだ五(ご)さいですが、自転車(じてんしゃ)に**乗(の)るのが上手(じょうず)**です。
My son is only five, but he's good at riding a bicycle.

～のが上手 · Examples (cont.)

娘(むすめ)は小(ちい)さいころから絵(え)を上手(じょうず)にかきました。
My daughter drew well from the time she was small.

山田(やまだ)さんは会議(かいぎ)で話(はなし)を**まとめるのが上手(じょうず)**なので、いつも司会(しかい)をお願(ねが)いしています。
Yamada is good at pulling a discussion together, so we always ask her to chair.

祖母(そぼ)は昔(むかし)から着物(きもの)を**着(き)るのが上手(じょうず)**で、近所(きんじょ)の人(ひと)にもよく教(おし)えています。
My grandmother has always been good at putting on a kimono, and she often teaches the neighbours too.

～のが上手 · Nuance & register

上手(じょうず) is a verdict. You are standing outside someone's performance and rating it, and that is exactly why the word behaves the way it does socially.

First, you do not normally use it about yourself. ❌私(わたし)は英語(えいご)が上手(じょうず)です is grammatical and sounds boastful; the natural equivalents are 英語(えいご)が得意(とくい)です, 英語(えいご)が少(すこ)し話(はな)せます, or the modest classic まだまだです.

Second - and this catches learners going the other way - praising a superior with 上手(じょうず) is also awkward, because rating someone above you is not your place. 部長(ぶちょう)、英語(えいご)がお上手(じょうず)ですね is softened by the honorific お, and is heard, but さすがですね or 勉強(べんきょう)になります is safer. Between equals and downward it is completely normal.

～のが上手 · Nuance & register (cont.)

Third, when someone says 日本語(にほんご)がお上手(じょうず)ですね to you, the expected reply is not ありがとうございます but a deflection: いいえ、まだまだです or そんなことないです. Accepting the compliment flatly is not wrong, just noticeably un-Japanese.

お上手(じょうず)ですね said with the wrong timing means "you flatterer" - お上手(じょうず)を言(い)って is a fixed phrase for exactly that. Context carries it, but be aware the word has that second life.

～のが上手 · ～のが上手(じょうず) vs ～のが得意(とくい)

Rule of thumb - 上手(じょうず) rates someone else; 得意(とくい) is how you talk about yourself. 得意(とくい) also carries confidence, not just skill.

林(はやし)さんはギターを**弾(ひ)くのが上手(じょうず)**です。
Hayashi is good at playing the guitar.

私(わたし)は人(ひと)の前(まえ)で**話(はな)すのが得意(とくい)**です。
I'm good at speaking in front of people.

The difference is where the judgement comes from. 上手(じょうず) is an outside assessment of quality, so turning it on yourself is self-congratulation. 得意(とくい) describes your own sense of what you are strong at and can be asserted without immodesty - it is what you write on a job application.

～のが上手 · ～のが上手(じょうず) vs ～のが得意(とくい) (cont.)

Note that 得意(とくい) is not a full escape either: said too readily it still sounds confident, so many speakers reach for 少(すこ)しできます or a plain potential form instead.

得意(とくい) about a third person is fine too, but it then reports their confidence: 弟(おとうと)は数学(すうがく)が得意(とくい)です suggests he would say so himself, whereas 弟(おとうと)は数学(すうがく)が上手(じょうず)です is your opinion of him - and for school subjects it sounds odd, because 上手(じょうず) wants a performed skill.

～のが上手 · 上手(じょうず) vs できる

Rule of thumb - 上手(じょうず) is how well; できる is whether at all.

妹(いもうと)はスペイン語(ご)ができます。
My little sister can speak Spanish.

妹(いもうと)はスペイン語(ご)を話(はな)すのが**上手(じょうず)**です。
My little sister speaks Spanish well.

The first says the ability exists; the second grades it. This is why できる and the potential form are the safe way to describe your own abilities - 少(すこ)し泳(およ)げます reports a fact, while 泳(およ)ぐのが上手(じょうず)です awards yourself a prize.

～のが上手 · 上手(じょうず) vs できる (cont.)

They also differ in what they accept. できる takes nouns and する-nouns (運転(うんてん)ができる) and, with ことが, verbs. 上手(じょうず) takes nouns and のが clauses, and refuses anything that is not a trainable skill: ❌背(せ)が高(たか)いのが上手(じょうず)です is nonsense.

～のが上手 · 上手(じょうず) vs うまい

Rule of thumb - same meaning, lower register. うまい is casual, slightly male-coded, and ambiguous with "tasty".

あいつ、歌(うた)うまいな。
Wow, he's a good singer.

先生(せんせい)はテニスが**お上手(じょうず)**ですね。
You're good at tennis, aren't you, sensei.

うまい is an い-adjective (上手(うま)い in kanji, though usually kana), so it takes うまくない, うまかった - a different paradigm from 上手(じょうず) entirely. It belongs to friends, sport and the internet, never to a business email.

～のが上手 · 上手(じょうず) vs うまい (cont.)

Its other meaning is "delicious", which is where the ambiguity bites: この店(みせ)、うまい in a restaurant is about the food, not the chef's technique. And the negative 下手(へた) has no equivalent slot here - the casual opposite of うまい is へた or へたくそ, which is blunter still.

～のが上手 · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私(わたし)は日本語(にほんご)が上手(じょうず)です。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は日本語(にほんご)が得意(とくい)です。／少(すこ)し話(はな)せます。
    Grammatical but boastful. 上手(じょうず) is a verdict on someone else.

  • ❌ ギターを上手(じょうず)です。 → ✅ ギターが上手(じょうず)です。
    上手(じょうず) is an adjective and has no direct object.

  • ❌ 泳(およ)ぐが上手(じょうず)です。 → ✅ 泳(およ)ぐのが上手(じょうず)です。
    A verb needs the nominaliser の before が.

  • ❌ 上手(じょうず)に人(ひと) → ✅ 上手(じょうず)な人(ひと)
    に modifies verbs, な modifies nouns.

～のが上手 · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 彼(かれ)は上手(じょうず)します。 → ✅ 彼(かれ)は上手(じょうず)です。／上手(じょうず)にします。
    上手(じょうず) is not a verb; it needs the copula, or に if it is describing how something is done.

～のが上手 · Practice 5

(b) reports a plain fact: Yamada has English at her disposal. It says nothing about how good she is, and it is the neutral thing to write in an email about who can handle a foreign client.

～のが下手

Meaning · to be bad at something - a lack of skill, plainly stated

下手(へた) is a な-adjective, the mirror image of 上手(じょうず). What you are bad at is marked with が, and an action has to be nominalised with の first. Because it is a blunt verdict, it is comfortable about yourself - where it reads as modesty - and uncomfortable about anyone else.

～のが下手 · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary form) Dictionary + のが + 下手(へた) 踊(おど)る → 踊(おど)るのが下手(へた)
Noun Noun + が + 下手(へた) テニス → テニスが下手(へた)
Person + thing Person + は + Thing + が + 下手(へた) 私(わたし)は絵(え)が下手(へた)です
Adverbially (modifying a verb) 下手(へた)に + Verb 下手(へた)に動(うご)く

Plain 下手(へた)だ 踊(おど)るのが下手(へた)だ

My handwriting is terrible, so would it be all right if I sent it by email?

～のが下手 · Nuance & register

Pointed at yourself, 下手(へた) is safe and even likeable. Japanese conversation runs on modesty, and 料理(りょうり)が下手(へた)なんです is a normal, friendly thing to say about your own cooking. There is no need to soften it.

Pointed at anyone else it is a judgement, and a harsh one. Telling a colleague 英語(えいご)が下手(へた)ですね is close to an insult in English terms. The two standard repairs are あまり上手(じょうず)じゃない (a hedged negative) and 苦手(にがて) (a weakness rather than a failing) - and about a superior you simply do not go there at all.

下手(へた)くそ exists and is much cruder; it is what one friend shouts at another over a video game, not something to try on strangers. Note also that unlike 上手(じょうず), 下手(へた) has no polite お-form in ordinary use - there is nothing to be honorific about.

～のが下手 · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - 下手(へた)に before a verb has drifted away from "badly". It means carelessly, without thinking, rashly: 下手(へた)に動(うご)くと危(あぶ)ないです「Move carelessly and you'll get hurt」. The literal "does it badly" sense is usually carried by 下手(へた)な絵(え) or あまり上手(じょうず)じゃない instead.

～のが下手 · 下手(へた) vs 苦手(にがて)

Rule of thumb - 下手(へた) is about skill; 苦手(にがて) is about how you feel about it. 苦手(にがて) covers things you have no skill in at all - food, insects, particular people.

私(わたし)は歌(うた)を**歌(うた)うのが下手(へた)**です。
I'm a bad singer.

私(わたし)はカラオケが**苦手(にがて)**です。
I'm not keen on karaoke.

The first is a claim about your voice. The second says karaoke makes you uncomfortable - perhaps because you cannot sing, perhaps because you hate being looked at. That extra emotional layer is why 苦手(にがて) reaches places 下手(へた) cannot: なっとうが苦手(にがて)です and あの人(ひと)は少(すこ)し苦手(にがて)です are both natural, while ❌なっとうが下手(へた)です is nonsense - you do not perform natto.

～のが下手 · 下手(へた) vs 苦手(にがて) (cont.)

苦手(にがて) is also the kinder word about other people, because "he finds it hard" is gentler than "he is bad at it": 山田(やまだ)さんは数字(すうじ)が苦手(にがて)みたいです.

～のが下手 · 下手(へた) vs あまり上手(じょうず)じゃない

Rule of thumb - about yourself, 下手(へた). About other people, the hedged negative あまり上手(じょうず)じゃない.

私(わたし)は**下手(へた)**ですが、やってみます。
I'm bad at this, but I'll give it a go.

彼(かれ)は料理(りょうり)があまり上手(じょうず)じゃないです。
He isn't a very good cook.

The hedge does two things at once. あまり plus a negative is the standard Japanese way of saying "not very", and phrasing the criticism as a shortfall of skill rather than presence of badness takes the sting out. Add みたいです or ようです and it softens again, because you are now reporting an impression rather than passing sentence.

～のが下手 · 下手(へた) vs あまり上手(じょうず)じゃない (cont.)

The asymmetry is the same one that governs 上手(じょうず), run in reverse: you praise other people and criticise yourself, never the other way round.

～のが下手 · Noun が下手(へた) vs Verb のが下手(へた)

Rule of thumb - if a single noun names the skill, use it bare. Reach for のが when the skill only exists as an action.

姉(あね)はピアノが**下手(へた)**です。
My older sister is bad at the piano.

姉(あね)はピアノを**人(ひと)の前(まえ)で弾(ひ)くのが下手(へた)**です。
My older sister is bad at playing the piano in front of an audience.

Nouns that already name an activity - ピアノ, テニス, 料理(りょうり), 運転(うんてん) - go straight in with が. Adding を弾(ひ)くのが is not wrong, it just spells the activity out, which is what you want as soon as the description gets more specific than the noun can carry.

～のが下手 · Noun が下手(へた) vs Verb のが下手(へた) (cont.)

And some skills have no noun at all. There is no single word for "remembering people's names" or "saying no", so the verb clause is the only route: 人(ひと)の名前(なまえ)を覚(おぼ)えるのが下手(へた)です. What you cannot do is drop the の and hand the raw verb to the adjective.

～のが下手 · Common mistakes

  • ❌ なっとうが**下手(へた)です。 → ✅ なっとうが苦手(にがて)**です。
    Food is not a skill. 苦手(にがて) is the word for things you would rather avoid.

  • ❌ 田中(たなか)さんは英語(えいご)が下手(へた)ですね。 → ✅ 田中(たなか)さんは英語(えいご)があまり上手(じょうず)じゃないですね。
    Direct 下手(へた) about another person is rude. Hedge it, or use 苦手(にがて).

  • ❌ 踊(おど)るが下手(へた)です。 → ✅ 踊(おど)るのが下手(へた)です。
    The verb has to be nominalised by の before it can take が.

  • ❌ 下手(へた)くないです。 → ✅ 下手(へた)じゃないです。
    下手(へた) is a な-adjective. The final た is part of the reading, not an adjective ending.

  • ❌ 下手(へた)の絵(え) → ✅ 下手(へた)な絵(え)
    な-adjectives take な before a noun.

～のが下手 · Practice 1

1. 私(わたし)は人(ひと)に道(みち)を説明(せつめい)する____下手(へた)です。

道(みち)を説明(せつめい)するのが下手(へた)です。

説明(せつめい)する is a verb, so の turns it into something が can mark. You could also say 説明(せつめい)が下手(へた)です, using the noun on its own.

～のが下手 · Practice 2

2. 課長(かちょう)に：「部長(ぶちょう)はゴルフが（下手(へた)です・あまり上手(じょうず)じゃないです）ね。」

部長(ぶちょう)はゴルフがあまり上手(じょうず)じゃないですね。

Even this is risky about a superior, but of the two it is the survivable one. A flat 下手(へた) about someone above you is a straight insult; the hedged negative reads as an observation rather than a verdict.

～のが下手 · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'm bad at getting up early."

私(わたし)は朝(あさ)早(はや)く**起(お)きるのが下手(へた)**です。

About yourself, 下手(へた) is the modest, natural choice. Many speakers would also say 苦手(にがて)です here, which adds "and I dislike it" to the meaning.

～のが下手 · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 私(わたし)は歌(うた)が下手(へた)くなかったです。

✅ 私(わたし)は歌(うた)が下手(へた)じゃなかったです。

下手(へた) only looks like an い-adjective. It is a な-adjective, so the negative past is じゃなかった, exactly as with 静(しず)かじゃなかった.

～のが下手 · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 私(わたし)は漢字(かんじ)を書(か)くのが**下手(へた)です。 (b) 私(わたし)は漢字(かんじ)が苦手(にがて)**です。

(a) is a statement about the output: your kanji come out badly formed. It says nothing about how you feel while writing them.

(b) is about your relationship with kanji - they are your weak point, you avoid them, tests involving them make you nervous. It may or may not imply that your handwriting is poor, and it is the sentence a learner is usually reaching for when they want to say "kanji are my nemesis".
