だ / です

Meaning · the copula - "X is Y"

だ and です are the word that glues a noun to what it is. They are the same word at two politeness levels: です is polite, だ is plain. Japanese has no verb "to be" in the English sense - this is what stands in for it.

だ / です · Formation

だ / です come at the very end of the sentence, after the noun or な-adjective they attach to. Nothing changes inside the noun.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + だ / です 先生せんせい → 先生せんせいだ / 先生せんせいです
な-adjective stem (no な) + だ / です 静しずかな → 静しずかだ / 静しずかです
い-adjective plain adjective + です (never だ) 高たかい → 高たかいです
の-phrase (possession) Noun + の + だ / です 兄あにの → 兄あにのです
Question (polite) Noun + ですか 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいですか
Question (plain) Noun + ? - the だ drops 学生がくせい → 学生がくせい?
Verb does not take です - use ます 行いく → 行いきます

だ / です · Formation (cont.)

Note - です after an い-adjective is doing a different job. It is not linking anything; the adjective already is the predicate, and です is bolted on purely to make the sentence polite. That is why 高たかいです is fine but ❌高たかいだ is not: だ has no politeness to add.

だ / です · Examples

山田やまださんは先生せんせいです。
Yamada-san is a teacher.

今日きょうは休やすみだ。
Today's a day off.

わたしの部屋へやは静しずかです。
My room is quiet.

その自転車じてんしゃは兄あにのです。
That bicycle is my older brother's.

だ / です · Examples (cont.)

明日あしたは雨あめですか。
Is it going to rain tomorrow?

この辞書じしょは新あたらしいです。
This dictionary is new.

待まち合あわせは六時ろくじに駅えきの前まえです。
We're meeting at six in front of the station.

「ご出身しゅっしんはどちらですか。」「大阪おおさかです。」
"Where are you from?" "Osaka."

だ / です · Nuance & register

です is the default polite ending and it is safe everywhere: with strangers, at work, in class, in a shop. If you are unsure which form to use, use です. It is neither stiff nor warm - it is simply neutral-polite, and nobody will read anything into it.

だ is the plain form, used with friends, family, and in your own head. It is also the form used in writing that has no listener - notes, diaries, newspapers, essays - where politeness would be meaningless. What makes だ tricky is that in actual speech it is very often dropped:

  • 「これ、だれのかさ?」「わたしの。」 - the だ is simply not said.
  • 「あの人ひと、学生がくせい?」 - a plain question does not keep だ.

だ / です · Nuance & register (cont.)

Spoken だ that is kept sounds assertive and a little blunt, and it is more common in men's casual speech. Women's casual speech drops it more often, or softens it with よ / ね (「そうよ」「そうだね」). A learner who ends every casual sentence with a hard だ sounds like a cartoon character; leaving it off is safer.

Two further forms you will meet but do not need to produce yet. である is the copula of formal written argument - reports, papers, legal text; it never appears in ordinary conversation. でございます is the humble-polite copula of shop staff and announcements: 「こちらが新あたらしいメニューでございます」. Recognising them is enough for now.

だ / です · だ vs です

Rule of thumb - same word, two registers. です for anyone you are being polite to; だ for people you are close to, and for writing with no reader in mind.

The choice says nothing about meaning and everything about the relationship.

こちらが私わたしの机つくえです。
This is my desk.

そこ、ぼくの席せきだよ。
Hey, that's my seat.

The second sentence is not ruder in content - it is simply aimed at a friend. Said to a stranger, it would land as abrupt.

だ / です · だ vs です (cont.)

Politeness in Japanese is decided at the end of the sentence, so this one word sets the tone for everything that came before it. Do not mix levels inside a single sentence without a reason.

だ / です · い-adjective + です vs な-adjective + だ / です

Rule of thumb - い-adjectives are already complete predicates: add です for politeness, and never だ. な-adjectives and nouns need the copula to stand up at all.

この荷物にもつは重おもいです。
This luggage is heavy.

この部屋へやは静しずかです。
This room is quiet.

Both end in です, but for different reasons - and the difference surfaces the moment you go plain. 重おもい stands alone as a whole sentence; 静しずか cannot, and needs だ:

だ / です · い-adjective + です vs な-adjective + だ / です (cont.)

この荷物にもつ、重おもい。
This luggage is heavy.

この部屋へや、静しずかだ。
This room is quiet.

So the test is: strip the politeness away. If what is left needs propping up, it takes だ.

だ / です · です vs ます

Rule of thumb - です makes a noun or adjective sentence polite; ます makes a verb sentence polite. You never need both.

English hides "is" inside every tense, so learners reach for です when a verb is already doing the work. Japanese verbs carry their own politeness.

田中たなかさんは医者いしゃです。
Tanaka-san is a doctor.

田中たなかさんは毎日まいにち走はしります。
Tanaka-san runs every day.

だ / です · です vs ます (cont.)

❌走はしるです and ❌走はしりますです are both broken. The one place a verb does meet です is through a nominaliser - 走はしるのが好すきです - and even there です is attached to 好すき, a な-adjective, not to the verb.

だ / です · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この本ほんは高たかいだ。 → ✅ この本ほんは高たかい。
    An い-adjective is already a predicate. It takes です for politeness, but never だ.

  • ❌ 静しずかだです。 → ✅ 静しずかです。
    だ and です are one word at two levels. You choose one; you never stack them.

  • ❌ 毎朝まいあさコーヒーを飲のむです。 → ✅ 毎朝まいあさコーヒーを飲のみます。
    Verbs are made polite with ます, not with です.

  • ❌ あの人ひとは学生がくせいだですか。 → ✅ あの人ひとは学生がくせいですか。
    In a polite question the whole copula is です, and か follows it directly.

だ / です · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ これは静しずかなです。 → ✅ これは静しずかです。
    A な-adjective keeps its な only in front of a noun (静しずかな部屋へや). Before the copula the な drops.

だ / です · Practice 1

1. あの建物たてものは図書館としょかん____。（polite）

あの建物たてものは図書館としょかんです。

A noun predicate needs the copula, and the polite copula is です. Nothing changes inside the noun itself.

だ / です · Practice 2

2. Choose: この問題もんだいは難むずかしい（だ・です・nothing）。

難むずかしいです (polite), or 難むずかしい on its own (plain).

難むずかしい is an い-adjective, so it is already a complete sentence by itself. Add です if you want to be polite, but ❌難むずかしいだ is never possible.

だ / です · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, politely: "That umbrella is my mother's."

そのかさは母ははのです。

The の carries the possession, and the noun it stands for ("umbrella") is simply left out, so です attaches straight onto の.

だ / です · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 山田やまださんは明日あした来くるですか。

✅ 山田やまださんは明日あした来きますか。

The predicate is a verb, and verbs take ます for politeness - です has no work to do here. The question particle か then follows ます.

だ / です · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in feel: (a) 明日あしたは休やすみです。 (b) 明日あしたは休やすみだ。

The information is identical: tomorrow is a day off. Only the relationship differs.

(a) is aimed at someone you are being polite to - a colleague, a customer, a teacher. (b) is aimed at a friend, or at nobody at all: it is also exactly how the sentence would be written in a diary or a memo. Said out loud to a stranger, (b) sounds abrupt.

じゃない・ではない

Meaning · the negative copula - "X is not Y"

じゃない and ではない are the negative of だ / です. They deny that one thing is the other. ではない is the full form; じゃない is its everyday contraction, and it is what you will hear almost all the time.

じゃない・ではない · Formation

では contracts to じゃ in speech, which is the whole story behind the two shapes. Everything below comes in pairs for that reason.

じゃない・ではない · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (plain) Noun + じゃない / ではない 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいじゃない
Noun (polite) Noun + じゃないです / じゃありません 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいじゃありません
Noun (formal) Noun + ではありません 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいではありません
な-adjective stem (no な) + じゃない 静しずかな → 静しずかじゃない
い-adjective ～い → ～くない (never じゃない) 高たかい → 高たかくない
Verb ～ない form (never じゃない) 行いく → 行いかない
Past じゃなかった / じゃありませんでした 学生がくせいじゃなかった
Joining on じゃなくて (て-form) Aじゃなくて、B

じゃない・ではない · Formation (cont.)

Note - the politeness ladder runs じゃない → じゃないです → じゃありません → ではありません → ではございません, from most casual to most formal. The middle three are all "polite"; they differ only in how starched they sound.

じゃない・ではない · Examples

これは私わたしのかさじゃない。
This isn't my umbrella.

今日きょうは木曜日もくようびじゃありません。
Today isn't Thursday.

その話はなしは本当ほんとうじゃないですよ。
That story isn't true.

試験しけんは来週らいしゅうじゃなくて、再来週さらいしゅうです。
The exam isn't next week - it's the week after.

じゃない・ではない · Examples (cont.)

冗談じょうだんじゃないよ。本当ほんとうの話はなしだ。
I'm not joking. It really happened.

大学だいがくのとき、わたしは真面目まじめな学生がくせいじゃなかった。
I wasn't a serious student back in university.

この辺へんは夜よるあまり静しずかじゃないので、窓まどを閉しめて寝ねています。
It isn't very quiet around here at night, so I sleep with the window shut.

申もうし訳わけありませんが、これは私わたしの担当たんとうではありませんので、係かかりの者ものを呼よびます。
I'm very sorry, but this isn't my area - I'll fetch the person in charge.

じゃない・ではない · Nuance & register

The split between じゃ and では is one of pronunciation, not of meaning. ではない is what you write; じゃない is what you say. Reading ではありません aloud in a friendly conversation sounds like reading from a form.

Within the polite forms there is a further, smaller split. じゃありません is the textbook polite negative and is perfectly normal in speech. じゃないです is a shade more conversational - it is built by taking the plain じゃない and adding です, the same trick い-adjectives use - and it is extremely common in modern spoken Japanese. ではありません belongs to announcements, written notices and stiff business speech, where nobody is trying to sound warm.

じゃない・ではない · Nuance & register (cont.)

One more thing about じゃない catches learners badly. With falling intonation it negates: 「山田やまださんじゃない。」 = "That's not Yamada." With rising intonation it does the opposite - it becomes a tag question asserting that something is so: 「山田やまださんじゃない?」 = "Isn't that Yamada?" This second use can attach to things the negative copula normally cannot, including い-adjectives and verbs (「高たかいじゃない!」 = "That's expensive!"). If you meet じゃない attached to an い-adjective, it is this tag-question use, not negation.

In casual speech じゃない shortens further to じゃん: 「いいじゃん」 = "that's fine, isn't it". It is friendly, young-sounding, and out of place at work.

じゃない・ではない · じゃない vs ～くない

Rule of thumb - じゃない negates nouns and な-adjectives. い-adjectives negate themselves by changing ～い to ～くない.

The two look interchangeable in English ("is not X") but they are not.

この問題もんだいは簡単かんたんじゃない。
This problem isn't easy.

この問題もんだいは難むずかしくない。
This problem isn't hard.

じゃない・ではない · じゃない vs ～くない (cont.)

簡単かんたん is a な-adjective, so it needs the copula and takes じゃない. 難むずかしい is an い-adjective, so it conjugates on its own. The same test as with だ: if the word needs the copula to stand up, it negates with じゃない.

いい is the irregular one worth learning now: it negates as よくない, never ❌いくない.

じゃない・ではない · じゃありません vs じゃないです vs ではありません

Rule of thumb - all three are polite. Pick by how formal the room is, not by what you mean.

それは私わたしのかばんじゃないです。
That's not my bag.

それは私わたしのかばんじゃありません。
That's not my bag.

当店とうてんは本日ほんじつ営業えいぎょうではありません。
Our shop is not open for business today.

じゃない・ではない · じゃありません vs じゃないです vs ではありません (cont.)

The first is what you say to a coworker; the second is what a textbook or a careful speaker says; the third is what a sign says. A learner who only ever produces じゃありません will never sound wrong, which makes it the safe default.

じゃない・ではない · じゃない vs 違う

Rule of thumb - じゃない denies an equation ("A is not B"); 違ちがう is a verb meaning "to differ / to be mistaken", and it is what you say to correct someone.

これは私わたしの傘かさじゃないです。
This isn't my umbrella.

「田中たなかさんですか。」「いいえ、違ちがいます。」
"Are you Tanaka-san?" "No, I'm not."

じゃない・ではない · じゃない vs 違う (cont.)

Both are heard as "no", but they work differently. 違ちがいます is a complete answer on its own and needs no noun in front of it, which is exactly why it is the standard way to say "that's not right". Notice that answering a question with a bare 「田中たなかじゃありません」 sounds blunter than 「違ちがいます」 - the verb is the softer, more idiomatic correction.

じゃない・ではない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この本ほんは高たかいじゃない。（meaning "this book isn't expensive"） → ✅ この本ほんは高たかくない。
    い-adjectives negate with ～くない. The string 高たかいじゃない does exist, but only as the rising tag question "it's expensive, isn't it!" - the opposite of what you meant.

  • ❌ この部屋へやは静しずかだじゃない。 → ✅ この部屋へやは静しずかじゃない。
    じゃない already contains the copula. Never leave だ in front of it.

  • ❌ 昨日きのうは雨あめじゃないでした。 → ✅ 昨日きのうは雨あめじゃありませんでした。
    The polite negative past is built on ありません, not on です. In plain form: 雨あめじゃなかった。

じゃない・ではない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ わたしはコーヒーを飲のむじゃない。 → ✅ わたしはコーヒーを飲のまない。
    Verbs have their own negative. じゃない never attaches to a verb to negate it.

じゃない・ではない · Practice 1

1. あの人ひとは先生せんせい____。（plain, negative）

あの人ひとは先生せんせいじゃない。

先生せんせい is a noun, so it needs the copula, and the plain negative copula is じゃない. The fuller ではない would be the written equivalent.

じゃない・ではない · Practice 2

2. Choose: この店みせは（安やすくない・安やすいじゃない）。 - "This shop isn't cheap."

この店みせは**安やすくない**。

安やすい is an い-adjective, so it negates by changing い to くない. The other option would be heard as a tag question meaning "it's cheap, isn't it!", which is the opposite claim.

じゃない・ではない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, politely: "It isn't Monday today - it's Tuesday."

今日きょうは月曜日げつようびじゃなくて、火曜日かようびです。

The て-form じゃなくて links the correction to what follows, so you get one smooth sentence instead of two. 「月曜日げつようびじゃありません。火曜日かようびです。」 also works and is a little more emphatic.

じゃない・ではない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 去年きょねんのアパートは便利べんりだじゃありませんでした。

✅ 去年きょねんのアパートは便利べんりじゃありませんでした。

便利べんり is a な-adjective, and the negative form already carries the copula, so the extra だ has to go. Only the stem is left in front of じゃ.

じゃない・ではない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 山田やまださんじゃない。 (b) 山田やまださんじゃない?

(a), said with falling intonation, is straightforward negation: "that isn't Yamada."

(b), with a rising tail, flips into a tag question and actually claims the opposite: "that's Yamada, isn't it?" The speaker is fairly sure and is inviting the listener to confirm. The written form is identical, so intonation carries the whole meaning here.

だった・でした

Meaning · the past copula - "X was Y"

だった and でした put the copula into the past. だった is plain, でした is polite. They are how you say what something was, where it was, and how it felt - for nouns and な-adjectives.

だった・でした · Formation

The past copula replaces だ / です entirely; nothing else in the sentence moves.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + だった / でした 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいだった / 学生がくせいでした
な-adjective stem (no な) + だった / でした 元気げんきな → 元気げんきだった / 元気げんきでした
い-adjective ～い → ～かった (+ です) 楽たのしい → 楽たのしかった / 楽たのしかったです
Negative past じゃなかった / じゃありませんでした 学生がくせいじゃなかった
Question Noun + でしたか / plain + ? 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいでしたか
Verb ～た / ～ました (never でした) 行いく → 行いった / 行いきました
Before a noun だった + Noun (でした cannot) 学生がくせいだった人ひと

だった・でした · Formation (cont.)

Note - the last row is worth pausing on. Inside a sentence, modifying a noun, only the plain だった is available: 学生がくせいだった人ひと = "a person who was a student". The politeness of the whole sentence is still decided at the very end.

だった・でした · Examples

昨日きのうのテストは簡単かんたんだった。
Yesterday's test was easy.

去年きょねんまで学生がくせいでした。
I was a student until last year.

あの映画えいが、どうだった?
How was that movie?

子こどものとき、野球やきゅうが好すきでした。
I liked baseball when I was a child.

だった・でした · Examples (cont.)

会議かいぎは水曜日すいようびじゃなかった。火曜日かようびだったよ。
The meeting wasn't Wednesday. It was Tuesday.

昨日きのうのパーティーはにぎやかでしたか。
Was yesterday's party lively?

十年前じゅうねんまえ、ここは大おおきい工場こうじょうでしたが、今いまは公園こうえんになっています。
Ten years ago this was a big factory, but now it's a park.

去年きょねんの旅行りょこうは雨あめばかりで大変たいへんでしたが、とても楽たのしかったです。
Last year's trip was nothing but rain and pretty rough, but it was a lot of fun.

だった・でした · Nuance & register

The register split is the same one だ and です already set up: でした for people you are being polite to, だった for friends, family and writing with no reader. Nothing about the past tense changes that calculation.

Unlike plain だ, plain だった is not dropped in casual speech. 「テスト、 簡単かんたん?」 works as a present-tense question with no copula at all, but to put it in the past you must say 「簡単かんたんだった?」 - there is no other way to carry the tense. This is the first place where beginners who have learned to drop だ get stuck.

だった・でした · Nuance & register (cont.)

でした also has a quiet second life as part of the polite negative past for adjectives and verbs: ～ませんでした, ～くありませんでした. In those it is not the copula any more, just the polite past ending being reused. That is exactly why ❌楽たのしいでした feels tempting and is wrong: い-adjectives make their own past with ～かった, and です may only be added afterwards.

One nuance about だった in speech: with a falling, drawn-out delivery (「そうだった～」) it often marks a sudden recollection - "oh, right, that's how it was." Learners hear this a lot and mistake it for ordinary past tense.

だった・でした · だった vs でした

Rule of thumb - identical meaning, different politeness. でした in polite speech, だった with friends and in plain-style writing.

先週せんしゅうの試合しあいは残念ざんねんでした。
Last week's match was a disappointment.

先週せんしゅうの試合しあい、残念ざんねんだったな。
Shame about last week's match.

There is one asymmetry worth knowing. だった can sit inside a sentence, modifying a noun or leading into と思おもう; でした cannot, because polite forms live only at the end.

だった・でした · だった vs でした (cont.)

昔むかし、ここは海うみだったと聞ききました。
I heard this used to be the sea.

The sentence is polite - but the politeness is carried by 聞ききました at the end, and the embedded copula stays plain.

だった・でした · い-adjective past vs でした

Rule of thumb - い-adjectives have their own past, ～かった. They never take でした.

This is the single most common past-tense error at N5, because English puts "was" in front of both kinds of word.

昨日きのうは寒さむかったです。
It was cold yesterday.

昨日きのうは暇ひまでした。
I was free yesterday.

だった・でした · い-adjective past vs でした (cont.)

寒さむい is an い-adjective, so the past is built inside the word and です is added only for politeness. 暇ひま is a な-adjective, so it has no past of its own and hands the job to the copula. ❌寒さむいでした and ❌寒さむかったでした are both wrong; ❌暇ひまかった is wrong for the same reason in reverse.

いい is irregular here too: the past is よかった, never ❌いかった.

だった・でした · でした vs ました

Rule of thumb - でした is the past of a noun / な-adjective sentence; ました is the past of a verb sentence.

去年きょねんの夏なつは北海道ほっかいどうにいました。
Last summer I was in Hokkaido.

去年きょねんの夏なつは北海道ほっかいどうにいた。
Last summer I was in Hokkaido.

だった・でした · でした vs ました (cont.)

Note that English "was" here is not the copula at all - it is existence, which Japanese handles with the verbs いる and ある, and therefore with ました / た. Saying ❌北海道ほっかいどうにでした is not possible.

The negative past follows the same division: ～ませんでした for verbs, じゃありませんでした for nouns.

だった・でした · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 映画えいがは面白おもしろいでした。 → ✅ 映画えいがは面白おもしろかったです。
    い-adjectives carry their own past in ～かった. でした belongs to nouns and な-adjectives.

  • ❌ 昨日きのうは忙いそがしかったでした。 → ✅ 昨日きのうは忙いそがしかったです。
    Once the adjective is already past, only です is added - the past is not marked twice.

  • ❌ 元気げんきだでした。 → ✅ 元気げんきでした。
    でした already is the copula. There is no だ in front of it.

  • ❌ 友ともだちと映画えいがを見みるでした。 → ✅ 友ともだちと映画えいがを見みました。
    A verb sentence goes past with ました, never with でした.

だった・でした · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 学生がくせいだったです。 → ✅ 学生がくせいでした。
    楽たのしかったです works because です is attaching to an adjective. After the copula だった it is not standard - use でした instead.

だった・でした · Practice 1

1. 先週せんしゅうの日曜日にちようびは雨あめ____。（polite）

先週せんしゅうの日曜日にちようびは雨あめでした。

雨あめ is a noun, so the past tense has to be carried by the copula, and the polite past copula is でした.

だった・でした · Practice 2

2. Choose: 旅行りょこうは（楽たのしいでした・楽たのしかったです）。

旅行りょこうは**楽たのしかったです**。

楽たのしい is an い-adjective, so it forms its own past by changing い to かった; です is then added purely for politeness. The first option marks the past in the wrong place.

だった・でした · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, plain form: "When I was little, my father was a teacher."

小ちいさいとき、父ちちは先生せんせいだった。

Both halves are past in English, but only the main clause needs marking: the とき clause takes its tense from the adjective, and the noun predicate 先生せんせい takes the plain past copula.

だった・でした · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昨日きのうのコンサートはとてもよかったでした。

✅ 昨日きのうのコンサートはとてもよかったです。

よかった is already the past of いい, so adding でした marks the past twice. Only です may follow, and it adds politeness alone.

だった・でした · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 田中たなかさんは先生せんせいでした。 (b) 田中たなかさんは先生せんせいだったんです。

Both say Tanaka used to be a teacher, politely. (a) is a flat report of a fact - the kind of thing you would write on a form or state in an introduction.

(b) wraps the same fact in the explanatory んです, so it comes across as background offered for a reason: "the thing is, he used to be a teacher" - explaining why he is so good with children, for instance. Notice the copula goes plain (だった) before んです, with the politeness moved to the very end.

は

Meaning · marks the topic - "as for X, …"

は does not mean anything by itself. It lifts a noun out of the sentence and hangs it up as the thing being talked about; everything after it is the comment. It is written with the kana は but pronounced わ.

は · Formation

は attaches to a noun or a noun phrase with no change to the noun. What matters is what it does to the particle that was already there.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + は 私わたし → 私わたしは
A subject marked が が is replaced 犬いぬが好すき → 犬いぬは好すき
An object marked を を is replaced ごはんを食たべる → ごはんは食たべる
に・で・と・から・まで は is added after them 東京とうきょうに → 東京とうきょうには
Time / adverb phrase phrase + は 今日きょう → 今日きょうは
Negative sentences は is the default marker 時間じかんはありません
Pronunciation written は, said わ -

は · Formation (cont.)

Note - that third and fourth row are the whole mechanical rule, and it is worth memorising as a pair. が and を disappear under は; every other particle survives and は stacks on top. There is no ❌がは or ❌をは, but には, では, とは, からは are all normal.

は · Examples

今日きょうは寒さむいですね。
It's cold today, isn't it.

この本ほんは面白おもしろいです。
This book is interesting.

野菜やさいは好すきですが、肉にくはあまり好すきじゃありません。
I like vegetables, but I'm not so keen on meat.

土曜日どようびには会議かいぎがありません。
There are no meetings on Saturdays.

は · Examples (cont.)

きのうはありがとうございました。
Thank you for yesterday.

あの店みせのラーメンは本当ほんとうにおいしいですよ。
The ramen at that place is genuinely good.

日本にほんの夏なつは暑あつくて、東京とうきょうでは夜よるも気温きおんが下さがりません。
Summer in Japan is hot, and in Tokyo the temperature doesn't even drop at night.

私わたしは来週らいしゅう大阪おおさかへ行いきますが、妹いもうとは家いえに残のこります。
I'm going to Osaka next week, but my younger sister is staying home.

は · Nuance & register

は is register-neutral. It is the same in plain speech, polite speech, and writing, and choosing it tells the listener nothing about how formal you are being.

Two habits will make you sound much more natural. The first is leaving it out. Once a topic is established, Japanese simply keeps talking about it - you do not restate it. A conversation that begins 「田中たなかさんは元気げんきですか」 continues 「はい、元気げんきです」, with no topic at all. Beginners who start every sentence with 私わたしは sound like a textbook, and after a while faintly self-absorbed; native speakers say it once, if at all, and then drop it.

は · Nuance & register (cont.)

The second is that in casual speech は is often just not pronounced - 「これ、おいしい」 - or replaced by って, which is the conversational topic marker: 「田中たなかさんって、面白おもしろいね」 = "Tanaka's funny, isn't he." って is friendly and slightly gossipy, and it is out of place in a report.

Finally, は always carries a faint edge of contrast, even when you do not intend one. Saying 「今日きょうは忙いそがしいです」 quietly implies "today in particular - other days, not so much." That side effect is very useful for softening refusals (「今日きょうはちょっと…」), and it is also why piling は onto something can sound pointed: 「あなたはいいですね」 can land as "well, it's all right for you."

は · は vs が

Rule of thumb - the new information goes after は, but before が.

This is the single hardest thing at N5, and the confusion starts from a bad label: は is not a subject marker. が marks the subject - the thing that does the verb. は marks the topic - the thing under discussion. They often land in the same slot in the sentence, which makes them look like alternatives, but they are answering different needs.

Watch where the news sits:

猫ねこは外そとにいます。
The cat is outside.

We both know which cat; the news is where it is, and it comes after は. This answers "where's the cat?"

は · は vs が (cont.)

猫ねこが外そとにいます。
There's a cat outside.

Here the news is the cat itself - presented fresh, before が. This answers "what's out there?", or it is simply you noticing.

Two consequences follow directly. First, a question word is by definition the unknown, so it must sit on the が side and can never take は: 「どれが山田やまださんのかばんですか」. Second, a brand-new thing is introduced with が and then talked about with は - which is why stories start one way and continue the other.

は · は vs が (cont.)

は also reaches further than が. A topic scopes over everything to the end of the sentence, while が only marks the subject of its own clause. That is why は cannot appear inside a clause that is merely modifying a noun:

これは姉あねが作つくったケーキです。
This is a cake my older sister made.

姉あねが作つくった is decorating ケーキ; the outer topic is これ. Putting は on 姉あね would try to make her the topic of the whole sentence, and it breaks.

And both can live in one sentence, doing their separate jobs:

は · は vs が (cont.)

日本にほんは電車でんしゃが便利べんりです。
Japan has convenient trains. (As for Japan - the trains are convenient.)

If you take one thing from this section: ask yourself which word in your sentence is the news. If the news is the noun in front of the particle, use が. If the news is what comes after it, use は.

は · は vs も

Rule of thumb - は says "as for this one"; も says "this one as well". Both replace が and を, and only one can be there at a time.

They occupy the same slot, so choosing between them is choosing what you imply about everything else.

兄あには大学生だいがくせいです。
My older brother is a university student.

兄あにも大学生だいがくせいです。
My older brother is a university student too.

は · は vs も (cont.)

The も version only makes sense if somebody else has already been named as a student. The は version, by contrast, faintly suggests a comparison going the other way - the brother, as opposed to whoever else we were discussing.

In negative sentences the pair splits cleanly: は gives "not this one (whatever about the others)", も gives "not this one either".

コーヒーは飲のみません。
I don't drink coffee. (tea might be another matter)

コーヒーも飲のみません。
I don't drink coffee either.

は · は with other particles: には・では・とは

Rule of thumb - は swallows が and を, but it rides on the back of every other particle.

The particle that shows the role of the noun cannot be thrown away, or the sentence stops making sense. So it stays, and は is added behind it.

京都きょうとには一度いちど行いきました。
I've been to Kyoto once. (Kyoto, at least - other places, who knows)

この店みせではカードが使つかえません。
You can't use a card at this shop.

は · は with other particles: には・では・とは (cont.)

Dropping the inner particle changes or destroys the meaning: ❌京都きょうとは行いきました loses the "to", and ❌この店みせは… would make the shop itself the topic rather than the place of the action. The order is always particle first, は second - never ❌はに, ❌はで.

は · Common mistakes

  • ❌ だれはこの窓まどを開あけましたか。 → ✅ だれがこの窓まどを開あけましたか。
    A question word is the unknown, and an unknown cannot be a topic. Question words take が.

  • ❌ 私わたしがは行いきます。 → ✅ 私わたしは行いきます。
    は replaces が; it never stacks with it. The same goes for を.

  • ❌ 大阪おおさかは住すんでいます。 → ✅ 大阪おおさかに住すんでいます。（or 大阪おおさかには…）
    住すむ needs に to show "in". は may be added on top of に, but it cannot take its place.

  • ❌ これは母ははは作つくった料理りょうりです。 → ✅ これは母ははが作つくった料理りょうりです。
    Inside a clause that modifies a noun, the subject takes が. A topic cannot be trapped in there.

は · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ Pronouncing は as ha when it is the particle. It is always wa. (The same
    kana is ha inside ordinary words, as in はな.)

は · Practice 1

1. 「すみません、トイレ____どこですか。」

トイレはどこですか。

The toilet is the thing you are asking about - old, shared information - so it is the topic. The unknown is どこ, and in a は sentence the unknown comes after the particle.

は · Practice 2

2. Choose: 「あ、あそこに人ひと（は・が）立たっていますよ。」

人ひとが立たっています。

You are reporting something you have just noticed, and the person is brand-new information. New information comes before が. は would imply the listener already had that person in mind.

は · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I don't eat meat, but I do eat fish."

肉にくは食たべませんが、魚さかなは食たべます。

Both nouns are objects, so both would normally take を - but the sentence is built on a contrast, and that is は's speciality. を is replaced, not stacked, in both halves.

は · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 来年らいねん、日本にほんは留学りゅうがくしたいです。

✅ 来年らいねん、日本にほんに留学りゅうがくしたいです。（or 日本にほんには…）

留学りゅうがくする needs に to mark the destination. は can be layered on top of に for emphasis or contrast, but on its own it leaves the sentence with no indication of where you are going.

は · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 今日きょうは忙いそがしいです。 (b) 今日きょう、忙いそがしいです。

(a) sets today up as the topic, and は drags a comparison in with it: "today I'm busy" - with the unspoken implication "unlike other days", or "so not today, please." This is why it is the polite way to turn an invitation down.

(b) has no topic marker and simply states the situation. It is flatter and more factual, with none of the "as opposed to" flavour. In casual speech the particle is often dropped for exactly this reason.

も

Meaning · also; too; as well

も adds its noun to something already on the table. Whatever was just said about A is now said about B as well. In negative sentences the same particle gives you "not … either", and with a number it turns into an expression of surprise: "as many as".

も · Formation

も sits exactly where は would sit, and follows the same replacement rule.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + も 私わたし → 私わたしも
A subject marked が が is replaced 雨あめが降ふる → 雨あめも降ふる
An object marked を を is replaced ケーキを食たべる → ケーキも食たべる
A topic marked は は is replaced 私わたしは → 私わたしも
に・で・と・から・まで も is added after them 京都きょうとに → 京都きょうとにも
A も B も both A and B (or, with a negative, neither) 英語えいごも中国語ちゅうごくごも
Question word + も + negative = not any 何なにも／だれも／どこも
Number + counter + も as many as … 三杯さんばいも

も · Formation (cont.)

Note - the replacement rule matters more here than anywhere else, because English lets you tack "too" onto the end of a finished sentence. Japanese does not: も goes on the noun that is being added, and it pushes が / を / は out of the way.

も · Examples

わたしも行いきます。
I'll go too.

これもください。
I'll take this one as well, please.

姉あねは英語えいごも中国語ちゅうごくごも話はなします。
My older sister speaks both English and Chinese.

昨日きのうは何なにも買かいませんでした。
I didn't buy anything yesterday.

も · Examples (cont.)

「わたし、甘あまいものが苦手にがてなんだ。」「え、わたしも。」
"I'm not big on sweet things." "Oh - me neither."

きのうは水みずを二にリットルも飲のんだ。
I drank a whole two litres of water yesterday.

田中たなかさんも同おなじ電車でんしゃに乗のっていて、駅えきでびっくりしました。
Tanaka was on the same train as well, and I got a surprise at the station.

あの店みせは値段ねだんが安やすくて、店員てんいんさんも親切しんせつなので、よく行いきます。
That shop is cheap and the staff are friendly too, so I go there often.

も · Nuance & register

も is register-neutral: identical in plain and polite speech, in writing, and in anyone's mouth. Nothing about it is casual or formal.

What it is sensitive to is context. も only works if the listener can supply the "as well as what". 「わたしも」 as a one-word reply - "me too" - is perfectly natural precisely because the other half was just said out loud. Dropped into a conversation with nothing to attach to, も sounds like the beginning of a sentence you forgot to finish.

Beyond plain addition, two extensions are worth having now.

With a number, も stops meaning "also" and starts meaning "as many as" - it marks the amount as more than expected: 「三時間さんじかんも待まった」 = "I waited a full three hours." With a small number plus a negative it flips to "not even": 「一度いちども行いったことがない」 = "I've never been, not even once."

も · Nuance & register (cont.)

With a question word, も plus a negative sweeps the whole category away: だれも来こない = "nobody came", 何なにもない = "there's nothing", どこにも行いかない = "I'm not going anywhere". This construction is fixed - the negative is not optional, and this is where beginners most often build a sentence that means the opposite of what they wanted.

も also has a softening, generous feel when offering things: 「コーヒーもいかがですか」 sounds like an addition rather than a demand, and shopkeepers use it constantly.

も · も vs は

Rule of thumb - は sets its noun apart from the others; も puts it alongside them.

They fight for the same slot in the sentence, so the choice is always meaningful.

弟おとうとはピアノを習ならっています。
My little brother takes piano lessons.

弟おとうともピアノを習ならっています。
My little brother takes piano lessons too.

The second sentence only stands up if somebody else was already named as a piano student. The first quietly does the reverse: it hints that the brother is the exception among the people under discussion.

も · も vs は (cont.)

This distinction is sharpest in refusals and admissions. 「今日きょうは 無理むりです」 leaves other days open; 「今日きょうも無理むりです」 admits today is one more in a run of no's.

も · も vs と

Rule of thumb - と glues nouns into one phrase ("A and B"); も marks each noun separately as an addition ("A as well, B as well").

English "and" covers both, which is why this goes wrong so often.

パンとたまごを買かいました。
I bought bread and eggs.

パンもたまごも買かいました。
I bought bread and eggs - both of them.

も · も vs と (cont.)

The と version is a neutral shopping list. The も…も version stresses the completeness of it, and it is the only one that can turn into "neither" simply by negating the verb: 「パンもたまごも買かいませんでした」 = "I bought neither."

Note also that と joins nouns only. To link two whole sentences, と is not available at all.

も · だれも / 何も vs だれか / 何か

Rule of thumb - question word + も needs a negative and means "none"; question word + か is positive and means "some".

The two look like near-twins and mean opposite things.

部屋へやにだれもいません。
There's nobody in the room.

部屋へやにだれかいますか。
Is somebody in the room?

も · だれも / 何も vs だれか / 何か (cont.)

Mixing them up produces sentences that are not just unnatural but ungrammatical: ❌だれも来きました cannot mean "somebody came" - だれも demands a negative verb, so the sentence simply breaks. Use だれか来きました for "somebody came."

も · Common mistakes

  • ❌ わたしはも行いきます。 → ✅ わたしも行いきます。
    も replaces は, が and を. It never stacks with them.

  • ❌ ケーキをも食たべました。 → ✅ ケーキも食たべました。
    Same rule for the object marker: を disappears under も.

  • ❌ 友ともだちもと行いきました。 → ✅ 友ともだちとも行いきました。
    With the particles that survive (に, で, と, から), the order is always particle first, も second.

  • ❌ 今日きょうは何なにも食たべました。 → ✅ 今日きょうは何なにか食たべました。
    何なにも can only be followed by a negative. For "I ate something", use 何なにか.

も · Practice 1

1. 「山田やまださんは来きますか。」「はい。田中たなかさん____来きますよ。」

田中たなかさんも来きますよ。

Yamada has already been established as coming, so Tanaka is being added to that group. も replaces the が that would otherwise mark the subject.

も · Practice 2

2. Choose: 冷蔵庫れいぞうこに何なに（も・か）ありませんでした。

冷蔵庫れいぞうこに何なにもありませんでした。

The verb is negative, and question word + も + negative is the fixed way to say "nothing at all". 何なにか would need a positive verb: 何なにかありました.

も · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "My brother can't swim, and I can't either."

兄あには泳およげません。わたしも泳およげません。

The も does the work of "either" - Japanese uses the same particle for "too" and "either", and the negative verb supplies the difference. Note that も replaces the は that marked the first speaker's topic.

も · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 去年きょねん、北海道ほっかいどうもへ行いきました。

✅ 去年きょねん、北海道ほっかいどうへも行いきました。

へ shows the direction and cannot be dropped, so も goes behind it, not in front. The rule is that も follows every particle except が, を and は, which it replaces.

も · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) ケーキを三みっつ食たべました。 (b) ケーキを三みっつも食たべました。

(a) is a plain count: three cakes were eaten, no comment attached.

(b) adds も to the number, which turns it into an evaluation - "I ate three whole cakes." The speaker is flagging the amount as more than expected, usually with a touch of guilt or amazement. Nothing about the quantity itself has changed; only the attitude toward it has.

か - or

Meaning · joins alternatives - "A or B"

Placed between two nouns, か offers a choice: either one will do. This is the everyday Japanese "or", and it is the same syllable as the question particle without being the same word - position tells them apart.

か - or · Formation

か goes between the items, not after the last one. Any case particle the phrase needs (を, に, で…) is attached to the whole group, once, at the end.

Attaches to Form Example
Two nouns A か B コーヒー か お茶ちゃ
Three or more A か B か C 月げつ か 火か か 水すい
With a case particle (A か B) + を・に・で コーヒーかお茶ちゃを飲のむ
Both items marked A か B か（、）… バスか電車でんしゃか、どちらか
Numbers / dates approximation: "…or…" 三日みっかか四日よっか
Two clauses plain form + か、plain form 映画えいがを見みるか、本ほんを読よむ

か - or · Formation (cont.)

Note - the case particle rule is the mechanical trap. ❌コーヒーをかお茶ちゃを is wrong; the を is not distributed over the alternatives. Build the choice first, then mark it: コーヒーかお茶ちゃを飲のみます.

か - or · Examples

コーヒーかお茶ちゃを飲のみます。
I'll have coffee or tea.

ペンかえんぴつ、ありますか。
Do you have a pen or a pencil?

土曜日どようびか日曜日にちようびに会あいましょう。
Let's meet on Saturday or Sunday.

バスか電車でんしゃか、どちらで行いきますか。
Bus or train - which will you take?

か - or · Examples (cont.)

三日みっかか四日よっかで終おわると思おもいます。
I think it'll be finished in three or four days.

明日あした雨あめなら、映画えいがを見みるか、家いえで本ほんを読よみます。
If it rains tomorrow, I'll watch a film or read at home.

夏休なつやすみは北海道ほっかいどうか沖縄おきなわに行いきたいと思おもっています。
For the summer holiday I'm thinking of going to Hokkaido or Okinawa.

すみません、受付うけつけは一階いっかいか二階にかいか、どちらでしょうか。
Excuse me - is reception on the first floor or the second?

か - or · Nuance & register

か is register-neutral. It is the same word in a text message and in a contract, and nobody will hear it as either casual or stiff.

Marking both items - 「AかBか」 - changes the feel slightly. It slows the sentence down and presents the choice as a genuine, deliberate fork: 「行いくか、 行いかないか」 = "whether to go or not". A single か between two nouns is the lighter, more offhand version, and it is what you will say most of the time.

か - or · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two related shapes to recognise. In relaxed speech, とか does the same job with a looser, "…or something like that" flavour: 「お茶ちゃとかコーヒーとか」. And in written or formal Japanese, または replaces か in instructions and forms: 「ペンまたはえんぴつでご記入きにゅうください」.

Note one limit. か does not translate every English "or". English uses "or" to threaten ("hurry, or we'll be late") and to correct ("Tuesday - or rather, Wednesday"); Japanese uses other constructions for both, and reaching for か there will not work.

Finally, do not read the choice as exclusive by default. 「コーヒーかお茶ちゃ」 offers two options; it does not insist you may not have both. If exclusivity matters, say so - 「どちらか一ひとつ」.

か - or · か vs と

Rule of thumb - と means and (both), か means or (either). They sit in exactly the same place, so a single syllable flips the meaning.

パンと牛乳ぎゅうにゅうを買かいます。
I'll buy bread and milk.

パンか牛乳ぎゅうにゅうを買かいます。
I'll buy bread or milk.

The first commits you to two items; the second to one, not yet decided. Both take a single を at the end, because in both cases the two nouns form one phrase together.

か - or · か vs それとも

Rule of thumb - か joins nouns inside one sentence; それとも joins two complete questions.

それとも is a conjunction, not a particle. It starts a new clause and cannot sit between bare nouns.

お茶ちゃかコーヒーはいかがですか。
Would you like tea or coffee?

お茶ちゃにしますか。それともコーヒーにしますか。
Will you have tea? Or would you rather have coffee?

か - or · か vs それとも (cont.)

The それとも version is more deliberate - it lays out two full alternatives and asks the listener to pick between them, which makes it useful when you want to be clearly polite about offering a real choice. ❌お茶ちゃそれともコーヒー is not possible.

か - or · か (or) vs か (question particle)

Rule of thumb - か between nouns means "or"; か at the end of a sentence makes a question. Position decides, every time.

Learners see the same kana and assume ambiguity. There is almost none, because the two uses never occupy the same slot.

紅茶こうちゃかコーヒーを飲のみます。
I'll have black tea or coffee.

紅茶こうちゃを飲のみますか。
Will you have some black tea?

か - or · か (or) vs か (question particle) (cont.)

And both can appear in one sentence without any clash, each doing its own job: 「紅茶こうちゃかコーヒーを飲のみますか。」 - "Will you have tea or coffee?" The first か offers the choice; the last one turns the whole thing into a question.

か - or · Common mistakes

  • ❌ コーヒーをかお茶ちゃを飲のみます。 → ✅ コーヒーかお茶ちゃを飲のみます。
    The case particle marks the whole choice once, at the end. It is not repeated on each alternative.

  • ❌ パンかたまごを買かいました。（when you bought both） → ✅ パンとたまごを買かいました。
    か is "or". If you took both items home, the word you want is と.

  • ❌ お茶ちゃそれともコーヒーがいいです。 → ✅ お茶ちゃかコーヒーがいいです。
    それとも links two whole questions, not two nouns.

か - or · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 明日あしたか行いきます。 → ✅ 明日あしたか明後日あさって行いきます。
    か needs something on both sides. A single item with a trailing か at the end of a noun phrase is not an alternative - it will be read as a question particle or as "something like".

か - or · Practice 1

1. 昼ひるごはんはうどん____そばにします。

うどんかそばにします。

Two nouns offered as alternatives take か between them, and the following particle - here に - attaches once, after the whole pair.

か - or · Practice 2

2. Choose: 会議かいぎは月曜日げつようび（と・か）火曜日かようびです。まだ決きまっていません。

月曜日げつようびか火曜日かようびです。

The second sentence says it hasn't been decided, so only one of the two days will happen. That is "or", which is か. と would claim the meeting runs on both days.

か - or · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Please write your name in pen or pencil."

ペンかえんぴつでお名前なまえを書かいてください。

The choice is built first, then marked once with で for the instrument. In a printed form you would meet the written equivalent, または, in the same slot.

か - or · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 飲のみ物ものはビールをかワインをお願ねがいします。

✅ 飲のみ物ものはビールかワインをお願ねがいします。

を cannot be attached to each alternative. Join the nouns with か first, and let the single を cover the resulting phrase.

か - or · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) バスか電車でんしゃで行いきます。 (b) バスか電車でんしゃか、どちらかで行いきます。

Both say you will travel by one of the two. (a) is light and offhand - you have not thought about it much, and either is fine.

(b) marks both options and then names the choice explicitly with どちらか, which slows the sentence down and presents the decision as a real, still-open fork. It is what you would say when the choice actually matters, or when you are being careful in front of someone.

か - question particle

Meaning · turns a statement into a question

Put か at the end of a sentence and it becomes a question. Nothing else moves - no word order changes, no auxiliary appears. This is the same syllable as the "or" particle, but at the end of the sentence rather than between two nouns.

か - question particle · Formation

か is the last thing in the sentence, after the predicate is already complete.

Attaches to Form Example
ます-form verb ～ます + か 行いきます → 行いきますか
Noun + です Noun + ですか 学生がくせいです → 学生がくせいですか
い-adjective + です ～いです + か 高たかいです → 高たかいですか
Past / negative ～ましたか・～ませんか 来きました → 来きましたか
Plain form plain + か (blunt or written) 行いく → 行いくか
Question word sentences question word … + か どこ → どこですか
Inside a sentence clause + か + わかりません etc. 何時なんじに来くるか、わかりません
Punctuation normally 。 - the か already asks 行いきますか。

か - question particle · Formation (cont.)

Note - after a noun in the plain form, the だ drops before か: 学生がくせいか, never ❌学生がくせいだか. In the polite form there is no such problem, because です stays: 学生がくせいですか.

か - question particle · Examples

お名前なまえは何なんですか。
What's your name?

明日あした来きますか。
Are you coming tomorrow?

すみません、コピー機きはどこですか。
Excuse me, where's the photocopier?

昨日きのうの試合しあいを見みましたか。
Did you watch yesterday's match?

か - question particle · Examples (cont.)

一緒いっしょにお昼ひるを食たべませんか。
Would you like to have lunch with me?

会議かいぎが何時なんじに終おわるか、まだわかりません。
I still don't know what time the meeting ends.

すみません、この席せきに座すわってもいいですか。
Excuse me, may I sit here?

傘かさを持もっていますか。午後ごごから降ふるそうですよ。
Do you have an umbrella with you? Apparently it's going to rain from the afternoon.

か - question particle · Nuance & register

か belongs with polite Japanese. Attached to です / ます it is simply the normal way to ask anything, and it carries no attitude at all.

Attached to a plain form it is a different animal. 「行いくか」 sounds blunt, brusque, and distinctly masculine - a boss barking at a subordinate, or a character in a film. Ordinary casual questions do not use か at all: you take the plain form and raise your pitch at the end.

  • 「行いく?」 - "You going?"
  • 「これ、食たべる?」 - "You want this?"

か - question particle · Nuance & register (cont.)

That is the single most useful register fact on this page. A learner who says 「食たべるか?」 to a friend sounds aggressive; 「食たべる?」 is warm and normal. In writing, plain form + か is fine and neutral - it is the everyday shape of a question in a book or a report, where nobody is being addressed.

か - question particle · Nuance & register (cont.)

Plain か also survives in a set of self-directed expressions that everybody uses regardless of gender: 「そうか」("ah, I see"), 「本当ほんとうか」, and the polite 「そうですか」. Watch the intonation on that last one. Rising 「そうですか?」 is a real question - "is that so?". Falling 「そうですか。」 is not a question at all; it is "I see", an acknowledgement. Beginners produce the falling version by accident and puzzle their listener.

か - question particle · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two more things worth knowing. か is normally followed by 。, not ?, in careful writing - the particle already marks the question. And a negative question, ～ませんか, is not really a question about ability or fact: it is an invitation. 「見みませんか」 = "won't you watch it with me?", not "do you not watch it?".

か - question particle · か vs rising intonation

Rule of thumb - polite speech asks with か. Casual speech asks by raising the pitch and adding nothing.

コーヒーを飲のみますか。
Would you like some coffee?

コーヒー、飲のむ**?**
Want some coffee?

か - question particle · か vs rising intonation (cont.)

Both are questions and neither is wrong, but they are for different people. The first is what you say to a client; the second to a friend. Fusing them - ❌「コーヒーを飲のむか?」 - produces the rough, confrontational register mentioned above, which is very rarely what a learner intends.

か - question particle · ～ますか vs ～ませんか

Rule of thumb - ～ますか asks for information. ～ませんか offers an invitation.

週末しゅうまつ、映画えいがを見みますか。
Do you watch films at the weekend?

週末しゅうまつ、映画えいがを見みませんか。
How about seeing a film at the weekend?

か - question particle · ～ますか vs ～ませんか (cont.)

The negative shape is doing politeness work, not negation: by phrasing the offer so that "no" is the easy answer, you leave the listener room to refuse. English does the same thing with "you wouldn't want to…, would you?" A step further along is ～ましょうか, which offers to do something for the listener: 「手伝てつだいましょうか」 = "shall I give you a hand?"

か - question particle · か vs の

Rule of thumb - か is the neutral polite question. Sentence-final の is the casual question that asks for an explanation.

どうして来きませんでしたか。
Why didn't you come?

どうして来こなかったの?
How come you didn't come?

か - question particle · か vs の (cont.)

の builds on the explanatory のだ / んです, so it asks about the reason or the situation behind something rather than the bare fact. It is soft and personal - used a great deal with children and between friends, and slightly more by women than by men in its bare form. Its polite counterpart is んですか: 「どうして来こなかったんですか」, which is gentler than a flat ませんでしたか and is often the better choice when a direct か question would feel like an interrogation.

か - question particle · Common mistakes

  • ❌ あの人ひとは学生がくせいだか。 → ✅ あの人ひとは学生がくせいですか。／学生がくせいか。
    In the polite form the copula is です; in the plain form the だ drops out entirely before か.

  • ❌ 「学生がくせいですか。」「はい、そうですか。」 → ✅ 「はい、そうです。」
    Falling 「そうですか」 means "I see" - it is what the listener says. Answering with it tells your questioner you have just learned your own occupation.

  • ❌ どこか行いきますか。（meaning "where are you going?"） → ✅ どこへ行いきますか。
    どこか is one word meaning "somewhere". The question word needs its ordinary particle instead.

か - question particle · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 友ともだちに「食たべるか?」と聞ききました。 → ✅ 「食たべる?」と聞ききました。
    Plain form + か addressed at a person is rough. Casual questions drop the particle and rely on intonation.

か - question particle · Practice 1

1. すみません、駅えきはどちら____。

駅えきはどちらですか。

The sentence ends in a noun predicate, so the polite copula です comes first and か follows it to make the question. Asking a stranger, the polite form is the only sensible choice.

か - question particle · Practice 2

2. Choose the more inviting version: 一緒いっしょに（帰かえりますか・帰かえりませんか）。

一緒いっしょに**帰かえりませんか**。

The negative question is Japanese's standard invitation. It leaves refusing easy, so it sounds considerate rather than pushy. 帰かえりますか would just be asking whether the person intends to go home.

か - question particle · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, politely: "Is this seat free?"

この席せきは空あいていますか。

The predicate is a verb in polite form, so か simply attaches to います. No word order changes and no extra word is needed to make it a question.

か - question particle · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 友ともだちに「明日あした来くるか?」と聞ききました。

✅ 友ともだちに「明日あした来くる?」と聞ききました。

Plain form plus か aimed at a listener sounds harsh and confrontational. Between friends the particle is dropped and the question is carried by rising intonation alone.

か - question particle · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) どうして会社かいしゃを休やすみましたか。 (b) どうして会社かいしゃを休やすんだんですか。

Both are polite and both ask why the person missed work. (a) is a bare request for a fact, and coming from a superior it can feel like being called in for questioning.

(b) uses the explanatory んです, which frames the question as "what's the story behind it?" - it acknowledges there is a situation and invites the person to explain it. That makes it warmer and more usual in real conversation, especially when the answer might be personal.
