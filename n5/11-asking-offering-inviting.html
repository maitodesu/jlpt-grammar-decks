～てください

Meaning · please do ~ - a request, and often an instruction

～てください attaches to the て-form and asks the listener to perform an action. It is polite in form - ください is the imperative of the honorific verb 下(くだ)さる - but it is not a soft or deferential way to ask. Teachers, doctors, station staff and forms all use it, and that is the flavour it carries: a clear directive, delivered courteously.

～てください · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) て-form + ください 待(ま)つ → 待(ま)ってください
Verb (る-verb) て-form + ください 見(み)る → 見(み)てください
Verb (する) して + ください 電話(でんわ)する → 電話(でんわ)してください
Verb (来(く)る) 来(き)て + ください 来(く)る → 来(き)てください

More polite ～てくださいませんか／～ていただけませんか 待(ま)っていただけませんか

To ask for a thing rather than an action, ください follows a noun with を instead - that pattern has its own page in this book.

～てください · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ください attaches to the て-form, never the dictionary form. Get the て-form right and the rest is automatic: 話(はな)す → 話(はな)して, 急(いそ)ぐ → 急(いそ)いで, 読(よ)む → 読(よ)んで, 行(い)く → 行(い)って.

Please, have a seat.

申(もう)し訳(わけ)ありませんが、明日(あした)は三十分(さんじゅっぷん)ほど早(はや)く来(き)てくださいませんか。
I'm terribly sorry, but could you possibly come in half an hour early tomorrow?

～てください · Nuance & register

The single most useful thing to know about ～てください is that it is not the polite form of a request. It is the neutral one. Because it tells rather than asks, it belongs to situations where the speaker has a right to direct: a teacher to a class, a doctor to a patient, an announcement to passengers, a form to whoever is filling it in. Used sideways or upward it can land as bossy - 部長(ぶちょう)、これをコピーしてください is grammatically impeccable and would raise eyebrows.

The ladder, roughly from most casual to most deferential:

～てください · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 待(ま)って - friends, family. て-form alone.
  • 待(ま)ってくれる？／待(ま)ってくれない？ - casual, but genuinely asking.
  • 待(ま)ってください - the neutral polite directive on this page.
  • 待(ま)ってくださいませんか - a real question, so noticeably softer.
  • 待(ま)っていただけませんか／待(ま)っていただけますか - humble; the workplace default upward.
  • お待(ま)ちください - honorific, used to customers by staff.

くださる and いただく describe the same event from opposite ends: くださる is "you kindly give", いただく is "I humbly receive". Both are polite; the いただく versions are the ones you reach for when you want to sound careful.

～てください · Nuance & register (cont.)

Small softeners matter more than learners expect. Front the request with すみませんが or 恐(おそ)れ入(い)りますが, add ちょっと, and finish with ね (気(き)をつけてくださいね) and the same sentence stops sounding like an order.

Note one friendly use that runs against all of this: with どうぞ, ～てください is an offer, not a command. 食(た)べてください at a dinner table means "please, help yourself".

In casual speech the bare て-form does all this work - 見(み)て！, 貸(か)して - and ちょうだい (貸(か)してちょうだい) is a softer, childish or motherly variant.

～てください · ～てください vs ～ていただけませんか

Rule of thumb - ～てください tells; ～ていただけませんか asks. Upward, and for anything that is a favour rather than a duty, ask.

この漢字(かんじ)の読(よ)み方(かた)を教(おし)えてください。
Tell me how to read this kanji.

この漢字(かんじ)の読(よ)み方(かた)を教(おし)えていただけませんか。
Would you mind telling me how to read this kanji?

The second is longer for a reason. It is a negative question, which leaves the listener room to say no, and it uses the humble いただく, which frames you as the one receiving a kindness. The pair ～てくださいませんか works the same way with くださる and is a shade less humble.

～てください · ～てください vs ～ていただけませんか (cont.)

A useful test: if a "no" would be perfectly reasonable, use the question form. If you are describing a step in a procedure - 靴(くつ)をぬいでください at the door - ～てください is exactly right and the long version would sound strange.

～てください · ～てください vs the plain て-form

Rule of thumb - drop ください and you have the casual request. Drop it with the wrong person and you have an order.

ちょっと手伝(てつだ)って。
Give me a hand a sec.

ちょっと手伝(てつだ)ってください。
Could you give me a hand, please.

The bare て-form is everywhere in speech between friends, within a family, and from older to younger. It is not rude in itself - it is simply unmarked for politeness, which means the relationship has to carry it. Adding ね or よ softens it (急(いそ)いでね); adding よ alone can sharpen it.

～てください · ～てください vs the plain て-form (cont.)

～てくれる？ and ～てくれない？ sit slightly above the bare form: still casual, but phrased as a question, so they ask rather than tell.

～てください · ～てください vs ～をください

Rule of thumb - ください after a て-form asks for an action; ください after a noun + を asks for a thing.

水(みず)をください。
Some water, please.

水(みず)を持(も)ってきてください。
Please bring me some water.

Both are natural; they differ in what you are asking for. The first hands over an object, the second requests the act of fetching. The mistake to avoid is trying to force a noun into the verb slot - ❌手伝(てつだ)いをしてください is possible but stilted, and ✅手伝(てつだ)ってください is what a speaker actually says.

～てください · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 食(た)べるてください → ✅ 食(た)べてください
    ください attaches to the て-form, not the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 待(ま)つてください → ✅ 待(ま)ってください
    つ-verbs make their て-form with a small っ: 待(ま)つ → 待(ま)って.

  • ❌ 行(い)かないてください → ✅ 行(い)かないでください
    The negative request uses ないで, never ないて.

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、これをコピーしてください。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、これをコピーしていただけませんか。
    Grammatical, but you are giving an instruction to someone senior. Ask instead.

  • ❌ ペンをしてください。 → ✅ ペンをください。
    Asking for an object needs no verb at all.

～てください · Practice 1

1. すみません、この字(じ)が読(よ)めません。ゆっくり話(はな)____。

ゆっくり話(はな)してください。

話(はな)す is a す-verb, so its て-form is 話(はな)して, and ください attaches directly to that. Adding すみませんが at the front, as the question does, already softens the request.

～てください · Practice 2

2. 社長(しゃちょう)に：「この書類(しょるい)を（見(み)てください・見(み)ていただけませんか）。」

この書類(しょるい)を見(み)ていただけませんか。

Looking over your document is a favour, not part of the president's duties, and he outranks you. The humble question form leaves him room to decline, which is exactly what politeness upward is made of.

～てください · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Please turn off the light when you leave the room."

部屋(へや)を出(で)るとき、電気(でんき)を消(け)してください。

消(け)す → 消(け)して + ください. This is the instructional use of the pattern - a house rule, not a personal favour - so plain ～てください is right.

～てください · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: あぶないですから、そこに立(た)つてください。

✅ あぶないですから、そこに立(た)たないでください。

Two things are wrong. The て-form of 立(た)つ is 立(た)って, not ❌立(た)つて; and the sense demands a negative - "it's dangerous, so please don't stand there" - which is built with ないで.

～てください · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 窓(まど)を開(あ)けてください。 (b) 窓(まど)を開(あ)けてくださいませんか。

(a) is a plain polite directive. In a classroom, or between people who know each other well, it is entirely normal - but it assumes compliance and does not invite a refusal.

(b) is phrased as a negative question, so it genuinely asks. It is longer, more deferential, and the natural choice when you are imposing on someone, addressing a stranger, or speaking to someone senior to you.

～をください

Meaning · please give me ~ - asking for an object

～をください names a thing and asks for it. It is the sentence you use in a shop, at a ticket window, at a counter, in a restaurant - anywhere the other person is there to hand you something. ください is the imperative of the honorific verb 下(くだ)さる "to give (to me)", which is why a form that is literally a command still counts as polite.

～をください · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + を + ください コーヒー → コーヒーをください
Noun + quantity Noun + を + counter + ください りんご → りんごを三(みっ)つください
Quantity (spoken, を dropped) Noun + counter + ください コーヒー二(ふた)つください
Verb (an action, not a thing) て-form + ください 待(ま)つ → 待(ま)ってください
Politer ～をお願(ねが)いします コーヒーをお願(ねが)いします
Politer still ～をいただけますか メニューをいただけますか
Casual ～ちょうだい コーヒーちょうだい

～をください · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the thing takes を, never が. ください is a verb of giving, so what is given is its direct object - the opposite of ほしい, which is an adjective and takes が.

～をください · Examples

コーヒーをください。
A coffee, please.

すみません、メニューをください。
Excuse me, could I have a menu.

この赤(あか)いりんごを五(いつ)つください。
Five of these red apples, please.

切符(きっぷ)を大人(おとな)二(に)まい、子(こ)ども一(いち)まいください。
Two adult tickets and one child's, please.

袋(ふくろ)は要(い)りませんので、レシートだけください。
I don't need a bag - just the receipt, please.

～をください · Examples (cont.)

もう少(すこ)し時間(じかん)をください。
Please give me a little more time.

すみません、温(あたた)かいお茶(ちゃ)と、砂糖(さとう)を少(すこ)しください。
Excuse me - a hot tea, and a little sugar, please.

白(しろ)いシャツの大(おお)きいサイズがあれば、それをください。
If you have that white shirt in a large, I'll take it.

～をください · Nuance & register

～をください is the customer's sentence. Between a customer and staff the balance of the situation makes it perfectly polite, and it is what Japanese speakers actually say at a counter - no hedging required.

Turn it around and the picture changes. Because ください is an imperative, asking a superior for something with it sounds like an instruction: 部長(ぶちょう)、ペンをください is the sort of thing that gets noticed. Upward you switch to a question - ペンをお借(か)りできますか, 資料(しりょう)をいただけますか - or to お願(ねが)いします.

Quantities behave loosely in speech. コーヒーを二(ふた)つください and コーヒー二(ふた)つください are both normal, and the second is more common at a counter; what you should not do is mark the number with を as well (❌コーヒーを二(ふた)つをください). Politeness particles cluster at the front and back instead: すみません、～ください and ～くださいね are both very common.

～をください · Nuance & register (cont.)

ちょうだい is the casual replacement - それちょうだい - and reads as childish or warmly familiar depending on the speaker; it is more common from women and from adults talking to children. In writing, ください survives in fixed instructions (ご記入(きにゅう)ください, 少々(しょうしょう)お待(ま)ちください), which are honorific forms rather than this pattern.

ください is not limited to physical objects. Abstract nouns work fine, and this is where the pattern turns up outside shops: 時間(じかん)をください, 返事(へんじ)をください, もう一度(いちど)チャンスをください.

～をください · ～をください vs ～をお願(ねが)いします

Rule of thumb - ください asks for a thing you can hand over; お願(ねが)いします asks for that or for a service, and sounds a touch more deferential.

生(なま)ビールをください。
A draught beer, please.

生(なま)ビールをお願(ねが)いします。
A draught beer, please. (slightly more polite)

Where they come apart is scope. お願(ねが)いします covers requests that have no object at all - 会計(かいけい)をお願(ねが)いします "the bill, please", 山田(やまだ)さんをお願(ねが)いします "could I speak to Yamada" - where ください would be wrong or comic. As a rule, if you are asking someone to do something on your behalf, お願(ねが)いします; if you are asking them to give you something, either works.

～をください · ～をください vs ～てください

Rule of thumb - noun + を + ください for an object; verb て-form + ください for an action.

地図(ちず)をください。
Could I have a map.

地図(ちず)を書(か)いてください。
Please draw me a map.

They share the word ください and nothing else. English blurs them - "give me a hand" looks like a noun request but is really an action - so the trap is real: ✅手伝(てつだ)ってください, not ❌手伝(てつだ)いをください.

～をください · ～をください vs ～てください (cont.)

When you want an object brought to you rather than simply handed over, the two combine: 水(みず)を持(も)ってきてください.

～をください · ～をください vs ～がほしいです

Rule of thumb - ほしい describes your inner state; ください performs the request. To a shop assistant, only the second one is a sentence that does anything.

この靴(くつ)の黒(くろ)をください。
I'll take these shoes in black.

新(あたら)しい靴(くつ)がほしいです。
I want new shoes.

The second is a fine thing to say to a friend and an odd thing to say to a salesperson - you have announced a feeling and left them to guess what to do about it. Note the particles as well: ほしい is an adjective and takes が, while ください is a verb and takes を. Mixing them up (❌これがください) is the single most common error with this pattern.

～をください · Common mistakes

  • ❌ これがください。 → ✅ これをください。
    ください is a verb of giving; the thing given is its object.

  • ❌ （レストランで）お水(みず)がほしいです。 → ✅ お水(みず)をください。
    Naming your desire at a server does not place an order.

  • ❌ 手伝(てつだ)いをください。 → ✅ 手伝(てつだ)ってください。
    Help is an action, not an object. Use the て-form.

  • ❌ コーヒーを二(ふた)つをください。 → ✅ コーヒーを二(ふた)つください。
    One を per object. The counter does not take a particle of its own.

  • ❌ 課長(かちょう)、はんこをください。 → ✅ 課長(かちょう)、はんこをいただけますか。
    ください is an imperative. Pointed upward it reads as an instruction.

～をください · Practice 1

ください comes from the verb 下(くだ)さる, so the thing you are asking for is its direct object and takes を. The が that goes with ほしい does not transfer.

～をください · Practice 2

In a shop you are placing a request, and ください is what performs it. ほしいです only reports how you feel, which leaves the assistant to work out what you actually want.

～をください · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Two coffees and one orange juice, please."

～をください · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: すみません、ちょっと手伝(てつだ)いをください。

✅ すみません、ちょっと手伝(てつだ)ってください。

What you want is an action, not an object, so it belongs in the て-form pattern. English "give me a hand" hides a verb inside a noun, and that is where the mistake comes from.

～をください · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) タクシーをください。 (b) タクシーをお願(ねが)いします。

(a) asks for a taxi as though it were an object to be handed across a counter, and it sounds odd for that reason - nobody gives you a taxi.

(b) is what a hotel guest actually says. お願(ねが)いします asks the listener to arrange something on your behalf, which covers services, phone calls and errands. That wider scope is the main practical reason to learn it alongside ください.

～ないでください

Meaning · please don't do ~ - the negative request

～ないでください is the negative counterpart of ～てください. Take the ない-form, add で, add ください. The で is not decoration: ないで is the negative て-form of a verb, and it does the same joining work elsewhere in the language - 傘(かさ)を持(も)たないで出(で)かけた「I went out without an umbrella」.

～ないでください · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ない-form + で + ください 吸(す)う → 吸(す)わないでください
Verb (る-verb) ない-form + で + ください 捨(す)てる → 捨(す)てないでください
Verb (する) しないで + ください 心配(しんぱい)する → 心配(しんぱい)しないでください
Verb (来(く)る) 来(こ)ないで + ください 来(く)る → 来(こ)ないでください
Casual ない-form + で 見(み)ないで／見(み)ないでね
More polite ～ないでいただけませんか 入(はい)らないでいただけませんか
Notices and signs ご遠慮(えんりょ)ください／おやめください 喫煙(きつえん)はご遠慮(えんりょ)ください

～ないでください · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - build the ない-form first, then swap ない for ないで. The ない-form is the hard part: 吸(す)う → 吸(す)わない (not ❌吸(す)あない), する → しない, 来(く)る → 来(こ)ない (read こない, never ❌きない).

Note - ないで on its own is a full form of the verb, not just a piece of this pattern. It joins clauses in the sense without doing (何(なに)も言(い)わないで出(で)て行(い)った), and it feeds other endings: 忘(わす)れないでね, 来(こ)ないでほしい, 見(み)ないでくれる？

～ないでください · Examples

ここでたばこを吸(す)わないでください。
Please don't smoke here.

まだ帰(かえ)らないでください。
Please don't leave yet.

心配(しんぱい)しないでください。
Please don't worry.

授業(じゅぎょう)中(ちゅう)はスマホを見(み)ないでください。
Please don't look at your phone during class.

この部屋(へや)には入(はい)らないでください。
Please don't go into this room.

～ないでください · Examples (cont.)

大切(たいせつ)な日(ひ)ですから、明日(あした)は遅(おく)れないでくださいね。
Tomorrow's an important day, so please don't be late.

すみませんが、この話(はなし)はまだ誰(だれ)にも言(い)わないでください。
Sorry, but please don't tell anyone about this yet.

恐(おそ)れ入(い)りますが、館内(かんない)では写真(しゃしん)を撮(と)らないでいただけますか。
I'm very sorry, but could I ask you not to take photographs inside the building?

～ないでください · Nuance & register

Everything true of ～てください is true here: it is polite in form and directive in function. A negative directive is inherently more confronting than a positive one, though, because you are telling someone to stop or not to start - so the softeners matter more.

The usual ones are すみませんが / 恐(おそ)れ入(い)りますが at the front, ちょっと in the middle, and ね at the end. 大声(おおごえ)を出(だ)さないでくださいね is noticeably gentler than the same sentence without it.

Public notices avoid the bare form, because a sign that tells thousands of people what not to do is at risk of sounding hectoring. Japanese signage prefers ご遠慮(えんりょ)ください「we ask that you refrain」, おやめください「please stop」, or a plain noun + 禁止(きんし)「prohibited」 on the sternest notices. If you learn to read those, you have covered most stations and museums.

～ないでください · Nuance & register (cont.)

Casually, ください drops off and the ない-form does the whole job: 見(み)ないで！, 言(い)わないで. Add ね to soften, よ to press. ～ないでくれる？ and ～ないでほしい are common alternatives among friends - the first asks, the second reports your wish.

One thing ～ないでください is not: a statement of prohibition. It is a request. If you need to say something is forbidden, ～てはいけません is the pattern, and it carries an authority that this one does not.

～ないでください · ～ないでください vs ～ないでいただけませんか

Rule of thumb - the negative request, then the negative question. Upward, and to strangers, ask rather than tell.

ここに車(くるま)をとめないでください。
Please don't park here.

ここに車(くるま)をとめないでいただけませんか。
Would you mind not parking here?

The second is what a neighbour says to a neighbour; the first is what the sign says. ～ないでくださいませんか sits between them, and the fully honorific ご遠慮(えんりょ)ください is the institutional voice - a shop, a train company, a museum.

～ないでください · ～ないでください vs ～ないでいただけませんか (cont.)

The relationship decides. Telling a stranger not to do something is delicate in any language, and Japanese solves it by turning the request into a question and letting the listener seem to choose.

～ないでください · ～ないで vs ～なくて

Rule of thumb - Japanese has two negative て-forms. ないで means without doing and is the one used in requests; なくて means and so, because … not.

朝(あさ)ごはんを食(た)べないで学校(がっこう)へ行(い)きました。
I went to school without eating breakfast.

バスが来(こ)なくて、遅(おく)れました。
The bus didn't come, and so I was late.

The first describes how the going happened; the second gives the reason for being late. Swap them and the sentences stop making sense.

～ないでください · ～ないで vs ～なくて (cont.)

Two consequences worth memorising. First, requests always use ないで - there is no ❌行(い)かなくてください. Second, なくて is the form that adjectives and nouns take when they are negated and joined (高(たか)くなくて安心(あんしん)しました, 雨(あめ)じゃなくてよかった), so ないで is verb-only territory.

～ないでください · ～ないでください vs ～てはいけません

Rule of thumb - ～ないでください asks; ～てはいけません forbids.

教室(きょうしつ)で走(はし)らないでください。
Please don't run in the classroom.

教室(きょうしつ)で走(はし)ってはいけません。
You must not run in the classroom.

The first is a request, however firmly meant; the second states a rule, and the speaker has to be in a position to state it - a teacher, a parent, a notice. Used by an equal it sounds like a scolding.

There is a third, gentler option that learners rarely meet early enough: ～ないようにしてください「please try not to…」, which frames the same content as advice. 夜(よる)遅(おそ)く電話(でんわ)しないようにしてください is the version that survives being said to almost anyone.

～ないでください · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 見(み)ないてください → ✅ 見(み)ないでください
    The negative て-form is ないで. There is no form ❌ないて.

  • ❌ 行(い)かなくてください → ✅ 行(い)かないでください
    なくて gives a reason; it cannot carry a request.

  • ❌ するないでください → ✅ しないでください
    する is irregular: its ない-form is しない, so the request is しないでください.

  • ❌ たばこを吸(す)うないでください → ✅ たばこを吸(す)わないでください
    ないで attaches to the ない-form, not the dictionary form. For う-verbs ending in う, the ない-form takes わ.

  • ❌ 来(き)ないでください → ✅ 来(こ)ないでください
    *Same kanji, different reading. The ない-form of 来(く)る is read こない.*

～ないでください · Practice 1

Japanese has two negative て-forms and only one of them can carry a request. なくて is for causes and consequences - 開(あ)けなくて寒(さむ)かった - so it cannot be followed by ください.

～ないでください · Practice 5

～ましょう

Meaning · let's - a proposal made on the assumption you already agree

～ましょう is the polite volitional. It turns a verb into "let's do it", and its defining feature is confidence: the speaker takes agreement as read. That makes it the right form for a plan already settled, for a leader moving a group along, and for a public slogan - and the wrong form for a genuine, open invitation, where ～ませんか is what you want.

A second use hides inside the first. With 私(わたし)が, ～ましょう is not "let's" at all but "I'll do it" - the speaker volunteering.

～ましょう · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + ましょう 行(い)きます → 行(い)きましょう
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + ましょう 食(た)べます → 食(た)べましょう
Verb (する) し + ましょう します → しましょう
Verb (来(く)る) き + ましょう 来(き)ます → 来(き)ましょう
Softened into a question ましょう + か 行(い)きましょうか
Casual equivalent plain volitional 行(い)く → 行(い)こう／食(た)べる → 食(た)べよう

Rule of thumb - ましょう replaces ます, so it builds off the same stem. 飲(の)みます → 飲(の)みましょう. It never attaches to the dictionary form: ❌飲(の)むましょう.

～ましょう · Formation (cont.)

Note - there is no negative ましょう. ❌行(い)きませんしょう does not exist. To propose not doing something, negate the content instead: 今日(きょう)は買(か)うのはやめましょう or 今日(きょう)は買(か)わないことにしましょう.

～ましょう · Examples

じゃあ、行(い)きましょう。
Right - let's go.

一緒(いっしょ)に昼(ひる)ごはんを食(た)べましょう。
Let's have lunch together.

七(しち)時(じ)に駅(えき)の前(まえ)で会(あ)いましょう。
Let's meet in front of the station at seven.

そろそろ始(はじ)めましょう。
Let's get started.

今日(きょう)はここまでにしましょう。
Let's call it a day there.

～ましょう · Examples (cont.)

はい、私(わたし)が運転(うんてん)しましょう。
All right - I'll drive.

雨(あめ)が降(ふ)りそうですから、傘(かさ)を持(も)って行(い)きましょう。
It looks like rain, so let's take umbrellas.

時間(じかん)がありませんから、細(こま)かいことは後(あと)で話(はな)しましょう。
We're out of time, so let's go through the details afterwards.

～ましょう · Nuance & register

ましょう is polite in form but assertive in function. You are announcing what the group is going to do. That is fine when the plan is agreed, when you are the one running the meeting, or when the proposal is obviously welcome (寒(さむ)いですね、中(なか)に入(はい)りましょう). It is presumptuous when the other person has not yet agreed to anything, and doubly so upward: 部長(ぶちょう)、飲(の)みに行(い)きましょう helps itself to the boss's evening. 飲(の)みに行(い)きませんか or いかがですか leaves the decision where it belongs.

The same assertiveness is why ましょう is the voice of public exhortation. Handwashing posters, road-safety signs and school announcements are full of it: 手(て)を洗(あら)いましょう, 気(き)をつけましょう, 時間(じかん)を守(まも)りましょう. Grammatically these are invitations; in practice they are instructions with a smile.

～ましょう · Nuance & register (cont.)

As an answer, ましょう is the standard yes. To 一緒(いっしょ)に行(い)きませんか you reply ええ、行(い)きましょう - the invitation was open, and ましょう closes it.

The 私(わたし)が use is worth practising: 私(わたし)が持(も)ちましょう「I'll carry that」. It is a shade more take-charge than 持(も)ちましょうか, which asks first, and noticeably more formal than 持(も)ちます. In service Japanese it is often replaced by the humble お持(も)ちしましょう.

Casually, the whole thing collapses to the plain volitional: 行(い)こう, 食(た)べよう, しよう, 来(こ)よう. Between friends ましょう sounds oddly ceremonious, like proposing a motion.

～ましょう · ～ましょう vs ～ませんか

Rule of thumb - ましょう assumes you'll agree; ませんか asks whether you will. If the other person could reasonably say no, use ませんか.

映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)きましょう。
Let's go and see a film.

映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)きませんか。
Would you like to go and see a film?

The first works when you have already decided to spend the afternoon together and are choosing what to do. The second works when you are asking someone out - it is a question, it is negative, and both of those give the listener room.

～ましょう · ～ましょう vs ～ませんか (cont.)

This is why the pair so often appears in sequence: one person invites with ませんか, the other accepts with ましょう. Reversing them is the classic learner error, and it is the difference between "would you like to?" and "we're going".

～ましょう · ～ましょう vs ～ましょうか

Rule of thumb - the か turns a decision into a question. ましょう announces; ましょうか checks, or offers to do something for the listener.

そろそろ出(で)ましょう。
Let's head off.

そろそろ出(で)ましょうか。
Shall we head off?

Adding か costs nothing and softens a great deal, so ましょうか is the safer default whenever you are not certain the other person is ready. It also unlocks a sense ましょう does not have: with the speaker as the doer, it becomes an offer - 荷物(にもつ)を持(も)ちましょうか「shall I take your bag?」 where 荷物(にもつ)を持(も)ちましょう is you announcing that you will.

～ましょう · ～ましょう vs the plain volitional

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different politeness. ましょう is the ます-level form; 行(い)こう and 食(た)べよう are the plain-level ones.

明日(あした)は八(はち)時(じ)に集(あつ)まりましょう。
Let's gather at eight tomorrow.

おなかすいた。なんか食(た)べよう。
I'm hungry. Let's get something to eat.

The plain forms are built like this: う-verbs change the final -u to -ou (行(い)く → 行(い)こう, 飲(の)む → 飲(の)もう), る-verbs swap る for よう (見(み)る → 見(み)よう), and the two irregulars are する → しよう, 来(く)る → 来(こ)よう.

～ましょう · ～ましょう vs the plain volitional (cont.)

Register maps straight across: use the plain volitional with friends, family and anyone you would speak to in plain form, and ましょう everywhere you are using ます. Note that the plain form can also take か - 行(い)こうか is the casual equivalent of 行(い)きましょうか.

～ましょう · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 飲(の)むましょう → ✅ 飲(の)みましょう
    ましょう replaces ます on the stem, not the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 行(い)きましょうですか → ✅ 行(い)きましょうか
    ましょう already contains the copula. Just add か.

  • ❌ 課長(かちょう)、一緒(いっしょ)に帰(かえ)りましょう。 → ✅ 課長(かちょう)、一緒(いっしょ)に帰(かえ)りませんか。
    Deciding a superior's movements for them. Ask instead of proposing.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)は雨(あめ)が降(ふ)りましょう。 → ✅ 明日(あした)は雨(あめ)が降(ふ)るでしょう。
    ましょう is a proposal, not a prediction. Guessing about the weather is でしょう.

  • ❌ 行(い)きませんしょう。 → ✅ 行(い)くのはやめましょう。
    There is no negative ましょう; negate the content instead.

～ましょう · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 来週(らいしゅう)の日曜日(にちようび)、テニスをするましょうか。

～ましょう · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 会議(かいぎ)を始(はじ)めましょう。 (b) 会議(かいぎ)を始(はじ)めましょうか。

(a) starts the meeting. The speaker has the floor, everyone is present, and the sentence is really an announcement dressed as a proposal - the chair's line.

(b) checks first. It reads as "shall we make a start?", which suits someone who is not chairing, or a chair who can see that two people are still on the phone. The か costs one syllable and buys a lot of room.

～ましょうか

Meaning · shall I? / shall we? - offering to do something, or proposing tentatively

～ましょうか is ～ましょう with a question mark built in, and that small か does two different jobs.

  • Shall I? - you offer to do something for the listener.
    窓(まど)を開(あ)けましょうか。
  • Shall we? - you propose something for both of you, but check first instead of
    announcing. そろそろ帰(かえ)りましょうか。

～ましょうか · Overview

Which one a listener hears is decided by the situation, not the grammar: if the action benefits them and only you can do it, it is an offer; if it is something you would do together, it is a proposal.

～ましょうか · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + ましょうか 持(も)ちます → 持(も)ちましょうか
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + ましょうか 開(あ)けます → 開(あ)けましょうか
Verb (する) し + ましょうか します → しましょうか
Verb (来(く)る) き + ましょうか 来(き)ます → 来(き)ましょうか
Humble offer (service Japanese) お + ます-stem + しましょうか お持(も)ちしましょうか
Casual plain volitional + か 開(あ)けようか／手伝(てつだ)おうか

Rule of thumb - mark the doer and the ambiguity disappears. 私(わたし)が運(はこ)びましょうか is unmistakably an offer; 一緒(いっしょ)に運(はこ)びましょうか is unmistakably a proposal.

～ましょうか · Examples

窓(まど)を開(あ)けましょうか。
Shall I open the window?

手伝(てつだ)いましょうか。
Shall I give you a hand?

何(なに)を飲(の)みましょうか。
What shall we have to drink?

暑(あつ)いですね。エアコンをつけましょうか。
It's hot, isn't it. Shall I put the air conditioning on?

どこで会(あ)いましょうか。
Where shall we meet?

～ましょうか · Examples (cont.)

写真(しゃしん)を撮(と)りましょうか。
Shall I take a photo for you?

道(みち)が混(こ)んでいますね。次(つぎ)の角(かど)を右(みぎ)に曲(ま)がりましょうか。
The traffic's heavy. Shall we turn right at the next corner?

会議(かいぎ)の資料(しりょう)、私(わたし)が人数分(にんずうぶん)コピーしておきましょうか。
Shall I run off enough copies of the meeting handouts for everyone?

～ましょうか · Nuance & register

ましょうか is the workhorse of everyday helpfulness. It is what you say when you notice someone struggling with a door, a suitcase, a printer. It is polite without being stiff, it commits you to nothing until they answer, and - crucially - it does not put the listener under obligation, because you are volunteering rather than asking.

Learn the replies as a set, because you will hear them constantly:

  • お願(ねが)いします - yes, please.
  • すみません、ありがとうございます - yes, with thanks and apology at once.
  • あ、大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)です - no, I'm fine. The everyday polite refusal.
  • いえ、結構(けっこう)です - no thank you. Firmer, and cooler.

～ましょうか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Upward, ましょうか is acceptable but plain. In service Japanese and towards superiors the humble frame is standard: お持(も)ちしましょうか, お調(しら)べしましょうか, ご案内(あんない)しましょうか. These raise the listener by lowering the action, and they are what hotel staff and careful employees use.

Casually the whole thing shortens to the plain volitional plus か: 開(あ)けようか, 手伝(てつだ)おうか, 買(か)ってこようか. Note that in casual speech the か is often dropped entirely and carried by intonation - 手伝(てつだ)おう？

One warning about intonation and reading: ましょうか is a question, and it takes a rising tone. Written down, it is often followed by 。 rather than ？ in Japanese prose, which does not make it any less of a question.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ましょう

Rule of thumb - ましょう announces; ましょうか checks. The か hands the decision back to the listener.

電気(でんき)をつけましょう。
Let's put the light on.

電気(でんき)をつけましょうか。
Shall I put the light on?

The first version assumes; the second asks. That is why the か version doubles as an offer and the bare version does not: an offer only makes sense if the other person can decline it.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ましょう (cont.)

Between the two, ましょうか is almost always the safer choice with people you do not know well. Use bare ましょう when the plan is already agreed and you are simply moving it along - 行(い)きましょう as you both stand up.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ませんか

Rule of thumb - both ask, but ませんか invites the listener to join you; ましょうか proposes a next step in something already underway, or offers to act on their behalf.

今度(こんど)の土曜日(どようび)、山(やま)に登(のぼ)りませんか。
How about climbing a mountain this Saturday?

雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ってきましたね。下(お)りましょうか。
It's started raining. Shall we head back down?

ませんか opens something new and can be refused without awkwardness - it is the form for asking someone out. ましょうか takes the shared activity as given and asks about the next move within it.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ませんか (cont.)

There is also a distribution difference that catches learners. For an offer, ませんか does not work: ❌荷物(にもつ)を持(も)ちませんか sounds like you are inviting the other person to carry luggage, not offering to carry theirs. Offers are ましょうか territory.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～てあげましょうか

Rule of thumb - plain ましょうか for offers. ～てあげましょうか announces that you are doing the listener a kindness, and that is not yours to announce.

駅(えき)まで車(くるま)で送(おく)りましょうか。
Shall I drive you to the station?

駅(えき)まで車(くるま)で送(おく)ってあげましょうか。
Shall I do you the favour of driving you to the station?

The second is grammatical and is used - among close friends, or lightly to a child - but あげる explicitly frames the act as a benefit conferred, and offering that to an equal or a superior sounds self-congratulatory. The neutral offer leaves the gratitude to the other person.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～てあげましょうか (cont.)

The polite move goes the other way, towards humility: お送(おく)りしましょうか lowers your own action, and is the form used in service and business. ～てあげる and お～する are the two ends of the same axis, and the safe default at N5 is plain ましょうか in the middle.

～ましょうか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 開(あ)けるましょうか → ✅ 開(あ)けましょうか
    Built on the ます-stem: 開(あ)けます → 開(あ)け + ましょうか.

  • ❌ 荷物(にもつ)を持(も)ちませんか。（as an offer） → ✅ 荷物(にもつ)を持(も)ちましょうか。
    ませんか invites the listener to do it. To offer your own help, use ましょうか.

  • ❌ 窓(まど)を開(あ)けましょうか。（meaning "please open the window"） → ✅ 窓(まど)を開(あ)けてください。
    ましょうか puts the action on you, not on the listener. It can never be a request.

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、コピーしてあげましょうか。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、コピーしましょうか。／お手伝(てつだ)いしましょうか。
    てあげる advertises your own kindness. Upward, drop it or go humble.

～ましょうか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 行(い)きましょうか、ですか。 → ✅ 行(い)きましょうか。
    ましょうか is already a complete question.

A joint action within a conversation already under way - the "shall we?" sense. Plain 決(き)めましょう would work too, but it announces the decision instead of checking that the other person is ready.

～ましょうか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: すみません、この字(じ)が読(よ)めません。読(よ)みましょうか。

The speaker wants the listener to read it, but ましょうか offers the speaker's own action - as written, it says "shall I read it?", which makes no sense after "I can't read it". A request needs the ください or いただけませんか family.

～ましょうか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) お茶(ちゃ)を入(い)れましょうか。 (b) お茶(ちゃ)を入(い)れてあげましょうか。

(a) is a plain, warm offer to make tea. It is the version that works with a guest, a colleague or a boss, because it says nothing about who is doing whom a favour.

(b) adds あげる, which explicitly marks the tea as a kindness you are bestowing. Between close friends it can be playful; to anyone senior it sounds like you are inviting thanks in advance. The humble お入(い)れしましょうか goes the opposite way and is the safest of the three in a workplace.

～ませんか

Meaning · won't you…? how about it? - a genuine, open invitation

～ませんか invites. It is built as a negative question, and that is the whole point: a negative question presupposes nothing, so it leaves the listener free to decline without embarrassment. Where ～ましょう announces a plan, ～ませんか asks whether you would like one - which is why it is the polite way to ask someone to do something with you.

～ませんか · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + ませんか 行(い)きます → 行(い)きませんか
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + ませんか 食(た)べます → 食(た)べませんか
Verb (する) し + ませんか します → しませんか
Verb (来(く)る) き + ませんか 来(き)ます → 来(き)ませんか
Casual ない-form, rising tone 行(い)かない？／食(た)べない？
Politer ～ませんでしょうか いらっしゃいませんでしょうか
Accepting ええ、～ましょう ええ、行(い)きましょう

～ませんか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ませんか is just the polite negative ません plus the question particle か, so it is built on the ます-stem like everything else in that family: 話(はな)します → 話(はな)しません → 話(はな)しませんか.

～ませんか · Examples

一緒(いっしょ)にお茶(ちゃ)を飲(の)みませんか。
Would you like to have a cup of tea with me?

今度(こんど)の日曜日(にちようび)、買(か)い物(もの)に行(い)きませんか。
How about going shopping this Sunday?

少(すこ)し休(やす)みませんか。
Shall we take a short break?

よかったら、夕(ゆう)ごはんを一緒(いっしょ)に食(た)べませんか。
If you're free, would you like to have dinner together?

この本(ほん)、よかったら読(よ)んでみませんか。
Would you like to give this book a read?

～ませんか · Examples (cont.)

来週(らいしゅう)の土曜日(どようび)、うちでバーベキューをしますが、来(き)ませんか。
We're having a barbecue at ours next Saturday - do you want to come?

来月(らいげつ)から朝(あさ)の勉強(べんきょう)会(かい)を始(はじ)めます。田中(たなか)さんも参加(さんか)しませんか。
We're starting a morning study group next month. Would you like to join, Tanaka?

もしお時間(じかん)があれば、駅(えき)の近(ちか)くにできた美術館(びじゅつかん)に一緒(いっしょ)に行(い)ってみませんか。
If you have time, would you like to go with me to the new museum near the station?

～ませんか · Nuance & register

ませんか is the polite invitation, full stop. It works upward, sideways and to strangers, and it is the form to reach for whenever the answer could genuinely be no. The politeness comes from the shape of the question: by asking in the negative, you build the refusal into the sentence and remove any pressure to accept.

Because it is a real question, it comes with a real conversational script. Accepting is almost never a bare はい:

  • ええ、行(い)きましょう。 - yes, let's.
  • いいですね。ぜひ。 - sounds good, I'd love to.

Declining almost never uses いいえ either. The standard refusal trails off and is never completed:

～ませんか · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • すみません、明日(あした)はちょっと… - sorry, tomorrow is a bit…
  • ありがとうございます。でも、その日(ひ)は用事(ようじ)があって… - thank you, but I have something on that day.

The unfinished ちょっと is doing real work. Saying the refusal out loud would be blunt; leaving it hanging lets both sides treat it as understood.

Casually, ませんか becomes the plain negative with a rising tone: 行(い)かない？, 食(た)べない？, 飲(の)みに行(い)かない？ These are extremely common among friends and carry exactly the same softness.

One caution: ～ませんか is also, literally, an ordinary negative question. Out of context, 食(た)べませんか could mean "aren't you eating?" Intonation and situation sort it out, and the invitation reading dominates when the sentence names a shared activity or includes 一緒(いっしょ)に.

～ませんか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Upward, ませんか is already good; いかがですか is the softer noun-based version, and in formal invitations you will meet ～ませんでしょうか and ご一緒(いっしょ)しませんか.

～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ましょう

Rule of thumb - ませんか asks, ましょう assumes. Invite with the first; accept with the second.

週末(しゅうまつ)、テニスをしませんか。
Would you like to play tennis at the weekend?

いいですね、しましょう。
Sounds good - let's.

That two-turn exchange is the natural home of both patterns, and it shows why they are not interchangeable. Opening with ましょう treats the other person's weekend as already spent; replying with ませんか would be asking them a question they have just asked you.

～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ましょう (cont.)

ましょう is fine as an opener only when agreement is safe - when you are already out together, or when you have the standing to direct the group.

～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ましょうか

Rule of thumb - ませんか invites the listener to join you; ましょうか offers your own action, or checks the next step of something already agreed.

明日(あした)、美術館(びじゅつかん)に行(い)きませんか。
How about going to the art museum tomorrow?

切符(きっぷ)は私(わたし)が買(か)っておきましょうか。
Shall I get the tickets in advance?

The first opens a plan; the second handles a detail inside a plan that already exists. And for offers of help the division is strict: 手伝(てつだ)いましょうか offers your hands, while ❌手伝(てつだ)いませんか asks the listener to come and help with something - possibly the opposite of what you meant.

～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ますか

Rule of thumb - ますか asks a question about what the listener will do; ませんか invites. The negative is what turns information-gathering into an invitation.

明日(あした)のパーティーに行(い)きますか。
Are you going to the party tomorrow?

明日(あした)のパーティーに行(い)きませんか。
Would you like to come to the party tomorrow?

The first is neutral enquiry - you want to know her plans, and you may not be going yourself. The second includes you, and asks her to come along.

～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ますか (cont.)

Using ますか where you meant to invite is a common learner slip. It is not rude, it simply does not invite: 食(た)べますか offers information-gathering where 食(た)べませんか offers company. The affirmative question can also feel a touch demanding, because it presupposes that going is on the table.

～ませんか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行(い)くませんか → ✅ 行(い)きませんか
    Built on the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 行(い)きませんですか → ✅ 行(い)きませんか
    ません is already polite. Do not add another です.

  • ❌ 荷物(にもつ)を持(も)ちませんか。（offering to carry it） → ✅ 荷物(にもつ)を持(も)ちましょうか。
    ませんか invites the other person to do the action. For your own offer, use ましょうか.

  • ❌ 「コーヒーを飲(の)みませんか。」「はい、飲(の)みません。」 → ✅ 「ええ、いただきます。」／「ありがとうございます。」
    Do not answer the negative literally. Accepting an invitation is ええ plus a positive verb.

～ませんか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、昼(ひる)ごはんを食(た)べに行(い)きましょう。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、昼(ひる)ごはんを食(た)べに行(い)きませんか。
    Upward, invite rather than announce.

～ませんか · Practice 1

1. 来週(らいしゅう)、一緒(いっしょ)に花見(はなみ)に行(い)____か。

一緒(いっしょ)に花見(はなみ)に行(い)きませんか。

行(い)く → ます-stem 行(い)き → ません + か. The negative question is what makes this an invitation rather than an announcement.

～ませんか · Practice 2

2. 重(おも)い箱(はこ)を運(はこ)んでいる同僚(どうりょう)に：「（手伝(てつだ)いませんか・手伝(てつだ)いましょうか）。」

手伝(てつだ)いましょうか。

You are offering your own help, and ましょうか is the form for that. 手伝(てつだ)いませんか would be inviting your colleague to come and help someone else - the wrong direction entirely.

～ませんか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Would you like to go to the cinema with me on Friday?"

金曜日(きんようび)、一緒(いっしょ)に映画館(えいがかん)に行(い)きませんか。

An open invitation to someone who must be able to refuse - the core use of the pattern. Adding よかったら at the front softens it further.

～ませんか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 明日(あした)、山(やま)に登(のぼ)るませんですか。

✅ 明日(あした)、山(やま)に登(のぼ)りませんか。

Two errors stacked. ません attaches to the ます-stem 登(のぼ)り, not the dictionary form; and ません is already the polite negative, so the extra です has nothing to do.

～ませんか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 今晩(こんばん)、飲(の)みに行(い)きますか。 (b) 今晩(こんばん)、飲(の)みに行(い)きませんか。

(a) asks about the listener's plans. It is a perfectly normal question - you might be checking whether she is joining a group that is already going - but it does not in itself invite her, and it can sound as though you expect an answer either way.

(b) invites. The negative form builds in the possibility of "no", so it puts no weight on the listener, and it implies that you are going too. For asking a colleague out for a drink, (b) is the sentence you want.

～はどうですか

Meaning · how about ~? / how is ~? - putting an option on the table, or asking for an impression

～はどうですか does two related jobs, and Japanese does not distinguish them grammatically:

  • Suggesting - you offer something and ask what the listener makes of it.
    土曜日(どようび)はどうですか。「How about Saturday?」
  • Asking for an evaluation - you ask how something is, or was.
    新(あたら)しい仕事(しごと)はどうですか。「How's the new job?」

～はどうですか · Overview

Underneath, both are the same question: what is your assessment of this? Tense and context decide which one the listener hears.

～はどうですか · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + はどうですか コーヒー → コーヒーはどうですか
Verb (proposing an action) Dictionary + のは + どうですか 歩(ある)く → 歩(ある)いて行(い)くのはどうですか
Verb (advising the listener) た-form + らどうですか 休(やす)む → 休(やす)んだらどうですか
Past (a finished event) ～はどうでしたか 旅行(りょこう) → 旅行(りょこう)はどうでしたか
Politer ～はいかがですか お茶(ちゃ)はいかがですか
Softer, more tentative ～はどうでしょうか 水曜日(すいようび)はどうでしょうか
Casual ～はどう？ これはどう？

～はどうですか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - どう asks how / in what state. It is not どんな, which asks what kind of and must be followed by a noun. ✅映画(えいが)はどうでしたか。 ✅どんな映画(えいが)でしたか。 ❌映画(えいが)はどんなでしたか。

～はどうですか · Examples

コーヒーはどうですか。
How about a coffee?

新(あたら)しい仕事(しごと)はどうですか。
How's the new job?

土曜日(どようび)の午後(ごご)はどうですか。
How about Saturday afternoon?

京都(きょうと)の旅行(りょこう)はどうでしたか。
How was the trip to Kyoto?

体(からだ)の調子(ちょうし)はどうですか。
How are you feeling?

～はどうですか · Examples (cont.)

この色(いろ)はどうですか。もう少(すこ)し明(あか)るいのもありますよ。
How about this colour? We have a slightly brighter one as well.

駅(えき)の近(ちか)くに新(あたら)しいそば屋(や)ができたそうですが、今日(きょう)の昼(ひる)はそこはどうですか。
Apparently a new soba place has opened near the station - how about there for lunch today?

会議(かいぎ)の時間(じかん)ですが、水曜日(すいようび)の午後(ごご)三(さん)時(じ)からというのはどうでしょうか。
About the time for the meeting - how would Wednesday from three in the afternoon be?

～はどうですか · Nuance & register

どうですか is polite and completely ordinary, but it is plain polite - the same level as です／ます and no higher. Two situations expose that.

The first is offering something to a superior or a customer. 部長(ぶちょう)、コーヒーはどうですか is not rude, exactly, but it is the register you would use with a colleague. お茶(ちゃ)はいかがですか is what waiters, hosts and careful employees say, and it costs nothing to learn.

The second is asking someone senior how something is going. 論文(ろんぶん)はどうですか to your supervisor can sound like you are checking up on them. いかがですか, or 進(すす)んでいますか, avoids the edge.

～はどうですか · Nuance & register (cont.)

For proposals, どうですか is genuinely soft, because it asks for an opinion rather than a commitment. It is the standard way to float a time, a place or an option in a conversation, and 水曜日(すいようび)はどうでしょうか is softer still - でしょうか adds a layer of tentativeness that is very common in business email.

Watch the tense. A finished event takes どうでしたか; using どうですか about last night's party is a real mistake, not a stylistic one.

Casually the copula drops: これ、どう？, 味(あじ)はどう？ Among friends this is the default, and it can be blunt in the sense of demanding a verdict - asking どう？ about someone's cooking while they watch you eat is as pointed in Japanese as in English.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～はいかがですか

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different altitude. いかがですか is the polite form of どうですか, and it is the default when offering something to a guest, customer or superior.

デザートはどうですか。
How about dessert?

デザートはいかがですか。
Would you care for a dessert?

いかが is not a different word class or a different construction - everything you can do with どう, you can do with いかが: いかがでしたか, いかがでしょうか. What changes is who you are speaking to.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～はいかがですか (cont.)

The one place どうですか is preferred is when you genuinely want a judgement between equals: この案(あん)はどうですか to a colleague asks for their honest read. いかがですか in the same slot can sound as though you are serving them something.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～ませんか

Rule of thumb - はどうですか proposes an option and asks what you think of it; ませんか invites the person to do something with you.

昼(ひる)ごはんは、そばとカレーとどちらがいいですか。ここはどうですか。
Soba or curry for lunch? How about here?

一緒(いっしょ)に昼(ひる)ごはんを食(た)べませんか。
Would you like to have lunch together?

The second commits you both to an activity; the first only puts a candidate forward inside a plan you are already making. That is why はどうですか is so useful in arranging things - dates, venues, options - while ませんか is what opens the arrangement in the first place.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～ませんか (cont.)

They stack naturally in one conversation: 食(た)べに行(い)きませんか opens it, 金曜日(きんようび)はどうですか settles the detail.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～たらどうですか

Rule of thumb - はどうですか offers an option; たらどうですか tells the listener what they should do. The second is advice, and it can sting.

この薬(くすり)はどうですか。
How about this medicine?

少(すこ)し休(やす)んだらどうですか。
Why don't you take a bit of a rest?

～たらどうですか is built on the conditional た-form and means "why don't you…". Said warmly it is caring; said flatly it implies the listener has failed to think of something obvious, which is why it carries a faint edge of impatience. Upward it is best avoided altogether - ～てはいかがでしょうか is the safe version.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～たらどうですか (cont.)

Casual speech shortens it to ～たら？ - 医者(いしゃ)に行(い)ったら？ - and the trailing, unfinished shape is exactly what makes it sound offhand.

～はどうですか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 昨日(きのう)のパーティーはどうですか。 → ✅ 昨日(きのう)のパーティーはどうでしたか。
    A finished event takes the past form.

  • ❌ 映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)くはどうですか。 → ✅ 映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)くのはどうですか。
    A verb needs the nominaliser の before は.

  • ❌ 新(あたら)しい先生(せんせい)はどんなですか。 → ✅ 新(あたら)しい先生(せんせい)はどうですか。／どんな人(ひと)ですか。
    どんな cannot stand alone; it must attach to a noun.

  • ❌ 社長(しゃちょう)、お飲(の)み物(もの)はどうですか。 → ✅ 社長(しゃちょう)、お飲(の)み物(もの)はいかがですか。
    Offering to someone senior calls for いかが.

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、もっと早(はや)く帰(かえ)ったらどうですか。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、もっと早(はや)くお帰(かえ)りになってはいかがでしょうか。
    Advice aimed upward needs a much softer frame.

～はどうですか · Practice 1

1. つかれた顔(かお)をしていますね。少(すこ)し休(やす)む____どうですか。

少(すこ)し休(やす)むのはどうですか。

A verb cannot be followed directly by は; the nominaliser の turns 休(やす)む into something the topic particle can mark. You could also say 休(やす)んだらどうですか, which is more directly advice.

～はどうですか · Practice 2

2. お客(きゃく)さんに：「お茶(ちゃ)は（どうですか・いかがですか）。」

お茶(ちゃ)はいかがですか。

いかが is the polite counterpart of どう, and offering refreshment to a guest or customer is exactly where it belongs. どうですか is not wrong, just level with a colleague rather than with a customer.

～はどうですか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "How was the exam yesterday?"

昨日(きのう)の試験(しけん)はどうでしたか。

The exam is over, so the question has to be in the past: どうでしたか. どうですか would be asking about an exam that is still going on.

～はどうですか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 新(あたら)しいアパートはどんなですか。

✅ 新(あたら)しいアパートはどうですか。

どんな is an attributive - it has to sit in front of a noun, as in どんなアパートですか. To ask how something is, on its own, the word is どう.

～はどうですか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 明日(あした)はどうですか。 (b) 明日(あした)会(あ)いませんか。

(a) puts a date forward as a candidate. It assumes you are already arranging to meet and asks the other person to evaluate the option - it is about the day, not about whether to meet at all.

(b) is the invitation itself. It asks whether the listener wants to meet you, and it is the sentence that opens the arrangement rather than filling in its details.
