～てください

Meaning · please do ~ - a request, and often an instruction

～てください attaches to the て-form and asks the listener to perform an action. It is polite in form - ください is the imperative of the honorific verb 下くださる - but it is not a soft or deferential way to ask. Teachers, doctors, station staff and forms all use it, and that is the flavour it carries: a clear directive, delivered courteously.

～てください · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) て-form + ください 待まつ → 待まってください
Verb (る-verb) て-form + ください 見みる → 見みてください
Verb (する) して + ください 電話でんわする → 電話でんわしてください
Verb (来くる) 来きて + ください 来くる → 来きてください
Negative request ない-form + で + ください 来くる → 来こないでください
Casual て-form on its own 待まって／待まってね
More polite ～てくださいませんか／～ていただけませんか 待まっていただけませんか

To ask for a thing rather than an action, ください follows a noun with を instead - that pattern has its own page in this book.

～てください · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ください attaches to the て-form, never the dictionary form. Get the て-form right and the rest is automatic: 話はなす → 話はなして, 急いそぐ → 急いそいで, 読よむ → 読よんで, 行いく → 行いって.

～てください · Examples

ちょっと**待まってください**。
Hang on a moment, please.

ここに名前なまえを**書かいてください**。
Please write your name here.

もう一度いちど言いってください。
Could you say that again, please.

どうぞ**座すわってください**。
Please, have a seat.

～てください · Examples (cont.)

駅えきに着ついたら**電話でんわしてください**。
Give me a call when you get to the station.

寒さむいので、ドアを**閉しめてください**。
It's cold, so please shut the door.

来週らいしゅうの会議かいぎの資料しりょうを、金曜日きんようびまでにメールで**送おくってください**。
Please email me the material for next week's meeting by Friday.

申もうし訳わけありませんが、明日あしたは三十分さんじゅっぷんほど早はやく来きてくださいませんか。
I'm terribly sorry, but could you possibly come in half an hour early tomorrow?

～てください · Nuance & register

The single most useful thing to know about ～てください is that it is not the polite form of a request. It is the neutral one. Because it tells rather than asks, it belongs to situations where the speaker has a right to direct: a teacher to a class, a doctor to a patient, an announcement to passengers, a form to whoever is filling it in. Used sideways or upward it can land as bossy - 部長ぶちょう、これをコピーしてください is grammatically impeccable and would raise eyebrows.

The ladder, roughly from most casual to most deferential:

～てください · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 待まって - friends, family. て-form alone.
  • 待まってくれる？／待まってくれない？ - casual, but genuinely asking.
  • 待まってください - the neutral polite directive on this page.
  • 待まってくださいませんか - a real question, so noticeably softer.
  • 待まっていただけませんか／待まっていただけますか - humble; the workplace default upward.
  • お待まちください - honorific, used to customers by staff.

～てください · Nuance & register (cont.)

くださる and いただく describe the same event from opposite ends: くださる is "you kindly give", いただく is "I humbly receive". Both are polite; the いただく versions are the ones you reach for when you want to sound careful.

Small softeners matter more than learners expect. Front the request with すみませんが or 恐おそれ入いりますが, add ちょっと, and finish with ね (気きをつけてくださいね) and the same sentence stops sounding like an order.

Note one friendly use that runs against all of this: with どうぞ, ～てください is an offer, not a command. 食たべてください at a dinner table means "please, help yourself".

～てください · Nuance & register (cont.)

In casual speech the bare て-form does all this work - 見みて！, 貸かして - and ちょうだい (貸かしてちょうだい) is a softer, childish or motherly variant.

～てください · ～てください vs ～ていただけませんか

Rule of thumb - ～てください tells; ～ていただけませんか asks. Upward, and for anything that is a favour rather than a duty, ask.

この漢字かんじの読よみ方かたを**教おしえてください**。
Tell me how to read this kanji.

この漢字かんじの読よみ方かたを**教おしえていただけませんか**。
Would you mind telling me how to read this kanji?

The second is longer for a reason. It is a negative question, which leaves the listener room to say no, and it uses the humble いただく, which frames you as the one receiving a kindness. The pair ～てくださいませんか works the same way with くださる and is a shade less humble.

～てください · ～てください vs ～ていただけませんか (cont.)

A useful test: if a "no" would be perfectly reasonable, use the question form. If you are describing a step in a procedure - 靴くつをぬいでください at the door - ～てください is exactly right and the long version would sound strange.

～てください · ～てください vs the plain て-form

Rule of thumb - drop ください and you have the casual request. Drop it with the wrong person and you have an order.

ちょっと**手伝てつだって**。
Give me a hand a sec.

ちょっと**手伝てつだってください**。
Could you give me a hand, please.

～てください · ～てください vs the plain て-form (cont.)

The bare て-form is everywhere in speech between friends, within a family, and from older to younger. It is not rude in itself - it is simply unmarked for politeness, which means the relationship has to carry it. Adding ね or よ softens it (急いそいでね); adding よ alone can sharpen it.

～てくれる？ and ～てくれない？ sit slightly above the bare form: still casual, but phrased as a question, so they ask rather than tell.

～てください · ～てください vs ～をください

Rule of thumb - ください after a て-form asks for an action; ください after a noun + を asks for a thing.

水みずをください。
Some water, please.

水みずを**持もってきてください**。
Please bring me some water.

～てください · ～てください vs ～をください (cont.)

Both are natural; they differ in what you are asking for. The first hands over an object, the second requests the act of fetching. The mistake to avoid is trying to force a noun into the verb slot - ❌手伝てつだいをしてください is possible but stilted, and ✅手伝てつだってください is what a speaker actually says.

～てください · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 食たべるてください → ✅ 食たべてください
    ください attaches to the て-form, not the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 待まつてください → ✅ 待まってください
    つ-verbs make their て-form with a small っ: 待まつ → 待まって.

  • ❌ 行いかないてください → ✅ 行いかないでください
    The negative request uses ないで, never ないて.

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、これをコピーしてください。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、これをコピーしていただけませんか。
    Grammatical, but you are giving an instruction to someone senior. Ask instead.

～てください · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ ペンをしてください。 → ✅ ペンをください。
    Asking for an object needs no verb at all.

～てください · Practice 1

1. すみません、この字じが読よめません。ゆっくり話はな____。

ゆっくり話はなしてください。

話はなす is a す-verb, so its て-form is 話はなして, and ください attaches directly to that. Adding すみませんが at the front, as the question does, already softens the request.

～てください · Practice 2

2. 社長しゃちょうに：「この書類しょるいを（見みてください・見みていただけませんか）。」

この書類しょるいを**見みていただけませんか**。

Looking over your document is a favour, not part of the president's duties, and he outranks you. The humble question form leaves him room to decline, which is exactly what politeness upward is made of.

～てください · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Please turn off the light when you leave the room."

部屋へやを出でるとき、電気でんきを**消けしてください**。

消けす → 消けして + ください. This is the instructional use of the pattern - a house rule, not a personal favour - so plain ～てください is right.

～てください · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: あぶないですから、そこに立たつてください。

✅ あぶないですから、そこに**立たたないでください**。

Two things are wrong. The て-form of 立たつ is 立たって, not ❌立たつて; and the sense demands a negative - "it's dangerous, so please don't stand there" - which is built with ないで.

～てください · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 窓まどを**開あけてください**。 (b) 窓まどを**開あけてくださいませんか**。

(a) is a plain polite directive. In a classroom, or between people who know each other well, it is entirely normal - but it assumes compliance and does not invite a refusal.

(b) is phrased as a negative question, so it genuinely asks. It is longer, more deferential, and the natural choice when you are imposing on someone, addressing a stranger, or speaking to someone senior to you.

～をください

Meaning · please give me ~ - asking for an object

～をください names a thing and asks for it. It is the sentence you use in a shop, at a ticket window, at a counter, in a restaurant - anywhere the other person is there to hand you something. ください is the imperative of the honorific verb 下くださる "to give (to me)", which is why a form that is literally a command still counts as polite.

～をください · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + を + ください コーヒー → コーヒーをください
Noun + quantity Noun + を + counter + ください りんご → りんごを三みっつください
Quantity (spoken, を dropped) Noun + counter + ください コーヒー二ふたつください
Verb (an action, not a thing) て-form + ください 待まつ → 待まってください
Politer ～をお願ねがいします コーヒーをお願ねがいします
Politer still ～をいただけますか メニューをいただけますか
Casual ～ちょうだい コーヒーちょうだい

～をください · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the thing takes を, never が. ください is a verb of giving, so what is given is its direct object - the opposite of ほしい, which is an adjective and takes が.

～をください · Examples

コーヒーをください。
A coffee, please.

すみません、メニューをください。
Excuse me, could I have a menu.

この赤あかいりんごを五いつつください。
Five of these red apples, please.

切符きっぷを大人おとな二にまい、子こども一いちまいください。
Two adult tickets and one child's, please.

～をください · Examples (cont.)

袋ふくろは要いりませんので、レシートだけください。
I don't need a bag - just the receipt, please.

もう少すこし時間じかんをください。
Please give me a little more time.

すみません、温あたたかいお茶ちゃと、砂糖さとうを少すこしください。
Excuse me - a hot tea, and a little sugar, please.

白しろいシャツの大おおきいサイズがあれば、それをください。
If you have that white shirt in a large, I'll take it.

～をください · Nuance & register

～をください is the customer's sentence. Between a customer and staff the balance of the situation makes it perfectly polite, and it is what Japanese speakers actually say at a counter - no hedging required.

Turn it around and the picture changes. Because ください is an imperative, asking a superior for something with it sounds like an instruction: 部長ぶちょう、ペンをください is the sort of thing that gets noticed. Upward you switch to a question - ペンをお借かりできますか, 資料しりょうをいただけますか - or to お願ねがいします.

～をください · Nuance & register (cont.)

Quantities behave loosely in speech. コーヒーを二ふたつください and コーヒー二ふたつください are both normal, and the second is more common at a counter; what you should not do is mark the number with を as well (❌コーヒーを二ふたつをください). Politeness particles cluster at the front and back instead: すみません、～ください and ～くださいね are both very common.

ちょうだい is the casual replacement - それちょうだい - and reads as childish or warmly familiar depending on the speaker; it is more common from women and from adults talking to children. In writing, ください survives in fixed instructions (ご記入きにゅうください, 少々しょうしょうお待まちください), which are honorific forms rather than this pattern.

～をください · Nuance & register (cont.)

ください is not limited to physical objects. Abstract nouns work fine, and this is where the pattern turns up outside shops: 時間じかんをください, 返事へんじをください, もう一度いちどチャンスをください.

～をください · ～をください vs ～をお願いします

Rule of thumb - ください asks for a thing you can hand over; お願ねがいします asks for that or for a service, and sounds a touch more deferential.

生なまビールをください。
A draught beer, please.

生なまビールをお願ねがいします。
A draught beer, please. (slightly more polite)

～をください · ～をください vs ～をお願いします (cont.)

Where they come apart is scope. お願ねがいします covers requests that have no object at all - 会計かいけいをお願ねがいします "the bill, please", 山田やまださんをお願ねがいします "could I speak to Yamada" - where ください would be wrong or comic. As a rule, if you are asking someone to do something on your behalf, お願ねがいします; if you are asking them to give you something, either works.

～をください · ～をください vs ～てください

Rule of thumb - noun + を + ください for an object; verb て-form + ください for an action.

地図ちずをください。
Could I have a map.

地図ちずを**書かいてください**。
Please draw me a map.

～をください · ～をください vs ～てください (cont.)

They share the word ください and nothing else. English blurs them - "give me a hand" looks like a noun request but is really an action - so the trap is real: ✅手伝てつだってください, not ❌手伝てつだいをください.

When you want an object brought to you rather than simply handed over, the two combine: 水みずを持もってきてください.

～をください · ～をください vs ～がほしいです

Rule of thumb - ほしい describes your inner state; ください performs the request. To a shop assistant, only the second one is a sentence that does anything.

この靴くつの黒くろをください。
I'll take these shoes in black.

新あたらしい靴くつがほしいです。
I want new shoes.

The second is a fine thing to say to a friend and an odd thing to say to a salesperson - you have announced a feeling and left them to guess what to do about it. Note the particles as well: ほしい is an adjective and takes が, while ください is a verb and takes を. Mixing them up (❌これがください) is the single most common error with this pattern.

～をください · Common mistakes

  • ❌ これがください。 → ✅ これをください。
    ください is a verb of giving; the thing given is its object.

  • ❌ （レストランで）お水みずがほしいです。 → ✅ お水みずをください。
    Naming your desire at a server does not place an order.

  • ❌ 手伝てつだいをください。 → ✅ 手伝てつだってください。
    Help is an action, not an object. Use the て-form.

  • ❌ コーヒーを二ふたつをください。 → ✅ コーヒーを二ふたつください。
    One を per object. The counter does not take a particle of its own.

～をください · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 課長かちょう、はんこをください。 → ✅ 課長かちょう、はんこをいただけますか。
    ください is an imperative. Pointed upward it reads as an instruction.

～をください · Practice 1

1. すみません、この本ほん____ください。

この本ほんをください。

ください comes from the verb 下くださる, so the thing you are asking for is its direct object and takes を. The が that goes with ほしい does not transfer.

～をください · Practice 2

2. 店みせで：「そのかばんを（ください・ほしいです）。」

そのかばんをください。

In a shop you are placing a request, and ください is what performs it. ほしいです only reports how you feel, which leaves the assistant to work out what you actually want.

～をください · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Two coffees and one orange juice, please."

コーヒーを二ふたつと、オレンジジュースを一ひとつください。

Counters go between the noun and ください, and only one を per item. In speech the を is often dropped altogether: コーヒー二ふたつとオレンジジュース一ひとつください.

～をください · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: すみません、ちょっと手伝てつだいをください。

✅ すみません、ちょっと**手伝てつだってください**。

What you want is an action, not an object, so it belongs in the て-form pattern. English "give me a hand" hides a verb inside a noun, and that is where the mistake comes from.

～をください · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) タクシーをください。 (b) タクシーをお願ねがいします。

(a) asks for a taxi as though it were an object to be handed across a counter, and it sounds odd for that reason - nobody gives you a taxi.

(b) is what a hotel guest actually says. お願ねがいします asks the listener to arrange something on your behalf, which covers services, phone calls and errands. That wider scope is the main practical reason to learn it alongside ください.

～ないでください

Meaning · please don't do ~ - the negative request

～ないでください is the negative counterpart of ～てください. Take the ない-form, add で, add ください. The で is not decoration: ないで is the negative て-form of a verb, and it does the same joining work elsewhere in the language - 傘かさを持もたないで出でかけた「I went out without an umbrella」.

～ないでください · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ない-form + で + ください 吸すう → 吸すわないでください
Verb (る-verb) ない-form + で + ください 捨すてる → 捨すてないでください
Verb (する) しないで + ください 心配しんぱいする → 心配しんぱいしないでください
Verb (来くる) 来こないで + ください 来くる → 来こないでください
Casual ない-form + で 見みないで／見みないでね
More polite ～ないでいただけませんか 入はいらないでいただけませんか
Notices and signs ご遠慮えんりょください／おやめください 喫煙きつえんはご遠慮えんりょください

～ないでください · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - build the ない-form first, then swap ない for ないで. The ない-form is the hard part: 吸すう → 吸すわない (not ❌吸すあない), する → しない, 来くる → 来こない (read こない, never ❌きない).

Note - ないで on its own is a full form of the verb, not just a piece of this pattern. It joins clauses in the sense without doing (何なにも言いわないで出でて行いった), and it feeds other endings: 忘わすれないでね, 来こないでほしい, 見みないでくれる？

～ないでください · Examples

ここでたばこを**吸すわないでください**。
Please don't smoke here.

まだ**帰かえらないでください**。
Please don't leave yet.

心配しんぱいしないでください。
Please don't worry.

授業じゅぎょう中ちゅうはスマホを**見みないでください**。
Please don't look at your phone during class.

～ないでください · Examples (cont.)

この部屋へやには**入はいらないでください**。
Please don't go into this room.

大切たいせつな日ひですから、明日あしたは**遅おくれないでください**ね。
Tomorrow's an important day, so please don't be late.

すみませんが、この話はなしはまだ誰だれにも**言いわないでください**。
Sorry, but please don't tell anyone about this yet.

恐おそれ入いりますが、館内かんないでは写真しゃしんを**撮とらないでいただけますか**。
I'm very sorry, but could I ask you not to take photographs inside the building?

～ないでください · Nuance & register

Everything true of ～てください is true here: it is polite in form and directive in function. A negative directive is inherently more confronting than a positive one, though, because you are telling someone to stop or not to start - so the softeners matter more.

The usual ones are すみませんが / 恐おそれ入いりますが at the front, ちょっと in the middle, and ね at the end. 大声おおごえを出ださないでくださいね is noticeably gentler than the same sentence without it.

Public notices avoid the bare form, because a sign that tells thousands of people what not to do is at risk of sounding hectoring. Japanese signage prefers ご遠慮えんりょください「we ask that you refrain」, おやめください「please stop」, or a plain noun + 禁止きんし「prohibited」 on the sternest notices. If you learn to read those, you have covered most stations and museums.

～ないでください · Nuance & register (cont.)

Casually, ください drops off and the ない-form does the whole job: 見みないで！, 言いわないで. Add ね to soften, よ to press. ～ないでくれる？ and ～ないでほしい are common alternatives among friends - the first asks, the second reports your wish.

One thing ～ないでください is not: a statement of prohibition. It is a request. If you need to say something is forbidden, ～てはいけません is the pattern, and it carries an authority that this one does not.

～ないでください · ～ないでください vs ～ないでいただけませんか

Rule of thumb - the negative request, then the negative question. Upward, and to strangers, ask rather than tell.

ここに車くるまをとめないでください。
Please don't park here.

ここに車くるまをとめないでいただけませんか。
Would you mind not parking here?

～ないでください · ～ないでください vs ～ないでいただけませんか (cont.)

The second is what a neighbour says to a neighbour; the first is what the sign says. ～ないでくださいませんか sits between them, and the fully honorific ご遠慮えんりょください is the institutional voice - a shop, a train company, a museum.

The relationship decides. Telling a stranger not to do something is delicate in any language, and Japanese solves it by turning the request into a question and letting the listener seem to choose.

～ないでください · ～ないで vs ～なくて

Rule of thumb - Japanese has two negative て-forms. ないで means without doing and is the one used in requests; なくて means and so, because … not.

朝あさごはんを**食たべないで**学校がっこうへ行いきました。
I went to school without eating breakfast.

バスが**来こなくて**、遅おくれました。
The bus didn't come, and so I was late.

The first describes how the going happened; the second gives the reason for being late. Swap them and the sentences stop making sense.

～ないでください · ～ないで vs ～なくて (cont.)

Two consequences worth memorising. First, requests always use ないで - there is no ❌行いかなくてください. Second, なくて is the form that adjectives and nouns take when they are negated and joined (高たかくなくて安心あんしんしました, 雨あめじゃなくてよかった), so ないで is verb-only territory.

～ないでください · ～ないでください vs ～てはいけません

Rule of thumb - ～ないでください asks; ～てはいけません forbids.

教室きょうしつで走はしらないでください。
Please don't run in the classroom.

教室きょうしつで走はしってはいけません。
You must not run in the classroom.

The first is a request, however firmly meant; the second states a rule, and the speaker has to be in a position to state it - a teacher, a parent, a notice. Used by an equal it sounds like a scolding.

～ないでください · ～ないでください vs ～てはいけません (cont.)

There is a third, gentler option that learners rarely meet early enough: ～ないようにしてください「please try not to…」, which frames the same content as advice. 夜よる遅おそく電話でんわしないようにしてください is the version that survives being said to almost anyone.

～ないでください · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 見みないてください → ✅ 見みないでください
    The negative て-form is ないで. There is no form ❌ないて.

  • ❌ 行いかなくてください → ✅ 行いかないでください
    なくて gives a reason; it cannot carry a request.

  • ❌ するないでください → ✅ しないでください
    する is irregular: its ない-form is しない, so the request is しないでください.

  • ❌ たばこを**吸すうないでください** → ✅ たばこを**吸すわないでください**
    ないで attaches to the ない-form, not the dictionary form. For う-verbs ending in う, the ない-form takes わ.

～ないでください · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 来きないでください → ✅ 来こないでください
    *Same kanji, different reading. The ない-form of 来くる is read こない.*

～ないでください · Practice 1

1. ここは図書館としょかんですから、大おおきい声こえで話はな____ください。

大おおきい声こえで話はなさないでください。

話はなす → ない-form 話はなさない → swap ない for ないで. A library is exactly the setting where the plain polite request is appropriate.

～ないでください · Practice 2

2. 窓まどを（開あけなくてください・開あけないでください）。

窓まどを**開あけないでください**。

Japanese has two negative て-forms and only one of them can carry a request. なくて is for causes and consequences - 開あけなくて寒さむかった - so it cannot be followed by ください.

～ないでください · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Please don't come to the office tomorrow."

明日あしたは会社かいしゃに**来こないでください**。

The ない-form of 来くる is 来こない - the kanji stays put and the reading changes to こ. Writing きない is the classic slip.

～ないでください · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 試験しけん中ちゅうは話はなしをするないでください。

✅ 試験しけん中ちゅうは話はなしをしないでください。

する is one of the two irregular verbs. Its ない-form is しない, not ❌するない, so the negative request is しないでください.

～ないでください · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) ここに荷物にもつを置おかないでください。 (b) ここに荷物にもつを置おいてはいけません。

(a) is a request. It is firm and unambiguous, but it is still the speaker asking, and a colleague could say it to another colleague without friction.

(b) states a rule and claims the authority to state it. It is what a member of staff, a teacher or a posted notice would use, and coming from an equal it sounds like being told off.

～ましょう

Meaning · let's - a proposal made on the assumption you already agree

～ましょう is the polite volitional. It turns a verb into "let's do it", and its defining feature is confidence: the speaker takes agreement as read. That makes it the right form for a plan already settled, for a leader moving a group along, and for a public slogan - and the wrong form for a genuine, open invitation, where ～ませんか is what you want.

A second use hides inside the first. With 私わたしが, ～ましょう is not "let's" at all but "I'll do it" - the speaker volunteering.

～ましょう · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + ましょう 行いきます → 行いきましょう
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + ましょう 食たべます → 食たべましょう
Verb (する) し + ましょう します → しましょう
Verb (来くる) き + ましょう 来きます → 来きましょう
Softened into a question ましょう + か 行いきましょうか
Casual equivalent plain volitional 行いく → 行いこう／食たべる → 食たべよう

～ましょう · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ましょう replaces ます, so it builds off the same stem. 飲のみます → 飲のみましょう. It never attaches to the dictionary form: ❌飲のむましょう.

Note - there is no negative ましょう. ❌行いきませんしょう does not exist. To propose not doing something, negate the content instead: 今日きょうは買かうのはやめましょう or 今日きょうは買かわないことにしましょう.

～ましょう · Examples

じゃあ、行いきましょう。
Right - let's go.

一緒いっしょに昼ひるごはんを**食たべましょう**。
Let's have lunch together.

七しち時じに駅えきの前まえで**会あいましょう**。
Let's meet in front of the station at seven.

そろそろ**始はじめましょう**。
Let's get started.

～ましょう · Examples (cont.)

今日きょうはここまでにしましょう。
Let's call it a day there.

はい、私わたしが**運転うんてんしましょう**。
All right - I'll drive.

雨あめが降ふりそうですから、傘かさを持もって**行いきましょう**。
It looks like rain, so let's take umbrellas.

時間じかんがありませんから、細こまかいことは後あとで**話はなしましょう**。
We're out of time, so let's go through the details afterwards.

～ましょう · Nuance & register

ましょう is polite in form but assertive in function. You are announcing what the group is going to do. That is fine when the plan is agreed, when you are the one running the meeting, or when the proposal is obviously welcome (寒さむいですね、中なかに入はいりましょう). It is presumptuous when the other person has not yet agreed to anything, and doubly so upward: 部長ぶちょう、飲のみに行いきましょう helps itself to the boss's evening. 飲のみに行いきませんか or いかがですか leaves the decision where it belongs.

The same assertiveness is why ましょう is the voice of public exhortation. Handwashing posters, road-safety signs and school announcements are full of it: 手てを洗あらいましょう, 気きをつけましょう, 時間じかんを守まもりましょう. Grammatically these are invitations; in practice they are instructions with a smile.

～ましょう · Nuance & register (cont.)

As an answer, ましょう is the standard yes. To 一緒いっしょに行いきませんか you reply ええ、行いきましょう - the invitation was open, and ましょう closes it.

The 私わたしが use is worth practising: 私わたしが持もちましょう「I'll carry that」. It is a shade more take-charge than 持もちましょうか, which asks first, and noticeably more formal than 持もちます. In service Japanese it is often replaced by the humble お持もちしましょう.

Casually, the whole thing collapses to the plain volitional: 行いこう, 食たべよう, しよう, 来こよう. Between friends ましょう sounds oddly ceremonious, like proposing a motion.

～ましょう · ～ましょう vs ～ませんか

Rule of thumb - ましょう assumes you'll agree; ませんか asks whether you will. If the other person could reasonably say no, use ませんか.

映画えいがを**見みに行いきましょう**。
Let's go and see a film.

映画えいがを**見みに行いきませんか**。
Would you like to go and see a film?

The first works when you have already decided to spend the afternoon together and are choosing what to do. The second works when you are asking someone out - it is a question, it is negative, and both of those give the listener room.

～ましょう · ～ましょう vs ～ませんか (cont.)

This is why the pair so often appears in sequence: one person invites with ませんか, the other accepts with ましょう. Reversing them is the classic learner error, and it is the difference between "would you like to?" and "we're going".

～ましょう · ～ましょう vs ～ましょうか

Rule of thumb - the か turns a decision into a question. ましょう announces; ましょうか checks, or offers to do something for the listener.

そろそろ**出でましょう**。
Let's head off.

そろそろ**出でましょうか**。
Shall we head off?

～ましょう · ～ましょう vs ～ましょうか (cont.)

Adding か costs nothing and softens a great deal, so ましょうか is the safer default whenever you are not certain the other person is ready. It also unlocks a sense ましょう does not have: with the speaker as the doer, it becomes an offer - 荷物にもつを持もちましょうか「shall I take your bag?」 where 荷物にもつを持もちましょう is you announcing that you will.

～ましょう · ～ましょう vs the plain volitional

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different politeness. ましょう is the ます-level form; 行いこう and 食たべよう are the plain-level ones.

明日あしたは八はち時じに**集あつまりましょう**。
Let's gather at eight tomorrow.

おなかすいた。なんか**食たべよう**。
I'm hungry. Let's get something to eat.

～ましょう · ～ましょう vs the plain volitional (cont.)

The plain forms are built like this: う-verbs change the final -u to -ou (行いく → 行いこう, 飲のむ → 飲のもう), る-verbs swap る for よう (見みる → 見みよう), and the two irregulars are する → しよう, 来くる → 来こよう.

Register maps straight across: use the plain volitional with friends, family and anyone you would speak to in plain form, and ましょう everywhere you are using ます. Note that the plain form can also take か - 行いこうか is the casual equivalent of 行いきましょうか.

～ましょう · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 飲のむましょう → ✅ 飲のみましょう
    ましょう replaces ます on the stem, not the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 行いきましょうですか → ✅ 行いきましょうか
    ましょう already contains the copula. Just add か.

  • ❌ 課長かちょう、一緒いっしょに帰かえりましょう。 → ✅ 課長かちょう、一緒いっしょに帰かえりませんか。
    Deciding a superior's movements for them. Ask instead of proposing.

  • ❌ 明日あしたは雨あめが降ふりましょう。 → ✅ 明日あしたは雨あめが降ふるでしょう。
    ましょう is a proposal, not a prediction. Guessing about the weather is でしょう.

～ましょう · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 行いきませんしょう。 → ✅ 行いくのはやめましょう。
    There is no negative ましょう; negate the content instead.

～ましょう · Practice 1

1. つかれましたね。ちょっと休やす____。

ちょっと休やすみましょう。

休やすむ → ます-stem 休やすみ → + ましょう. Both people are already tired, so agreement is safe to assume and the assertive form is natural here.

～ましょう · Practice 2

2. 初はじめて話はなす人ひとに：「今度こんどうちに（来きましょう・来きませんか）。」

今度こんどうちに**来きませんか**。

You are inviting someone you have only just met, so they must be free to decline. ましょう would announce the visit as settled, which is presumptuous with a stranger.

～ましょう · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "It's cold, so let's go into the café."

寒さむいですから、喫茶店きっさてんに**入はいりましょう**。

Here the assumption of agreement is entirely safe - you are both cold - which is exactly the situation ましょう is built for.

～ましょう · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 来週らいしゅうの日曜日にちようび、テニスをするましょうか。

✅ 来週らいしゅうの日曜日にちようび、テニスをしましょうか。

ましょう builds on the ます-stem, and the ます-form of する is します, so the stem is し. ❌するましょう doubles up the verb.

～ましょう · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 会議かいぎを始はじめましょう。 (b) 会議かいぎを始はじめましょうか。

(a) starts the meeting. The speaker has the floor, everyone is present, and the sentence is really an announcement dressed as a proposal - the chair's line.

(b) checks first. It reads as "shall we make a start?", which suits someone who is not chairing, or a chair who can see that two people are still on the phone. The か costs one syllable and buys a lot of room.

～ましょうか

Meaning · shall I? / shall we? - offering to do something, or proposing tentatively

～ましょうか is ～ましょう with a question mark built in, and that small か does two different jobs.

  • Shall I? - you offer to do something for the listener.
    窓まどを開あけましょうか。
  • Shall we? - you propose something for both of you, but check first instead of
    announcing. そろそろ帰かえりましょうか。

～ましょうか · Overview

Which one a listener hears is decided by the situation, not the grammar: if the action benefits them and only you can do it, it is an offer; if it is something you would do together, it is a proposal.

～ましょうか · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + ましょうか 持もちます → 持もちましょうか
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + ましょうか 開あけます → 開あけましょうか
Verb (する) し + ましょうか します → しましょうか
Verb (来くる) き + ましょうか 来きます → 来きましょうか
Humble offer (service Japanese) お + ます-stem + しましょうか お持もちしましょうか
Casual plain volitional + か 開あけようか／手伝てつだおうか

～ましょうか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - mark the doer and the ambiguity disappears. 私わたしが運はこびましょうか is unmistakably an offer; 一緒いっしょに運はこびましょうか is unmistakably a proposal.

～ましょうか · Examples

窓まどを**開あけましょうか**。
Shall I open the window?

手伝てつだいましょうか。
Shall I give you a hand?

何なにを**飲のみましょうか**。
What shall we have to drink?

暑あついですね。エアコンをつけましょうか。
It's hot, isn't it. Shall I put the air conditioning on?

～ましょうか · Examples (cont.)

どこで**会あいましょうか**。
Where shall we meet?

写真しゃしんを**撮とりましょうか**。
Shall I take a photo for you?

道みちが混こんでいますね。次つぎの角かどを右みぎに**曲まがりましょうか**。
The traffic's heavy. Shall we turn right at the next corner?

会議かいぎの資料しりょう、私わたしが人数分にんずうぶんコピーしておきましょうか。
Shall I run off enough copies of the meeting handouts for everyone?

～ましょうか · Nuance & register

ましょうか is the workhorse of everyday helpfulness. It is what you say when you notice someone struggling with a door, a suitcase, a printer. It is polite without being stiff, it commits you to nothing until they answer, and - crucially - it does not put the listener under obligation, because you are volunteering rather than asking.

Learn the replies as a set, because you will hear them constantly:

  • お願ねがいします - yes, please.
  • すみません、ありがとうございます - yes, with thanks and apology at once.
  • あ、大丈夫だいじょうぶです - no, I'm fine. The everyday polite refusal.
  • いえ、結構けっこうです - no thank you. Firmer, and cooler.

～ましょうか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Upward, ましょうか is acceptable but plain. In service Japanese and towards superiors the humble frame is standard: お持もちしましょうか, お調しらべしましょうか, ご案内あんないしましょうか. These raise the listener by lowering the action, and they are what hotel staff and careful employees use.

Casually the whole thing shortens to the plain volitional plus か: 開あけようか, 手伝てつだおうか, 買かってこようか. Note that in casual speech the か is often dropped entirely and carried by intonation - 手伝てつだおう？

One warning about intonation and reading: ましょうか is a question, and it takes a rising tone. Written down, it is often followed by 。 rather than ？ in Japanese prose, which does not make it any less of a question.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ましょう

Rule of thumb - ましょう announces; ましょうか checks. The か hands the decision back to the listener.

電気でんきをつけましょう。
Let's put the light on.

電気でんきをつけましょうか。
Shall I put the light on?

The first version assumes; the second asks. That is why the か version doubles as an offer and the bare version does not: an offer only makes sense if the other person can decline it.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ましょう (cont.)

Between the two, ましょうか is almost always the safer choice with people you do not know well. Use bare ましょう when the plan is already agreed and you are simply moving it along - 行いきましょう as you both stand up.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ませんか

Rule of thumb - both ask, but ませんか invites the listener to join you; ましょうか proposes a next step in something already underway, or offers to act on their behalf.

今度こんどの土曜日どようび、山やまに**登のぼりませんか**。
How about climbing a mountain this Saturday?

雨あめが降ふってきましたね。下おりましょうか。
It's started raining. Shall we head back down?

ませんか opens something new and can be refused without awkwardness - it is the form for asking someone out. ましょうか takes the shared activity as given and asks about the next move within it.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ませんか (cont.)

There is also a distribution difference that catches learners. For an offer, ませんか does not work: ❌荷物にもつを持もちませんか sounds like you are inviting the other person to carry luggage, not offering to carry theirs. Offers are ましょうか territory.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～てあげましょうか

Rule of thumb - plain ましょうか for offers. ～てあげましょうか announces that you are doing the listener a kindness, and that is not yours to announce.

駅えきまで車くるまで**送おくりましょうか**。
Shall I drive you to the station?

駅えきまで車くるまで**送おくってあげましょうか**。
Shall I do you the favour of driving you to the station?

The second is grammatical and is used - among close friends, or lightly to a child - but あげる explicitly frames the act as a benefit conferred, and offering that to an equal or a superior sounds self-congratulatory. The neutral offer leaves the gratitude to the other person.

～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～てあげましょうか (cont.)

The polite move goes the other way, towards humility: お送おくりしましょうか lowers your own action, and is the form used in service and business. ～てあげる and お～する are the two ends of the same axis, and the safe default at N5 is plain ましょうか in the middle.

～ましょうか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 開あけるましょうか → ✅ 開あけましょうか
    Built on the ます-stem: 開あけます → 開あけ + ましょうか.

  • ❌ 荷物にもつを**持もちませんか**。（as an offer） → ✅ 荷物にもつを**持もちましょうか**。
    ませんか invites the listener to do it. To offer your own help, use ましょうか.

  • ❌ 窓まどを**開あけましょうか**。（meaning "please open the window"） → ✅ 窓まどを**開あけてください**。
    ましょうか puts the action on you, not on the listener. It can never be a request.

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、コピーしてあげましょうか。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、コピーしましょうか。／お手伝てつだいしましょうか。
    てあげる advertises your own kindness. Upward, drop it or go humble.

～ましょうか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 行いきましょうか、ですか。 → ✅ 行いきましょうか。
    ましょうか is already a complete question.

～ましょうか · Practice 1

1. 重おもそうですね。私わたしが持も____。

私わたしが持もちましょうか。

持もつ → ます-stem 持もち → + ましょうか. The 私わたしが makes it unambiguously an offer rather than a proposal that you both carry it.

～ましょうか · Practice 2

2. 友ともだちが寒さむそうです。（エアコンを消けしましょうか・エアコンを消けしませんか）

エアコンを**消けしましょうか**。

You are offering to do something for your friend's benefit, and that is exactly what ましょうか is for. 消けしませんか would be inviting them to switch it off, which is not what you mean.

～ましょうか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Shall we decide the date now?"

今いま、日ひにちを**決きめましょうか**。

A joint action within a conversation already under way - the "shall we?" sense. Plain 決きめましょう would work too, but it announces the decision instead of checking that the other person is ready.

～ましょうか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: すみません、この字じが読よめません。読よみましょうか。

✅ すみません、この字じが読よめません。読よんでいただけませんか。

The speaker wants the listener to read it, but ましょうか offers the speaker's own action - as written, it says "shall I read it?", which makes no sense after "I can't read it". A request needs the ください or いただけませんか family.

～ましょうか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) お茶ちゃを**入いれましょうか**。 (b) お茶ちゃを**入いれてあげましょうか**。

(a) is a plain, warm offer to make tea. It is the version that works with a guest, a colleague or a boss, because it says nothing about who is doing whom a favour.

(b) adds あげる, which explicitly marks the tea as a kindness you are bestowing. Between close friends it can be playful; to anyone senior it sounds like you are inviting thanks in advance. The humble お入いれしましょうか goes the opposite way and is the safest of the three in a workplace.

～ませんか

Meaning · won't you…? how about it? - a genuine, open invitation

～ませんか invites. It is built as a negative question, and that is the whole point: a negative question presupposes nothing, so it leaves the listener free to decline without embarrassment. Where ～ましょう announces a plan, ～ませんか asks whether you would like one - which is why it is the polite way to ask someone to do something with you.

～ませんか · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + ませんか 行いきます → 行いきませんか
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + ませんか 食たべます → 食たべませんか
Verb (する) し + ませんか します → しませんか
Verb (来くる) き + ませんか 来きます → 来きませんか
Casual ない-form, rising tone 行いかない？／食たべない？
Politer ～ませんでしょうか いらっしゃいませんでしょうか
Accepting ええ、～ましょう ええ、行いきましょう

～ませんか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ませんか is just the polite negative ません plus the question particle か, so it is built on the ます-stem like everything else in that family: 話はなします → 話はなしません → 話はなしませんか.

～ませんか · Examples

一緒いっしょにお茶ちゃを**飲のみませんか**。
Would you like to have a cup of tea with me?

今度こんどの日曜日にちようび、買かい物ものに**行いきませんか**。
How about going shopping this Sunday?

少すこし**休やすみませんか**。
Shall we take a short break?

よかったら、夕ゆうごはんを一緒いっしょに**食たべませんか**。
If you're free, would you like to have dinner together?

～ませんか · Examples (cont.)

この本ほん、よかったら**読よんでみませんか**。
Would you like to give this book a read?

来週らいしゅうの土曜日どようび、うちでバーベキューをしますが、来きませんか。
We're having a barbecue at ours next Saturday - do you want to come?

来月らいげつから朝あさの勉強べんきょう会かいを始はじめます。田中たなかさんも**参加さんかしませんか**。
We're starting a morning study group next month. Would you like to join, Tanaka?

もしお時間じかんがあれば、駅えきの近ちかくにできた美術館びじゅつかんに一緒いっしょに**行いってみませんか**。
If you have time, would you like to go with me to the new museum near the station?

～ませんか · Nuance & register

ませんか is the polite invitation, full stop. It works upward, sideways and to strangers, and it is the form to reach for whenever the answer could genuinely be no. The politeness comes from the shape of the question: by asking in the negative, you build the refusal into the sentence and remove any pressure to accept.

Because it is a real question, it comes with a real conversational script. Accepting is almost never a bare はい:

  • ええ、行いきましょう。 - yes, let's.
  • いいですね。ぜひ。 - sounds good, I'd love to.

Declining almost never uses いいえ either. The standard refusal trails off and is never completed:

～ませんか · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • すみません、明日あしたはちょっと… - sorry, tomorrow is a bit…
  • ありがとうございます。でも、その日ひは用事ようじがあって… - thank you, but I have something on that day.

The unfinished ちょっと is doing real work. Saying the refusal out loud would be blunt; leaving it hanging lets both sides treat it as understood.

Casually, ませんか becomes the plain negative with a rising tone: 行いかない？, 食たべない？, 飲のみに行いかない？ These are extremely common among friends and carry exactly the same softness.

～ませんか · Nuance & register (cont.)

One caution: ～ませんか is also, literally, an ordinary negative question. Out of context, 食たべませんか could mean "aren't you eating?" Intonation and situation sort it out, and the invitation reading dominates when the sentence names a shared activity or includes 一緒いっしょに.

Upward, ませんか is already good; いかがですか is the softer noun-based version, and in formal invitations you will meet ～ませんでしょうか and ご一緒いっしょしませんか.

～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ましょう

Rule of thumb - ませんか asks, ましょう assumes. Invite with the first; accept with the second.

週末しゅうまつ、テニスをしませんか。
Would you like to play tennis at the weekend?

いいですね、しましょう。
Sounds good - let's.

That two-turn exchange is the natural home of both patterns, and it shows why they are not interchangeable. Opening with ましょう treats the other person's weekend as already spent; replying with ませんか would be asking them a question they have just asked you.

～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ましょう (cont.)

ましょう is fine as an opener only when agreement is safe - when you are already out together, or when you have the standing to direct the group.

～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ましょうか

Rule of thumb - ませんか invites the listener to join you; ましょうか offers your own action, or checks the next step of something already agreed.

明日あした、美術館びじゅつかんに**行いきませんか**。
How about going to the art museum tomorrow?

切符きっぷは私わたしが**買かっておきましょうか**。
Shall I get the tickets in advance?

～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ましょうか (cont.)

The first opens a plan; the second handles a detail inside a plan that already exists. And for offers of help the division is strict: 手伝てつだいましょうか offers your hands, while ❌手伝てつだいませんか asks the listener to come and help with something - possibly the opposite of what you meant.

～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ますか

Rule of thumb - ますか asks a question about what the listener will do; ませんか invites. The negative is what turns information-gathering into an invitation.

明日あしたのパーティーに**行いきますか**。
Are you going to the party tomorrow?

明日あしたのパーティーに**行いきませんか**。
Would you like to come to the party tomorrow?

The first is neutral enquiry - you want to know her plans, and you may not be going yourself. The second includes you, and asks her to come along.

～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ますか (cont.)

Using ますか where you meant to invite is a common learner slip. It is not rude, it simply does not invite: 食たべますか offers information-gathering where 食たべませんか offers company. The affirmative question can also feel a touch demanding, because it presupposes that going is on the table.

～ませんか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行いくませんか → ✅ 行いきませんか
    Built on the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 行いきませんですか → ✅ 行いきませんか
    ません is already polite. Do not add another です.

  • ❌ 荷物にもつを**持もちませんか**。（offering to carry it） → ✅ 荷物にもつを**持もちましょうか**。
    ませんか invites the other person to do the action. For your own offer, use ましょうか.

  • ❌ 「コーヒーを飲のみませんか。」「はい、飲のみません。」 → ✅ 「ええ、いただきます。」／「ありがとうございます。」
    Do not answer the negative literally. Accepting an invitation is ええ plus a positive verb.

～ませんか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、昼ひるごはんを食たべに**行いきましょう**。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、昼ひるごはんを食たべに**行いきませんか**。
    Upward, invite rather than announce.

～ませんか · Practice 1

1. 来週らいしゅう、一緒いっしょに花見はなみに行い____か。

一緒いっしょに花見はなみに行いきませんか。

行いく → ます-stem 行いき → ません + か. The negative question is what makes this an invitation rather than an announcement.

～ませんか · Practice 2

2. 重おもい箱はこを運はこんでいる同僚どうりょうに：「（手伝てつだいませんか・手伝てつだいましょうか）。」

手伝てつだいましょうか。

You are offering your own help, and ましょうか is the form for that. 手伝てつだいませんか would be inviting your colleague to come and help someone else - the wrong direction entirely.

～ませんか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Would you like to go to the cinema with me on Friday?"

金曜日きんようび、一緒いっしょに映画館えいがかんに**行いきませんか**。

An open invitation to someone who must be able to refuse - the core use of the pattern. Adding よかったら at the front softens it further.

～ませんか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 明日あした、山やまに登のぼるませんですか。

✅ 明日あした、山やまに**登のぼりませんか**。

Two errors stacked. ません attaches to the ます-stem 登のぼり, not the dictionary form; and ません is already the polite negative, so the extra です has nothing to do.

～ませんか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 今晩こんばん、飲のみに**行いきますか**。 (b) 今晩こんばん、飲のみに**行いきませんか**。

(a) asks about the listener's plans. It is a perfectly normal question - you might be checking whether she is joining a group that is already going - but it does not in itself invite her, and it can sound as though you expect an answer either way.

(b) invites. The negative form builds in the possibility of "no", so it puts no weight on the listener, and it implies that you are going too. For asking a colleague out for a drink, (b) is the sentence you want.

～はどうですか

Meaning · how about ~? / how is ~? - putting an option on the table, or asking for an impression

～はどうですか does two related jobs, and Japanese does not distinguish them grammatically:

  • Suggesting - you offer something and ask what the listener makes of it.
    土曜日どようびはどうですか。「How about Saturday?」
  • Asking for an evaluation - you ask how something is, or was.
    新あたらしい仕事しごとはどうですか。「How's the new job?」

～はどうですか · Overview

Underneath, both are the same question: what is your assessment of this? Tense and context decide which one the listener hears.

～はどうですか · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + はどうですか コーヒー → コーヒーはどうですか
Verb (proposing an action) Dictionary + のは + どうですか 歩あるく → 歩あるいて行いくのはどうですか
Verb (advising the listener) た-form + らどうですか 休やすむ → 休やすんだらどうですか
Past (a finished event) ～はどうでしたか 旅行りょこう → 旅行りょこうはどうでしたか
Politer ～はいかがですか お茶ちゃはいかがですか
Softer, more tentative ～はどうでしょうか 水曜日すいようびはどうでしょうか
Casual ～はどう？ これはどう？

～はどうですか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - どう asks how / in what state. It is not どんな, which asks what kind of and must be followed by a noun. ✅映画えいがはどうでしたか。 ✅どんな映画えいがでしたか。 ❌映画えいがはどんなでしたか。

～はどうですか · Examples

コーヒーはどうですか。
How about a coffee?

新あたらしい仕事しごとはどうですか。
How's the new job?

土曜日どようびの午後ごごはどうですか。
How about Saturday afternoon?

京都きょうとの旅行りょこうはどうでしたか。
How was the trip to Kyoto?

～はどうですか · Examples (cont.)

体からだの調子ちょうしはどうですか。
How are you feeling?

この色いろはどうですか。もう少すこし明あかるいのもありますよ。
How about this colour? We have a slightly brighter one as well.

駅えきの近ちかくに新あたらしいそば屋やができたそうですが、今日きょうの昼ひるはそこはどうですか。
Apparently a new soba place has opened near the station - how about there for lunch today?

会議かいぎの時間じかんですが、水曜日すいようびの午後ごご三さん時じからというのはどうでしょうか。
About the time for the meeting - how would Wednesday from three in the afternoon be?

～はどうですか · Nuance & register

どうですか is polite and completely ordinary, but it is plain polite - the same level as です／ます and no higher. Two situations expose that.

The first is offering something to a superior or a customer. 部長ぶちょう、コーヒーはどうですか is not rude, exactly, but it is the register you would use with a colleague. お茶ちゃはいかがですか is what waiters, hosts and careful employees say, and it costs nothing to learn.

The second is asking someone senior how something is going. 論文ろんぶんはどうですか to your supervisor can sound like you are checking up on them. いかがですか, or 進すすんでいますか, avoids the edge.

～はどうですか · Nuance & register (cont.)

For proposals, どうですか is genuinely soft, because it asks for an opinion rather than a commitment. It is the standard way to float a time, a place or an option in a conversation, and 水曜日すいようびはどうでしょうか is softer still - でしょうか adds a layer of tentativeness that is very common in business email.

Watch the tense. A finished event takes どうでしたか; using どうですか about last night's party is a real mistake, not a stylistic one.

～はどうですか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Casually the copula drops: これ、どう？, 味あじはどう？ Among friends this is the default, and it can be blunt in the sense of demanding a verdict - asking どう？ about someone's cooking while they watch you eat is as pointed in Japanese as in English.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～はいかがですか

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different altitude. いかがですか is the polite form of どうですか, and it is the default when offering something to a guest, customer or superior.

デザートはどうですか。
How about dessert?

デザートはいかがですか。
Would you care for a dessert?

いかが is not a different word class or a different construction - everything you can do with どう, you can do with いかが: いかがでしたか, いかがでしょうか. What changes is who you are speaking to.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～はいかがですか (cont.)

The one place どうですか is preferred is when you genuinely want a judgement between equals: この案あんはどうですか to a colleague asks for their honest read. いかがですか in the same slot can sound as though you are serving them something.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～ませんか

Rule of thumb - はどうですか proposes an option and asks what you think of it; ませんか invites the person to do something with you.

昼ひるごはんは、そばとカレーとどちらがいいですか。ここはどうですか。
Soba or curry for lunch? How about here?

一緒いっしょに昼ひるごはんを**食たべませんか**。
Would you like to have lunch together?

The second commits you both to an activity; the first only puts a candidate forward inside a plan you are already making. That is why はどうですか is so useful in arranging things - dates, venues, options - while ませんか is what opens the arrangement in the first place.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～ませんか (cont.)

They stack naturally in one conversation: 食たべに行いきませんか opens it, 金曜日きんようびはどうですか settles the detail.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～たらどうですか

Rule of thumb - はどうですか offers an option; たらどうですか tells the listener what they should do. The second is advice, and it can sting.

この薬くすりはどうですか。
How about this medicine?

少すこし**休やすんだらどうですか**。
Why don't you take a bit of a rest?

～たらどうですか is built on the conditional た-form and means "why don't you…". Said warmly it is caring; said flatly it implies the listener has failed to think of something obvious, which is why it carries a faint edge of impatience. Upward it is best avoided altogether - ～てはいかがでしょうか is the safe version.

～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～たらどうですか (cont.)

Casual speech shortens it to ～たら？ - 医者いしゃに行いったら？ - and the trailing, unfinished shape is exactly what makes it sound offhand.

～はどうですか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 昨日きのうのパーティーはどうですか。 → ✅ 昨日きのうのパーティーはどうでしたか。
    A finished event takes the past form.

  • ❌ 映画えいがを見みに行いくはどうですか。 → ✅ 映画えいがを見みに行いくのはどうですか。
    A verb needs the nominaliser の before は.

  • ❌ 新あたらしい先生せんせいはどんなですか。 → ✅ 新あたらしい先生せんせいはどうですか。／どんな人ひとですか。
    どんな cannot stand alone; it must attach to a noun.

  • ❌ 社長しゃちょう、お飲のみ物ものはどうですか。 → ✅ 社長しゃちょう、お飲のみ物ものはいかがですか。
    Offering to someone senior calls for いかが.

～はどうですか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、もっと早はやく**帰かえったらどうですか**。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、もっと早はやくお帰かえりになってはいかがでしょうか。
    Advice aimed upward needs a much softer frame.

～はどうですか · Practice 1

1. つかれた顔かおをしていますね。少すこし休やすむ____どうですか。

少すこし休やすむのはどうですか。

A verb cannot be followed directly by は; the nominaliser の turns 休やすむ into something the topic particle can mark. You could also say 休やすんだらどうですか, which is more directly advice.

～はどうですか · Practice 2

2. お客きゃくさんに：「お茶ちゃは（どうですか・いかがですか）。」

お茶ちゃはいかがですか。

いかが is the polite counterpart of どう, and offering refreshment to a guest or customer is exactly where it belongs. どうですか is not wrong, just level with a colleague rather than with a customer.

～はどうですか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "How was the exam yesterday?"

昨日きのうの試験しけんはどうでしたか。

The exam is over, so the question has to be in the past: どうでしたか. どうですか would be asking about an exam that is still going on.

～はどうですか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 新あたらしいアパートはどんなですか。

✅ 新あたらしいアパートはどうですか。

どんな is an attributive - it has to sit in front of a noun, as in どんなアパートですか. To ask how something is, on its own, the word is どう.

～はどうですか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 明日あしたはどうですか。 (b) 明日あした会あいませんか。

(a) puts a date forward as a candidate. It assumes you are already arranging to meet and asks the other person to evaluate the option - it is about the day, not about whether to meet at all.

(b) is the invitation itself. It asks whether the listener wants to meet you, and it is the sentence that opens the arrangement rather than filling in its details.
