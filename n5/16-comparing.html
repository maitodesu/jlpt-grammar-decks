N5 - Comparing

  • いちばん
  • ～のなかで～がいちばん
  • ～より～のほうが～

いちばん

Meaning · the most; the best; number one

Japanese adjectives have no -est ending. いちばん supplies it: put this one word in front of an adjective and it becomes a superlative. Literally "number one", and it is written either as いちばん or with the kanji 一番(いちばん).

いちばん · Usage

いちばん is an adverb, so it goes directly in front of the word it makes superlative - an adjective, a な-adjective, or an adverb - and nothing comes between them.

  • Before an い-adjective: いちばん高(たか)い, いちばん新(あたら)しい
  • Before a な-adjective: いちばん静(しず)か（な）, いちばん便利(べんり)（な）
  • Before an adverb: いちばん早(はや)く来(き)ました, "arrived earliest"
  • Before 好(す)き, which behaves as a な-adjective:
    いちばん好(す)きな色(いろ)

It can also stand alone as a noun meaning "the top one" - 日本(にほん)で一番(いちばん)になりたい, "I want to be number one in Japan" - and it feeds the ordinal 一番目(いちばんめ), "the first".

いちばん · Usage (cont.)

The set of things being compared is usually left unsaid and understood from context. When you do want to state it explicitly, Japanese wraps いちばん in the frame ～の中(なか)で～がいちばん…, which has its own page.

いちばん · Examples

この店(みせ)のケーキがいちばんおいしいです。
The cake at this shop is the best.

私(わたし)は夏(なつ)がいちばん好(す)きです。
Summer is my favourite season.

駅(えき)までは、この道(みち)がいちばん近(ちか)いですよ。
This road is the shortest way to the station.

試験(しけん)の前(まえ)は、よく寝(ね)るのがいちばん大切(たいせつ)だと思(おも)います。
Before an exam, I think sleeping well is the most important thing of all.

いちばん · Nuance & register

Register-neutral and extremely frequent in ordinary speech. Nothing about it marks age, gender or formality.

In kanji it is 一番(いちばん), and both spellings are normal; textbooks and signage tend to use the kanji, casual writing the kana. The reading never changes.

The formal written equivalent is 最(もっと)も. A newspaper writes 最(もっと)も高(たか)い山(やま); a person talking to you says いちばん高(たか)い山(やま). Using 最(もっと)も in conversation sounds like you are reading from a report.

Do not confuse いちばん with もっと. もっと means "more" - a comparative, not a superlative - so もっと安(やす)い店(みせ) is "a cheaper shop" while いちばん安(やす)い店(みせ) is "the cheapest shop". The two are one syllable apart and constantly mixed up.

いちばん · Nuance & register (cont.)

Spoken Japanese also uses いちばん loosely as a rough intensifier of preference - 「やっぱりこれがいちばん」, "this really is the best" - where no real ranking has been carried out. That usage is casual and warm, and perfectly standard.

いちばん · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この山(やま)は高(たか)いいちばんです。 → ✅ この山(やま)がいちばん高(たか)いです。
    いちばん goes in front of the adjective, never after it. Word order carries the whole meaning here.

  • ❌ 犬(いぬ)よりいちばん好(す)きです。 → ✅ 犬(いぬ)より猫(ねこ)の方(ほう)が好(す)きです。
    より compares exactly two things, and a superlative needs three or more. The two patterns cannot be combined in one sentence.

  • ❌ いちばんの好(す)きな食(た)べ物(もの) → ✅ いちばん好(す)きな食(た)べ物(もの)
    No の between いちばん and the adjective. いちばん is an adverb here, not a noun.

  • ❌ この部屋(へや)がいちばん静(しず)かなです。 → ✅ この部屋(へや)がいちばん静(しず)かです。
    The な of a な-adjective appears only before a noun. いちばん changes nothing about how the adjective itself is conjugated.

いちばん · Practice 1

1. クラスで山田(やまだ)さんが____足(あし)が速(はや)いです。

クラスで山田(やまだ)さんがいちばん足(あし)が速(はや)いです。

いちばん marks the top of the group. It sits in front of the part being ranked, and the person holding that rank is marked with が, not は.

いちばん · Practice 2

2. Find and fix the error: この辞書(じしょ)はいちばんの便利(べんり)です。

✅ この辞書(じしょ)が**いちばん便利(べんり)**です。

Delete the の: いちばん attaches straight onto the adjective. Changing は to が is also natural, because picking out the top item of a set is exactly the job が does.

いちばん · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) もっと大(おお)きいかばんがほしいです。 (b) いちばん大(おお)きいかばんがほしいです。

(a) is a comparative: the speaker has seen a bag and wants a bigger one than that. No ranking of the whole shop is implied.

(b) is a superlative: out of everything available, the speaker wants the single biggest one. もっと moves you one step up the scale; いちばん takes you to the top of it.

～のなかで～がいちばん

Meaning · of all ～, ～ is the most ～ - naming the top of a stated group

This is the frame that makes a superlative explicit. The first slot names the field of comparison, the second names the winner. Japanese marks that winner with が, never は, because the sentence exists precisely to single one item out.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Formation

Two halves that go together: ～の中(なか)で sets the field, ～がいちばん + adjective announces the winner.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Group noun Noun + の中(なか)で クラス → クラスの中(なか)で
Category noun Noun + の中(なか)で スポーツ → スポーツの中(なか)で
Listed items A と B と C + の中(なか)で 赤(あか)と青(あお)と黒(くろ)の中(なか)で
Place or period Noun + で（no 中(なか)） 世界(せかい)で／一年(いちねん)で
Winner Noun + が + いちばん + adjective 夏(なつ)がいちばん暑(あつ)いです
Question (people) だれが いちばん …か だれがいちばん強(つよ)いですか
Question (closed set) どれが いちばん …か どれがいちばん安(やす)いですか
Question (place / time) どこが／いつが いちばん …か どこがいちばん寒(さむ)いですか

～のなかで～がいちばん · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the winner takes が. Using は there （❌夏(なつ)はいちばん暑(あつ)いです）turns the sentence into a topic statement and loses the "out of all of them" force. が selects; は merely comments.

The question word matches the field: だれ for people, どれ for a closed set of listed things, なに for an open category, どこ for places, いつ for times.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Examples

果物(くだもの)の中(なか)で、りんごがいちばん好(す)きです。
Of all fruit, I like apples best.

クラスの中(なか)で、林(はやし)さんがいちばん背(せ)が高(たか)いです。
Hayashi is the tallest in the class.

家族(かぞく)の中(なか)で、だれがいちばん早(はや)く起(お)きますか。
Who gets up earliest in your family?

一年(いちねん)で、八月(はちがつ)がいちばん暑(あつ)いです。
August is the hottest month of the year.

この三(みっ)つの中(なか)で、どれがいちばん軽(かる)いですか。
Which of these three is the lightest?

～のなかで～がいちばん · Examples (cont.)

兄弟(きょうだい)の中(なか)で、妹(いもうと)がいちばんピアノが上手(じょうず)です。
Of us siblings, my little sister is the best at the piano.

この店(みせ)のメニューの中(なか)で、この定食(ていしょく)がいちばん人気(にんき)があります。
Of everything on this restaurant's menu, this set meal is the most popular.

今(いま)まで見(み)た映画(えいが)の中(なか)で、あの作品(さくひん)がいちばんおもしろかったです。
Of all the films I've seen, that one was the most interesting.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. The whole frame works unchanged in plain speech - just drop です - and it is one of the first things a learner is asked in conversation practice: 日本(にほん)の食(た)べ物(もの)の中(なか)で、何(なに)がいちばん好(す)きですか。

The 中(なか) is often left out when the field is a place or a period of time, because those are already containers: 世界(せかい)でいちばん高(たか)い山(やま), 一年(いちねん)でいちばん寒(さむ)い日(ひ), クラスでいちばん元気(げんき)な人(ひと). Saying 世界(せかい)の中(なか)で is not wrong, but it is heavier than a native speaker would usually bother with. With a category noun - fruit, sports, films - the の中(なか)で is the normal choice.

In writing you will see 最(もっと)も in place of いちばん, and ～のうちで in place of ～の中(なか)で. Both are stiffer. Neither changes the grammar.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Nuance & register (cont.)

Answering such a question, you normally repeat the frame only if it is not already obvious. Asked 季節(きせつ)の中(なか)でいつがいちばん好(す)きですか, you answer simply 秋(あき)がいちばん好(す)きです - or just 秋(あき)です.

～の中(なか)で～がいちばん vs ～より～のほうが

Rule of thumb - count the candidates. Two → より. Three or more → いちばん.

スポーツの中(なか)で、サッカーがいちばん好(す)きです。
Of all sports, I like football best.

サッカーより野球(やきゅう)の方(ほう)が好(す)きです。
I like baseball more than football.

Mixing the two produces sentences that are simply broken: ❌ サッカーよりいちばん好(す)きです. If a より phrase is present, the comparison is between two named things and there is no top of any ranking to point at.

～のなかで～がいちばん · が vs は in the winner slot

Rule of thumb - this pattern answers "which one?", and answers to "which one?" always take が.

四人(よにん)の中(なか)で、田中(たなか)さんがいちばん若(わか)いです。
Tanaka is the youngest of the four.

Replace が with は and the sentence stops selecting: it would be read as "as for Tanaka, he is the youngest", which is a comment about Tanaka rather than the result of comparing four people. The same logic governs the question - the question word だれ cannot take は at all.

は does appear one step earlier in the sentence, on the field, when the field is being contrasted: 日本(にほん)の山(やま)の中(なか)では…, "in Japan at least, …".

～のなかで～がいちばん · の中(なか)で vs で

Rule of thumb - with a group, keep の中(なか)で. With a place or a stretch of time, the plain で is enough.

日本(にほん)で富士山(ふじさん)がいちばん高(たか)いです。
Mt. Fuji is the highest mountain in Japan.

日本(にほん)の山(やま)の中(なか)で富士山(ふじさん)がいちばん高(たか)いです。
Of all the mountains in Japan, Mt. Fuji is the highest.

Both are correct. The first treats Japan as a location; the second explicitly assembles the set of Japanese mountains and ranks it. What you must not do is drop the particle altogether: ❌日本(にほん)富士山(ふじさん)がいちばん高(たか)いです.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 果物(くだもの)の中(なか)で、みかんはいちばん安(やす)いです。 → ✅ 果物(くだもの)の中(なか)で、みかんがいちばん安(やす)いです。
    The item being crowned takes が. This is the error learners make most often with the pattern, because English "oranges are the cheapest" looks like a topic sentence.

  • ❌ スポーツの中(なか)に、テニスがいちばん好(す)きです。 → ✅ スポーツの中(なか)で、テニスがいちばん好(す)きです。
    で marks the domain within which the comparison is carried out. に would mark a location of existence, which is not what is going on.

  • ❌ 世界(せかい)の中(なか)でいちばん大(おお)きい国(くに) → ✅ 世界(せかい)でいちばん大(おお)きい国(くに)
    Not ungrammatical, but a place name plus の中(なか)で is clumsy. Places and time spans take the bare で.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この三(みっ)つの中(なか)で、**何(なに)**がいちばんいいですか。 → ✅ この三(みっ)つの中(なか)で、どれがいちばんいいですか。
    When the candidates have been listed, the question word is どれ. なに asks about an open field with no stated members.

  • ❌ 兄弟(きょうだい)の中(なか)で、弟(おとうと)がいちばん元気(げんき)なです。 → ✅ 兄弟(きょうだい)の中(なか)で、弟(おとうと)がいちばん元気(げんき)です。
    いちばん does not change how the adjective ends. A な-adjective keeps its な only in front of a noun.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Practice 1

1. 飲(の)み物(もの)の中(なか)で、お茶(ちゃ)____いちばん好(す)きです。

飲(の)み物(もの)の中(なか)で、お茶(ちゃ)がいちばん好(す)きです。

が marks the item selected out of the group. It is also the particle 好(す)き normally takes for the thing liked, so the two requirements agree here.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Practice 2

2. Choose: 春(はる)と秋(あき)と（どちら・どれ）がいちばん好(す)きですか。

春(はる)と秋(あき)とどちらが好(す)きですか。

With exactly two candidates you use どちら - and then いちばん has to go, since there is no ranking of three or more to head. どれ needs at least three listed options.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Which season do you like best?"

季節(きせつ)の中(なか)で、いつがいちばん好(す)きですか。 （also natural: どの季節(きせつ)がいちばん好(す)きですか。）

Seasons are times, so いつ fits; どの + 季節(きせつ) is the other standard way to ask. Either way the question word carries が, never は.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 日本(にほん)の町(まち)の中(なか)に、京都(きょうと)はいちばんきれいだと思(おも)います。

✅ 日本(にほん)の町(まち)の中(なか)で、京都(きょうと)がいちばんきれいだと思(おも)います。

Two particles are wrong. The field of comparison takes で, not に, and the selected item takes が, not は.

～のなかで～がいちばん · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この店(みせ)のパンはおいしいです。 (b) この辺(へん)の店(みせ)の中(なか)で、この店(みせ)のパンがいちばんおいしいです。

(a) is an unranked judgement - the bread here is good, and nothing is said about anywhere else.

(b) sets up a field (the shops around here), compares them, and awards first place. The listener now knows the speaker has tried the others. That explicit field is exactly what the pattern adds over a bare adjective.

～より～のほうが～

Meaning · ～ is more ～ than ～ - comparing exactly two things

Japanese adjectives have no comparative form: 高(たか)い covers both "tall" and "taller". The comparison is carried entirely by the particles. より marks the loser, の方(ほう)が marks the winner, and the adjective stays exactly as it is.

～より～のほうが～ · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (winner) Noun + の方(ほう)が 猫(ねこ) → 猫(ねこ)の方(ほう)が
Noun (loser) Noun + より 犬(いぬ) → 犬(いぬ)より
Verb plain form + 方(ほう)が（no の） 歩(ある)く → 歩(ある)く方(ほう)が
い-adjective plain form + 方(ほう)が 安(やす)い → 安(やす)い方(ほう)が
な-adjective stem + な + 方(ほう)が 静(しず)かだ → 静(しず)かな方(ほう)が
Question AとBと、どちらが + adjective + か どちらが安(やす)いですか
Answer Bの方(ほう)が + adjective + です Bの方(ほう)が安(やす)いです
Short form Aより + adjective 去年(きょねん)より寒(さむ)いです

～より～のほうが～ · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the order is free. AよりBの方(ほう)が高(たか)いです and Bの方(ほう)がAより高(たか)いです say the same thing. What is fixed is which particle goes on which noun: より on the one that loses, の方(ほう)が on the one that wins. Swap the particles and you reverse the sentence.

Either half can also stand alone. 去年(きょねん)より寒(さむ)いです ("colder than last year") and こちらの方(ほう)が安(やす)いです ("this one is cheaper") are both complete, ordinary sentences.

～より～のほうが～ · Examples

犬(いぬ)より猫(ねこ)の方(ほう)が好(す)きです。
I like cats more than dogs.

バスより電車(でんしゃ)の方(ほう)が速(はや)いです。
The train is faster than the bus.

今日(きょう)は昨日(きのう)より暖(あたた)かいですね。
It's warmer today than yesterday, isn't it.

東京(とうきょう)と大阪(おおさか)と、どちらの方(ほう)が人(ひと)が多(おお)いですか。
Which has more people, Tokyo or Osaka?

肉(にく)より魚(さかな)の方(ほう)をよく食(た)べます。
I eat fish more often than meat.

～より～のほうが～ · Examples (cont.)

私(わたし)の方(ほう)が姉(あね)より少(すこ)し背(せ)が高(たか)いです。
I'm slightly taller than my older sister.

車(くるま)で行(い)くより、歩(ある)く方(ほう)が早(はや)いですよ。
It's quicker to walk than to go by car.

冬(ふゆ)の北海道(ほっかいどう)は、思(おも)っていたよりずっと寒(さむ)かったです。
Hokkaido in winter was far colder than I had expected.

～より～のほうが～ · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. In casual speech の方(ほう)が is often reduced to の方(ほう) with the が dropped - こっちの方(ほう)安(やす)いよ - and the whole より half is regularly left out when it is obvious what the comparison is against.

Degree adverbs slot in before the adjective and are very common: ずっと (far, by a long way), もっと (more), 少(すこ)し and ちょっと (a little). 去年(きょねん)よりずっと忙(いそが)しいです.

The question form is AとBと、どちらが…. Note three things about it: どちら not どれ (two candidates, not three); が not は; and the polite どちら has a casual counterpart どっち that you will hear constantly in conversation - 紅茶(こうちゃ)とコーヒー、どっちがいい?

の方(ほう) literally means "side, direction", and that image is worth keeping: you are pointing at one side of a pair. It is why the pattern feels wrong with three or more items - there is no "other side" to point away from.

～より～のほうが～ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, より has a second life in formal writing and announcements where it means "from": 九時(くじ)より開(ひら)きます, "opening from nine". Same character, unrelated job.

～より～の方(ほう)が vs ～の中(なか)で～がいちばん

Rule of thumb - two candidates take より; three or more take いちばん. They never appear in the same clause.

コーヒーより紅茶(こうちゃ)の方(ほう)が好(す)きです。
I prefer tea to coffee.

飲(の)み物(もの)の中(なか)で紅茶(こうちゃ)がいちばん好(す)きです。
Of all drinks, tea is my favourite.

The first ranks two named things and says nothing about anything else - the speaker may dislike both. The second crowns a winner over a whole field.

～より～のほうが～ · comparative より vs "from" より

Rule of thumb - if より is followed by an adjective, it is comparing. If it is followed by a verb of starting, opening or beginning, it means "from" and is a formal replacement for から.

この映画(えいが)は前(まえ)の作品(さくひん)より長(なが)いです。
This film is longer than the previous one.

受付(うけつけ)は十時(じゅうじ)より始(はじ)まります。
Reception opens from ten o'clock.

The second belongs to notices and announcements. In speech you would say 十時(じゅうじ)から.

～より～のほうが～ · ～の方(ほう)が vs ～方(ほう)がいい

Rule of thumb - the same word 方(ほう), two different jobs. With an adjective it compares; with いい and a verb it advises.

新(あたら)しいパソコンの方(ほう)が軽(かる)いです。
The new laptop is lighter.

新(あたら)しいパソコンを買(か)った方(ほう)がいいですよ。
You'd better buy a new laptop.

The advice pattern normally takes the た-form of the verb and ends in いい. If you find yourself saying 方(ほう)がいい when you only meant "more", you have accidentally told someone what to do.

～より～のほうが～ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 猫(ねこ)の方(ほう)が犬(いぬ)が好(す)きです。 → ✅ 犬(いぬ)より猫(ねこ)の方(ほう)が好(す)きです。
    The losing item needs より. Without it there is nothing to compare against and the sentence has two competing が.

  • ❌ 歩(ある)くの方(ほう)が早(はや)いです。 → ✅ 歩(ある)く方(ほう)が早(はや)いです。
    Nouns take の before 方(ほう); verbs and い-adjectives attach directly. Only な-adjectives insert anything, and what they insert is な.

  • ❌ 猫(ねこ)方(ほう)が好(す)きです。 → ✅ 猫(ねこ)の方(ほう)が好(す)きです。
    The mirror-image error. After a noun the の is compulsory.

  • ❌ コーヒーと紅茶(こうちゃ)と、どちらがいちばん好(す)きですか。 → ✅ コーヒーと紅茶(こうちゃ)と、どちらが好(す)きですか。
    いちばん needs three or more candidates. With two, どちら alone already asks the question.

～より～のほうが～ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 今日(きょう)は昨日(きのう)よりもっと暖(あたた)かいの方(ほう)がです。 → ✅ 今日(きょう)は昨日(きのう)より暖(あたた)かいです。
    The adjective itself never changes shape, and the two halves of the pattern do not both attach to it. Comparison lives in the particles, not in the adjective.

～より～のほうが～ · Practice 1

1. 飛行機(ひこうき)は新幹線(しんかんせん)____速(はや)いです。

飛行機(ひこうき)は新幹線(しんかんせん)より速(はや)いです。

より attaches to the thing being beaten. The winner here is already the topic, marked with は, so の方(ほう)が is not needed - though 新幹線(しんかんせん)より飛行機(ひこうき)の方(ほう)が速(はや)いです is equally correct.

～より～のほうが～ · Practice 2

2. Choose: 春(はる)と夏(なつ)と、（どちら・どれ）が好(す)きですか。

春(はる)と夏(なつ)と、どちらが好(す)きですか。

Two options means どちら (casually どっち). どれ requires a set of at least three, and choosing it here would leave the listener looking for a third season.

～より～のほうが～ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "This room is quieter than that one."

あの部屋(へや)よりこの部屋(へや)の方(ほう)が静(しず)かです。

Both halves are nouns, so both take their own particle, and 静(しず)かです stays in its ordinary form - there is no comparative ending to add. Reversing the two halves is also fine.

～より～のほうが～ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 私(わたし)は兄(あに)の方(ほう)が背(せ)が高(たか)いです。

✅ 私(わたし)より兄(あに)の方(ほう)が背(せ)が高(たか)いです。

As written, は and の方(ほう)が are fighting over which noun is the subject. Marking the speaker with より makes it clear who is being compared with whom - and, incidentally, who is taller.

～より～のほうが～ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この辞書(じしょ)の方(ほう)が便利(べんり)です。 (b) この辞書(じしょ)を使(つか)った方(ほう)がいいです。

(a) is a comparison. Some other dictionary is in play, and this one is the more useful of the two. The speaker is describing, not instructing.

(b) is advice: you should use this dictionary. The た-form plus 方(ほう)がいい is a recommendation, and depending on tone it can sound quite firm. Same noun 方(ほう), entirely different social act.
