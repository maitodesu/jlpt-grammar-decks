これ・それ・あれ・どれ

Meaning · this one / that one / that one over there / which one - the four words that stand in for a thing without naming it

These four are pronouns. Each one is a noun: it takes particles, it sits where a noun sits, and it can be the entire answer to a question. Which one you pick depends on whose territory the thing is in - yours (これ), your listener's (それ), or neither of yours (あれ) - and どれ asks the listener to choose one out of several.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Formation

Nothing attaches to these words; they are the noun. The table shows what each one picks out and how it slots into a sentence.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Nothing - これ is itself a noun これ = a thing in the speaker's reach これ、おいしいですね
Nothing それ = a thing in the listener's reach, or one just mentioned それを三(みっ)つください
Nothing あれ = a thing away from both of you あれは何(なん)ですか
Nothing どれ = which one, out of three or more どれが安(やす)いですか
Particles これ／それ／あれ／どれ + は・が・を・に・で・と それと同(おな)じものをください
A following noun ❌ これ + Noun → use この + Noun ❌ これかばん → ✅ このかばん
Two choices only どれ → どちら（casual どっち） コーヒーとお茶(ちゃ)、どちらにしますか

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Polite equivalent これ／それ／あれ／どれ → こちら／そちら／あちら／どちら こちらが新(あたら)しいメニューです

Rule of thumb - if a noun follows, you need この・その・あの・どの. If nothing follows, you need これ・それ・あれ・どれ. A word in this series is never sitting directly in front of the thing it points at.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Examples

これは姉(あね)のかさです。
This is my older sister's umbrella.

それ、ちょっと見(み)せてください。
Could you let me see that for a second?

あれは駅(えき)のビルです。
That building over there is the station.

メニューの中(なか)でどれが一番(いちばん)安(やす)いですか。
Which is the cheapest thing on the menu?

それはいい考(かんが)えですね。
That's a good idea.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Examples (cont.)

すみません、これとそれをください。
Excuse me - I'll take this one and that one.

去年(きょねん)京都(きょうと)で買(か)ったカップ、あれはどこにしまいましたか。
That cup we bought in Kyoto last year - where did you put it away?

説明(せつめい)は分(わ)かりましたが、それが本当(ほんとう)かどうかは別(べつ)の話(はなし)です。
I follow the explanation, but whether it is true is another matter.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Nuance & register

The series itself is register-neutral. これは…です is the first full sentence most learners ever build, and it stays usable for life.

Three things are worth knowing before you aim one of these words at a person.

Never point at a human with これ／それ／あれ. They are for objects. For people, use この人(ひと) at the blunt end and こちら when you are being polite - こちらは田中(たなか)さんです is the standard way to introduce someone. こいつ／そいつ／あいつ also exist for people and they are rough: tolerable between close friends about an absent third party, insulting anywhere else.

こちら・そちら・あちら・どちら are the polite upgrade, and they double as the two-item version of どれ. In a shop you will hear こちら where a friend would say これ - こちらでよろしいでしょうか, "will this one be all right?"

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Nuance & register (cont.)

あれ on its own is also an interjection meaning "huh?" - the noise you make when something is not where you left it: あれ、かぎがない. Same word on the page, completely different job, and context makes it obvious.

In speech the particle is routinely dropped: これ、いくら? rather than これはいくらですか. Keep the particle in writing and in ます-speech.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ vs この・その・あの・どの

Rule of thumb - the れ-series replaces the noun; the の-series introduces it. これ = "this one". この = "this ___", and the blank is compulsory.

This is the commonest error in the whole topic, because English uses one word - "this" - for both jobs.

これは重(おも)いです。
This one is heavy.

このはこは重(おも)いです。
This box is heavy.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ vs この・その・あの・どの (cont.)

You cannot mix them: ❌これはこ and ❌このは重(おも)いです are both broken. And unlike the place words, the れ-series does not take の in front of a noun either - ❌これの本(ほん) is not how you say "this book"; この本(ほん) is.

それ vs あれ - pointing back at the conversation

Rule of thumb - for something said rather than seen: それ picks up what one of you has just mentioned; あれ picks up something you both already know.

Once nothing is physically in the room, distance stops being about metres. それ handles information that has only just entered the conversation, from one side or the other.

「来週(らいしゅう)、試験(しけん)があるよ。」「えっ、それ、本当(ほんとう)?」
"There's an exam next week." "Wait - is that true?"

あれ reaches instead for shared memory: something you have both experienced, which is why it can turn up with no antecedent in the sentence at all.

去年(きょねん)二人(ふたり)で見(み)た映画(えいが)、あれはよかったね。
That film the two of us saw last year - that was a good one, wasn't it.

それ vs あれ - pointing back at the conversation (cont.)

Use あれ when the listener does not share the memory and you will sound as if you are talking to yourself.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · どれ vs どちら・どっち

Rule of thumb - どれ needs three or more candidates. With exactly two, Japanese switches to どちら, or どっち in casual speech.

English "which" does not care how many there are, so this has to be learned on purpose.

この三(みっ)つのうち、どれにしますか。
Which of these three will you have?

赤(あか)と青(あお)、どちらがいいですか。
Red or blue - which would you prefer?

どちら is also simply the polite form of どれ, so a shop assistant may use it even facing a whole shelf of options. どっち is casual and extremely frequent in speech; keep it out of email.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ これかばんは私(わたし)のです。 → ✅ このかばんは私(わたし)のです。
    A noun follows, so the の-series is required. これ can only stand alone.

  • ❌ これの本(ほん)を読(よ)みました。 → ✅ この本(ほん)を読(よ)みました。
    の does not rescue it. Unlike ここの店(みせ), which is perfectly normal, これの本(ほん) is not a thing.

  • ❌ 紅茶(こうちゃ)とコーヒー、どれがいいですか。 → ✅ 紅茶(こうちゃ)とコーヒー、どちらがいいですか。
    Two candidates means どちら／どっち. どれ starts at three.

  • ❌ あれは山田(やまだ)さんです。 → ✅ あちらは山田(やまだ)さんです。
    あれ is for objects. Aimed at a person it is genuinely rude; あちら

  • or あの人(ひと) among friends - is what you want.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 「これは何(なん)ですか。」「はい、これはペンです。」 → ✅ 「…」「それはペンです。」
    Once the object is in your listener's hands it has moved into their sphere, so the answer flips to それ. Replying with これ claims you are the one holding it.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Practice 1

1. すみません、____をください。（you are pointing at a cake inside the display case, out of reach of both of you）

すみません、あれをください。

The cake is behind glass, away from both you and the shop assistant, so it sits in neither person's sphere - which is exactly what あれ is for. こちら would be the staff's word, not yours.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Practice 2

2. 靴(くつ)が四(よん)足(そく)並(なら)んでいます。（どれ・どちら）が好(す)きですか。

どれが好(す)きですか。

Four pairs means three or more candidates, so どれ. どちら would tell the listener you are offering a choice between exactly two - or that you are being markedly polite.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Is that yours? - No, this one is mine."

それはあなたのですか。 - いいえ、これが私(わたし)のです。

The thing asked about is near the listener, hence それ; the speaker then points at the one in their own reach, hence これ. The answer uses が because "this one" is being singled out as the correct one among several.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: これのシャツは小(ちい)さいですから、大(おお)きいのをください。

✅ このシャツは小(ちい)さいですから、大(おお)きいのをください。 "This shirt is too small, so please give me a bigger one."

これの is not a possible modifier. When a noun follows, switch to the の-series. The の later in the sentence is a different の - the pronoun "one", standing in for シャツ.

これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 昨日(きのう)の話(はなし)、それはもう終(お)わりました。 (b) 昨日(きのう)の話(はなし)、あれはもう終(お)わりました。

(a) treats yesterday's matter as something that has just been raised in this conversation - typically the listener brought it up and you are answering their words. It is the neutral, businesslike choice.

(b) treats it as a file you both already have open: shared history you do not need to re-explain. It sounds warmer and more familiar, and it would be strange if the listener had no idea what you were referring to.

この・その・あの・どの

Meaning · this ~ / that ~ / that ~ over there / which ~ - pointing words that modify a noun instead of replacing it

この and its siblings are adnominals: they do nothing on their own and must be followed immediately by a noun. この本(ほん) is "this book", never "this". The こ／そ／あ／ど split is the same one the rest of the series uses - the speaker's side, the listener's side, away from both, and the question.

この・その・あの・どの · Formation

Nothing attaches to these words. They attach to the noun on their right, with nothing allowed in between except an adjective.

この・その・あの・どの · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun この + Noun - in the speaker's territory この傘(かさ)
Noun その + Noun - in the listener's territory, or just mentioned その話(はなし)
Noun あの + Noun - away from both, or shared memory あの店(みせ)
Noun どの + Noun - which ~, out of three or more どの電車(でんしゃ)
Adjective + Noun この + Adj + Noun この赤(あか)いくつ
The particle この + Noun + particle - never この + particle この傘(かさ)は私(わたし)のです
Nothing ❌ この on its own → use これ ❌ このは大(おお)きい → ✅ これは大(おお)きい
Two choices only どの → どちらの（casual どっちの） どちらの席(せき)がいいですか

この・その・あの・どの · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Polite equivalent この／その／あの → こちらの／そちらの／あちらの そちらの方(かた)にお聞(き)きください

Rule of thumb - この is a hook with nothing on it. Something must hang from it, and that something is a noun. If you have no noun to supply, you wanted これ・それ・あれ・どれ instead.

この・その・あの・どの · Examples

このペンは父(ちち)のです。
This pen is my father's.

そのいすに座(すわ)ってください。
Please sit on that chair.

あの白(しろ)い建物(たてもの)は図書館(としょかん)です。
That white building over there is the library.

どのバスが空港(くうこう)へ行(い)きますか。
Which bus goes to the airport?

その話(はなし)はもう聞(き)きました。
I have already heard that story.

この・その・あの・どの · Examples (cont.)

あのとき、もっと早(はや)く出(で)ればよかった。
I should have left earlier that time.

この辺(へん)は夜(よる)になると静(しず)かですから、勉強(べんきょう)するのにいいですよ。
This area goes quiet after dark, so it's a good place to study.

注文(ちゅうもん)はあの背(せ)の高(たか)い店員(てんいん)さんにお願(ねが)いしました。
I gave my order to that tall member of staff over there.

この・その・あの・どの · Nuance & register

Neutral in itself, and unavoidable - you cannot get through a paragraph of Japanese without it.

あの is also the standard hesitation noise, usually stretched to あのう. It opens a request to a stranger the way "excuse me…" does in English: あのう、すみません、ちょっとよろしいですか. Beginners hear it constantly and assume it is the demonstrative; it is not, and it points at nothing.

その is the workhorse of written and business Japanese. In an email, その件(けん)について ("regarding that matter") refers back to whatever the previous sentence or the previous email established. Anaphoric その is far more common in writing than the physical "near you" sense you learn first.

Politeness rises through こちらの. あの人(ひと) is fine among friends but too blunt about a customer or a superior; あちらの方(かた) is the safe formal version. あいつ and friends are the rough end of the same scale and take a の too: あいつの - avoid in polite company.

この・その・あの・どの · Nuance & register (cont.)

Casual speech shortens どの to どっちの when there are two, and drops the noun altogether when it can be guessed: 「どっち?」rather than どちらの席(せき)がいいですか.

この・その・あの・どの vs これ・それ・あれ・どれ

Rule of thumb - この modifies, これ replaces. この must be followed by a noun; これ must not be.

They are the same four distances packaged for two different grammatical slots, and mixing them up is the single most frequent mistake in this topic.

その時計(とけい)はいくらですか。
How much is that watch?

それはいくらですか。
How much is that one?

Both are correct; the first names the object, the second assumes you can see which object is meant. What you may never write is ❌それ時計(とけい) or ❌この、いくらですか.

この・その・あの・どの vs これ・それ・あれ・どれ (cont.)

There is one asymmetry worth memorising: the place words do take の before a noun (ここの店(みせ), "the shop here"), but the thing pronouns do not. ❌これの店(みせ) is wrong; この店(みせ) is right.

この・その・あの・どの · その vs あの

Rule of thumb - その = it is on your listener's side, or it entered the conversation a moment ago. あの = it is on nobody's side, or we both already know it.

Physically the split is easy. In conversation, where nothing is visible, it is about who owns the information.

「新(あたら)しいカフェができたよ。」「へえ、そのカフェ、どこ?」
"A new café has opened." "Oh - where is that café?"

The speaker has never seen it. It exists for them only because the listener just said so, so その.

学生(がくせい)のとき、よく二人(ふたり)で行(い)ったよね。あのカフェ、まだあるかな。
We used to go together as students, didn't we. I wonder if that café is still there.

この・その・あの・どの · その vs あの (cont.)

Here the café is in both memories, so あの. Using あの for something the listener has never heard of sounds as though you are muttering to yourself - which is precisely why writers use it for that effect.

この・その・あの・どの · どの vs どんな

Rule of thumb - どの picks one out of a known set. どんな asks what kind of thing it is, with no set in front of you.

Both sit in front of a noun and both come out as "what/which" in English, so they collapse together unless you separate them deliberately.

棚(たな)の本(ほん)のうち、どの本(ほん)が一番(いちばん)役(やく)に立(た)ちますか。
Of the books on the shelf, which book is the most useful?

どんな本(ほん)をよく読(よ)みますか。
What kind of books do you usually read?

この・その・あの・どの · どの vs どんな (cont.)

Answer the first with a book you can point at; answer the second with a category - 歴史(れきし)の本(ほん), 小説(しょうせつ). A quick test: if the English is "which of these", it is どの; if it is "what sort of", it is どんな.

この・その・あの・どの · Common mistakes

  • ❌ このは私(わたし)のかばんです。 → ✅ これは私(わたし)のかばんです。
    この cannot stand alone. Either supply a noun (このかばんは私(わたし)のです) or switch to これ.

  • ❌ それ本(ほん)を取(と)ってください。 → ✅ その本(ほん)を取(と)ってください。
    A noun follows, so the の-series is required. それ never sits in front of a noun.

  • ❌ あのの人(ひと)は先生(せんせい)です。 → ✅ あの人(ひと)は先生(せんせい)です。
    この・その・あの・どの already end in the の they need. Adding a second one is doubling up.

  • ❌ 肉(にく)と魚(さかな)、どの料理(りょうり)にしますか。 → ✅ 肉(にく)と魚(さかな)、どちらの料理(りょうり)にしますか。
    Two options means どちらの. どの needs three or more to choose between.

この・その・あの・どの · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ どの音楽(おんがく)が好(す)きですか。（meaning "what kind of music do you like?"）→ ✅ どんな音楽(おんがく)が好(す)きですか。
    どの would be asking the listener to point at one specific piece of music from a list in front of them. For a genre, you want どんな.

この・その・あの・どの · Practice 1

1. ____建物(たてもの)は百年(ひゃくねん)前(まえ)に建(た)てられました。（the building is a long way off, visible from where you both stand）

あの建物(たてもの)は百年(ひゃくねん)前(まえ)に建(た)てられました。

It is away from both of you, so あの. A noun follows, so it must be the の-series and not あれ.

この・その・あの・どの · Practice 2

2. 会議室(かいぎしつ)が五(いつ)つあります。（どの・どんな）部屋(へや)を使(つか)いますか。

どの部屋(へや)を使(つか)いますか。

There is a defined set of five rooms and you are asking the listener to pick one out of it. どんな部屋(へや) would be asking what sort of room they want - big, quiet, with a projector - which is a different question.

この・その・あの・どの · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I don't know that word. Could you write it on the board?"

その言葉(ことば)は知(し)りません。ホワイトボードに書(か)いていただけますか。

The word came from the listener's mouth, so it belongs to their side of the conversation: その. あの would wrongly imply you both already know it, which is the opposite of what you are saying.

この・その・あの・どの · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: すみません、これシャツはいくらですか。

✅ すみません、このシャツはいくらですか。 "Excuse me, how much is this shirt?"

シャツ is a noun and it follows immediately, so the modifying form この is required. The alternative repair is to drop the noun: これはいくらですか.

この・その・あの・どの · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) その映画(えいが)は面白(おもしろ)かったですか。 (b) あの映画(えいが)は面白(おもしろ)かったですか。

(a) refers to a film the listener has just brought up, and which the speaker knows nothing about beyond that mention. It is the natural reply to "I went to the cinema yesterday."

(b) presupposes that both of you already have a particular film in mind - one you discussed before, or one everybody in the room has heard of. Said out of nowhere it would leave the listener asking 「どの映画(えいが)?」.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ

Meaning · here / there / over there / where - the four words that name a place without naming it

These are place pronouns. Like これ they stand alone and take particles, but they denote locations, which changes which particles they attract: に for existence and destination, で for where something happens, から／まで for the ends of a route. Note the shape of the third one - it is あそこ, not あこ. That is the one irregularity in the whole こそあど system.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Formation

Nothing attaches to these words; they are the place noun. What follows is a particle.

Attaches to Form Example
Nothing - ここ is itself a noun ここ = where the speaker is ここで待(ま)ちます
Nothing そこ = where the listener is, or a place just mentioned そこに置(お)いてください
Nothing あそこ = a place away from both of you あそこまで歩(ある)きましょう
Nothing どこ = where どこに住(す)んでいますか
Particle に Place + に - existence or destination ここに名前(なまえ)を書(か)きます
Particle で Place + で - where an action happens あそこで会(あ)いましょう
から／まで Place + から／まで - route ends ここから駅(えき)まで十分(じゅっぷん)
Noun modification ここ／そこ／あそこ／どこ + の + Noun ここの水(みず)はおいしい

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Polite equivalent ここ／そこ／あそこ／どこ → こちら／そちら／あちら／どちら どちらにお住(す)まいですか

Rule of thumb - に puts something at the place, で makes something happen at the place. ここに本(ほん)がある versus ここで本(ほん)を読(よ)む. Getting this pair wrong is the single most audible error learners make with place words.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Examples

かばんはここに置(お)きます。
I'll put my bag here.

出口(でぐち)はどこですか。
Where is the exit?

そこ、ちょっと危(あぶ)ないですよ。
It's a bit dangerous where you're standing.

あそこの角(かど)を右(みぎ)に曲(ま)がってください。
Turn right at that corner over there.

ここのパンは朝(あさ)六時(ろくじ)に焼(や)き上(あ)がります。
The bread here comes out of the oven at six in the morning.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Examples (cont.)

旅行(りょこう)の写真(しゃしん)、どこでとったんですか。
Where did you take these holiday photos?

駅(えき)を出(で)たら、そこから三(みっ)つ目(め)の信号(しんごう)を左(ひだり)に曲(ま)がってください。
Once you leave the station, turn left at the third set of lights from there.

子(こ)どものころ住(す)んでいた町(まち)に戻(もど)ったが、あそこはもうすっかり変(か)わってしまった。
I went back to the town I lived in as a child, but the place has changed completely.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Nuance & register

Neutral, and used constantly. Three extensions are worth knowing.

They point at abstract "places" too. In an explanation or an argument, ここ means "this point in what I am saying" and そこ means "that point you just made": ここが大切(たいせつ)です ("this is the important bit"), そこが問題(もんだい)なんです ("that is exactly the problem"). This use is extremely common and completely idiomatic; a learner who only knows the physical sense will misread it.

ここ also stretches over time. ここ一週間(いっしゅうかん) means "these last/next seven days", and ここ数年(すうねん) "these past few years". そこ and あそこ do not do this.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Nuance & register (cont.)

こちら・そちら・あちら・どちら are the polite register, and in service or business Japanese they replace the plain words wholesale. Asked for your address you will hear どちらにお住(す)まいですか, never どこに住(す)んでいますか. どちら is also how you ask a customer where they are from. Meanwhile ここら／そこら ("round about here / there") is the casual end of the scale.

One caution on そこ with people: そこの人(ひと), shouted, is the Japanese equivalent of "oi, you there" - accurate grammar, terrible manners.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ vs これ・それ・あれ and この・その・あの

Rule of thumb - three parallel series, three different jobs. これ is a thing standing alone, この modifies a following noun, and ここ is a place standing alone.

Everything in the こそあど system shares the same four-way distance split; the series you pick is decided by what grammatical slot you need to fill.

ここは銀行(ぎんこう)です。
This place is a bank. (You are standing in it.)

これは銀行(ぎんこう)のかぎです。
This is the key to the bank. (An object in your hand.)

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ vs これ・それ・あれ and この・その・あの (cont.)

The place words have one privilege the thing pronouns lack: they take の before a noun. ここの店員(てんいん) ("the staff here") and どこの会社(かいしゃ) ("which company / a company from where") are both normal, whereas ❌これの店員(てんいん) is impossible - you would have to say この店員(てんいん). So ここの is a genitive, and この is a demonstrative; they are not interchangeable.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · ここに vs ここで

Rule of thumb - に marks the place a thing is or ends up; で marks the stage on which an action is performed.

English "here" and "there" carry no such distinction, so this must be chosen consciously every time.

ここに自転車(じてんしゃ)があります。
There is a bicycle here.

ここで自転車(じてんしゃ)を直(なお)します。
I fix bicycles here.

Existence verbs and verbs of arriving or putting take に; everything active takes で. The test that catches most cases: if you can rephrase the English as "there is / it goes onto", use に; if it is "the activity takes place at", use で.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · どこ vs どちら

Rule of thumb - same question, two registers. どこ is neutral, どちら is polite, and どちら additionally means "which of two".

トイレはどこですか。
Where's the toilet?

お国(くに)はどちらですか。
Where are you from? (polite)

Asking a customer, a client or an older stranger どこですか is not ungrammatical, just noticeably plain. In the other direction, どっち is the casual contraction and belongs with friends. Note that どちら doing double duty - "where" and "which of two" - is resolved by context alone; nobody finds it ambiguous in practice.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ あこに公園(こうえん)があります。 → ✅ あそこに公園(こうえん)があります。
    The あ-form of the place series is irregular. これ→あれ and この→あの drop the そ, but ここ→あそこ keeps it.

  • ❌ ここで花(はな)があります。 → ✅ ここに花(はな)があります。
    で marks the setting of an action. Existing is not an action, so it takes に.

  • ❌ ここに働(はたら)いています。 → ✅ ここで働(はたら)いています。
    The mirror image: working is an action, so the place takes で.

  • ❌ これは入口(いりぐち)です。（pointing at a doorway you are standing in）→ ✅ ここが入口(いりぐち)です。
    A location needs the place series, not the thing series. これ would mean you were holding the entrance.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ レストランが三(さん)軒(げん)あります。どこの店(みせ)にしますか。 → ✅ どの店(みせ)にしますか。
    どこの店(みせ) asks which area or chain a shop belongs to. To pick one out of a set in front of you, use どの.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Practice 1

1. すみません、この荷物(にもつ)は____に置(お)けばいいですか。

すみません、この荷物(にもつ)はどこに置(お)けばいいですか。 "Excuse me, where should I put this luggage?"

置(お)く is a verb of putting, so the landing place takes に, not で. The question word for a place is どこ; in a formal setting you could raise it to どちら.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Practice 2

2. 図書館(としょかん)（に・で）宿題(しゅくだい)をします。

図書館(としょかん)で宿題(しゅくだい)をします。

Doing homework is an activity, and the library is the stage it happens on, so で. You would use に only for existence or arrival - for example 図書館(としょかん)に行(い)きます.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The coffee at that place over there is cheap and good."

あそこのコーヒーは安(やす)くておいしいです。

Place words take の to modify a following noun, which is how you say "the coffee of that place". あそこ because the shop is away from both speaker and listener.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: パスポートはあこにありますか。

✅ パスポートはあそこにありますか。 "Is the passport over there?"

The word is あそこ, with そ in the middle. Learners regularise it to ❌あこ by analogy with あれ and あの; the place series is simply irregular here.

ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) そこが一番(いちばん)難(むずか)しいところです。 (b) ここが一番(いちばん)難(むずか)しいところです。

Neither has to involve a physical location. (a) picks up the point the listener has just raised - "that, what you just mentioned, is the hard part" - so it acknowledges their contribution.

(b) points at what the speaker is currently explaining: "this bit here, the bit I am showing you now, is the hard part." A teacher at a whiteboard uses (b); someone agreeing with a colleague's diagnosis uses (a).

Question words

Meaning · the words that ask what, who, when, where, how, why, what kind and how much

Japanese does not rearrange a sentence to ask a question. The question word simply occupies the slot the answer will occupy, the word order stays exactly as it was, and か goes on the end. 田中(たなか)さんが来(き)ます becomes 誰(だれ)が来(き)ますか - one word swapped, nothing moved.

Question words · Formation

One row per word. Each sits in the slot its answer would fill.

Question words · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun slot 何(なに)／何(なん) - what これは何(なん)ですか
Noun slot 誰(だれ) - who（polite どなた） 誰(だれ)が来(き)ますか
Noun slot いつ - when（never takes に） 試験(しけん)はいつですか
Noun slot どこ - where（polite どちら） 受付(うけつけ)はどこですか
Adverb slot どう - how, in what state コーヒーはどうですか
Adverb slot どうして - why（neutral, spoken and written） どうして遅(おく)れたんですか
Adverb slot なぜ - why（formal, mostly written） なぜ人(ひと)は働(はたら)くのか
Before a noun どんな + Noun - what kind of どんな音楽(おんがく)が好(す)きですか
Noun slot いくら - how much（price, amount） これはいくらですか

Question words · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - a question word that is the subject takes が, never は. は marks something already known, and the whole point of a question word is that it is not known. ✅誰(だれ)が作(つく)りましたか ／ ❌誰(だれ)は作(つく)りましたか. The reply keeps the が too: 母(はは)が作(つく)りました.

Reading rule for 何(なに) - it is read なん before a sound in the た／だ／な rows (で・と・に・だ・です・の) and before every counter; it is read なに everywhere else. So 何(なん)ですか, 何(なん)の本(ほん), 何人(なんにん), 何時(なんじ) - but 何(なに)が, 何(なに)を, 何(なに)か, 何(なに)も.

Question words · Examples

このマークは**何(なん)**ですか。
What is this symbol?

**誰(だれ)**がピアノを弾(ひ)きますか。
Who is going to play the piano?

いつ日本(にほん)へ来(き)ましたか。
When did you come to Japan?

この漢字(かんじ)はどう読(よ)みますか。
How do you read this kanji?

どんな映画(えいが)をよく見(み)ますか。
What sort of films do you watch a lot?

Question words · Examples (cont.)

地下鉄(ちかてつ)の乗(の)り換(か)えはどこでしますか。
Where do you change subway lines?

どうして昨日(きのう)学校(がっこう)を休(やす)んだんですか。
Why were you off school yesterday?

送料(そうりょう)も入(い)れると、全部(ぜんぶ)でいくらになりますか。
Including postage, how much does it come to altogether?

報告書(ほうこくしょ)には、なぜこの方法(ほうほう)を選(えら)んだのかを書(か)いてください。
In the report, please write why you chose this method.

Question words · Nuance & register

The words themselves are neutral; what varies is politeness and how blunt the question sounds.

Politeness ladder. 誰(だれ) → どなた and どこ → どちら are the two upgrades you will actually need. 「失礼(しつれい)ですが、どなたですか」is how you ask a stranger who they are; 誰(だれ)ですか at the same moment is abrupt. いくら has a formal partner too - おいくらですか, and in shops おいくらになりますか.

Softening with ん／の. A bare どうして来(こ)なかったんですか is softer than どうして来(こ)ませんでしたか, because the ん frames the question as asking for an explanation you are ready to accept rather than demanding one. Without it, "why" questions can land as accusations in Japanese just as in English.

Question words · Nuance & register (cont.)

Three words for "why". なんで is the casual spoken one, どうして the everyday neutral one, なぜ the formal and written one. なんで from a subordinate to a boss sounds childish; なぜ between friends sounds like a lecture.

Rising intonation replaces か in casual speech, and the question word carries the stress: 「どこ行(い)くの?」「何(なに)食(た)べる?」 Adding か to a plain form - ❌どこ行(い)くか? - sounds rough or interrogational, so leave か for the ます-form.

Finally, note that all of these words also serve as the building blocks for indefinites: add か and 何(なに) becomes "something", add も with a negative and it becomes "nothing". Those are separate patterns with their own pages.

Question words · どうして vs なぜ vs なんで

Rule of thumb - all three mean "why". なんで is casual speech, どうして is the neutral default, なぜ is formal and belongs mainly to writing.

There is no difference in meaning, only in register, which is why choosing wrongly is so noticeable.

なんで遅(おく)れたの?
How come you're late?

なぜ事故(じこ)が起(お)きたのか、まだ分(わ)かっていません。
It is still not understood why the accident occurred.

Question words · どうして vs なぜ vs なんで (cont.)

どうして sits comfortably in both of those situations and is the safe choice if you are unsure. One extra wrinkle: どうして can also mean "by what means, how" in older or set expressions, and なんで written in kanji as 何(なん)で is ambiguous with "by what means" - see the reading section below.

Question words · どう vs どんな

Rule of thumb - どう asks about a state or a manner and stands alone; どんな asks what kind of thing and must be followed by a noun.

Both come out as "how" or "what" in English and both are asking for a description, so they blur together fast.

新(あたら)しい仕事(しごと)はどうですか。
How's the new job?

どんな仕事(しごと)ですか。
What kind of job is it?

Question words · どう vs どんな (cont.)

The first invites an evaluation - busy, fun, tiring. The second asks for the category - sales, teaching, night shifts. Grammatically, ❌どう仕事(しごと) is impossible, because どう is an adverb and cannot modify a noun; and ❌仕事(しごと)はどんなですか is impossible, because どんな cannot stand alone. If you want the adjective-like form on its own, it is どんな感(かん)じ.

何(なに) vs 何(なん) - reading the same kanji two ways

Rule of thumb - なん before で・と・に・だ・です・の and before any counter; なに everywhere else, including on its own.

The kanji never changes, so this is invisible on the page and audible the moment you speak.

何時(なんじ)に何(なに)を食(た)べましたか。
What did you eat, and at what time?

Both readings in one sentence: なんじ because a counter follows, なに because を follows. Further examples: 何人(なんにん), 何回(なんかい), 何日(なんにち), 何曜日(なんようび) all take なん - but 何色(なにいろ) ("what colour") and 何語(なにご) ("what language") take なに, because those are not counters.

The pair that actually causes trouble is 何(なん)で versus 何(なに)で.

何(なに) vs 何(なん) - reading the same kanji two ways (cont.)

何(なん)で会社(かいしゃ)をやめたんですか。
Why did you quit the company?

何(なに)で会社(かいしゃ)まで来(き)ましたか。
What did you come to the office by?

Same three characters, two readings, two meanings: なんで is "why", なにで is "by what means". In speech people avoid the ambiguity by saying どうして for the first and 何(なに)に乗(の)って for the second.

Note - 何(なに)に is the one place the rule wobbles. Following the な-row rule it should be なんに, and 「何(なん)になさいますか」("what will you have?") is indeed なん. But 「何(なに)に使(つか)うの?」("what's it for?") is normally なに. Both readings are heard; treat this cell as genuinely variable rather than as a rule you have got wrong.

Question words · Common mistakes

  • ❌ これはなにですか。 → ✅ これはなんですか。
    です begins with a だ-row sound, so the reading is なん. The kanji is identical, which is why this survives for years unnoticed.

  • ❌ 誰(だれ)はこの絵(え)をかきましたか。 → ✅ 誰(だれ)がこの絵(え)をかきましたか。
    は presents known information. A question word is by definition unknown, so it takes が.

  • ❌ どう人(ひと)が好(す)きですか。 → ✅ どんな人(ひと)が好(す)きですか。
    どう is an adverb and cannot sit in front of a noun. To ask "what kind of", you need どんな.

  • ❌ 駅(えき)までいくらかかりますか。（asking about time）→ ✅ 駅(えき)までどのくらいかかりますか。
    いくら asks about money or quantity. For duration and distance the word is どのくらい／どれくらい. With かかる both are possible, so state which you mean.

Question words · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、なんでこの案(あん)にしたんですか。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、どうしてこの案(あん)にされたんですか。
    Not a grammar error but a register one: なんで to a superior sounds like a child demanding an explanation.

Question words · Practice 1

1. ____がこのケーキを作(つく)りましたか。

**誰(だれ)**がこのケーキを作(つく)りましたか。 "Who made this cake?"

The missing element is the subject, so the question word goes straight into the subject slot with が - not は, because an unknown person cannot be a known topic.

Question words · Practice 2

2. 週末(しゅうまつ)は（どう・どんな）でしたか。

週末(しゅうまつ)はどうでしたか。 "How was your weekend?"

どんな cannot stand alone in front of です; it always needs a noun after it. To use どんな here you would have to say どんな週末(しゅうまつ)でしたか, which asks what sort of weekend it was rather than how it went.

Question words · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, formally, for a written report: "Why did the number of customers fall?"

なぜお客(きゃく)さんの数(かず)が減(へ)ったのでしょうか。

なぜ is the register the task asks for - どうして would be acceptable but plainer, and なんで would be wrong in a report. のでしょうか keeps the tone measured rather than accusatory.

Question words · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: すみません、この荷物(にもつ)は何(なに)ですか。何(なん)を入(い)れましたか。

✅ すみません、この荷物(にもつ)は**何(なん)**ですか。**何(なに)**を入(い)れましたか。 "Excuse me, what is this parcel? What did you put in it?"

The two readings are swapped. です triggers なん; を triggers なに. The written sentence looks identical either way, so only reading it aloud exposes the error.

Question words · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 何(なん)で東京(とうきょう)へ行(い)きますか。 (b) 何(なに)で東京(とうきょう)へ行(い)きますか。

They are the same three characters and differ only in reading. (a), read なんで, asks why you are going to Tokyo - it is the casual equivalent of どうして.

(b), read なにで, asks by what means - train, plane, car. Because writing cannot distinguish them, careful writers avoid 何(なん)で altogether and use どうして for the reason and 何(なに)に乗(の)って for the method.

何か・誰か・どこか

Meaning · something / someone / somewhere - an unspecified one of a kind, rather than a request to identify it

Add か to a question word and it stops asking and starts referring. 何(なに) demands "what?"; 何(なに)か says "some thing or other, and I am not saying which - possibly because I do not know, possibly because it does not matter". The reading is なにか, not なんか.

何か・誰か・どこか · Formation

The か fuses onto the question word. What comes after it is an ordinary predicate.

Attaches to Form Example
何(なに) 何(なに) + か → 何(なに)か - something, anything 何(なに)か食(た)べる?
誰(だれ) 誰(だれ) + か → 誰(だれ)か - someone（polite どなたか） 誰(だれ)か呼(よ)んでください
どこ どこ + か → どこか - somewhere（polite どちらか） どこかで休(やす)みましょう
いつ いつ + か → いつか - sometime, one day いつか話(はな)します
どれ どれ + か → どれか - one of them（three or more） どれか選(えら)んでください
どちら どちら + か → どちらか - one of the two どちらかに決(き)めてください
Particles が・を・は dropped ❌何(なに)かを買(か)う → ✅何(なに)か買(か)う

何か・誰か・どこか · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Particles に・で・へ・と・から kept, after the か 誰(だれ)かに聞(き)く／どこかで会(あ)った

Rule of thumb - a か-word makes the sentence a yes/no question, not a content question. 何(なに)か買(か)いましたか is answered はい or いいえ. 何(なに)を買(か)いましたか demands a list of purchases. Same English word "what", opposite conversations.

何か・誰か・どこか · Examples

何(なに)か飲(の)みますか。
Would you like something to drink?

誰(だれ)かいますか。
Is anybody there?

冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)に何(なに)か残(のこ)っていますか。
Is there anything left in the fridge?

週末(しゅうまつ)、どこかへ行(い)きませんか。
Shall we go somewhere at the weekend?

誰(だれ)かに道(みち)を聞(き)いたほうがいいですね。
We had better ask somebody for directions.

何か・誰か・どこか · Examples (cont.)

いつか北海道(ほっかいどう)で馬(うま)に乗(の)ってみたい。
One day I want to try riding a horse in Hokkaido.

さっきからどこかで電話(でんわ)が鳴(な)っているが、誰(だれ)も出(で)ない。
A phone has been ringing somewhere for a while now, but nobody is picking up.

会議(かいぎ)の前(まえ)に何(なに)か確認(かくにん)しておきたいことはありますか。
Is there anything you would like to check before the meeting?

何か・誰か・どこか · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and very high frequency in speech, largely because Japanese prefers a vague offer to a specific one.

The particle drop is the natural form. 何(なに)かを食(た)べる is not exactly ungrammatical, but it is stiff and rarely said; 何(なに)か食(た)べる is what people actually produce. The same goes for が - 誰(だれ)か来(き)たよ, not 誰(だれ)かが来(き)たよ in ordinary speech, although the が version does appear in writing when the subject needs emphasis. は never attaches at all, because は announces a known topic and a か-word is by definition unknown.

Vagueness is politeness. Offering 何(なに)か飲(の)みますか lets the guest steer, whereas naming a drink pushes them into accepting or refusing that drink. The same softening drives どこか行(い)きたいところある? when planning a day out.

何か・誰か・どこか · Nuance & register (cont.)

なんか is a different word. Written the same way in kanji, but なんか functions as "or something / like / kind of": コーヒーなんかどうですか ("how about a coffee or something?"), or as pure filler - なんか、疲(つか)れた. It is casual and, used as filler, slightly juvenile. Do not read 何(なに)か as なんか.

何(なに)か can also mean "somehow, somewhat" in front of an adjective: 何(なに)かおかしい, "something's not right". No noun is being referred to at all.

Polite upgrades exist and are worth having: どなたか for 誰(だれ)か, and どちらか for どこか when you mean a place. On the phone: どなたかいらっしゃいますか.

何(なに)か・誰(だれ)か・どこか vs 何(なに)も・誰(だれ)も・どこも

Rule of thumb - か is the positive indefinite and works with any predicate; も is the negative one and requires a negative verb. They are a matched pair, and a question in か is usually answered in も.

This is the pairing to drill, because the two sets are one syllable apart and mean opposite things.

昼(ひる)ごはん、何(なに)か食(た)べましたか。
Did you eat anything for lunch?

いいえ、いそがしくて何(なに)も食(た)べませんでした。
No - I was busy and didn't eat anything.

何(なに)か・誰(だれ)か・どこか vs 何(なに)も・誰(だれ)も・どこも (cont.)

Note that the も-answer cannot be positive: ❌何(なに)も食(た)べました is broken Japanese. And the か-question cannot be negated into a "nothing": ❌誰(だれ)か来(き)ませんでした does not mean "nobody came" - for that you need 誰(だれ)も来(こ)ませんでした.

何(なに)か食(た)べましたか vs 何(なに)を食(た)べましたか

Rule of thumb - with か you are asking whether; with を you are asking what. The first expects はい／いいえ, the second expects a noun.

English "did you eat something / anything" versus "what did you eat" makes the same split, but learners import the を version by default and end up interrogating people.

朝(あさ)、何(なに)か飲(の)みましたか。
Did you have anything to drink this morning?

朝(あさ)、何(なに)を飲(の)みましたか。
What did you drink this morning?

The second presupposes that you drank; it is fine between friends and pushy from a stranger. Use the か form when you do not yet know whether the event happened at all.

何か・誰か・どこか · 何(なに)か vs 何(なん)でも

Rule of thumb - か means "some one thing, unspecified". でも means "any at all, take your pick". か narrows to one; でも opens the whole set.

Both land on English "anything", which hides the difference completely.

何(なに)か質問(しつもん)はありますか。
Are there any questions?

何(なん)でも聞(き)いてください。
Ask me anything you like.

The first asks whether at least one question exists. The second grants permission across the entire range. So 誰(だれ)か手伝(てつだ)って means "could someone help", while 誰(だれ)でも手伝(てつだ)える means "anyone at all is able to help".

何か・誰か・どこか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ reading 何(なに)か as なんか → ✅ なにか
    なんか is a separate colloquial word meaning "or something / kind of". The indefinite pronoun is なにか, always.

  • ❌ 誰(だれ)か来(き)ませんでした。 → ✅ 誰(だれ)も来(こ)ませんでした。
    Negating a か-word does not give you "nobody". The か-sentence would mean something closer to "it wasn't the case that someone came", which is not what you want; switch to the も-series.

  • ❌ 誰(だれ)かはこの漢字(かんじ)が読(よ)めますか。 → ✅ 誰(だれ)かこの漢字(かんじ)が読(よ)めますか。
    は marks a known topic and cannot attach to an indefinite. が and を are also dropped after a か-word.

  • ❌ いつかに食事(しょくじ)でもしましょう。 → ✅ いつか食事(しょくじ)でもしましょう。
    いつか is already adverbial, like いつ itself. Time words of this kind take no に.

何か・誰か・どこか · Practice 1

1. 部屋(へや)が暗(くら)いですね。____電気(でんき)をつけてくれませんか。

誰(だれ)か電気(でんき)をつけてくれませんか。 "Could somebody turn the light on?"

You are addressing a room rather than a named person, so you need the indefinite "someone". No particle follows か here, because the subject marker が drops.

何か・誰か・どこか · Practice 2

2. 夏休(なつやす)みに（どこか・どこも）旅行(りょこう)に行(い)きたいです。

夏休(なつやす)みにどこか旅行(りょこう)に行(い)きたいです。

The predicate is positive - you want to go - so the か-series is required. どこも only works with a negative, and would give "I don't want to travel anywhere", the opposite meaning.

何か・誰か・どこか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I think I've met him somewhere before."

彼(かれ)には前(まえ)にどこかで会(あ)ったことがあると思(おも)います。

どこか supplies the vague place, and で stays on because meeting is an action that happens somewhere. Note the particle sits after the か, never before it.

何か・誰か・どこか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: すみません、何(なに)かをお願(ねが)いしたいことがあるんですが。

✅ すみません、何(なに)かお願(ねが)いしたいことがあるんですが。 "Excuse me, there's something I'd like to ask you to do."

The を after か has to go. Object and subject particles are dropped after a か-word; only に, で, へ, と and から survive.

何か・誰か・どこか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 週末(しゅうまつ)、何(なに)かしましたか。 (b) 週末(しゅうまつ)、何(なに)をしましたか。

(a) is a yes/no question: did anything happen at all? It leaves the listener free to say 「いいえ、何(なに)もしませんでした」without feeling they have failed to answer.

(b) assumes the weekend contained activities and asks for them by name. Between friends that is perfectly friendly; asked of someone you barely know it can feel like it is prying. This is why (a) is the safer opener with a new colleague.

何も・誰も・どこも

Meaning · nothing / no one / nowhere - a question word plus も, finished off with a negative verb

Japanese builds "nothing" out of two halves. 何(なに)も supplies the "any", and the negative ending on the verb supplies the "not". Neither half works alone: 何(なに)も食(た)べません is "I don't eat anything", while ❌何(なに)も食(た)べます is not a sentence. English puts the negation in the pronoun; Japanese puts it in the verb, every time.

何も・誰も・どこも · Formation

The negative sits on the predicate at the end of the clause, however far away that is.

何も・誰も・どこも · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
何(なに) 何(なに) + も + negative - nothing 何(なに)も見(み)えない
誰(だれ) 誰(だれ) + も + negative - no one 誰(だれ)も知(し)らない
どこ どこ + も + negative - nowhere どこも開(あ)いていない
どれ どれ + も + negative - none of them どれも気(き)に入(い)らない
Particles が・を・は dropped ❌何(なに)もを → ✅何(なに)も
Particles に・で・へ・と inserted before the も 誰(だれ)にも／どこへも／誰(だれ)とも
Emphatic 何(なに)も → 何(なん)にも（なんにも） 何(なん)にも残(のこ)っていない
Positive predicate どこも／どれも + affirmative = every どこも混(こ)んでいる

何も・誰も・どこも · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the particle goes inside, not outside. It is 誰(だれ)にも言(い)わない, not ❌誰(だれ)もに言(い)わない. Think of it as question word + particle + も, with が, を and は simply deleted at that first slot.

何も・誰も・どこも · Examples

朝(あさ)から何(なに)も食(た)べていません。
I haven't eaten anything since this morning.

教室(きょうしつ)には誰(だれ)もいません。
There is no one in the classroom.

昨日(きのう)はどこへも行(い)きませんでした。
I didn't go anywhere yesterday.

店(みせ)に入(はい)ったが、何(なに)も買(か)わなかった。
I went into the shop but didn't buy anything.

この話(はなし)は誰(だれ)にも言(い)わないでください。
Please don't tell anyone about this.

何も・誰も・どこも · Examples (cont.)

三(さん)着(ちゃく)試(ため)してみたが、どれもサイズが合(あ)わなかった。
I tried three of them on, but none of them fitted.

誰(だれ)も手(て)を上(あ)げなかったので、先生(せんせい)は困(こま)った顔(かお)をしていた。
Nobody put their hand up, so the teacher looked stuck.

連休(れんきゅう)中(ちゅう)はどこも人(ひと)でいっぱいだから、家(いえ)でゆっくりしようと思(おも)う。
Everywhere is packed over the long weekend, so I think I'll take it easy at home.

引(ひ)っ越(こ)してきたばかりで、この町(まち)には知(し)り合(あ)いが誰(だれ)もいない。
I've only just moved here, so I don't know a soul in this town.

何も・誰も・どこも · Nuance & register

Neutral in register; what varies is force.

なんにも is the emphatic version. Written 何(なん)にも, it is 何(なに)も with feeling - 冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)に何(なん)にもない means the fridge is completely empty. Spoken, slightly whiny, very common. Its counterpart for people is 誰(だれ)一人(ひとり)…ない, which is the formal intensifier: 誰(だれ)一人(ひとり)反対(はんたい)しなかった, "not a single person objected".

Not every も-word is negative. This is the trap. いつも means "always" and takes a positive verb. どこも and どれも swing both ways: with a negative they mean "nowhere" and "none", with a positive they mean "everywhere" and "every one of them" - どれもおいしかった, "they were all delicious". Only 何(なに)も is reliably negative-only. 誰(だれ)も is negative in ordinary sentences, but 誰(だれ)もが, with が added, flips to "everyone" and belongs to formal writing: 誰(だれ)もが知(し)っている話(はなし).

何も・誰も・どこも · Nuance & register (cont.)

It is the standard answer to a か-question. 「何(なに)か買(か)った?」 「ううん、何(なに)も」 - the negative verb can even be left off entirely when the context supplies it, and trailing off after 何(なに)も is completely natural in speech.

Word order is flexible but the negative is not. You can move 誰(だれ)にも around the sentence freely; what you cannot do is let the verb come out positive.

何(なに)も・誰(だれ)も・どこも vs 何(なに)か・誰(だれ)か・どこか

Rule of thumb - か is the positive indefinite, も the negative one. か sentences end in a positive verb; も sentences end in a negative one.

They are the same nine question words wearing opposite hats, and a か-question is normally answered with a も-sentence.

部屋(へや)に誰(だれ)かいますか。
Is there anybody in the room?

いいえ、誰(だれ)もいません。
No, there's no one.

The two cannot be crossed. ❌誰(だれ)かいません does not mean "there is nobody" - it is at best a strange way of denying that someone is present. And ❌誰(だれ)もいます is simply ungrammatical.

何も・誰も・どこも · 何(なに)も vs 何(なん)でも

Rule of thumb - も with a negative = "not any". でも with a positive = "any at all, whichever". One closes the set, the other throws it open.

English "anything" covers both, so the two get swapped constantly.

昨日(きのう)は何(なに)もしませんでした。
I did nothing yesterday.

明日(あした)なら何(なん)でもできます。
Tomorrow I can do anything you like.

The same split runs through the whole set: 誰(だれ)も来(こ)ない ("nobody comes") versus 誰(だれ)でも来(こ)られる ("anybody can come"); どこにも売(う)っていない ("it isn't sold anywhere") versus どこでも売(う)っている ("it's sold everywhere").

何も・誰も・どこも · どこも with a negative vs どこも with a positive

Rule of thumb - どこも and どれも take their meaning from the verb. Negative verb → "nowhere / none". Positive verb → "everywhere / all of them".

This is the one place where the "も means negative" shortcut misleads you, and the resulting misreadings are total reversals of meaning.

正月(しょうがつ)はどこも開(あ)いていなかった。
Nothing was open over New Year.

正月(しょうがつ)はどこも静(しず)かだった。
Everywhere was quiet over New Year.

何も・誰も・どこも · どこも with a negative vs どこも with a positive (cont.)

何(なに)も does not behave this way - it stays negative - and 誰(だれ)も only turns positive in the fixed written pattern 誰(だれ)もが. So the swing is a property of どこも and どれも specifically. Read to the end of the sentence before deciding what a も-word means.

何も・誰も・どこも · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 昨日(きのう)は何(なに)も食(た)べました。 → ✅ 昨日(きのう)は何(なに)も食(た)べませんでした。
    何(なに)も carries no negation of its own. The verb has to supply it.

  • ❌ パーティーで誰(だれ)もと話(はな)さなかった。 → ✅ パーティーで誰(だれ)とも話(はな)さなかった。
    The particle goes between the question word and も, not after it. Likewise 誰(だれ)にも, どこへも, どこでも.

  • ❌ 夏休(なつやす)みはどこも行(い)きませんでした。 → ✅ 夏休(なつやす)みはどこへも（or どこにも）行(い)きませんでした。
    With a verb of motion the destination particle is required. Bare どこも before 行(い)く is heard casually but reads as sloppy, and it collides with the "everywhere" sense.

何も・誰も・どこも · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ お皿(さら)に何(なに)もを残(のこ)さないでください。 → ✅ お皿(さら)に何(なに)も残(のこ)さないでください。
    を, が and は are all deleted after a も-word. Only に, で, へ and と survive, and they go inside.

  • ❌ reading 何(なに)も as なんも → ✅ なにも
    も is not in the た／だ／な rows, so 何(なに) keeps its なに reading. なんも exists in some regional speech but is not standard.

何も・誰も・どこも · Practice 1

1. 財布(さいふ)を開(あ)けたが、中(なか)には____入(はい)っていなかった。

中(なか)には何(なに)も入(はい)っていなかった。 "I opened my wallet, but there was nothing in it."

The verb is already negative, so the slot needs the も-series, not the か-series. 何(なに)か here would produce a strange denial rather than "nothing".

何も・誰も・どこも · Practice 2

2. すみません、この質問(しつもん)について（誰(だれ)か・誰(だれ)も）知(し)っている人(ひと)はいませんか。

誰(だれ)か知(し)っている人(ひと)はいませんか。

Although the sentence ends in a negative, that negative belongs to いませんか, which is a polite invitation - "isn't there anyone who…?" You are hoping the answer is yes, so the positive indefinite か is correct. 誰(だれ)も would flatly assert that no such person exists.

何も・誰も・どこも · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Don't say anything to anyone."

誰(だれ)にも何(なに)も言(い)わないでください。

Two も-words can share one negative verb without any conflict - Japanese has no double-negative rule. に sits inside 誰(だれ)にも because it marks the person spoken to, while 何(なに)も drops its を.

何も・誰も・どこも · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 連休(れんきゅう)に温泉(おんせん)へ行(い)ったが、どれもホテルが空(あ)いていました。

✅ 連休(れんきゅう)に温泉(おんせん)へ行(い)ったが、どのホテルも空(あ)いていませんでした。 "I went to a hot spring over the long weekend, but none of the hotels had rooms."

Two problems. どれも cannot be followed by the noun it refers to - attach も to the noun instead: どのホテルも. And the intended meaning is "none", so the verb must be negative; as written, the sentence claimed every hotel was free.

何も・誰も・どこも · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 店(みせ)はどこも込(こ)んでいた。 (b) 店(みせ)はどこも込(こ)んでいなかった。

(a) has a positive verb, so どこも reads as "every one of them": every shop was crowded. (b) has a negative verb, so the same どこも reads as "not any of them": no shop was crowded anywhere.

The two sentences are identical up to the ending, which is why you must read a も-word all the way to the verb before deciding what it means. With 何(なに)も this ambiguity never arises - it is negative-only.
