N5 - Advice, intention, guessing

  • ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to
  • ～つもり - plan to; intend to
  • だろう・でしょう - probably; … right?
  • んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to

Meaning · you'd better do it - doing this is the better of the two options

ほう means "side" or "alternative", so ～ほうがいい literally weighs one choice against another: the doing-it side is better. That comparison never fully goes away, and it is what gives the pattern its bite. Saying it means you have judged the listener's situation and found their current course wanting.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Formation

For advice, the verb goes in the た-form. This is the form you will use ninety percent of the time, and it is not a past tense.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (advice) た-form + ほうがいい 休(やす)む → 休(やす)んだほうがいい
Verb (negative advice) ない-form + ほうがいい 行(い)く → 行(い)かないほうがいい
Verb (general comparison) dictionary form + ほうがいい 歩(ある)く → 歩(ある)くほうがいい
い-adjective plain form + ほうがいい 安(やす)い → 安(やす)いほうがいい
な-adjective な + ほうがいい 静(しず)かだ → 静(しず)かなほうがいい
Noun の + ほうがいい バス → バスのほうがいい

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - affirmative advice takes た; negative advice takes ない. There is no ❌～なかったほうがいい. The two halves are not symmetrical, and this is the single most common error with the pattern.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Examples

早(はや)く寝(ね)たほうがいいよ。
You should get to bed early.

今日(きょう)は行(い)かないほうがいいです。
It'd be better not to go today.

薬(くすり)を飲(の)んだほうがいいですよ。
You ought to take some medicine.

かさを持(も)っていったほうがいいよ。午後(ごご)から雨(あめ)だって。
Take an umbrella - apparently it's going to rain this afternoon.

あの店(みせ)は高(たか)いから、こっちで買(か)ったほうがいいと思(おも)う。
That shop's expensive, so I think you're better off buying it here.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Examples (cont.)

夜(よる)遅(おそ)くにこの道(みち)は通(とお)らないほうがいいです。暗(くら)くて危(あぶ)ないですから。
You'd better not take this road late at night - it's dark and it's dangerous.

熱(ねつ)があるなら、会社(かいしゃ)を休(やす)んで病院(びょういん)に行(い)ったほうがいいと思(おも)いますよ。
If you've got a fever, I really think you should take the day off and see a doctor.

上司(じょうし)に相談(そうだん)してから決(き)めたほうがいいんじゃないでしょうか。
Wouldn't it be better to decide after you've talked it over with your manager?

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Nuance & register

ほうがいい is often taught as a gentle "should", and that is the source of a lot of accidental rudeness. It is a recommendation with weight behind it. The comparison built into ほう means that when you recommend option A, you are simultaneously saying option B - the one the listener is currently on - is worse. English "you'd better" carries the same faint edge, and for the same reason.

Some practical consequences:

Do not aim it upward. 部長(ぶちょう)、早(はや)く帰(かえ)ったほうがいいですよ is grammatical, polite in form, and still presumptuous, because you are telling a superior what to do. To a superior, drop the directive framing entirely: ～てはいかがでしょうか, ～てもよろしいかと思(おも)います, or just describe the situation and let them decide.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Nuance & register (cont.)

よ is doing real work. In speech, ～たほうがいいよ reads as friendly and concerned; bare ～たほうがいい can land flat, like a verdict. ～たほうがいいですよ is the everyday polite advice form you will actually hear.

Hedges are normal, not weakness. ～と思(おも)います, ～んじゃないですか, ～かもしれません all take the edge off without changing the advice. Native speakers use them constantly.

～ないほうがいい is blunter than the affirmative. Telling someone not to do something presupposes they were about to. Reserve it for cases where you mean it - warnings, danger, genuine bad ideas.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two spelling and shape notes. ほう is very often written 方(ほう), and you should expect 方(ほう)がいい in print. And いい swaps freely for its politer relatives: ～たほうがよろしいかと思(おも)います is standard business Japanese, while ～たほうがよかった shifts the whole thing into regret - "I should have."

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · ～たほうがいい vs ～るほうがいい

Rule of thumb - た-form gives advice about this situation. Dictionary form compares two options in the abstract.

This is the distinction the pattern is built on, and it is why the "past tense" form is the normal one for advice.

薬(くすり)を飲(の)んだほうがいいです。
You should take some medicine.

The た-form presents the action as a completed package - "having taken it" - and holds that up against the listener's actual, present situation. It points at you, now.

薬(くすり)を飲(の)むほうがいいです。
Taking medicine is the better approach.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · ～たほうがいい vs ～るほうがいい (cont.)

The dictionary form floats free of anyone in particular. It reads as a general policy statement, which is why using it on a sick friend sounds oddly detached, as though you were reading from a leaflet.

Dictionary form comes into its own when the two options are actually spelled out:

バスで行(い)くより、電車(でんしゃ)で行(い)くほうがいいですよ。
Going by train is better than going by bus.

There the sentence really is a comparison, and より makes the other side explicit. That is the same ほうがいい you meet as the comparison pattern.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · ～ないほうがいい vs ～なくてもいい

Rule of thumb - ～ないほうがいい = don't. ～なくてもいい = you don't have to. One forbids, the other releases.

明日(あした)は来(こ)ないほうがいいです。
You'd better not come tomorrow. (something is wrong with coming)

明日(あした)は来(こ)なくてもいいです。
You don't have to come tomorrow. (it's optional; come if you like)

Mixing these up produces one of the more embarrassing N5 errors, because the two sentences are almost opposite in social effect. ～なくてもいい grants permission; ～ないほうがいい withdraws it.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · ～ほうがいい vs ～たらどうですか

Rule of thumb - ～ほうがいい says the alternative is worse. ～たらどうですか merely puts an idea on the table.

先生(せんせい)に聞(き)いたほうがいいですよ。
You should ask the teacher.

先生(せんせい)に聞(き)いたらどうですか。
How about asking the teacher?

The second leaves the decision visibly with the listener, which makes it the safer choice when you are not sure the advice is wanted, or when the person outranks you. It is not always softer - said sharply, ～たらどうですか can sound impatient - but its default is more open.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · ～ほうがいい vs ～たらどうですか (cont.)

Further up the scale sit ～なければなりません ("must") and ～べきです ("ought to", moral weight). ほうがいい sits between "how about" and "must": stronger than a suggestion, weaker than an obligation.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行(い)かなかったほうがいいです。 → ✅ 行(い)かないほうがいいです。
    Negative advice uses the plain ない-form. The た of ～たほうがいい has no negative counterpart in this pattern.

  • ❌ 風邪(かぜ)ですか。休(やす)むほうがいいですよ。 → ✅ 休(やす)んだほうがいいですよ。
    For advice aimed at the person in front of you, use the た-form. The dictionary form turns it into a detached general statement.

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、もっと早(はや)く帰(かえ)ったほうがいいですよ。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、そろそろお帰(かえ)りになってはいかがでしょうか。
    The grammar is fine; the direction is not. ほうがいい implies the listener is currently choosing wrong, which you do not say upward.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)は来(こ)ないほうがいいです。（intending "you don't have to come"）→ ✅ 明日(あした)は来(こ)なくてもいいです。
    ～ないほうがいい tells someone not to do it. Optionality is ～なくてもいい.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この本(ほん)を読(よ)んだほうがいいでした。 → ✅ この本(ほん)を読(よ)んだほうがよかったです。
    いい is an い-adjective. Its past is よかった, never ❌いいでした.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Practice 1

1. 風邪(かぜ)ですね。今日(きょう)は休(やす)____ほうがいいですよ。

今日(きょう)は休(やす)んだほうがいいですよ。

Affirmative advice takes the た-form. 休(やす)む is a う-verb ending in む, so む → んだ. The た-form here is not past tense - it is what makes the advice land on this person, in this situation.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Practice 2

2. 危(あぶ)ないですから、あの道(みち)は（通(とお)らない・通(とお)らなかった）ほうがいいです。

あの道(みち)は通(とお)らないほうがいいです。

Affirmative and negative are not symmetrical here. Affirmative advice uses た, but negative advice uses the plain ない-form. ❌通(とお)らなかったほうがいい is not a form of this pattern at all.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "You'd better take an umbrella."

かさを持(も)っていったほうがいいですよ。 （also fine: かさを持(も)ったほうがいいですよ。）

た-form because this is advice for the person in front of you. The ですよ is worth adding - よ frames it as helpful information rather than a verdict, which is how this advice is normally delivered in speech.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the problem: 部長(ぶちょう)、その資料(しりょう)はもう一度(いちど)確認(かくにん)したほうがいいですよ。

The grammar is correct - the problem is the register.

✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、その資料(しりょう)はもう一度(いちど)確認(かくにん)なさってはいかがでしょうか。 （or: …確認(かくにん)していただいたほうがよろしいかと思(おも)います。）

ほうがいい carries the implication that the listener's current course is the worse option, so aiming it at a superior is presumptuous however polite the ending is. Reframe as a question or a hedged opinion.

～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) もっと野菜(やさい)を食(た)べたほうがいいです。 (b) もっと野菜(やさい)を食(た)べるほうがいいです。

(a) is advice directed at the listener: "you should eat more vegetables." The た-form ties the recommendation to this particular person's situation, and implies the speaker has looked at how they currently eat.

(b) is a general comparison: "eating more vegetables is the better option." It floats free of any individual and reads like a statement of policy - fine in a health article, oddly impersonal said to a friend over dinner.

～つもり - plan to; intend to

Meaning · I intend to; I'm planning to - a decision already made in the speaker's head

つもり is a noun meaning something like "intention", so a sentence with it literally says my intention is to do X. That is why it feels more settled than "I think I'll" and less fixed than a diary entry: the plan exists, it belongs to the speaker, and it could still change.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Formation

つもり is a noun, so it needs だ or です after it, and things attach to it the way they attach to any noun.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb dictionary form + つもりだ 買(か)う → 買(か)うつもりだ
Verb (negative) ない-form + つもりだ 買(か)う → 買(か)わないつもりだ
Polite つもりだ → つもりです 買(か)うつもりです
Denying the intention つもりはない 買(か)うつもりはない
Intention that changed つもりだった 買(か)うつもりだった
Question つもりですか 買(か)うつもりですか
Noun noun + の + つもり 冗談(じょうだん) → 冗談(じょうだん)のつもり
Verb (た-form) た-form + つもり = "under the impression" 言(い)った → 言(い)ったつもり

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the verb before つもり stays plain. ❌行(い)きますつもりです is one of the most recognisable learner errors in the language.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Examples

今日(きょう)は早(はや)く寝(ね)るつもりです。
I'm planning to go to bed early tonight.

来年(らいねん)、日本(にほん)へ行(い)くつもりです。
I intend to go to Japan next year.

今年(ことし)こそたばこをやめるつもりだ。
This year I really am going to quit smoking.

夏休(なつやす)みはどうするつもりですか。
What are you thinking of doing over the summer?

甘(あま)いものは、しばらく食(た)べないつもりです。
I'm planning to stay off sweet things for a while.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Examples (cont.)

新(あたら)しいパソコンを買(か)うつもりだったが、お金(かね)が足(た)りなかった。
I'd meant to buy a new computer, but I didn't have enough money.

大学(だいがく)を出(で)たら国(くに)に帰(かえ)って、日本語(にほんご)を教(おし)えるつもりです。
After I finish university I intend to go home and teach Japanese.

彼(かれ)にはもう会(あ)うつもりはありません。連絡(れんらく)もしないでください。
I have no intention of seeing him again. Please don't contact me either.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Nuance & register

つもり is volitional and personal. It reports a decision the speaker has taken about their own behaviour, which puts two hard limits on it.

No intending what you cannot control. ❌雨(あめ)が降(ふ)るつもりです is impossible - the rain has no plans. The same rule blocks つもり with verbs like できる, 分(わ)かる or ある. For predictions, でしょう or と思(おも)います does the job.

Careful with other people's intentions. 田中(たなか)さんは来(く)るつもりです claims direct access to Tanaka's mind. Add evidence: 来(く)るつもりらしいです, 来(く)るつもりだと言(い)っていました.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Nuance & register (cont.)

どうするつもりですか can be an accusation. Asked flatly, "what do you intend to do?" carries the tone of a parent facing a broken window. Aimed at a superior it is worse - 部長(ぶちょう)はいつ帰(かえ)るつもりですか sounds like a challenge. Upward, ask about their ご予定(よてい) instead: 部長(ぶちょう)のご予定(よてい)はいかがでしょうか.

つもりはない is a door slamming. It denies that the intention exists at all, and in an argument it is very cold. ～ないつもりです is the mild version.

つもりだった almost always implies the plan failed or changed. Nobody says 行(い)くつもりだった about a trip they took; it sets up a "but".

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Nuance & register (cont.)

A second life as a plain noun. 冗談(じょうだん)のつもりだった ("I meant it as a joke"), 言(い)ったつもりだったんだけど ("I was sure I'd told you"). Here つもり is about what you believed, not what you planned, and the belief is usually wrong. Register-wise つもり itself is neutral; つもりです is fine in a job interview, and つもり bare is fine with friends.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · ～つもりです vs ～予定(よてい)です

Rule of thumb - つもり is what you have decided. 予定(よてい) is what is on the calendar. Intention lives in your head; a schedule exists outside it.

来月(らいげつ)、引(ひ)っ越(こ)すつもりです。
I'm planning to move next month. (I've decided; nothing is booked yet)

来月(らいげつ)、引(ひ)っ越(こ)す予定(よてい)です。
I'm due to move next month. (it's arranged - date set, movers booked)

Because 予定(よてい) points at an arrangement rather than a state of mind, it is also the polite way to ask about someone senior, where つもり would be intrusive.

～つもりです vs ～(よ)うと思(おも)います

Rule of thumb - つもり reports a decision already taken. ～(よ)うと思(おも)います presents the thought as forming now, which makes it softer.

週末(しゅうまつ)は海(うみ)へ行(い)くつもりです。
I'm planning to go to the sea at the weekend.

週末(しゅうまつ)は海(うみ)へ行(い)こうと思(おも)います。
I think I'll head to the sea at the weekend.

The second is the safer answer to a casual question, because it does not commit you. The first can sound slightly firm, even stubborn, if the listener was hoping to change your mind.

～ないつもりです vs ～つもりはありません

Rule of thumb - ～ないつもり = I plan not to. ～つもりはない = I have no such plan, and I resent the suggestion.

今日(きょう)はお酒(さけ)を飲(の)まないつもりです。
I'm planning not to drink today.

お酒(さけ)を飲(の)むつもりはありません。
I have no intention of drinking.

The first describes a resolution you have made for yourself. The second rejects the whole idea, and depending on the tone it can be a refusal, a defence, or a rebuke. It is a useful sentence to understand and a risky one to use.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日(あした)、行(い)きますつもりです。 → ✅ 明日(あした)、行(い)くつもりです。
    つもり follows a plain form. The politeness goes on です at the end of the sentence, once.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)は雨(あめ)が降(ふ)るつもりです。 → ✅ 明日(あした)は雨(あめ)が降(ふ)るでしょう。
    Only a person with a choice can have an intention. Weather takes でしょう.

  • ❌ 山田(やまだ)さんは来(こ)ないつもりです。 → ✅ 山田(やまだ)さんは来(こ)ないつもりらしいです。
    Asserting someone else's intentions flatly claims to read their mind. Mark the evidence.

  • ❌ これは冗談(じょうだん)つもりでした。 → ✅ これは冗談(じょうだん)のつもりでした。
    つもり is a noun, so another noun joins it with の.

  • ❌ 社長(しゃちょう)、いつ海外(かいがい)へ行(い)くつもりですか。 → ✅ 社長(しゃちょう)、海外(かいがい)へはいつ行(い)かれるご予定(よてい)ですか。
    The grammar is sound; asking a superior about their つもり sounds like an interrogation.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Practice 1

1. 来週(らいしゅう)から毎日(まいにち)３０分(ぷん)走(はし)____つもりです。

毎日(まいにち)３０分(ぷん)走(はし)るつもりです。

つもり takes the plain dictionary form, so 走(はし)る stays as it is. The politeness is carried by です at the end - never by the verb inside.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Practice 2

2. 来年(らいねん)、結婚(けっこん)する（つもり・予定(よてい)）です。式場(しきじょう)も、もう予約(よやく)しました。

来年(らいねん)、結婚(けっこん)する**予定(よてい)**です。

The venue is already booked, so this is an arrangement in the world, not a plan in the speaker's head. 予定(よてい) is what fits. つもり would oddly suggest nothing had been settled yet.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I was going to call you yesterday, but I forgot."

きのう電話(でんわ)をするつもりだったんですが、忘(わす)れてしまいました。

つもりだった is the form for an intention that did not survive contact with reality, and it practically demands a が or けど clause explaining what went wrong.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the problem: 部長(ぶちょう)は明日(あした)の会議(かいぎ)に出(で)るつもりです。

✅ 部長(ぶちょう)は明日(あした)の会議(かいぎ)に出(で)られるご予定(よてい)です。 （or, if you were told so: …出(で)るつもりだとおっしゃっていました。）

Two problems at once: you cannot assert another person's intention as fact, and つもり is too familiar to use of a superior. Switch to ご予定(よてい), or quote your source.

～つもり - plan to; intend to · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 会議(かいぎ)には出(で)ないつもりです。 (b) 会議(かいぎ)に出(で)るつもりはありません。

(a) is a plan: the speaker has decided to skip the meeting, and the sentence invites a follow-up reason without any heat in it.

(b) denies that the intention exists at all. It is flat and final, and in a workplace it would read as a refusal - the kind of thing said when someone has just pressed you to attend. Same facts, very different temperature.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right?

Meaning · probably - or, said with a rising tone, "…don't you think?"

だろう and でしょう are the same word in two politeness levels, and that word does one job: it stops a sentence being a flat assertion. Whether it comes out as a guess or as an appeal for agreement is decided almost entirely by intonation - falling for "probably", rising for "right?". Learners are usually taught only the first half.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Formation

でしょう attaches to plain forms, and the だ of nouns and な-adjectives disappears in front of it.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + でしょう 降(ふ)る → 降(ふ)るでしょう
Verb (past) た-form + でしょう 降(ふ)った → 降(ふ)ったでしょう
Verb (negative) ない-form + でしょう 来(く)る → 来(こ)ないでしょう
い-adjective plain form + でしょう 高(たか)い → 高(たか)いでしょう
な-adjective drop だ + でしょう 便利(べんり)だ → 便利(べんり)でしょう
Noun noun + でしょう 雨(あめ) → 雨(あめ)でしょう
Plain / written style でしょう → だろう 降(ふ)るだろう
Casual spoken でしょう → でしょ／だろ 降(ふ)るでしょ
Common partners たぶん, きっと, もしかしたら たぶん降(ふ)るでしょう

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - no だ and no ます in front. ❌学生(がくせい)だでしょう, ❌行(い)きますでしょう. The politeness of the sentence is already carried by でしょう itself.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Examples

明日(あした)は雨(あめ)でしょう。
It will probably rain tomorrow.

たぶん田中(たなか)さんは来(こ)ないでしょう。
Tanaka probably won't come.

この問題(もんだい)、難(むずか)しいでしょう？
This question's hard, isn't it?

今夜(こんや)はかなり冷(ひ)えるだろう。
It'll get pretty cold tonight.

彼(かれ)はもう空港(くうこう)に着(つ)いているでしょう。
He's probably already arrived at the airport.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Examples (cont.)

ほら、言(い)ったでしょう？だから傘(かさ)を持(も)っていけばよかったのに。
See? I told you. You should have taken an umbrella.

午後(ごご)から風(かぜ)が強(つよ)くなるでしょう。おでかけの方(かた)はご注意(ちゅうい)ください。
The wind will strengthen from the afternoon. Those going out should take care.

その映画(えいが)、もう見(み)たでしょう？どうだった？
You've already seen that film, haven't you? What did you think?

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Nuance & register

The two senses are one form. Falling intonation gives conjecture: the speaker is guessing and knows they are guessing. Rising intonation turns the sentence outward and asks the listener to agree. In writing there is no intonation, so a ？ marks the second sense - and a でしょう at the end of a written forecast is always the first.

だろう is plain, and in speech it leans male. In writing - essays, novels, news commentary - だろう is simply the plain-style equivalent of でしょう and carries no gender at all. In conversation, bare 来(く)るだろう and the clipped だろ？ sound distinctly masculine and rather rough; women's casual speech prefers でしょ？ or ～よね？. でしょ is the everyday spoken shortening for everyone.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Nuance & register (cont.)

Weather forecasts live on でしょう. Japanese meteorologists do not assert; they say 晴(は)れるでしょう, 雪(ゆき)になるでしょう. Hearing that register helps fix the falling, conjectural sense in your ear.

The rising でしょう？ has an edge. It presses the listener toward your view. だから言(い)ったでしょう is a genuine "I told you so", and used upward it is bad news: 部長(ぶちょう)、これでいいでしょう？ is telling your boss what to think. The upward version is a question - これでよろしいでしょうか - where でしょう has become deference rather than pressure.

Do not guess about your own choices. ❌私(わたし)は明日(あした)行(い)くでしょう is strange for the same reason "I will probably go, I suppose" is strange about a decision that is yours to make. Use 行(い)きます or 行(い)くつもりです. でしょう about yourself is only natural for things outside your control: 私(わたし)も太(ふと)るでしょうね.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · だろう vs でしょう

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different level. でしょう is the ます／です-style form; だろう is the plain-style form and, in speech, noticeably blunt.

来週(らいしゅう)には終(お)わるでしょう。
It'll probably be finished by next week.

来週(らいしゅう)には終(お)わるだろう。
It'll probably be finished by next week.

The English is identical; the Japanese is not interchangeable. Use でしょう with anyone you would use です with. Use だろう in writing, in a diary, or with close friends - and be aware that a man and a woman saying だろう out loud will be heard differently, fairly or not.

Falling でしょう vs rising でしょう

Rule of thumb - falling = I think so, but I'm not sure. Rising = you know this too, don't you? One looks inward at the speaker's uncertainty; the other reaches out to the listener.

山田(やまだ)さんはこのニュースを知(し)らないでしょう。
Yamada probably doesn't know about this news. (falling - a guess)

山田(やまだ)さんもこのニュース、知(し)っているでしょう？
Yamada knows about this news too, right? (rising - checking with you)

The rising version assumes the listener is in a position to confirm, so it is used about things they can be expected to know - shared experience, obvious facts, or something you told them earlier. Using it about something they could not possibly know sounds like an interrogation.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · でしょう vs かもしれません

Rule of thumb - a rough scale of confidence: かもしれません (maybe, 50/50) → でしょう (probably) → はずです (it should be, by reasoning) → に違(ちが)いありません (it must be).

午後(ごご)から雪(ゆき)になるでしょう。
It'll probably turn to snow this afternoon.

午後(ごご)から雪(ゆき)になるかもしれません。
It might turn to snow this afternoon.

と思(おも)います sits alongside these but does something different: it labels the statement as the speaker's personal opinion rather than measuring likelihood. 来(こ)ないでしょう is a forecast about the world; 来(こ)ないと思(おも)います is what I think - which is why the second is often the politer choice when you might be wrong.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 彼(かれ)は学生(がくせい)だでしょう。 → ✅ 彼(かれ)は学生(がくせい)でしょう。
    Nouns and な-adjectives lose their だ before でしょう.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)、雨(あめ)が降(ふ)りますでしょう。 → ✅ 明日(あした)、雨(あめ)が降(ふ)るでしょう。
    でしょう follows plain forms. Doubling the politeness sounds like a bad announcement voice.

  • ❌ 私(わたし)は来年(らいねん)大学(だいがく)に入(はい)るでしょう。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は来年(らいねん)大学(だいがく)に入(はい)るつもりです。
    You do not speculate about your own decisions.

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、この案(あん)でいいでしょう？ → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、この案(あん)でよろしいでしょうか。
    Rising でしょう？ pushes the listener to agree. Upward, ask.

  • ❌ 静(しず)かだろうでしょう。 → ✅ 静(しず)かでしょう。
    だろう and でしょう are the same word; they never stack.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Practice 1

1. 天気(てんき)予報(よほう)によると、明日(あした)は寒(さむ)____。

明日(あした)は寒(さむ)いでしょう。

い-adjectives keep their い and simply take でしょう. A forecast is the textbook home of falling-tone でしょう: the speaker is predicting, not asserting.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Practice 2

2. 一緒(いっしょ)に習(なら)ったんだから、この漢字(かんじ)は読(よ)める（でしょう？・かもしれません）。

この漢字(かんじ)は読(よ)めるでしょう？

You studied it together, so you are appealing to shared knowledge and expecting agreement - that is rising でしょう？. かもしれません would mean you have no idea whether they can read it, which contradicts the first half of the sentence.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "He's probably still at work."

彼(かれ)はまだ会社(かいしゃ)にいるでしょう。 （plain: 彼(かれ)はまだ会社(かいしゃ)にいるだろう。）

でしょう attaches straight to the plain いる. Choose だろう only if the surrounding sentences are also plain style.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the problem: あの人(ひと)はたぶん先生(せんせい)だでしょう。

✅ あの人(ひと)はたぶん先生(せんせい)でしょう。

The だ has to go. でしょう is itself the conjectural form of だ／です, so keeping both is like saying "is probably is". Note how naturally たぶん pairs with the falling sense.

だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この店(みせ)、高(たか)いでしょう。 (b) この店(みせ)、高(たか)いでしょう？

(a), said with a falling tone, is a guess about a shop the speaker does not know first-hand: "this place is probably expensive." It might be a reason for not going in.

(b), rising, assumes the listener already knows - they have been there, or the prices are on the board in front of you - and asks them to agree: "pricey, isn't it?" Same eight characters; the tone decides who is supposed to know the answer.

んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation

Meaning · here's the background - or, as a question, so what's the story?

んです does not change what a sentence says. It changes what the sentence is for. Adding it tells the listener that this statement is tied to the situation you are both in: it explains something, or asks the other person to explain. んです is the spoken form, のです the written one, and the ん is simply の worn down by speech.

んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Formation

の is a nominaliser, so everything attaches to it the way it attaches to a noun - which is why nouns and な-adjectives need な in between.

んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + んです 遅(おく)れる → 遅(おく)れるんです
Verb (past) た-form + んです 遅(おく)れた → 遅(おく)れたんです
Verb (negative) ない-form + んです 行(い)かない → 行(い)かないんです
い-adjective plain form + んです 痛(いた)い → 痛(いた)いんです
な-adjective drop だ + な + んです 大変(たいへん)だ → 大変(たいへん)なんです
Noun noun + な + んです 学生(がくせい) → 学生(がくせい)なんです
Plain style んです → んだ 痛(いた)いんだ
Written / formal んです → のです 痛(いた)いのです
Question んですか どうした → どうしたんですか

んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Preface, softener んですが／んですけど 聞(き)きたいんですが

Rule of thumb - な after nouns and な-adjectives, nothing after verbs and い-adjectives. ❌学生(がくせい)だんです is the classic slip; 学生(がくせい)なんです is what you want.

んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Examples

どうしたんですか。
What's the matter?

頭(あたま)が痛(いた)いんです。
I've got a headache. (that's why I'm asking to go home)

すみません、道(みち)が分(わ)からないんですが…
Excuse me - I'm a bit lost…

彼女(かのじょ)はフランス人(じん)なんです。だからワインにくわしいんですよ。
She's French. That's why she knows so much about wine.

この漢字(かんじ)、なんて読(よ)むんですか。
How do you read this kanji?

んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Examples (cont.)

「どうして遅(おく)れたんですか。」「事故(じこ)で電車(でんしゃ)が止(と)まったんです。」
"Why were you late?" "The trains were stopped because of an accident."

明日(あした)、休(やす)ませていただけませんか。国(くに)から母(はは)が来(く)るんです。
Could I take tomorrow off? My mother is coming over from home.

実(じつ)は来月(らいげつ)引(ひ)っ越(こ)すのです。長(なが)い間(あいだ)お世話(せわ)になりました。
The fact is, I'm moving next month. Thank you for everything over the years.

Nuance & register

What んです adds is connection. 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っています reports the weather. 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っているんです offers it as the reason for something - why I'm wet, why the match is off, why I'd rather not go out. Nothing about the rain has changed; the sentence has been plugged into the conversation.

That is also its danger. A learner who ends every sentence with んです sounds as if they are permanently explaining themselves, or making excuses. In neutral self-introduction and plain description, use plain です／ます. 東京(とうきょう)に住(す)んでいます is a fact about you; 東京(とうきょう)に住(す)んでいるんです answers a question that was asked, out loud or in the air.

Nuance & register (cont.)

んですか can accuse. Because the question demands an explanation, tone decides everything. 何(なに)をしているんですか asked warmly is "oh, what are you up to?"; asked flatly it is "what do you think you're doing?" Similarly どうして来(こ)なかったんですか presses for a justification in a way that 来(こ)られませんでしたか does not. When you only want information and not a defence, drop the ん.

～んですが is one of the most useful shapes in the language. It sets up a request by explaining the situation first and then trailing off, leaving the listener to offer: ちょっとお願(ねが)いがあるんですが…, すみません、この席(せき)、空(あ)いてるんですけど…. The unfinished ending is the politeness.

Nuance & register (cont.)

Register. んです is spoken and polite; のです is written and formal, at home in essays, reports and public announcements, and it sounds stiff in conversation. Plain style gives んだ／のだ (忙(いそが)しいんだ) and, very commonly, bare の for questions and soft statements: どうしたの？ is neutral for everyone, while ～のよ and ～のね are female-leaning and ～んだよ male-leaning. The same の turns up as the casual reason connector んで (a worn-down ので): ちょっと急(いそ)いでるんで.

んです vs です

Rule of thumb - です states. んです explains. Ask yourself whether the sentence is answering a question, spoken or unspoken. If it is not, you probably do not need ん.

かぜを引(ひ)きました。
I've caught a cold. (a report - new information)

かぜを引(ひ)いたんです。
I've caught a cold. (that's why I look terrible / why I'm not coming)

The second sentence needs a context to lean on. Said out of nowhere with nothing to explain, it makes the listener hunt for the missing connection.

～んですか vs ～ですか

Rule of thumb - ～ですか asks for a fact. ～んですか asks for the story behind something you have just noticed.

明日(あした)、大阪(おおさか)へ行(い)きますか。
Are you going to Osaka tomorrow? (simple question)

明日(あした)、大阪(おおさか)へ行(い)くんですか。
Oh, you're going to Osaka tomorrow? (I've seen the ticket / heard something)

The ん version presupposes evidence. That makes it warm and interested with a friend, and prickly with a stranger or a subordinate, because you are asking them to account for something. Learners over-produce んですか in exactly the situations where it stings.

のです vs んです vs んだ

Rule of thumb - one grammar point, three registers: のです on paper, んです in polite speech, んだ／の among friends.

この問題(もんだい)は一人(ひとり)では解決(かいけつ)できないのです。
This problem cannot be solved alone. (written, formal)

一人(ひとり)じゃ無理(むり)なんだ。手伝(てつだ)ってくれない？
I can't manage on my own. Could you give me a hand?

Note that the plain form of the noun／な-adjective pattern is なんだ, not ❌だんだ - the な survives at every register.

Common mistakes

  • ❌ 彼(かれ)は医者(いしゃ)だんです。 → ✅ 彼(かれ)は医者(いしゃ)なんです。
    Nouns join の with な. The same goes for な-adjectives: ❌静(しず)かだんです → ✅静(しず)かなんです.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)行(い)きますんです。 → ✅ 明日(あした)行(い)くんです。
    んです follows plain forms; the politeness is already in the です at the end.

  • ❌ はじめまして。田中(たなか)なんです。 → ✅ はじめまして。田中(たなか)です。
    An introduction explains nothing, so there is no ん. Using it here sounds like you are correcting a misunderstanding.

  • ❌ (to a colleague, neutrally) どうして会議(かいぎ)に来(こ)なかったんですか。 → ✅ 昨日(きのう)の会議(かいぎ)、来(こ)られませんでしたか。
    んですか demands an explanation. If you do not want to sound like you are taking someone to task, ask the plain question.

んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Practice 1

1. どうしたんですか。 - - 財布(さいふ)をなくした____。

財布(さいふ)をなくしたんです。

The question asked for the story, so the answer supplies it. んです after the た-form marks this as the explanation the listener was after, rather than a bare announcement.

んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Practice 2

2. はじめまして、リンです。中国(ちゅうごく)から来(き)ました。（学生(がくせい)です・学生(がくせい)なんです）。

学生(がくせい)です。

A self-introduction is new information, not an explanation of anything, so plain です is right. 学生(がくせい)なんです would imply you were clearing up a misunderstanding - as if someone had just assumed you worked here.

んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Excuse me, I'd like to ask you something…"

すみません、ちょっと聞(き)きたいんですが…

The んですが preface explains your situation and then stops, leaving the listener room to respond. Finishing the sentence would make it heavier and slightly more demanding.

んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the problem: 来週(らいしゅう)は休(やす)みます。旅行(りょこう)だんです。

✅ 来週(らいしゅう)は休(やす)みます。旅行(りょこう)なんです。

旅行(りょこう) is a noun, so it joins んです with な, never with だ. The んです itself is well chosen here - the second sentence explains the first.

んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 毎日(まいにち)走(はし)っていますか。 (b) 毎日(まいにち)走(はし)っているんですか。

(a) is a neutral question - do you run every day? It could come out of nowhere, on a questionnaire or in small talk.

(b) is triggered by something: you have seen their running shoes by the door, or they just mentioned a marathon. It reads as "oh, so you run every day?" - engaged and curious with a friend, but slightly probing with someone you have just met, because it asks them to account for what you noticed.
