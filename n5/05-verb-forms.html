～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした

Meaning · the polite verb endings - non-past and past, affirmative and negative

These four are one system, not four separate words. ます is a politeness marker that happens to carry tense as well: it tells your listener you are speaking to them carefully. Every Japanese verb has a plain form and a ます form, and the choice between them is a choice about the relationship, not about the meaning.

Note - Japanese verbs fall into three classes, and every textbook names them differently. う-verbs are also called 五段ごだん (godan) verbs, or Group 1; る-verbs are also called 一段いちだん (ichidan) verbs, or Group 2; する and 来くる are the two irregulars, sometimes called Group 3. This page says う-verb and る-verb throughout.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Formation

All four endings attach to the same place - the ます-stem. Get the stem right and the rest is just picking one of four endings.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) final -u → -i, + ます 書かく → 書かきます
Verb (る-verb) drop る, + ます 食たべる → 食たべます
Verb (する) し + ます する → します
Verb (来くる) き + ます 来くる → 来きます

Then the four endings, which never change shape:

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Formation (cont.)

Tense and polarity Ending Example
Non-past, affirmative ます 飲のみます
Non-past, negative ません 飲のみません
Past, affirmative ました 飲のみました
Past, negative ませんでした 飲のみませんでした

For う-verbs, the final vowel moves from the う row to the い row. Every ending behaves the same way:

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - a verb ending in る is only a る-verb if the vowel before the る is i or e. But plenty of verbs that pass that test are still う-verbs: 帰かえる, 入はいる, 走はしる, 切きる, 知しる, 要いる. They give 帰かえります, 入はいります, 走はしります, 切きります, 知しります, 要いります - never ❌帰かえます. 着きる "wear" is a genuine る-verb (着きます); 切きる "cut" is not (切きります). Same sound, different class.

The ます-stem is not only for ます. It is the hub the rest of the language hangs off: 飲のみたい, 飲のみながら, 飲のみましょう, 飲のみやすい, 飲のみに行いく. Learning to find it once pays off many times.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Examples

毎朝まいあさ6時じ半はんに**起おきます**。
I get up at half past six every morning.

わたしはお酒さけを**飲のみません**。
I don't drink alcohol.

昨日きのうは一日中いちにちじゅう雨あめが**降ふりました**。
It rained all day yesterday.

すみません、この漢字かんじの読よみ方かたがわかりません。
Sorry - I don't know how to read this kanji.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Examples (cont.)

来週らいしゅうの金曜日きんようび、大阪おおさかから部長ぶちょうが**来きます**。
The department manager is coming up from Osaka next Friday.

先週せんしゅうのテストは、あまりよくできませんでした。
I didn't do very well on last week's test.

遅おそくまで仕事しごとをしていたので、昨日きのうは晩ばんご飯はんを**食たべませんでした**。
I was working late, so I didn't have any dinner yesterday.

明日あしたの会議かいぎは9時じに**始はじまります**ので、8時じ半はんまでに会社かいしゃに来きてください。
Tomorrow's meeting starts at nine, so please be at the office by eight thirty.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Nuance & register

ます is 丁寧語ていねいご - the polite, or "distal", style. It is the default with anyone you are not close to: shop staff, colleagues, teachers, strangers, and anyone you have just met. The plain forms (飲のむ, 飲のんだ, 飲のまない) are the "close" style, used with family, friends, and people clearly junior to you.

Three things learners get wrong about that split:

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ます is not honorific. It says nothing about respecting the subject of
    the sentence. Honorific and humble language (召めし上あがります, いたします) is a separate layer stacked on top.
  • Plain is not sloppy. Newspapers, essays, academic writing and most novels
    are written in plain form throughout. ます in an essay looks like a letter to the reader.
  • Plain form is grammatically required inside sentences. Before と思おもう,
    つもり, とき, ので, から and inside a clause modifying a noun, the verb goes plain regardless of how polite the sentence ends up: 明日あした行いくと思おもいます, not ❌行いきますと思おもいます. ます lives at the end of the sentence.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Nuance & register (cont.)

The non-past ます covers habits and the future alike - 毎日まいにち走はしります "I run every day" and 明日あした走はしります "I'll run tomorrow" - but never an action happening at this moment; that needs ～ています.

Within a single conversation you normally hold one register. Dropping one plain sentence into polite speech is a real and natural move - it reads as thinking aloud, or as a flash of warmth - but a whole reply in plain form to a stranger is rude. Going the other way, switching from plain back to ます with a friend signals distance, and Japanese speakers use it deliberately when they are annoyed.

In speech, ます is very often followed by softeners: ～ますね, ～ますよ, ～ますけど. Bare ～ます sentence after sentence sounds like a public announcement.

～ます vs the dictionary form

Rule of thumb - the ます-form is about who you are talking to. The plain form is what the grammar around the verb demands. They answer different questions, so they are not really alternatives.

来年らいねん、国くにに**帰かえります**。
I'm going home to my country next year.

来年らいねん、国くにに**帰かえる**と思おもいます。
I think I'll go home to my country next year.

The second sentence is exactly as polite as the first - the politeness is carried by 思おもいます at the end. The embedded verb has to be plain.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · ～ません vs ～ないです

Rule of thumb - the meaning is identical. ～ません is the tidier, more formal negative; ～ないです is the plain negative with a polite です bolted on, and belongs to relaxed speech.

今日きょうは時間じかんがありません。
I don't have time today. (careful, businesslike)

今日きょうはちょっと時間じかんがないです。
I haven't really got time today. (softer, conversational)

The same split runs through the past: ～ませんでした is the formal past negative, ～なかったです the colloquial one. In writing, an email to a client, or any situation with a script, use the ません column. With a colleague you get on with, ないです is friendlier.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · ～ませんか vs ～ましょう

Rule of thumb - ～ませんか asks whether you'd like to; ～ましょう assumes you would. Both are built on the same ます-stem.

一緒いっしょにお昼ひるを**食たべませんか**。
Would you like to have lunch together?

じゃ、そろそろ**行いきましょう**。
Right, let's get going then.

～ませんか leaves the other person free to say no, so it is the polite way to open an invitation. ～ましょう is for when the plan is already agreed, or when you are the one leading - a tour guide, a teacher, the person organising the evening. Using ～ましょう to make the first invitation to someone senior is pushy.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 食たべるます → ✅ 食たべます
    ます attaches to the ます-stem, never to the dictionary form. Drop the る first.

  • ❌ 飲のみませんかった → ✅ 飲のみませんでした
    ません has no past form of its own. You put the past tense on the copula behind it: ません + でした.

  • ❌ 帰かえます → ✅ 帰かえります
    帰かえる ends in る but is an う-verb, so the stem is 帰かえり, not 帰かえ.

  • ❌ この本ほんは高たかいますか。 → ✅ この本ほんは**高たかいですか**。
    ます is for verbs only. Adjectives and nouns take です.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 明日あした行いきますと思おもいます。 → ✅ 明日あした行いくと思おもいます。
    Inside a clause the verb is plain. ます only appears at the very end of the sentence.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Practice 1

1. 山田やまださんは毎日まいにち電車でんしゃで会社かいしゃに行い____。

行いきます

行いく is an う-verb, so the final -ku becomes -ki before ます: 行いく → 行いき + ます. The non-past covers habits, which is what 毎日まいにち calls for.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Practice 2

2. 昨日きのうは雪ゆきが（降ふりません・降ふりませんでした）。

昨日きのうは雪ゆきが**降ふりませんでした**。

昨日きのう fixes the sentence in the past, so the negative has to be past too. Japanese marks tense on the verb even when the time word already says it - you cannot leave the ending non-past the way English can.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I didn't watch television last night."

昨日きのうの夜よるはテレビを**見みませんでした**。

見みる is a る-verb, so the stem is just 見み: drop る, add ませんでした. The plain-form answer 見みなかった is equally correct if you are talking to a friend.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 今朝けさは朝あさご飯はんを食たべるませんでした。

✅ 今朝けさは朝あさご飯はんを**食たべませんでした**。

The dictionary form and the polite ending cannot both be there. 食たべる is a る-verb: remove る to get the stem 食たべ, then attach ませんでした.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 今いま、時間じかんがありますか。 (b) 今いま、時間じかんある？

They ask exactly the same question; only the relationship differs. (a) is the polite style - what you say to a colleague, a teacher, or anyone you are not close to. (b) is plain form with the question mark carried by rising intonation alone, which is what you use with a friend or a family member.

Say (b) to your manager and it sounds startlingly familiar; say (a) to a close friend and it sounds like you are about to deliver bad news.

する

Meaning · to do - and the machine that turns nouns into verbs

する is the busiest verb in Japanese. On its own it means "do", but its real job is bigger than that: stick する on the back of almost any noun and you have made a verb. 勉強べんきょう "study (n.)" → 勉強べんきょうする "to study". A very large share of the verbs you will ever meet are built this way.

Note - Japanese has exactly two irregular verbs: する and 来くる. All other verbs are う-verbs (五段ごだん / godan / Group 1) or る-verbs (一段いちだん / ichidan / Group 2). This page uses う-verb and る-verb. する is irregular because its stem changes vowel from form to form - し, さ, せ - so it cannot be predicted from the dictionary form.

する · Formation

する attaches to nouns. What it attaches to determines whether を can appear between them.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (漢語かんご compound) Noun + する 掃除そうじ → 掃除そうじする
Noun (with the object marker) Noun + を + する 掃除そうじ → 掃除そうじをする
Noun (loanword) Word + する コピー → コピーする
Noun (activity, no する-verb) Noun + を + する テニス → テニスをする
な-adjective / Noun ～に + する 静しずか → 静しずかにする
い-adjective ～く + する 短みじかい → 短みじかくする

する · Formation (cont.)

The verb itself conjugates like this. Nothing here follows the う-verb or る-verb rules, which is what "irregular" means - these have to be memorised:

する · Formation (cont.)

Form する With a noun
Dictionary する 運動うんどうする
Polite します 運動うんどうします
Plain negative しない 運動うんどうしない
Polite negative しません 運動うんどうしません
Plain past した 運動うんどうした
Polite past しました 運動うんどうしました
Plain past negative しなかった 運動うんどうしなかった

する · Formation (cont.)

Form する With a noun
Potential できる 運動うんどうできる
Conditional したら／すれば 運動うんどうしたら

Rule of thumb - the potential of する is できる, a completely separate word. There is no ❌されます, no ❌しられます. And because できる is intransitive, its object takes が: 料理りょうりができます, never ❌料理りょうりをできます.

する also carries several meanings that have nothing to do with "doing":

する · Formation (cont.)

  • Sensations, with が - 音おとがする "there's a sound",
    においがする "there's a smell", 味あじがする "it tastes of…".
  • Deciding, with に - わたしはカレーにします "I'll have the curry."
  • Wearing accessories - ネクタイをする, マスクをする, 時計とけいをする.
  • Costing - この傘かさは5000円えんしました "this umbrella cost
    5,000 yen."

する · Examples

今いま、宿題しゅくだいをしています。
I'm doing my homework right now.

わたしはコーヒーにします。
I'll have the coffee.

毎晩まいばん30分ぷんぐらい**運動うんどうします**。
I exercise for about thirty minutes every evening.

土曜日どようびに部屋へやの**掃除そうじをしました**。
I cleaned my room on Saturday.

する · Examples (cont.)

台所だいどころのほうから変へんな音おとがしました。
There was a strange noise from the direction of the kitchen.

あの人ひとはいつも青あおいネクタイをしている。
That guy always wears a blue tie.

来月らいげつ京都きょうとに行いくので、きのうホテルを**予約よやくしました**。
I'm going to Kyoto next month, so I booked a hotel yesterday.

息子むすこは東京とうきょうの大学だいがくで経済けいざいの**勉強べんきょうをしています**。
My son is studying economics at a university in Tokyo.

する · Nuance & register

Plain する is register-neutral: します is polite, する is plain, and nothing about the word itself is stiff or casual. What is worth knowing is that する sits at the bottom of a three-storey politeness building, and the other two storeys are irregular too:

  • 致いたす - humble. わたしがいたします "I'll do it", offering your own
    action deferentially. Extremely common in service and business Japanese: 説明せつめいいたします, ご案内あんないいたします.
  • なさる - honorific, for someone else's action. Note the irregular polite
    form なさいます (not ❌なさります). ゴルフをなさいますか。

する · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because noun + する is so productive, it is the standard way Japanese absorbs foreign words. Anything that arrives in katakana can become a verb overnight: チェックする, キャンセルする, ダウンロードする, シェアする. If you know the noun, you know the verb - this is the single fastest way to expand your active vocabulary.

One register caution: the 漢語かんご compounds (確認かくにんする, 購入こうにゅうする) are a shade more formal than their native equivalents (確たしかめる, 買かう). In an email they sound crisp and professional; in casual chat with a friend they sound like you are reading from a manual.

する · Nuance & register (cont.)

する also underlies fixed expressions that never translate word for word: 気きにする "worry about it", 大切たいせつにする "treasure something", 無理むりをする "overdo it". Meet these as vocabulary, not as grammar.

する · 勉強する vs 勉強をする

Rule of thumb - both are correct and mean the same thing. Insert を and the noun becomes a genuine object, which has two consequences: it can now be modified, and it now blocks a second を in the same clause.

日本語にほんごを**勉強べんきょうします**。
I study Japanese.

文法ぶんぽうの**勉強べんきょうをします**。
I do grammar study.

する · 勉強する vs 勉強をする (cont.)

The first sentence already has a を on 日本語にほんご, so ❌日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうをします is out - one clause, one を. The second sentence needs the を version, because 文法ぶんぽうの has to attach to a noun; ❌文法ぶんぽうの勉強べんきょうします does not work.

How to choose: bare する when the compound is behaving as a plain verb, をする when you want to describe or measure the activity itself - 軽かるい運動うんどうをする, 1時間じかん練習れんしゅうをする. Not every noun takes を equally happily: activity nouns (洗濯せんたく, 散歩さんぽ, 買かい物もの) accept it readily, while nouns naming a change of state (成功せいこうする, 完成かんせいする) essentially never appear with it.

する · ～にする vs ～になる

Rule of thumb - する means someone makes it so; なる means it comes to be so. Choosing from a menu is する, because you are the one deciding.

テレビの音おとを小ちいさくしてください。
Please turn the television down.

外そとが急きゅうに暗くらくなった。
It suddenly got dark outside.

する · ～にする vs ～になる (cont.)

The same pair runs through nouns: 先生せんせいにする "make someone a teacher / decide on the teacher" against 先生せんせいになる "become a teacher". If a person is deliberately causing the change, it is する; if the change simply happens, it is なる. Japanese leans on なる far more than English leans on "become", so when in doubt about a change of state, なる is usually the more natural choice.

する · する vs やる

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register. やる is the casual, slightly more physical word: it is what friends say to each other.

宿題しゅくだい、もうやった？
Have you done your homework yet?

宿題しゅくだいはもうしました。
I have already done my homework.

する · する vs やる (cont.)

やる cannot fuse directly onto a noun the way する does. You can say 勉強べんきょうする, and you can say 勉強べんきょうをやる in relaxed speech, but ❌勉強べんきょうやる is not a word. やる also means "give (to an animal, a plant, or someone clearly junior)" - 花はなに水みずをやる - and it can sound rough or blunt from the wrong mouth, so it is a poor default in polite company.

する · Common mistakes

  • ❌ するます → ✅ します
    する is irregular: the ます-stem is し, not ❌すり or ❌する.

  • ❌ 日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうをします。 → ✅ 日本語にほんごを**勉強べんきょうします**。
    Two を in one clause. Once 日本語にほんご has taken the object marker, 勉強べんきょう must fuse onto する directly.

  • ❌ テニスを遊あそびます。 → ✅ テニスをします。
    Interference from English "play tennis". 遊あそぶ means to play in the sense of having fun; sports and games take する.

  • ❌ わたしは料理りょうりをできます。 → ✅ わたしは料理りょうりができます。
    できる is the potential of する and it is intransitive, so what you can do is marked with が.

する · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 外そとで大おおきい音おとをしました。 → ✅ 外そとで大おおきい音おとがしました。
    Sensations - sounds, smells, tastes - take が with する. Nobody is doing anything here; the sound simply occurred.

する · Practice 1

1. 明日あしたまでにこの書類しょるいを確認かくにん____おきます。

確認かくにんしておきます

おく attaches to the て-form, and the て-form of する is して - irregular, not built from a stem. The whole compound 確認かくにんする conjugates exactly as する does.

する · Practice 2

2. 来週らいしゅうから新あたらしい仕事しごとを（します・なります）。

来週らいしゅうから新あたらしい仕事しごとをします。

The speaker is the one taking the job, so it is a deliberate action: する. なる would need a different frame entirely, such as 来週らいしゅうから新あたらしい仕事しごとになります, which would mean the job itself is what changes.

する · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "My daughter practises the piano for an hour every day."

娘むすめは毎日まいにち1時間じかんピアノの**練習れんしゅうをします**。

Both 練習れんしゅうします and 練習れんしゅうをします are possible, but here ピアノの has to modify a noun, so the を version is the natural one. Alternatively: 娘むすめは毎日まいにち1時間じかんピアノを練習れんしゅうします。

する · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この部屋へやは静しずかなしてください。

✅ この部屋へやでは**静しずかにして**ください。

To make something take on a quality you use the adverbial form, and for a な-adjective that is に, not な. な only appears directly before a noun.

する · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部屋へやを きれいに しました。 (b) 部屋へやが きれいに なりました。

(a) says somebody tidied the room - する puts an agent behind the change, and the room is marked with を as the thing acted on. (b) simply reports that the room ended up clean, with が marking it as the subject that changed.

Japanese speakers often prefer (b) even when they did the work themselves, because なる sounds less like claiming credit. That preference for describing outcomes rather than agents runs right through the language.

て-form

Meaning · the connective form - the shape a verb takes when the sentence isn't finished with it yet

The て-form is not a tense and not a politeness level. It is the form a word wears when something else is going to attach to it, or when the sentence carries on past it. On its own it says roughly "…and then", but its real importance is structural: ～ている, ～てください, ～てもいい, ～てから, ～てしまう, ～てあげる and dozens more all plug into it. Learn the て-form and a large slice of the grammar you meet later costs you nothing extra.

Note - verbs come in three classes: う-verbs (五段ごだん / godan / Group 1), る-verbs (一段いちだん / ichidan / Group 2), and the two irregulars する and 来くる (Group 3). This page says う-verb and る-verb. Only う-verbs have complicated て-forms; the other two classes are trivial.

て-form · Formation

る-verbs and the irregulars are one-liners. う-verbs change according to their final syllable, and that table is the thing to memorise.

て-form · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb, う・つ・る) → って 買かう → 買かって
Verb (う-verb, む・ぶ・ぬ) → んで 読よむ → 読よんで
Verb (う-verb, く) → いて 書かく → 書かいて
Verb (う-verb, ぐ) → いで 泳およぐ → 泳およいで
Verb (う-verb, す) → して 話はなす → 話はなして
Verb (行いく, exception) → って 行いく → 行いって
Verb (る-verb) drop る, + て 開あける → 開あけて
Verb (する) して 掃除そうじする → 掃除そうじして
Verb (来くる) きて 来くる → 来きて

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (negative) ない → なくて／ないで 行いかない → 行いかなくて／行いかないで
い-adjective い → くて 寒さむい → 寒さむくて

Noun + で 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいで

The う-verb rows again, one verb at a time, because this is the table you will come back to:

て-form · Formation (cont.)

て-form · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the た-form uses exactly these sound changes, with た/だ in place of て/で. 読よんで → 読よんだ, 書かいて → 書かいた, 話はなして → 話はなした, 行いって → 行いった. Learn one table and you have learned both. This is the single best return on memorisation effort in beginner Japanese.

Note - 行いく is the exception every learner trips over. By the く rule it ought to be ❌行いいて; it is 行いって. Nothing else in ordinary Japanese behaves this way. Watch out too for う-verbs that look like る-verbs: 帰かえる → 帰かえって (not ❌帰かえて), 走はしる → 走はしって, 入はいる → 入はいって.

What the て-form is for, in rough order of how soon you will need it:

て-form · Formation (cont.)

  • Linking events in sequence - 起おきて、顔かおを洗あらって、出でかけます。
  • A continuing action or a state - ～ている.
  • Requests - ～てください.
  • Permission and prohibition - ～てもいい, ～てはいけない.
  • Cause, when the second clause is a feeling or a result you did not choose.
  • Manner or means - 歩あるいて行いく "go on foot".

て-form · Examples

ちょっと**待まって**。
Hang on a second.

朝あさご飯はんを**食たべて**、会社かいしゃに行いきます。
I have breakfast and then go to work.

弟おとうとは今いまシャワーを**浴あびて**います。
My brother is in the shower right now.

駅えきまで**歩あるいて**10分ぷんぐらいかかります。
It's about a ten-minute walk to the station.

て-form · Examples (cont.)

電気でんきを**消けして**、部屋へやを出でました。
I turned the light off and left the room.

田中たなかさんは背せが**高たかくて**、髪かみの長ながい人ひとです。
Tanaka is the tall one with long hair.

昨日きのうは朝あさから雨あめが**降ふって**、試合しあいが中止ちゅうしになりました。
It rained from the morning yesterday and the match was called off.

この辺へんは夜よるになると店みせが全部ぜんぶ閉しまって、人ひとも歩あるいていません。
Around here everything shuts at night and there's nobody about.

て-form · Nuance & register

The て-form carries no politeness of its own - politeness is decided by whatever ends the sentence. 食たべて行いく and 食たべて行いきます differ only at the tail. That is exactly why it is so useful: a long chain of て-forms lets you build a complicated sentence and only worry about register at the very end.

A bare て-form left at the end of a sentence is a casual request: ちょっと待まって, 見みせて, 手伝てつだって. It is ～てください with the polite part bitten off, so it is friendly among equals and rude upwards. Softening it with ね - 待まってね - makes it gentler; adding よ makes it more insistent.

Two spoken contractions to expect early, because they are everywhere:

て-form · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ～ている → ～てる: 何なにしてるの？ , 待まってるよ.
  • ～ておく → ～とく, ～てしまう → ～ちゃう: 買かっとく,
    食たべちゃった.

In formal writing, long chains of て-forms look chatty. Written Japanese often replaces the linking て with the bare ます-stem instead - 朝あさ6時じに 起おき、新聞しんぶんを読よむ - which is compact and noticeably literary. You do not need to produce it at N5, but you will see it.

One real limitation: the て-form does not mark why two events are next to each other. It only says they belong together, and the listener infers sequence, cause, or simple accompaniment from context. When you need the reason to be unmistakable, use から or ので instead.

て-form · ～て vs ～てから

Rule of thumb - plain ～て says the events belong together; ～てから says the first one finished before the second started, and puts weight on that order.

手てを**洗あらって**、ご飯はんを食たべます。
I'll wash my hands and eat.

手てを**洗あらってから**、ご飯はんを食たべます。
I'll eat after washing my hands.

The difference is emphasis. With plain て the two actions are simply a routine; with てから you are insisting on the order - first this, then that. So てから is what you want in instructions and in warnings, and it sounds fussy when the order was never in doubt.

て-form · ～なくて vs ～ないで

Rule of thumb - ～なくて gives a reason; ～ないで describes how something was done, or asks someone not to do it.

バスが**来こなくて**、会議かいぎに遅おくれました。
The bus didn't come, so I was late for the meeting.

朝あさご飯はんを**食たべないで**学校がっこうに行いきました。
I went to school without eating breakfast.

て-form · ～なくて vs ～ないで (cont.)

Swap them and you get nonsense: ❌バスが来こないで、会議かいぎに 遅おくれました. A useful test - if you could put "so" or "because" in the English, use なくて; if you could put "without", use ないで. Requests are always ないで: ここに車くるまを止とめないでください.

Adjectives and nouns only have the なくて option: 安やすくなくて, 学生がくせいじゃなくて.

て-form · ～て (cause) vs ～から

Rule of thumb - ～て can only introduce a cause when the second clause is something outside your control: a feeling, a result, an inability. If the second clause is a request, an invitation, or a decision, you must use から or ので.

ニュースを聞きいて、びっくりしました。
I was startled when I heard the news.

今日きょうは忙いそがしいから、早はやく帰かえってください。
I'm busy today, so please go home early.

て-form · ～て (cause) vs ～から (cont.)

The second sentence cannot use て: ❌忙いそがしくて、早はやく 帰かえってください is ungrammatical, because a て cause clause refuses to be followed by a command, a request, an invitation or a volitional. This is the most common way learners misuse the て-form, and it is invisible in translation - English "so" covers both.

て-form · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行いいて → ✅ 行いって
    行いく is the one exception to the く → いて rule. Everything else with く behaves normally: 書かいて, 聞きいて, 歩あるいて.

  • ❌ 飲のんて → ✅ 飲のんで
    む・ぶ・ぬ take んで with a voiced で, never んて. Same for 遊あそんで and 死しんで.

  • ❌ 切きて → ✅ 切きって
    切きる is an う-verb even though it ends in る, so it takes って. Contrast 着きる "wear", which really is a る-verb: 着きて.

  • ❌ この部屋へやは広ひろいで明あかるいです。 → ✅ この部屋へやは**広ひろくて**明あかるいです。
    で is the connective for nouns and な-adjectives. い-adjectives drop the い and take くて.

て-form · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 食たべるてください。 → ✅ 食たべてください。
    ください attaches to the て-form, not to the dictionary form. Drop the る before adding て.

て-form · Practice 1

1. 窓まどを開あけ____、部屋へやの空気くうきを入いれかえた。

開あけて

開あける is a る-verb: drop る and add て. Two actions in sequence, so the first verb goes into the て-form and only the last one carries the tense.

て-form · Practice 2

2. 時間じかんが（なくて・ないで）、昼ひるご飯はんが食たべられませんでした。

時間じかんがなくて、昼ひるご飯はんが食たべられませんでした。

This is a cause - "because there was no time" - and causes take なくて. ないで would describe the manner of doing something, which makes no sense with an inability in the second clause.

て-form · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I got up at six, read the newspaper, and left the house at seven."

6時じに**起おきて**、新聞しんぶんを**読よんで**、7時じに家いえを出でました。

Only the final verb takes tense and politeness; the て-forms in front of it are neutral. 起おきる is a る-verb (起おきて) and 読よむ is an う-verb ending in む, so it takes んで.

て-form · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 雨あめが降ふって、傘かさを持もって行いってください。

✅ 雨あめが**降ふっているから**、傘かさを持もって行いってください。

A て clause used as a cause cannot be followed by a request. Swap it for から or ので. The second 持もって is fine - that one is manner, not cause.

て-form · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 薬くすりを飲のんで、寝ねます。 (b) 薬くすりを飲のんでから、寝ねます。

Both describe the same two actions in the same order. (a) simply chains them - it is how you would describe your evening routine, with no particular stress on sequence. (b) foregrounds the order, so it reads as "not before the medicine - medicine first, then bed."

That is why (b) is what a pharmacist or a parent would say: てから is the form you reach for when getting the order right actually matters.

た-form / plain past

Meaning · the plain past - "did", "was" - and the base that a whole family of patterns attaches to

The た-form is the plain-style counterpart of ～ました. It says an event happened, or that something is now finished. It is also structural: ～たら, ～たり, ～たことがある, ～たあとで and ～たほうがいい are all built on it, and none of them will make sense until this form is automatic.

Note - verbs come in three classes: う-verbs (五段ごだん / godan / Group 1), る-verbs (一段いちだん / ichidan / Group 2), and the irregulars する and 来くる (Group 3). This page says う-verb and る-verb.

た-form / plain past · Formation

Rule of thumb - the た-form is the て-form with た instead of て. Every sound change is identical; only the last letter differs, and で becomes だ. 飲のんで → 飲のんだ. 書かいて → 書かいた. If you know one, you already know the other.

た-form / plain past · Examples (cont.)

これは去年きょねん京都きょうとで**撮とった**写真しゃしんです。
This is a photo I took in Kyoto last year.

大学だいがくのとき習ならった中国語ちゅうごくごを、すっかり**忘わすれた**。
I've completely forgotten the Chinese I learned at university.

空港くうこうに**着ついた**ら、すぐ連絡れんらくしますので、心配しんぱいしないでください。
I'll get in touch as soon as I land, so please don't worry.

た-form / plain past · Nuance & register

The た-form is plain style. Ending a sentence with it to a stranger or a superior is too familiar - 食たべた to your boss should be 食たべました. Inside a sentence, though, plain is compulsory regardless of politeness: the verb before とき, ので, から, あとで, and before any noun it modifies, goes into the た-form even when the sentence ends politely. 買かった本ほんを読よんでいます is correct; ❌買かいました本ほん is not.

The た-form does more than past tense. Three uses that surprise learners:

た-form / plain past · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • Completion, not past time. バスが来きた! - the bus is arriving right
    now; you are reporting that the awaited event has landed. Likewise あ、あった "there it is", said while still looking at the thing.
  • A sudden recollection. 明日あした、休やすみだったね - "oh, tomorrow's
    a day off, isn't it." Nothing is past; the た marks the fact newly clicking into place.
  • A sharp command in casual speech. 帰かえった、帰かえった! -
    "go on, off you go!", typical of a shopkeeper closing up. Rough, and worth recognising rather than using.

た-form / plain past · Nuance & register (cont.)

In writing, plain past is the default: novels, newspapers and reports are written in た, not ました. So the た-form is not "informal Japanese", it is plain-style Japanese - a different axis from politeness.

In casual speech the た-form very often turns up inside the contraction ～ちゃった (from ～てしまった), and ～じゃった after ん-forms: 忘わすれちゃった "oh no, I forgot", 飲のんじゃった "I went and drank it." Learners hear these long before they are taught them.

た-form / plain past · ～た vs ～ました

Rule of thumb - same tense, different register. ました is polite style; た is plain style, and is also the only option inside a sentence.

先週せんしゅう、新あたらしい自転車じてんしゃを**買かいました**。
I bought a new bicycle last week.

先週せんしゅう買かった自転車じてんしゃが、もう壊こわれました。
The bicycle I bought last week has already broken.

In the second sentence the politeness sits on 壊こわれました at the end. The relative clause modifying 自転車じてんしゃ has to be plain - this is a hard grammatical rule, not a stylistic preference.

た-form / plain past · ～た vs ～ていた

Rule of thumb - ～た reports an event as a single point; ～ていた looks inside it and describes it as ongoing, repeated, or a state that held for a while.

昨日きのう、本ほんを**読よんだ**。
I read a book yesterday.

昨日きのうの夜よるはずっと本ほんを**読よんでいた**。
I was reading all last night.

た-form / plain past · ～た vs ～ていた (cont.)

The trap is verbs of change. 結婚けっこんした means the wedding happened; 結婚けっこんしている means the person is married now. 知しる behaves the same way: "I know" is 知しっています, never a plain 知しります, while the negative flips back - 知しりません "I don't know", 知しりませんでした "I didn't know."

た-form / plain past · ～た vs ～たことがある

Rule of thumb - ～た reports a particular occasion; ～たことがある reports that the thing is somewhere in your experience, with no occasion attached.

2019年ねんに北海道ほっかいどうへ**行いった**。
I went to Hokkaido in 2019.

北海道ほっかいどうへ**行いったことがある**。
I've been to Hokkaido before.

た-form / plain past · ～た vs ～たことがある (cont.)

Because たことがある is about experience rather than occasion, it clashes with a specific time word: ❌昨日きのう行いったことがあります. It also implies the experience is unusual enough to be worth mentioning, so it sounds odd about everyday things - 今日きょうご飯はんを食たべたことが あります is grammatical and absurd.

た-form / plain past · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 寒さむいでした → ✅ 寒さむかったです
    い-adjectives carry their own past tense. The い becomes かった, and です is added afterwards for politeness without changing anything.

  • ❌ 行いいた → ✅ 行いった
    行いく breaks the く → いた rule. Compare 聞きいた, 着ついた, which are regular.

  • ❌ 遊あそんた → ✅ 遊あそんだ
    む・ぶ・ぬ verbs take the voiced んだ. So do ぐ verbs: 泳およいだ, never ❌泳およいた.

  • ❌ 食たべるた → ✅ 食たべた
    For a る-verb you replace る with た; you never keep both.

た-form / plain past · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 昨日きのう、映画えいがを見みます。 → ✅ 昨日きのう、映画えいがを**見みました**。
    A past time word does not conjugate the verb for you. Japanese marks tense on the verb every time.

た-form / plain past · Practice 1

1. 先週せんしゅう、友達ともだちと山やまに登のぼ____。

登のぼった（or 登のぼりました）

登のぼる is an う-verb ending in る, so it takes った - the same sound change as the て-form 登のぼって. Note that る-looking う-verbs are common; 登のぼる is one of them.

た-form / plain past · Practice 2

2. 富士山ふじさんに（登のぼった・登のぼったことがある）？ - うん、2回かいあるよ。

富士山ふじさんに**登のぼったことがある**？

The reply counts occasions, so the question is about experience in general, not about one particular climb. Plain 登のぼった would be asking whether you climbed it on some specific occasion both speakers already have in mind.

た-form / plain past · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The film I watched last night wasn't very interesting."

昨日きのうの夜よる見みた映画えいがは、あまり面白おもしろくなかった。

The verb inside the relative clause modifying 映画えいが must be plain past: 見みた, not ❌見みました. The final adjective carries its own past negative, 面白おもしろくなかった.

た-form / plain past · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 去年きょねんの冬ふゆはあまり雪ゆきが降ふりませんだった。

✅ 去年きょねんの冬ふゆはあまり雪ゆきが**降ふりませんでした**。

ません takes でした, not ❌んだった. The plain-form equivalent is 降ふらなかった - note that the plain past negative is built on ない, which inflects like an い-adjective.

た-form / plain past · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 財布さいふを落おとした。 (b) 財布さいふを落おとしてしまった。

(a) is a neutral report: the wallet got dropped, and that is the fact. (b) adds ～てしまう, which colours the same event with regret or with the sense that it is now irreversible - closer to "I've gone and dropped my wallet."

Both are plain past. The tense is identical; what changes is how the speaker feels about it, which is a job Japanese hands to an auxiliary rather than to intonation.

ない-form

Meaning · the plain negative - "doesn't", "won't", "isn't" - and the base a whole set of later patterns attaches to

The ない-form is the plain-style negative, the partner of ～ません. It is also the foundation of ～ないでください, ～なくてもいい, ～なければならない, ～ないほうがいい and ～ないで. Get the stem wrong here and every one of those patterns comes out wrong, which is why it repays care.

傘かさを**持もって行いかない**と、ぬれるよ。
If you don't take an umbrella you'll get soaked.

ここでは写真しゃしんを**撮とらないで**ください。
Please don't take photographs here.

試験しけんが来週らいしゅうなのに、弟おとうとは全然ぜんぜん勉強べんきょうしない。
The exam is next week and my brother isn't studying at all.

給料きゅうりょうが何年なんねんも**上あがらない**ので、新あたらしい仕事しごとを探さがしている。
My pay hasn't gone up in years, so I'm looking for a new job.

ない-form · Nuance & register

ない is plain style, so as a sentence ending it belongs with friends and family. To a customer or a superior you want ～ません. The middle option, ～ないです, is polite but relaxed, and is what colleagues say to each other all day.

Bare ない at the end of a question - 行いかない？ - is a casual invitation, not a plain question: "want to come?" It is the plain-style twin of ～ませんか, and it is extremely common. 今晩こんばんご飯はん 食たべない？

The negative also softens requests, exactly as it does in English. Compare 窓まどを開あけてくれますか with 窓まどを開あけてくれませんか - the negative version is more polite, because a negative question makes it easier to refuse.

ない-form · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two things to keep separate. ない is the plain negative of a verb; the adjective ない meaning "there isn't" is a different word that happens to look the same. And in speech, ん is a very common contraction of ない in the Kansai area and in men's rough speech (分わからん, 知しらん) - recognise it, but stick to ない yourself until your speech is firmly anchored somewhere.

Written formal Japanese prefers ず and ぬ in fixed phrases - 知しらずに, やむを得えず - and uses ではない where speech uses じゃない. じゃない is a contraction of ではない, and it is casual: never write it in a report.

ない-form · ～ない vs ～ません vs ～ないです

Rule of thumb - one meaning, three registers. ない plain, ないです polite-casual, ません polite-formal.

明日あしたは学校がっこうに**行いかない**。
I'm not going to school tomorrow. (to a friend)

明日あしたは学校がっこうに**行いきません**。
I will not be going to school tomorrow. (to a teacher)

ない-form · ～ない vs ～ません vs ～ないです (cont.)

Note the different bases: ません is built on the ます-stem (行いき), while ない is built on the あ-row stem (行いか). They are not two endings on the same stem, which is why you cannot convert one to the other by ear.

ない-form · The verb ない vs the adjective ない

Rule of thumb - three different negatives all end in ない, and each is formed from a different base: verbs take あ-row + ない, い-adjectives swap い for くない, and nouns and な-adjectives take じゃない.

このカメラは**動うごかない**。
This camera doesn't work.

このカメラは**新あたらしくない**。
This camera isn't new.

このカメラは**防水ぼうすいじゃない**。
This camera isn't waterproof.

ない-form · The verb ない vs the adjective ない (cont.)

All three then inflect identically, because ない is an い-adjective: 動うごかなかった, 新あたらしくなかった, 防水ぼうすいじゃなかった. That shared tail is why ❌動うごかないでした and ❌新あたらしくないでした are wrong - an い-adjective never takes でした.

ない-form · ある → ない vs いる → いない

Rule of thumb - both mean "there isn't", but they split the world the same way their positives do: ある/ない for things, いる/いない for people and animals.

冷蔵庫れいぞうこに何なにもない。
There's nothing in the fridge.

教室きょうしつにはだれもいない。
There's nobody in the classroom.

いる is a perfectly regular る-verb, so its negative is formed the ordinary way: drop る, add ない. ある is the odd one out - it simply becomes ない. The polite forms are ありません and いません respectively, and both keep the thing-versus-creature split.

ない-form · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 買かあない → ✅ 買かわない
    う-verbs ending in う insert わ, not あ. Same for 言いわない and 会あわない.

  • ❌ お金かねがあらない → ✅ お金かねがない
    ある has no regular negative. Its negative is the word ない, and the polite form is ありません.

  • ❌ 食たべるない → ✅ 食たべない
    For a る-verb you replace る with ない. Keeping both is the commonest beginner slip of all.

  • ❌ この部屋へやは広ひろいない → ✅ この部屋へやは**広ひろくない**
    い-adjectives do not take the verb negative. Swap the final い for くない.

ない-form · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 来くる → 来きない → ✅ 来くる → 来こない
    The kanji stays the same but the reading changes to こ in the negative. Say it aloud when you practise, or the mistake will be invisible on paper.

ない-form · Practice 1

1. 明日あしたは日曜日にちようびだから、会社かいしゃに行い____。

行いかない

行いく is an う-verb ending in く, so the final -ku drops to -ka before ない. The polite version is 行いきません, built on a different stem altogether.

ない-form · Practice 2

2. 冷蔵庫れいぞうこに牛乳ぎゅうにゅうが（ない・いない）。

冷蔵庫れいぞうこに牛乳ぎゅうにゅうがない。

Milk is not alive, so the existence verb is ある, whose negative is the irregular ない. いない is the negative of いる, reserved for people and animals.

ない-form · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I don't drink coffee at night, so I sleep well."

夜よるはコーヒーを**飲のまない**ので、よく寝ねられます。

飲のむ is an う-verb ending in む, so the negative stem is 飲のま. ので requires the plain form in front of it even when the sentence ends politely.

ない-form · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昨日きのうは友達ともだちに会ああなかった。

✅ 昨日きのうは友達ともだちに**会あわなかった**。

会あう ends in う, so the negative stem is 会あわ, not ❌会ああ. The past negative is then formed the い-adjective way: ない → なかった.

ない-form · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 朝あさご飯はんを食たべないで出でかけた。 (b) 朝あさご飯はんを食たべなくて、おなかがすいた。

(a) uses ないで, which describes the manner of the second action: he went out, and the not-eating is how he did it - "without eating breakfast."

(b) uses なくて, which gives the reason for the second clause: because he didn't eat, he got hungry. Substituting one for the other produces something that a native speaker will hear as broken, even though English uses the same word "not" in both.
