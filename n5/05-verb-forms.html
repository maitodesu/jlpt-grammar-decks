～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした

Meaning · the polite verb endings - non-past and past, affirmative and negative

These four are one system, not four separate words. ます is a politeness marker that happens to carry tense as well: it tells your listener you are speaking to them carefully. Every Japanese verb has a plain form and a ます form, and the choice between them is a choice about the relationship, not about the meaning.

Note - Japanese verbs fall into three classes, and every textbook names them differently. う-verbs are also called 五段(ごだん) (godan) verbs, or Group 1; る-verbs are also called 一段(いちだん) (ichidan) verbs, or Group 2; する and 来(く)る are the two irregulars, sometimes called Group 3. This page says う-verb and る-verb throughout.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Formation

All four endings attach to the same place - the ます-stem. Get the stem right and the rest is just picking one of four endings.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) final -u → -i, + ます 書(か)く → 書(か)きます
Verb (る-verb) drop る, + ます 食(た)べる → 食(た)べます
Verb (する) し + ます する → します
Verb (来(く)る) き + ます 来(く)る → 来(き)ます

Then the four endings, which never change shape:

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Formation (cont.)

Tense and polarity Ending Example
Non-past, affirmative ます 飲(の)みます
Non-past, negative ません 飲(の)みません
Past, affirmative ました 飲(の)みました
Past, negative ませんでした 飲(の)みませんでした

For う-verbs, the final vowel moves from the う row to the い row. Every ending behaves the same way:

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - a verb ending in る is only a る-verb if the vowel before the る is i or e. But plenty of verbs that pass that test are still う-verbs: 帰(かえ)る, 入(はい)る, 走(はし)る, 切(き)る, 知(し)る, 要(い)る. They give 帰(かえ)ります, 入(はい)ります, 走(はし)ります, 切(き)ります, 知(し)ります, 要(い)ります - never ❌帰(かえ)ます. 着(き)る "wear" is a genuine る-verb (着(き)ます); 切(き)る "cut" is not (切(き)ります). Same sound, different class.

The ます-stem is not only for ます. It is the hub the rest of the language hangs off: 飲(の)みたい, 飲(の)みながら, 飲(の)みましょう, 飲(の)みやすい, 飲(の)みに行(い)く. Learning to find it once pays off many times.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Examples

毎朝(まいあさ)6時(じ)半(はん)に起(お)きます。
I get up at half past six every morning.

わたしはお酒(さけ)を飲(の)みません。
I don't drink alcohol.

昨日(きのう)は一日中(いちにちじゅう)雨(あめ)が降(ふ)りました。
It rained all day yesterday.

すみません、この漢字(かんじ)の読(よ)み方(かた)がわかりません。
Sorry - I don't know how to read this kanji.

来週(らいしゅう)の金曜日(きんようび)、大阪(おおさか)から部長(ぶちょう)が来(き)ます。
The department manager is coming up from Osaka next Friday.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Examples (cont.)

先週(せんしゅう)のテストは、あまりよくできませんでした。
I didn't do very well on last week's test.

遅(おそ)くまで仕事(しごと)をしていたので、昨日(きのう)は晩(ばん)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べませんでした。
I was working late, so I didn't have any dinner yesterday.

明日(あした)の会議(かいぎ)は9時(じ)に始(はじ)まりますので、8時(じ)半(はん)までに会社(かいしゃ)に来(き)てください。
Tomorrow's meeting starts at nine, so please be at the office by eight thirty.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Nuance & register

ます is 丁寧語(ていねいご) - the polite, or "distal", style. It is the default with anyone you are not close to: shop staff, colleagues, teachers, strangers, and anyone you have just met. The plain forms (飲(の)む, 飲(の)んだ, 飲(の)まない) are the "close" style, used with family, friends, and people clearly junior to you.

Three things learners get wrong about that split:

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ます is not honorific. It says nothing about respecting the subject of
    the sentence. Honorific and humble language (召(め)し上(あ)がります, いたします) is a separate layer stacked on top.
  • Plain is not sloppy. Newspapers, essays, academic writing and most novels
    are written in plain form throughout. ます in an essay looks like a letter to the reader.
  • Plain form is grammatically required inside sentences. Before と思(おも)う,
    つもり, とき, ので, から and inside a clause modifying a noun, the verb goes plain regardless of how polite the sentence ends up: 明日(あした)行(い)くと思(おも)います, not ❌行(い)きますと思(おも)います. ます lives at the end of the sentence.

The non-past ます covers habits and the future alike - 毎日(まいにち)走(はし)ります "I run every day" and 明日(あした)走(はし)ります "I'll run tomorrow" - but never an action happening at this moment; that needs ～ています.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Nuance & register (cont.)

Within a single conversation you normally hold one register. Dropping one plain sentence into polite speech is a real and natural move - it reads as thinking aloud, or as a flash of warmth - but a whole reply in plain form to a stranger is rude. Going the other way, switching from plain back to ます with a friend signals distance, and Japanese speakers use it deliberately when they are annoyed.

In speech, ます is very often followed by softeners: ～ますね, ～ますよ, ～ますけど. Bare ～ます sentence after sentence sounds like a public announcement.

～ます vs the dictionary form

Rule of thumb - the ます-form is about who you are talking to. The plain form is what the grammar around the verb demands. They answer different questions, so they are not really alternatives.

来年(らいねん)、国(くに)に帰(かえ)ります。
I'm going home to my country next year.

来年(らいねん)、国(くに)に帰(かえ)ると思(おも)います。
I think I'll go home to my country next year.

The second sentence is exactly as polite as the first - the politeness is carried by 思(おも)います at the end. The embedded verb has to be plain.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · ～ません vs ～ないです

Rule of thumb - the meaning is identical. ～ません is the tidier, more formal negative; ～ないです is the plain negative with a polite です bolted on, and belongs to relaxed speech.

今日(きょう)は時間(じかん)がありません。
I don't have time today. (careful, businesslike)

今日(きょう)はちょっと時間(じかん)がないです。
I haven't really got time today. (softer, conversational)

The same split runs through the past: ～ませんでした is the formal past negative, ～なかったです the colloquial one. In writing, an email to a client, or any situation with a script, use the ません column. With a colleague you get on with, ないです is friendlier.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · ～ませんか vs ～ましょう

Rule of thumb - ～ませんか asks whether you'd like to; ～ましょう assumes you would. Both are built on the same ます-stem.

一緒(いっしょ)にお昼(ひる)を食(た)べませんか。
Would you like to have lunch together?

じゃ、そろそろ行(い)きましょう。
Right, let's get going then.

～ませんか leaves the other person free to say no, so it is the polite way to open an invitation. ～ましょう is for when the plan is already agreed, or when you are the one leading - a tour guide, a teacher, the person organising the evening. Using ～ましょう to make the first invitation to someone senior is pushy.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 食(た)べるます → ✅ 食(た)べます
    ます attaches to the ます-stem, never to the dictionary form. Drop the る first.

  • ❌ 飲(の)みませんかった → ✅ 飲(の)みませんでした
    ません has no past form of its own. You put the past tense on the copula behind it: ません + でした.

  • ❌ 帰(かえ)ます → ✅ 帰(かえ)ります
    帰(かえ)る ends in る but is an う-verb, so the stem is 帰(かえ)り, not 帰(かえ).

  • ❌ この本(ほん)は高(たか)いますか。 → ✅ この本(ほん)は高(たか)いですか。
    ます is for verbs only. Adjectives and nouns take です.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)行(い)きますと思(おも)います。 → ✅ 明日(あした)行(い)くと思(おも)います。
    Inside a clause the verb is plain. ます only appears at the very end of the sentence.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Practice 1

1. 山田(やまだ)さんは毎日(まいにち)電車(でんしゃ)で会社(かいしゃ)に行(い)____。

行(い)きます

行(い)く is an う-verb, so the final -ku becomes -ki before ます: 行(い)く → 行(い)き + ます. The non-past covers habits, which is what 毎日(まいにち) calls for.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Practice 2

2. 昨日(きのう)は雪(ゆき)が（降(ふ)りません・降(ふ)りませんでした）。

昨日(きのう)は雪(ゆき)が降(ふ)りませんでした。

昨日(きのう) fixes the sentence in the past, so the negative has to be past too. Japanese marks tense on the verb even when the time word already says it - you cannot leave the ending non-past the way English can.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I didn't watch television last night."

昨日(きのう)の夜(よる)はテレビを見(み)ませんでした。

見(み)る is a る-verb, so the stem is just 見(み): drop る, add ませんでした. The plain-form answer 見(み)なかった is equally correct if you are talking to a friend.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 今朝(けさ)は朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べるませんでした。

✅ 今朝(けさ)は朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べませんでした。

The dictionary form and the polite ending cannot both be there. 食(た)べる is a る-verb: remove る to get the stem 食(た)べ, then attach ませんでした.

～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 今(いま)、時間(じかん)がありますか。 (b) 今(いま)、時間(じかん)ある？

They ask exactly the same question; only the relationship differs. (a) is the polite style - what you say to a colleague, a teacher, or anyone you are not close to. (b) is plain form with the question mark carried by rising intonation alone, which is what you use with a friend or a family member.

Say (b) to your manager and it sounds startlingly familiar; say (a) to a close friend and it sounds like you are about to deliver bad news.

する

Meaning · to do - and the machine that turns nouns into verbs

する is the busiest verb in Japanese. On its own it means "do", but its real job is bigger than that: stick する on the back of almost any noun and you have made a verb. 勉強(べんきょう) "study (n.)" → 勉強(べんきょう)する "to study". A very large share of the verbs you will ever meet are built this way.

Note - Japanese has exactly two irregular verbs: する and 来(く)る. All other verbs are う-verbs (五段(ごだん) / godan / Group 1) or る-verbs (一段(いちだん) / ichidan / Group 2). This page uses う-verb and る-verb. する is irregular because its stem changes vowel from form to form - し, さ, せ - so it cannot be predicted from the dictionary form.

する · Formation

する attaches to nouns. What it attaches to determines whether を can appear between them.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (漢語(かんご) compound) Noun + する 掃除(そうじ) → 掃除(そうじ)する
Noun (with the object marker) Noun + を + する 掃除(そうじ) → 掃除(そうじ)をする
Noun (loanword) Word + する コピー → コピーする
Noun (activity, no する-verb) Noun + を + する テニス → テニスをする
な-adjective / Noun ～に + する 静(しず)か → 静(しず)かにする
い-adjective ～く + する 短(みじか)い → 短(みじか)くする

する · Formation (cont.)

The verb itself conjugates like this. Nothing here follows the う-verb or る-verb rules, which is what "irregular" means - these have to be memorised:

する · Formation (cont.)

Form する With a noun
Dictionary する 運動(うんどう)する
Polite します 運動(うんどう)します
Plain negative しない 運動(うんどう)しない
Polite negative しません 運動(うんどう)しません
Plain past した 運動(うんどう)した
Polite past しました 運動(うんどう)しました
Plain past negative しなかった 運動(うんどう)しなかった
て-form して 運動(うんどう)して
Volitional しよう／しましょう 運動(うんどう)しよう

する · Formation (cont.)

Form する With a noun
Potential できる 運動(うんどう)できる
Conditional したら／すれば 運動(うんどう)したら

Rule of thumb - the potential of する is できる, a completely separate word. There is no ❌されます, no ❌しられます. And because できる is intransitive, its object takes が: 料理(りょうり)ができます, never ❌料理(りょうり)をできます.

する also carries several meanings that have nothing to do with "doing":

する · Formation (cont.)

  • Sensations, with が - 音(おと)がする "there's a sound",
    においがする "there's a smell", 味(あじ)がする "it tastes of…".
  • Deciding, with に - わたしはカレーにします "I'll have the curry."
  • Wearing accessories - ネクタイをする, マスクをする, 時計(とけい)をする.
  • Costing - この傘(かさ)は5000円(えん)しました "this umbrella cost
    5,000 yen."

する · Examples

今(いま)、宿題(しゅくだい)をしています。
I'm doing my homework right now.

わたしはコーヒーにします。
I'll have the coffee.

毎晩(まいばん)30分(ぷん)ぐらい運動(うんどう)します。
I exercise for about thirty minutes every evening.

土曜日(どようび)に部屋(へや)の掃除(そうじ)をしました。
I cleaned my room on Saturday.

台所(だいどころ)のほうから変(へん)な音(おと)がしました。
There was a strange noise from the direction of the kitchen.

する · Examples (cont.)

あの人(ひと)はいつも青(あお)いネクタイをしている。
That guy always wears a blue tie.

来月(らいげつ)京都(きょうと)に行(い)くので、きのうホテルを予約(よやく)しました。
I'm going to Kyoto next month, so I booked a hotel yesterday.

息子(むすこ)は東京(とうきょう)の大学(だいがく)で経済(けいざい)の勉強(べんきょう)をしています。
My son is studying economics at a university in Tokyo.

する · Nuance & register

Plain する is register-neutral: します is polite, する is plain, and nothing about the word itself is stiff or casual. What is worth knowing is that する sits at the bottom of a three-storey politeness building, and the other two storeys are irregular too:

  • 致(いた)す - humble. わたしがいたします "I'll do it", offering your own
    action deferentially. Extremely common in service and business Japanese: 説明(せつめい)いたします, ご案内(あんない)いたします.
  • なさる - honorific, for someone else's action. Note the irregular polite
    form なさいます (not ❌なさります). ゴルフをなさいますか。

する · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because noun + する is so productive, it is the standard way Japanese absorbs foreign words. Anything that arrives in katakana can become a verb overnight: チェックする, キャンセルする, ダウンロードする, シェアする. If you know the noun, you know the verb - this is the single fastest way to expand your active vocabulary.

One register caution: the 漢語(かんご) compounds (確認(かくにん)する, 購入(こうにゅう)する) are a shade more formal than their native equivalents (確(たし)かめる, 買(か)う). In an email they sound crisp and professional; in casual chat with a friend they sound like you are reading from a manual.

する also underlies fixed expressions that never translate word for word: 気(き)にする "worry about it", 大切(たいせつ)にする "treasure something", 無理(むり)をする "overdo it". Meet these as vocabulary, not as grammar.

する · 勉強(べんきょう)する vs 勉強(べんきょう)をする

Rule of thumb - both are correct and mean the same thing. Insert を and the noun becomes a genuine object, which has two consequences: it can now be modified, and it now blocks a second を in the same clause.

日本語(にほんご)を勉強(べんきょう)します。
I study Japanese.

文法(ぶんぽう)の勉強(べんきょう)をします。
I do grammar study.

The first sentence already has a を on 日本語(にほんご), so ❌日本語(にほんご)を勉強(べんきょう)をします is out - one clause, one を. The second sentence needs the を version, because 文法(ぶんぽう)の has to attach to a noun; ❌文法(ぶんぽう)の勉強(べんきょう)します does not work.

する · 勉強(べんきょう)する vs 勉強(べんきょう)をする (cont.)

How to choose: bare する when the compound is behaving as a plain verb, をする when you want to describe or measure the activity itself - 軽(かる)い運動(うんどう)をする, 1時間(じかん)練習(れんしゅう)をする. Not every noun takes を equally happily: activity nouns (洗濯(せんたく), 散歩(さんぽ), 買(か)い物(もの)) accept it readily, while nouns naming a change of state (成功(せいこう)する, 完成(かんせい)する) essentially never appear with it.

する · ～にする vs ～になる

Rule of thumb - する means someone makes it so; なる means it comes to be so. Choosing from a menu is する, because you are the one deciding.

テレビの音(おと)を小(ちい)さくしてください。
Please turn the television down.

外(そと)が急(きゅう)に暗(くら)くなった。
It suddenly got dark outside.

The same pair runs through nouns: 先生(せんせい)にする "make someone a teacher / decide on the teacher" against 先生(せんせい)になる "become a teacher". If a person is deliberately causing the change, it is する; if the change simply happens, it is なる. Japanese leans on なる far more than English leans on "become", so when in doubt about a change of state, なる is usually the more natural choice.

する · する vs やる

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register. やる is the casual, slightly more physical word: it is what friends say to each other.

宿題(しゅくだい)、もうやった？
Have you done your homework yet?

宿題(しゅくだい)はもうしました。
I have already done my homework.

やる cannot fuse directly onto a noun the way する does. You can say 勉強(べんきょう)する, and you can say 勉強(べんきょう)をやる in relaxed speech, but ❌勉強(べんきょう)やる is not a word. やる also means "give (to an animal, a plant, or someone clearly junior)" - 花(はな)に水(みず)をやる - and it can sound rough or blunt from the wrong mouth, so it is a poor default in polite company.

する · Common mistakes

  • ❌ するます → ✅ します
    する is irregular: the ます-stem is し, not ❌すり or ❌する.

  • ❌ 日本語(にほんご)を勉強(べんきょう)をします。 → ✅ 日本語(にほんご)を勉強(べんきょう)します。
    Two を in one clause. Once 日本語(にほんご) has taken the object marker, 勉強(べんきょう) must fuse onto する directly.

  • ❌ テニスを遊(あそ)びます。 → ✅ テニスをします。
    Interference from English "play tennis". 遊(あそ)ぶ means to play in the sense of having fun; sports and games take する.

  • ❌ わたしは料理(りょうり)をできます。 → ✅ わたしは料理(りょうり)ができます。
    できる is the potential of する and it is intransitive, so what you can do is marked with が.

する · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 外(そと)で大(おお)きい音(おと)をしました。 → ✅ 外(そと)で大(おお)きい音(おと)がしました。
    Sensations - sounds, smells, tastes - take が with する. Nobody is doing anything here; the sound simply occurred.

する · Practice 1

1. 明日(あした)までにこの書類(しょるい)を確認(かくにん)____おきます。

確認(かくにん)しておきます

おく attaches to the て-form, and the て-form of する is して - irregular, not built from a stem. The whole compound 確認(かくにん)する conjugates exactly as する does.

する · Practice 2

2. 来週(らいしゅう)から新(あたら)しい仕事(しごと)を（します・なります）。

来週(らいしゅう)から新(あたら)しい仕事(しごと)をします。

The speaker is the one taking the job, so it is a deliberate action: する. なる would need a different frame entirely, such as 来週(らいしゅう)から新(あたら)しい仕事(しごと)になります, which would mean the job itself is what changes.

する · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "My daughter practises the piano for an hour every day."

娘(むすめ)は毎日(まいにち)1時間(じかん)ピアノの練習(れんしゅう)をします。

Both 練習(れんしゅう)します and 練習(れんしゅう)をします are possible, but here ピアノの has to modify a noun, so the を version is the natural one. Alternatively: 娘(むすめ)は毎日(まいにち)1時間(じかん)ピアノを練習(れんしゅう)します。

する · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この部屋(へや)は静(しず)かなしてください。

✅ この部屋(へや)では静(しず)かにしてください。

To make something take on a quality you use the adverbial form, and for a な-adjective that is に, not な. な only appears directly before a noun.

する · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部屋(へや)を きれいに しました。 (b) 部屋(へや)が きれいに なりました。

(a) says somebody tidied the room - する puts an agent behind the change, and the room is marked with を as the thing acted on. (b) simply reports that the room ended up clean, with が marking it as the subject that changed.

Japanese speakers often prefer (b) even when they did the work themselves, because なる sounds less like claiming credit. That preference for describing outcomes rather than agents runs right through the language.

て-form

Meaning · the connective form - the shape a verb takes when the sentence isn't finished with it yet

The て-form is not a tense and not a politeness level. It is the form a word wears when something else is going to attach to it, or when the sentence carries on past it. On its own it says roughly "…and then", but its real importance is structural: ～ている, ～てください, ～てもいい, ～てから, ～てしまう, ～てあげる and dozens more all plug into it. Learn the て-form and a large slice of the grammar you meet later costs you nothing extra.

Note - verbs come in three classes: う-verbs (五段(ごだん) / godan / Group 1), る-verbs (一段(いちだん) / ichidan / Group 2), and the two irregulars する and 来(く)る (Group 3). This page says う-verb and る-verb. Only う-verbs have complicated て-forms; the other two classes are trivial.

Rule of thumb - the た-form uses exactly these sound changes, with た/だ in place of て/で. 読(よ)んで → 読(よ)んだ, 書(か)いて → 書(か)いた, 話(はな)して → 話(はな)した, 行(い)って → 行(い)った. Learn one table and you have learned both. This is the single best return on memorisation effort in beginner Japanese.

Note - 行(い)く is the exception every learner trips over. By the く rule it ought to be ❌行(い)いて; it is 行(い)って. Nothing else in ordinary Japanese behaves this way. Watch out too for う-verbs that look like る-verbs: 帰(かえ)る → 帰(かえ)って (not ❌帰(かえ)て), 走(はし)る → 走(はし)って, 入(はい)る → 入(はい)って.

What the て-form is for, in rough order of how soon you will need it:

て-form · Formation (cont.)

  • Linking events in sequence - 起(お)きて、顔(かお)を洗(あら)って、出(で)かけます。
  • A continuing action or a state - ～ている.
  • Requests - ～てください.
  • Permission and prohibition - ～てもいい, ～てはいけない.
  • Cause, when the second clause is a feeling or a result you did not choose.
  • Manner or means - 歩(ある)いて行(い)く "go on foot".

て-form · Examples

ちょっと待(ま)って。
Hang on a second.

朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べて、会社(かいしゃ)に行(い)きます。
I have breakfast and then go to work.

弟(おとうと)は今(いま)シャワーを浴(あ)びています。
My brother is in the shower right now.

駅(えき)まで歩(ある)いて10分(ぷん)ぐらいかかります。
It's about a ten-minute walk to the station.

電気(でんき)を消(け)して、部屋(へや)を出(で)ました。
I turned the light off and left the room.

て-form · Examples (cont.)

田中(たなか)さんは背(せ)が高(たか)くて、髪(かみ)の長(なが)い人(ひと)です。
Tanaka is the tall one with long hair.

昨日(きのう)は朝(あさ)から雨(あめ)が降(ふ)って、試合(しあい)が中止(ちゅうし)になりました。
It rained from the morning yesterday and the match was called off.

この辺(へん)は夜(よる)になると店(みせ)が全部(ぜんぶ)閉(し)まって、人(ひと)も歩(ある)いていません。
Around here everything shuts at night and there's nobody about.

て-form · Nuance & register

The て-form carries no politeness of its own - politeness is decided by whatever ends the sentence. 食(た)べて行(い)く and 食(た)べて行(い)きます differ only at the tail. That is exactly why it is so useful: a long chain of て-forms lets you build a complicated sentence and only worry about register at the very end.

A bare て-form left at the end of a sentence is a casual request: ちょっと待(ま)って, 見(み)せて, 手伝(てつだ)って. It is ～てください with the polite part bitten off, so it is friendly among equals and rude upwards. Softening it with ね - 待(ま)ってね - makes it gentler; adding よ makes it more insistent.

Two spoken contractions to expect early, because they are everywhere:

  • ～ている → ～てる: 何(なに)してるの？ , 待(ま)ってるよ.
  • ～ておく → ～とく, ～てしまう → ～ちゃう: 買(か)っとく,
    食(た)べちゃった.

て-form · Nuance & register (cont.)

In formal writing, long chains of て-forms look chatty. Written Japanese often replaces the linking て with the bare ます-stem instead - 朝(あさ)6時(じ)に 起(お)き、新聞(しんぶん)を読(よ)む - which is compact and noticeably literary. You do not need to produce it at N5, but you will see it.

One real limitation: the て-form does not mark why two events are next to each other. It only says they belong together, and the listener infers sequence, cause, or simple accompaniment from context. When you need the reason to be unmistakable, use から or ので instead.

て-form · ～て vs ～てから

Rule of thumb - plain ～て says the events belong together; ～てから says the first one finished before the second started, and puts weight on that order.

手(て)を洗(あら)って、ご飯(はん)を食(た)べます。
I'll wash my hands and eat.

手(て)を洗(あら)ってから、ご飯(はん)を食(た)べます。
I'll eat after washing my hands.

The difference is emphasis. With plain て the two actions are simply a routine; with てから you are insisting on the order - first this, then that. So てから is what you want in instructions and in warnings, and it sounds fussy when the order was never in doubt.

て-form · ～なくて vs ～ないで

Rule of thumb - ～なくて gives a reason; ～ないで describes how something was done, or asks someone not to do it.

バスが来(こ)なくて、会議(かいぎ)に遅(おく)れました。
The bus didn't come, so I was late for the meeting.

朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べないで学校(がっこう)に行(い)きました。
I went to school without eating breakfast.

Swap them and you get nonsense: ❌バスが来(こ)ないで、会議(かいぎ)に 遅(おく)れました. A useful test - if you could put "so" or "because" in the English, use なくて; if you could put "without", use ないで. Requests are always ないで: ここに車(くるま)を止(と)めないでください.

Adjectives and nouns only have the なくて option: 安(やす)くなくて, 学生(がくせい)じゃなくて.

て-form · ～て (cause) vs ～から

Rule of thumb - ～て can only introduce a cause when the second clause is something outside your control: a feeling, a result, an inability. If the second clause is a request, an invitation, or a decision, you must use から or ので.

ニュースを聞(き)いて、びっくりしました。
I was startled when I heard the news.

今日(きょう)は忙(いそが)しいから、早(はや)く帰(かえ)ってください。
I'm busy today, so please go home early.

The second sentence cannot use て: ❌忙(いそが)しくて、早(はや)く 帰(かえ)ってください is ungrammatical, because a て cause clause refuses to be followed by a command, a request, an invitation or a volitional. This is the most common way learners misuse the て-form, and it is invisible in translation - English "so" covers both.

て-form · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行(い)いて → ✅ 行(い)って
    行(い)く is the one exception to the く → いて rule. Everything else with く behaves normally: 書(か)いて, 聞(き)いて, 歩(ある)いて.

  • ❌ 飲(の)んて → ✅ 飲(の)んで
    む・ぶ・ぬ take んで with a voiced で, never んて. Same for 遊(あそ)んで and 死(し)んで.

  • ❌ 切(き)て → ✅ 切(き)って
    切(き)る is an う-verb even though it ends in る, so it takes って. Contrast 着(き)る "wear", which really is a る-verb: 着(き)て.

  • ❌ この部屋(へや)は広(ひろ)いで明(あか)るいです。 → ✅ この部屋(へや)は広(ひろ)くて明(あか)るいです。
    で is the connective for nouns and な-adjectives. い-adjectives drop the い and take くて.

  • ❌ 食(た)べるてください。 → ✅ 食(た)べてください。
    ください attaches to the て-form, not to the dictionary form. Drop the る before adding て.

て-form · Practice 1

1. 窓(まど)を開(あ)け____、部屋(へや)の空気(くうき)を入(い)れかえた。

開(あ)けて

開(あ)ける is a る-verb: drop る and add て. Two actions in sequence, so the first verb goes into the て-form and only the last one carries the tense.

て-form · Practice 2

2. 時間(じかん)が（なくて・ないで）、昼(ひる)ご飯(はん)が食(た)べられませんでした。

時間(じかん)がなくて、昼(ひる)ご飯(はん)が食(た)べられませんでした。

This is a cause - "because there was no time" - and causes take なくて. ないで would describe the manner of doing something, which makes no sense with an inability in the second clause.

て-form · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I got up at six, read the newspaper, and left the house at seven."

6時(じ)に起(お)きて、新聞(しんぶん)を読(よ)んで、7時(じ)に家(いえ)を出(で)ました。

Only the final verb takes tense and politeness; the て-forms in front of it are neutral. 起(お)きる is a る-verb (起(お)きて) and 読(よ)む is an う-verb ending in む, so it takes んで.

て-form · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)って、傘(かさ)を持(も)って行(い)ってください。

✅ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っているから、傘(かさ)を持(も)って行(い)ってください。

A て clause used as a cause cannot be followed by a request. Swap it for から or ので. The second 持(も)って is fine - that one is manner, not cause.

て-form · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 薬(くすり)を飲(の)んで、寝(ね)ます。 (b) 薬(くすり)を飲(の)んでから、寝(ね)ます。

Both describe the same two actions in the same order. (a) simply chains them - it is how you would describe your evening routine, with no particular stress on sequence. (b) foregrounds the order, so it reads as "not before the medicine - medicine first, then bed."

That is why (b) is what a pharmacist or a parent would say: てから is the form you reach for when getting the order right actually matters.

た-form / plain past

Meaning · the plain past - "did", "was" - and the base that a whole family of patterns attaches to

The た-form is the plain-style counterpart of ～ました. It says an event happened, or that something is now finished. It is also structural: ～たら, ～たり, ～たことがある, ～たあとで and ～たほうがいい are all built on it, and none of them will make sense until this form is automatic.

Note - verbs come in three classes: う-verbs (五段(ごだん) / godan / Group 1), る-verbs (一段(いちだん) / ichidan / Group 2), and the irregulars する and 来(く)る (Group 3). This page says う-verb and る-verb.

た-form / plain past · Formation

た-form / plain past · Examples (cont.)

This is a photo I took in Kyoto last year.

大学(だいがく)のとき習(なら)った中国語(ちゅうごくご)を、すっかり忘(わす)れた。
I've completely forgotten the Chinese I learned at university.

空港(くうこう)に着(つ)いたら、すぐ連絡(れんらく)しますので、心配(しんぱい)しないでください。
I'll get in touch as soon as I land, so please don't worry.

た-form / plain past · Nuance & register

The た-form is plain style. Ending a sentence with it to a stranger or a superior is too familiar - 食(た)べた to your boss should be 食(た)べました. Inside a sentence, though, plain is compulsory regardless of politeness: the verb before とき, ので, から, あとで, and before any noun it modifies, goes into the た-form even when the sentence ends politely. 買(か)った本(ほん)を読(よ)んでいます is correct; ❌買(か)いました本(ほん) is not.

The た-form does more than past tense. Three uses that surprise learners:

た-form / plain past · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • Completion, not past time. バスが来(き)た! - the bus is arriving right
    now; you are reporting that the awaited event has landed. Likewise あ、あった "there it is", said while still looking at the thing.
  • A sudden recollection. 明日(あした)、休(やす)みだったね - "oh, tomorrow's
    a day off, isn't it." Nothing is past; the た marks the fact newly clicking into place.
  • A sharp command in casual speech. 帰(かえ)った、帰(かえ)った! -
    "go on, off you go!", typical of a shopkeeper closing up. Rough, and worth recognising rather than using.

In writing, plain past is the default: novels, newspapers and reports are written in た, not ました. So the た-form is not "informal Japanese", it is plain-style Japanese - a different axis from politeness.

た-form / plain past · Nuance & register (cont.)

In casual speech the た-form very often turns up inside the contraction ～ちゃった (from ～てしまった), and ～じゃった after ん-forms: 忘(わす)れちゃった "oh no, I forgot", 飲(の)んじゃった "I went and drank it." Learners hear these long before they are taught them.

た-form / plain past · ～た vs ～ました

Rule of thumb - same tense, different register. ました is polite style; た is plain style, and is also the only option inside a sentence.

先週(せんしゅう)、新(あたら)しい自転車(じてんしゃ)を買(か)いました。
I bought a new bicycle last week.

先週(せんしゅう)買(か)った自転車(じてんしゃ)が、もう壊(こわ)れました。
The bicycle I bought last week has already broken.

In the second sentence the politeness sits on 壊(こわ)れました at the end. The relative clause modifying 自転車(じてんしゃ) has to be plain - this is a hard grammatical rule, not a stylistic preference.

た-form / plain past · ～た vs ～ていた

Rule of thumb - ～た reports an event as a single point; ～ていた looks inside it and describes it as ongoing, repeated, or a state that held for a while.

昨日(きのう)、本(ほん)を読(よ)んだ。
I read a book yesterday.

昨日(きのう)の夜(よる)はずっと本(ほん)を読(よ)んでいた。
I was reading all last night.

The trap is verbs of change. 結婚(けっこん)した means the wedding happened; 結婚(けっこん)している means the person is married now. 知(し)る behaves the same way: "I know" is 知(し)っています, never a plain 知(し)ります, while the negative flips back - 知(し)りません "I don't know", 知(し)りませんでした "I didn't know."

た-form / plain past · ～た vs ～たことがある

Rule of thumb - ～た reports a particular occasion; ～たことがある reports that the thing is somewhere in your experience, with no occasion attached.

2019年(ねん)に北海道(ほっかいどう)へ行(い)った。
I went to Hokkaido in 2019.

北海道(ほっかいどう)へ行(い)ったことがある。
I've been to Hokkaido before.

Because たことがある is about experience rather than occasion, it clashes with a specific time word: ❌昨日(きのう)行(い)ったことがあります. It also implies the experience is unusual enough to be worth mentioning, so it sounds odd about everyday things - 今日(きょう)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べたことが あります is grammatical and absurd.

た-form / plain past · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 寒(さむ)いでした → ✅ 寒(さむ)かったです
    い-adjectives carry their own past tense. The い becomes かった, and です is added afterwards for politeness without changing anything.

  • ❌ 行(い)いた → ✅ 行(い)った
    行(い)く breaks the く → いた rule. Compare 聞(き)いた, 着(つ)いた, which are regular.

  • ❌ 遊(あそ)んた → ✅ 遊(あそ)んだ
    む・ぶ・ぬ verbs take the voiced んだ. So do ぐ verbs: 泳(およ)いだ, never ❌泳(およ)いた.

  • ❌ 食(た)べるた → ✅ 食(た)べた
    For a る-verb you replace る with た; you never keep both.

  • ❌ 昨日(きのう)、映画(えいが)を見(み)ます。 → ✅ 昨日(きのう)、映画(えいが)を見(み)ました。
    A past time word does not conjugate the verb for you. Japanese marks tense on the verb every time.

た-form / plain past · Practice 1

1. 先週(せんしゅう)、友達(ともだち)と山(やま)に登(のぼ)____。

登(のぼ)った（or 登(のぼ)りました）

登(のぼ)る is an う-verb ending in る, so it takes った - the same sound change as the て-form 登(のぼ)って. Note that る-looking う-verbs are common; 登(のぼ)る is one of them.

た-form / plain past · Practice 2

2. 富士山(ふじさん)に（登(のぼ)った・登(のぼ)ったことがある）？ - うん、2回(かい)あるよ。

富士山(ふじさん)に登(のぼ)ったことがある？

The reply counts occasions, so the question is about experience in general, not about one particular climb. Plain 登(のぼ)った would be asking whether you climbed it on some specific occasion both speakers already have in mind.

た-form / plain past · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The film I watched last night wasn't very interesting."

昨日(きのう)の夜(よる)見(み)た映画(えいが)は、あまり面白(おもしろ)くなかった。

The verb inside the relative clause modifying 映画(えいが) must be plain past: 見(み)た, not ❌見(み)ました. The final adjective carries its own past negative, 面白(おもしろ)くなかった.

た-form / plain past · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 去年(きょねん)の冬(ふゆ)はあまり雪(ゆき)が降(ふ)りませんだった。

✅ 去年(きょねん)の冬(ふゆ)はあまり雪(ゆき)が降(ふ)りませんでした。

ません takes でした, not ❌んだった. The plain-form equivalent is 降(ふ)らなかった - note that the plain past negative is built on ない, which inflects like an い-adjective.

た-form / plain past · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 財布(さいふ)を落(お)とした。 (b) 財布(さいふ)を落(お)としてしまった。

(a) is a neutral report: the wallet got dropped, and that is the fact. (b) adds ～てしまう, which colours the same event with regret or with the sense that it is now irreversible - closer to "I've gone and dropped my wallet."

Both are plain past. The tense is identical; what changes is how the speaker feels about it, which is a job Japanese hands to an auxiliary rather than to intonation.

ない-form

Meaning · the plain negative - "doesn't", "won't", "isn't" - and the base a whole set of later patterns attaches to

The ない-form is the plain-style negative, the partner of ～ません. It is also the foundation of ～ないでください, ～なくてもいい, ～なければならない, ～ないほうがいい and ～ないで. Get the stem wrong here and every one of those patterns comes out wrong, which is why it repays care.

Note - verbs come in three classes: う-verbs (五段(ごだん) / godan / Group 1), る-verbs (一段(いちだん) / ichidan / Group 2), and the irregulars する and 来(く)る (Group 3). This page says う-verb and る-verb.

ない-form · Formation

Verb (る-verb) drop る, + ない 見(み)る → 見(み)ない
Verb (する) しない 練習(れんしゅう)する → 練習(れんしゅう)しない

Verb (ある, irregular) ない ある → ない
い-adjective い → くない 安(やす)い → 安(やす)くない
な-adjective + じゃない／ではない 暇(ひま) → 暇(ひま)じゃない
Noun + じゃない／ではない 学生(がくせい) → 学生(がくせい)じゃない

ない-form · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - verbs ending in う take わない, not ❌あない. 買(か)う → 買(か)わない, 言(い)う → 言(い)わない, 会(あ)う → 会(あ)わない, 歌(うた)う → 歌(うた)わない. The reason is historical - these verbs used to end in a -wu sound, and the w only survives before あ - but for learning purposes it is simply the rule.

Rule of thumb - ある has no ない-form. Its negative is the word ない itself. There is no ❌あらない. Polite: ありません. 時間(じかん)がない "there's no time", 時間(じかん)がなかった "there was no time". This one irregularity catches almost everybody once.

Note - 来(く)る is read three different ways depending on the form: 来(く)る, 来(き)ます／来(き)た／来(き)て, and 来(こ)ない. The kanji does not change, so the negative is read こない, never ❌きない.

ない-form · Formation (cont.)

Once ない is attached, the whole thing behaves as an い-adjective, and inflects like one:

Form Rule Example
Plain negative ない 行(い)かない
Past negative ない → なかった 行(い)かなかった
て-form (cause) ない → なくて 行(い)かなくて
Manner / request ない + で 行(い)かないで
Before a noun ない + Noun 行(い)かない人(ひと)
Polite ない + です 行(い)かないです

ない-form · Examples

わたしは肉(にく)を食(た)べない。
I don't eat meat.

今日(きょう)は授業(じゅぎょう)がない。
There's no class today.

田中(たなか)さん、まだ来(こ)ないね。
Tanaka still isn't here, is he.

お金(かね)が足(た)りないから、また今度(こんど)にします。
I haven't got enough money, so I'll leave it for another time.

傘(かさ)を持(も)って行(い)かないと、ぬれるよ。
If you don't take an umbrella you'll get soaked.

ない-form · Examples (cont.)

ここでは写真(しゃしん)を撮(と)らないでください。
Please don't take photographs here.

試験(しけん)が来週(らいしゅう)なのに、弟(おとうと)は全然(ぜんぜん)勉強(べんきょう)しない。
The exam is next week and my brother isn't studying at all.

給料(きゅうりょう)が何年(なんねん)も上(あ)がらないので、新(あたら)しい仕事(しごと)を探(さが)している。
My pay hasn't gone up in years, so I'm looking for a new job.

ない-form · Nuance & register

ない is plain style, so as a sentence ending it belongs with friends and family. To a customer or a superior you want ～ません. The middle option, ～ないです, is polite but relaxed, and is what colleagues say to each other all day.

Bare ない at the end of a question - 行(い)かない？ - is a casual invitation, not a plain question: "want to come?" It is the plain-style twin of ～ませんか, and it is extremely common. 今晩(こんばん)ご飯(はん) 食(た)べない？

The negative also softens requests, exactly as it does in English. Compare 窓(まど)を開(あ)けてくれますか with 窓(まど)を開(あ)けてくれませんか - the negative version is more polite, because a negative question makes it easier to refuse.

ない-form · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two things to keep separate. ない is the plain negative of a verb; the adjective ない meaning "there isn't" is a different word that happens to look the same. And in speech, ん is a very common contraction of ない in the Kansai area and in men's rough speech (分(わ)からん, 知(し)らん) - recognise it, but stick to ない yourself until your speech is firmly anchored somewhere.

Written formal Japanese prefers ず and ぬ in fixed phrases - 知(し)らずに, やむを得(え)ず - and uses ではない where speech uses じゃない. じゃない is a contraction of ではない, and it is casual: never write it in a report.

ない-form · ～ない vs ～ません vs ～ないです

Rule of thumb - one meaning, three registers. ない plain, ないです polite-casual, ません polite-formal.

明日(あした)は学校(がっこう)に行(い)かない。
I'm not going to school tomorrow. (to a friend)

明日(あした)は学校(がっこう)に行(い)きません。
I will not be going to school tomorrow. (to a teacher)

Note the different bases: ません is built on the ます-stem (行(い)き), while ない is built on the あ-row stem (行(い)か). They are not two endings on the same stem, which is why you cannot convert one to the other by ear.

ない-form · The verb ない vs the adjective ない

Rule of thumb - three different negatives all end in ない, and each is formed from a different base: verbs take あ-row + ない, い-adjectives swap い for くない, and nouns and な-adjectives take じゃない.

このカメラは動(うご)かない。
This camera doesn't work.

このカメラは新(あたら)しくない。
This camera isn't new.

このカメラは防水(ぼうすい)じゃない。
This camera isn't waterproof.

ない-form · The verb ない vs the adjective ない (cont.)

All three then inflect identically, because ない is an い-adjective: 動(うご)かなかった, 新(あたら)しくなかった, 防水(ぼうすい)じゃなかった. That shared tail is why ❌動(うご)かないでした and ❌新(あたら)しくないでした are wrong - an い-adjective never takes でした.

ない-form · ある → ない vs いる → いない

Rule of thumb - both mean "there isn't", but they split the world the same way their positives do: ある/ない for things, いる/いない for people and animals.

冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)に何(なに)もない。
There's nothing in the fridge.

教室(きょうしつ)にはだれもいない。
There's nobody in the classroom.

いる is a perfectly regular る-verb, so its negative is formed the ordinary way: drop る, add ない. ある is the odd one out - it simply becomes ない. The polite forms are ありません and いません respectively, and both keep the thing-versus-creature split.

ない-form · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 買(か)あない → ✅ 買(か)わない
    う-verbs ending in う insert わ, not あ. Same for 言(い)わない and 会(あ)わない.

  • ❌ お金(かね)があらない → ✅ お金(かね)がない
    ある has no regular negative. Its negative is the word ない, and the polite form is ありません.

  • ❌ 食(た)べるない → ✅ 食(た)べない
    For a る-verb you replace る with ない. Keeping both is the commonest beginner slip of all.

  • ❌ この部屋(へや)は広(ひろ)いない → ✅ この部屋(へや)は広(ひろ)くない
    い-adjectives do not take the verb negative. Swap the final い for くない.

  • ❌ 来(く)る → 来(き)ない → ✅ 来(く)る → 来(こ)ない
    The kanji stays the same but the reading changes to こ in the negative. Say it aloud when you practise, or the mistake will be invisible on paper.

ない-form · Practice 1

1. 明日(あした)は日曜日(にちようび)だから、会社(かいしゃ)に行(い)____。

行(い)かない

行(い)く is an う-verb ending in く, so the final -ku drops to -ka before ない. The polite version is 行(い)きません, built on a different stem altogether.

ない-form · Practice 2

2. 冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)に牛乳(ぎゅうにゅう)が（ない・いない）。

冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)に牛乳(ぎゅうにゅう)がない。

Milk is not alive, so the existence verb is ある, whose negative is the irregular ない. いない is the negative of いる, reserved for people and animals.

ない-form · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I don't drink coffee at night, so I sleep well."

夜(よる)はコーヒーを飲(の)まないので、よく寝(ね)られます。

飲(の)む is an う-verb ending in む, so the negative stem is 飲(の)ま. ので requires the plain form in front of it even when the sentence ends politely.

ない-form · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昨日(きのう)は友達(ともだち)に会(あ)あなかった。

✅ 昨日(きのう)は友達(ともだち)に会(あ)わなかった。

会(あ)う ends in う, so the negative stem is 会(あ)わ, not ❌会(あ)あ. The past negative is then formed the い-adjective way: ない → なかった.

ない-form · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べないで出(で)かけた。 (b) 朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べなくて、おなかがすいた。

(a) uses ないで, which describes the manner of the second action: he went out, and the not-eating is how he did it - "without eating breakfast."

(b) uses なくて, which gives the reason for the second clause: because he didn't eat, he got hungry. Substituting one for the other produces something that a native speaker will hear as broken, even though English uses the same word "not" in both.
