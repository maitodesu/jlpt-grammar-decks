～ている - is doing; is in a state

Meaning · the action is going on now - or its result is still with us

～ている is not the English present progressive, and treating it as one is the single most expensive mistake in beginner Japanese. It says that something connected to the verb is true right now. Whether that something is the action still running, or the state left behind after the action finished, is decided by the verb itself - not by ている.

Rule of thumb - verbs that take time (食たべる, 読よむ, 走はしる) give ongoing action. Verbs that happen in an instant and change something (結婚けっこんする, 死しぬ, 開あく, 知しる) give resulting state. Same ている, two readings, and the verb decides.

There is a third, quieter use - habitual action (毎朝まいあさ走はしっています) - and a fourth for accumulated record (三回さんかい優勝ゆうしょうしている, "has won three times"). Both are common; the first is essential at N5.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · Nuance & register

ている is where Japanese talks about the present. The dictionary form does not mean "now": 食たべる is "will eat" or "eats (habitually)", never "is eating". If something is happening as you speak, ている is not optional.

てる is the normal spoken shape. 何なにしてるの？, 待まってるよ, 知しってた？ - the い drops in casual speech, including in ていた → てた. It is informal but extremely common; keep the full form in writing, in ます speech and in the exam.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · Nuance & register (cont.)

States you would never think of as verbs. Japanese uses ている for a large class of descriptions that English handles with adjectives or the simple present: 住すんでいます (live), 持もっています (have), 勤つとめています (work for), 似にている (resemble), 太ふとっている (be heavy-set), 曲まがっている (be bent). Several of these are only natural in ている form - you cannot describe a present resemblance with ❌似にる.

Politeness. Honorific speech gives ～ていらっしゃる and ～ておられる; humble speech gives ～ております (承知しょうちしております). These are N4 and beyond, but you will hear ております on the phone from day one.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · Nuance & register (cont.)

The negative is where the special cases hide. 食たべていません means "hasn't eaten (yet)" - the action has not started or is not underway. But some state verbs refuse the negative ている altogether, and 知しる is the famous one: see below.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · Ongoing action vs resulting state

Rule of thumb - ask how long the verb takes. If the action itself has duration, ている catches it mid-flow. If the action is over in an instant and leaves a changed world, ている describes the change that is still there.

弟おとうとは今いま、手紙てがみを**書かいています**。
My little brother is writing a letter right now. (still writing)

おじいさんは去年きょねん亡なくなっています。
My grandfather passed away last year. (and is dead now)

～ている - is doing; is in a state · Ongoing action vs resulting state (cont.)

The second is not "is dying". 死しぬ and 亡なくなる take no time at all, so there is no flow for ている to catch - only the state afterwards. The same logic explains 結婚けっこんしている (is married, not "is getting married"), 来きている (is here, having come), ドアが閉しまっている (the door is shut) and 覚おぼえている (remembers).

電気でんきがついています。誰だれかいるのかもしれません。
The light is on. Someone might be in.

A useful test: if the English translation is an adjective or "is/has been", you are looking at a resulting state.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · ～ている vs the dictionary form

Rule of thumb - ている = now. Dictionary form = will, or generally does. The overlap English speakers expect between "I eat" and "I'm eating" does not exist here.

毎日まいにち新聞しんぶんを読よみます。
I read the newspaper every day. (habit - plain present is fine)

今いま、新聞しんぶんを**読よんでいます**。
I'm reading the newspaper right now.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · ～ている vs the dictionary form (cont.)

Habitual actions can take either form, with a difference of framing: ています presents the habit as a current, ongoing arrangement of your life - 銀行ぎんこうで働はたらいています is how you say what you do for a living, and 銀行ぎんこうで働はたらきます would sound like a plan for tomorrow.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · 知っている vs 知らない

Rule of thumb - "I know" is 知しっています. "I don't know" is 知しりません. There is no ❌知しっていません in ordinary use.

その話はなしなら**知しっています**。
Yes, I know about that.

すみません、知しりません。
Sorry, I don't know.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · 知っている vs 知らない (cont.)

知しる means "to come to know", so 知しっている is the state of having come to know - and its opposite is simply never having done so, which is 知しらない. Answering a question with ❌知しっていません marks you immediately as a learner; answering with a flat 知しりません can sound curt, so soften it: ちょっと分わかりません, すみません、知しらないんです.

分わかる behaves differently again: it is already a state, so 分わかります／分わかりません need no ている at all.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · Common mistakes

  • ❌ その人ひとは知しっていません。 → ✅ その人ひとは知しりません。
    The negative of 知しっている is 知しらない. This one is worth memorising as a pair rather than as a rule.

  • ❌ 今いま、昼ひるごはんを食たべます。（intending "I'm eating lunch now"）→ ✅ 今いま、昼ひるごはんを食たべています。
    The plain present is future or habitual. It cannot describe what is happening as you speak.

  • ❌ 兄あには結婚けっこんします。（intending "my brother is married"）→ ✅ 兄あには結婚けっこんしています。
    Without ている you have announced a wedding that has not happened yet.

  • ❌ 東京とうきょうに住すみます。 → ✅ 東京とうきょうに住すんでいます。
    Living somewhere is a state. The dictionary form would mean you are about to move there.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 駅えきで待まちています。 → ✅ 駅えきで待まっています。
    待まつ is a つ verb, so its て-form is 待まって. ている never attaches to the ます-stem.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · Practice 1

1. 母ははは台所だいどころで料理りょうりを____います。

料理りょうりをしています。

する becomes して, and cooking takes time, so this is the ongoing reading: she is in the middle of it right now.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · Practice 2

2. 山田やまださんの電話番号でんわばんごうを（知しっていません・知しりません）。

知しっている has no negative form of its own - not knowing is expressed by the plain negative 知しらない／知しりません. The affirmative and the negative simply do not match in shape.

開あく is an instantaneous change of state, so ている gives the resulting state - the door is currently open, not "is opening". English speakers often reach for an adjective here; Japanese uses the verb.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the problem: 今いま、田中たなかさんは電話でんわを使つかいます。あとでかけ直なおしてください。

✅ 今いま、田中たなかさんは電話でんわを使つかっています。あとでかけ直なおしてください。

The second sentence makes it clear the phone is in use at this moment, so the first needs ています. As written, it says Tanaka will use the phone - which gives the listener no reason to call back later.

～ている - is doing; is in a state · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 窓まどを開あけています。 (b) 窓まどが開あいています。

(a) uses the transitive 開あける with を: somebody is in the act of opening the window, and the sentence is about their action.

(b) uses the intransitive 開あく with が: the window is open. Nobody is mentioned, nobody is doing anything, and the sentence simply describes the state of the room. The particle and the verb pair together - change one and you must change the other.

まだ - still; not yet

Meaning · still (it hasn't changed) - or, with a negative, not yet (it hasn't happened)

まだ marks a situation as unchanged. If the sentence is positive, what has not changed is a state that continues: still. If the sentence is negative, what has not changed is an event everyone is waiting for: not yet. One word, one idea, two English translations.

まだ - still; not yet · Usage

まだ is an adverb. It does not conjugate and it attaches to nothing - you simply put it before the part of the sentence it applies to, usually near the front.

With an affirmative predicate it means still: まだ降ふっています (still raining), まだ子こどもです (still a child), まだ新あたらしい (still new). Verbs, い-adjectives, な-adjectives and nouns all take it equally.

With a negative predicate it means not yet: まだ決きまっていません (not decided yet), まだ来きません (hasn't come yet). The most important pattern of this kind is まだ～ていません, which has a page of its own.

まだ - still; not yet · Usage (cont.)

まだ also stands alone as a complete answer. To もう食たべましたか you can reply simply まだです, or casually まだ. And doubled, まだまだ means "nowhere near yet": 日本語にほんごはまだまだです.

One extra use worth knowing: before a quantity or a time, まだ means only - まだ三時さんじですよ ("it's only three"), まだ半分はんぶん残のこっている ("there's still half left"). The nuance is that there is more of it than the listener assumed.

まだ - still; not yet · Examples

まだ時間じかんがありますよ。
There's still time.

父ちちはまだ寝ねています。
My father is still asleep.

バスがまだ来きません。
The bus still hasn't come.

駅前えきまえのパン屋や、まだやっているかな。もう九時くじだけど。
I wonder if the bakery by the station is still open - it's gone nine, though.

まだ - still; not yet · Nuance & register

まだ is register-neutral. It sits comfortably in a business email and in a text message to a friend, and it carries no politeness of its own - the ending of the sentence does that work.

What it does carry is a faint sense of expectation not yet met, and that is where tone creeps in. A bare まだ？ to someone who is running late is impatient in any language. The polite answer まだです is neutral, but as a question - まだですか - it presses. Aimed upward, replace it with something that does not ask the person to account for the delay: 恐おそれ入いりますが、いかがでしょうか.

In the other direction, まだまだ is a standard humility formula. Told that your Japanese is good, いえいえ、まだまだです is the expected reply - literally "not by a long way yet", and understood as modesty rather than self-criticism.

まだ - still; not yet · まだ vs もう

Rule of thumb - まだ says nothing has changed; もう says the change has already happened. They divide the timeline at the moment of change, and they are each other's natural opposite in both positive and negative sentences.

外そとはまだ明あかるいですね。
It's still light outside.

外そとはもう暗くらいですね。
It's already dark outside.

The negative pairing is the one to watch, because the English flips too:

まだ - still; not yet · まだ vs もう (cont.)

兄あにはまだ働はたらいていません。
My brother isn't working yet. (he will - he's still studying)

兄あにはもう働はたらいていません。
My brother isn't working any more. (he has retired or quit)

まだ + negative looks forward to something expected; もう + negative looks back at something finished. Choosing the wrong one does not produce a slightly odd sentence - it produces the opposite fact.

まだ - still; not yet · Common mistakes

  • ❌ まだ昼ひるごはんを食たべませんでした。 → ✅ まだ昼ひるごはんを食たべていません。
    ませんでした closes the door on the event: it did not happen and that is that. まだ means it is still coming, so it needs the ていません form.

  • ❌ レポートはまだ終おわりました。 → ✅ レポートはもう終おわりました。
    まだ cannot introduce a completed event. Once something is done, the adverb you want is もう.

  • ❌ もうまだ着ついていません。 → ✅ まだ着ついていません。
    もう and まだ are opposites and do not stack. Pick one.

まだ - still; not yet · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、あの件けん、まだですか。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、あの件けんはいかがでしょうか。
    Grammatical, but it asks a superior why they have not finished. Ask about the status instead of the delay.

まだ - still; not yet · Practice 1

1. 「もう帰かえりますか。」「いいえ、____仕事しごとが残のこっているので、もう少すこしいます。」

いいえ、まだ仕事しごとが残のこっているので…

The work is unfinished and the situation is unchanged, which is exactly まだ with an affirmative verb: still there is work left. もう would claim the opposite.

まだ - still; not yet · Practice 2

2. 子こどもたちは（まだ・もう）起おきています。寝ねかせないと。

子こどもたちはまだ起おきています。

The second sentence - "I need to get them to bed" - tells you the children ought to be asleep already and are not. That is a continuing state contrary to expectation, so まだ. もう起おきています would mean they are already awake, which fits a morning, not a bedtime.

まだ - still; not yet · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) まだ三時さんじですよ。 (b) もう三時さんじですよ。

Both state the same time and disagree entirely about what it means.

(a) presents three o'clock as early: there is more of the day left than the listener thought - sit down, finish your coffee. (b) presents it as late: time has run away and something should have happened by now. まだ measures what remains; もう measures what has already gone.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet

Meaning · I haven't done it yet - and it is still on the list

This is the standard answer to もう～ましたか. The two halves each do a job: まだ says the situation is unchanged, and ～ていません says the action has not reached completion. Together they describe something that has not happened and is still expected to - which is why "yet" is such a good translation and "didn't" is such a bad one.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the negative goes on いる, not on the main verb. It is 食たべて + いません, never ❌食たべない + います and never ❌食たべませんでした.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Examples

いいえ、まだ読よんでいません。
No, I haven't read it yet.

宿題しゅくだいはまだ終おわっていません。
I haven't finished my homework yet.

ホテルはまだ予約よやくしていません。
I haven't booked the hotel yet.

「もう返事へんじが来きましたか。」「いいえ、まだ来きていません。」
"Has the reply arrived?" "No, not yet."

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Examples (cont.)

そのドラマ、まだ見みてないから言いわないで。
I haven't watched that show yet, so don't tell me.

新あたらしい住所じゅうしょはまだ誰だれにも教おしえていません。
I haven't given anyone my new address yet.

来月らいげつの旅行りょこうのことは、両親りょうしんにまだ話はなしていないんです。
I haven't told my parents about next month's trip yet.

注文ちゅうもんした品物しなものがまだ届とどいていないのですが、確認かくにんしていただけますか。
The item I ordered still hasn't arrived - could you look into it?

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Nuance & register

It promises the action is coming. まだ払はらっていません means the bill is unpaid and that paying is still on the table. That is why it makes such a good excuse and such a good apology: it admits the gap and implies you will close it. If the thing genuinely will never happen, this pattern is the wrong choice.

It is softer than it looks. Compared with a flat いいえ, answering まだです or まだ決きめていません leaves the door open and buys time. In business Japanese this is a standard hedge: 社内しゃないでまだ検討けんとうしております - "we are still considering it internally" - says no without saying no.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is carried by the ending, not by まだ. まだ～ていません polite, まだ～ていない plain, まだ～てない casual and extremely common in speech. まだ？ on its own is blunt and impatient; まだですか presses; and neither should be aimed at a superior.

Watch the stative verbs. A handful of verbs describe states already and do not take ている in the negative: it is まだ分わかりません ("I don't know yet"), まだ要いりません ("I don't need it yet"), and まだありません. The most notorious is 知しる - "I don't know yet" is まだ知しりません, never ❌まだ知しっていません.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · まだ～ていません vs ～ませんでした

Rule of thumb - ～ませんでした closes the book: it didn't happen, end of story. まだ～ていません leaves it open: it hasn't happened so far.

お昼ひるはまだ食たべていません。
I haven't had lunch yet. (and I intend to)

昨日きのうはお昼ひるを食たべませんでした。
I didn't have lunch yesterday. (I skipped it - that day is over)

English uses "haven't" and "didn't" for this distinction and Japanese uses the aspect of the verb, so the mapping is clean once you see it. If you can add "yet" to the English, you need まだ～ていません.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · まだ～ていません vs もう～ました

Rule of thumb - these two are the answer pair to the same question. もう～ました = done. まだ～ていません = not done. Nothing else belongs in that slot.

「もう薬くすりを飲のみましたか。」「はい、もう飲のみました。」
"Have you taken your medicine?" "Yes, I've taken it."

「もう薬くすりを飲のみましたか。」「いいえ、まだ飲のんでいません。」
"Have you taken your medicine?" "No, not yet."

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · まだ～ていません vs もう～ました (cont.)

Note the asymmetry that trips everyone up: the affirmative answer uses the plain past, but the negative answer cannot. ❌いいえ、飲のみませんでした would mean you skipped it altogether - a different, and in a hospital a dangerous, answer.

まだ～ていません vs ～たことがありません

Rule of thumb - "not yet" implies you expect to. "Never" makes no such promise. まだ～ていません is about a pending task; ～たことがありません is about an empty life-experience column.

来週らいしゅうの会議かいぎの資料しりょうは、まだ作つくっていません。
I haven't made the materials for next week's meeting yet.

北海道ほっかいどうへは行いったことがありません。
I've never been to Hokkaido.

まだ～ていません vs ～たことがありません (cont.)

Saying 北海道ほっかいどうへはまだ行いっていません is not wrong, but it means something narrower: a trip is planned, or expected of you, and it has not happened yet. Use it when there is a plan; use ～たことがありません when there is only an absence.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Common mistakes

  • ❌ まだレポートを出だしませんでした。 → ✅ まだレポートを出だしていません。
    まだ cannot sit with a plain past negative. The two contradict each other: one says "still pending", the other says "over and done with".

  • ❌ もう荷物にもつが届とどいていません。 → ✅ まだ荷物にもつが届とどいていません。
    もう～ていません means "not any more". With a parcel that never came, you want まだ.

  • ❌ まだ知しっていません。 → ✅ まだ知しりません。
    知しる has no negative ている form. This exception survives inside the まだ pattern too.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ まだ食たべないです。 → ✅ まだ食たべていません。
    食たべない means "won't eat" - a refusal, not a delay. The negative belongs on いる.

  • ❌ お寿司すしはまだ食たべていません。（meaning "I've never eaten sushi"）→ ✅ お寿司すしは食たべたことがありません。
    まだ promises that it is going to happen. For a plain absence of experience, use ～たことがありません.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Practice 1

1. 「もうチケットを買かいましたか。」「いいえ、まだ買か____。」

いいえ、まだ**買かっていません**。

The negative attaches to いる, giving 買かって + いません. The answer also implies you still mean to buy them, which is exactly what the questioner wants to know.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Practice 2

2. 昨日きのうは忙いそがしくて、晩ばんごはんを（食たべませんでした・まだ食たべていません）。

昨日きのうは忙いそがしくて、晩ばんごはんを**食たべませんでした**。

Yesterday is finished, so the meal was skipped rather than postponed. まだ～ていません would claim that yesterday's dinner is still coming, which time no longer allows.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I haven't decided yet."

まだ決きめていません。 （casual: まだ決きめてない。）

決きめる → 決きめて → 決きめていません. This is one of the most useful sentences in the language, because it declines to answer without refusing to answer.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the problem: メールをまだ送おくりませんでした。すみません、これから送おくります。

✅ メールをまだ送おくっていません。すみません、これから送おくります。

The second sentence says you are about to send it, so the first cannot use ませんでした - that would mean the sending is off the table. まだ送おくっていません keeps the action pending, which matches the apology.

まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) その本ほんはまだ読よんでいません。 (b) その本ほんは読よんだことがありません。

(a) says the book is on your list. It is sitting on the shelf, someone recommended it, perhaps it was assigned - and you fully expect to get to it.

(b) simply reports that reading it has never been part of your life. There is no implication of a plan, and none of guilt. If your professor asks whether you have read the set text, (a) is a promise and (b) is a confession.

もう - already; anymore

Meaning · the change has happened - already, by now, or (with a negative) not any more

もう marks a threshold as crossed. Whatever the situation used to be, it is not that any longer: the meal is eaten, the shop is shut, the pain has stopped. Point it at a positive sentence and you get already; point it at a negative one and you get not any more.

もう - already; anymore · Usage

もう is an adverb and never changes shape. It normally comes early in the sentence, though it can sit right before the word it modifies.

With a past or completed predicate it means already: もう終おわりました, もう出でかけた, もう大丈夫だいじょうぶです. In a question it is the standard way to ask whether something is done: もう見みましたか.

With a negative predicate it means no longer: もう吸すいません (I don't smoke any more), もう子こどもじゃない (not a child any more). The change has already happened and there is no going back.

もう - already; anymore · Usage (cont.)

Before a number or quantity it means another, more: もう一杯いっぱい (one more glass), もう一度いちど (once more), もう少すこし (a bit more), もうちょっと. This use has nothing to do with time, and learners often meet it before they meet the other two.

Before a time expression it means the moment has arrived: もうすぐ着つきます (arriving shortly), もう帰かえる時間じかんだ (time to go home). And on its own, もう is an interjection of exasperation - see below.

もう - already; anymore · Examples

もう昼ひるごはんを食たべましたか。
Have you had lunch already?

もうお酒さけは飲のみません。
I don't drink any more.

コーヒーをもう一杯いっぱいいかがですか。
Would you like another cup of coffee?

もうこんな時間じかん。そろそろ失礼しつれいします。
Look at the time already. I ought to be going.

もう - already; anymore · Nuance & register

もう itself is neutral - it belongs equally to a text message and to a meeting. What varies is what surrounds it, and もう has a few spoken lives worth knowing.

もう？ is surprise. Said with a rising tone in reply to news, it means "that soon?" - え、もう終おわったの？. It is friendly, and it is one of the easiest natural-sounding reactions to learn.

もう！ is exhaustion. Alone, drawn out, it expresses irritation or fond exasperation: もう、遅おそいよ！. This interjection leans female and very casual, and it is the もう you will hear most often in drama.

もう - already; anymore · Nuance & register (cont.)

もういい needs care. It means "that's enough" and lands anywhere between "I'm full, thank you" and "forget it, I'm done with this," depending entirely on tone. もういいです said flatly to a shop assistant sounds like giving up on them; 結構けっこうです is the safe refusal.

Formal writing prefers すでに. Reports and notices use すでに完了かんりょうしております rather than もう. もう is not rude, just conversational.

もう - already; anymore · もう vs まだ

Rule of thumb - もう is on the far side of the change; まだ is on the near side. Every もう sentence has a まだ mirror image, and the mirror flips in the negative as well as the positive.

会議かいぎはもう始はじまりました。
The meeting has already started.

会議かいぎはまだ始はじまっていません。
The meeting hasn't started yet.

Now the negatives, which is where English gives you two completely different words:

足あしはもう痛いたくありません。
My leg doesn't hurt any more. (it did; it stopped)

もう - already; anymore · もう vs まだ (cont.)

足あしはまだ痛いたいです。
My leg still hurts. (nothing has changed)

Keep hold of the direction rather than the English: もう + negative looks back at something that has ended, まだ + negative looks forward to something that has not begun. もう働はたらいていません is a retirement; まだ働はたらいていません is a student.

もう - already; anymore · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 宿題しゅくだいはもうしています。（intending "I've already done it"）→ ✅ 宿題しゅくだいはもうしました。
    もう～ている says you are already in the middle of it. Completion in the past takes the plain past: もう～ました.

  • ❌ バスがもう来きていません。（intending "the bus hasn't come yet"）→ ✅ バスがまだ来きていません。
    With a negative, もう means "not any more" - you would be saying the bus service has stopped running.

  • ❌ コーヒーをもう一いちください。 → ✅ コーヒーをもう一杯いっぱいください。
    もう needs a full counter phrase after it: 一杯いっぱい, 一ひとつ, 一度いちど, 少すこし.

もう - already; anymore · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ （in a written report）作業さぎょうはもう終おわりました。 → ✅ 作業さぎょうはすでに完了かんりょうしております。
    Not ungrammatical, just conversational. Formal writing reaches for すでに.

もう - already; anymore · Practice 1

1. 「映画えいがのチケット、____買かいましたか。」「はい、昨日きのう買かいました。」

「映画えいがのチケット、もう買かいましたか。」

Asking whether something is done by now is the core use of もう with the past tense. It is also the question that まだ～ていません exists to answer in the negative.

もう - already; anymore · Practice 2

2. 昔むかしはたばこを吸すっていましたが、（もう・まだ）吸すっていません。

昔むかしはたばこを吸すっていましたが、もう吸すっていません。

The first half establishes that the speaker used to smoke, so the habit has ended: "not any more." まだ would mean they had never started, which contradicts 昔むかしは吸すっていました.

もう - already; anymore · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) もう少すこし待まってください。 (b) もう待まちません。

(a) uses the quantity sense: もう少すこし is "a little more", so the speaker is asking for extra time. It is polite and cooperative.

(b) uses the negative sense: the waiting is over and will not continue. Depending on the tone it is either a decision to give up or an outright threat. The same adverb, doing two of its jobs, in sentences that could not be further apart.

～たことがある

Meaning · to have done something before - an experience you carry with you

～たことがある is not a past tense. It says that somewhere in your life there exists an instance of doing this thing. Literally: "there is a time-when-I-did-it." That is why it uses ある - the experience is treated as something you possess, not as an event on a calendar.

～たことがある · Formation

Take the た-form, then add ことがある.

～たことがある · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb た-form + ことがある 行いく → 行いった → 行いったことがある
Verb (る-verb) た-form + ことがある 食たべる → 食たべた → 食たべたことがある
Verb (する／来くる) した／来きた + ことがある する → したことがある
い-adjective かった + ことがある 忙いそがしい → 忙いそがしかったことがある
な-adjective だった + ことがある 大変たいへんだ → 大変たいへんだったことがある
Noun だった + ことがある 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいだったことがある
Polite ある → あります 行いったことがあります
Negative ある → ない／ありません 行いったことがない
Past ある → あった／ありました 行いったことがありました

～たことがある · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the た is compulsory and is not the past tense. It is the form the pattern is built on, the way ～ておく is built on the て-form. A dictionary form here gives you a different pattern entirely (see Contrast).

～たことがある · Examples

おすしを**食たべたことがあります**か。
Have you ever eaten sushi?

北海道ほっかいどうに**行いったことがあります**。
I've been to Hokkaido.

私わたしは馬うまに**乗のったことがない**。
I've never ridden a horse.

富士山ふじさんに**登のぼったことある**?
Have you ever climbed Mt. Fuji?

～たことがある · Examples (cont.)

学生がくせいのとき、パン屋やで**働はたらいたことがあります**。
When I was a student I worked at a bakery for a while.

一度いちどだけ、テレビで見みた人ひとに道みちで**会あったことがある**。
Just once, I've run into someone on the street that I'd seen on TV.

日本にほんに三年さんねん住すんでいましたが、京都きょうとには一度いちども**行いったことがありません**。
I lived in Japan for three years, but I've never once been to Kyoto.

こんなに美うつくしい景色けしきは今いままで**見みたことがない**。
I have never seen scenery this beautiful in my life.

～たことがある · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. あります polite, ある plain, and nothing about the pattern marks age or gender.

Two contractions dominate spoken Japanese and will ambush you:

  • が drops. 行いったことある? - this is the ordinary spoken form of the
    question. So is 見みたことない, for the negative.
  • が becomes は for emphasis. 見みたことはない - "I've certainly
    never seen it (whatever else I may have done)." The は adds the contrast that は always adds.

～たことがある · Nuance & register (cont.)

The pattern carries a built-in sense of distance. The experience has to feel like part of your history, not like something from this morning. That is why a specific recent time and ～たことがある fight each other: ❌ きのう、おすしを食たべたことがあります. Say 食たべました. Vague time expressions are fine, and in fact common: 前まえに, 昔むかし, 一度いちど, 子こどものとき, 今いままで.

It also implies the experience is worth remarking on. Everyday, everyone-does-it actions sound strange: ❌ ご飯はんを食たべたことがあります is odd, because of course you have. The exception is when you are genuinely drawing a line - 納豆なっとうは食たべたことがありません is fine, because plenty of people haven't.

～たことがある · Nuance & register (cont.)

一度いちども + negative is the fixed way to say "not even once", and the two must agree: 一度いちども行いったことがありません.

～たことがある · ～たことがある vs the plain past

Rule of thumb - plain past puts the event on a timeline. ～たことがある puts it on your résumé.

去年きょねん、北海道ほっかいどうに行いきました。
I went to Hokkaido last year.

That is a report of one occasion, anchored to a date. Which occasion matters.

北海道ほっかいどうに**行いったことがあります**。
I've been to Hokkaido.

That is a fact about me. When I went, or how often, is beside the point - what is being claimed is that Hokkaido is inside my experience.

～たことがある · ～たことがある vs the plain past (cont.)

This is why a definite time expression forces the plain past. 去年きょねん, きのう, 三時さんじに all pin the event down, and pinning it down is exactly what ～たことがある refuses to do. English "I have been to Hokkaido" behaves the same way - you cannot say "I have been there yesterday" either.

～たことがある · ～たことがある vs ～たことがない

Rule of thumb - the negation lands on ある, never on the verb.

海外かいがいに**行いったことがありません**。
I've never been abroad.

Learners reach for the verb and produce ❌行いかなかったことがあります. That is not a negative - it means "there have been occasions when I didn't go", which is a real but very different sentence.

The negative is used far more than the affirmative in practice, and it has its own set of companions: 一度いちども (not once), まだ (not yet), 今いままで (up to now).

～たことがある · ～たことがある vs ～たことがない (cont.)

まだ新あたらしい店みせに行いったことがないんです。
I haven't been to the new place yet.

Past tense on ある gives you "hadn't, at that point": 去年きょねんまで一度いちども飛行機ひこうきに乗のったことがありませんでした - "until last year I'd never flown."

～たことがある · ～たことがある vs ～ることがある

Rule of thumb - た-form = "I have done it". Dictionary form = "it sometimes happens".

Same three words, and swapping the verb form changes the meaning completely.

電車でんしゃが**遅おくれたことがあります**。
The train has been late before. (I've experienced it)

電車でんしゃが**遅おくれることがあります**。
The train is sometimes late. (it happens from time to time)

The dictionary-form version is about frequency in the present, so it works with ときどき, たまに, and with negatives: 朝あさごはんを食たべないことがあります - "there are times when I skip breakfast."

～たことがある · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 北海道ほっかいどうに**行いくことがあります**。（meaning "I've been"）→ ✅ 北海道ほっかいどうに**行いったことがあります**。
    Dictionary form gives you "I sometimes go there", which is a different claim. Experience needs the た-form.

  • ❌ きのう、おすしを食たべたことがあります。 → ✅ きのう、おすしを**食たべました**。
    A specific recent time pins the event to the calendar, and ～たことがある is precisely the form that refuses to be pinned. Use the plain past.

  • ❌ 日本にほんに行いったことをあります。 → ✅ 日本にほんに行いったことがあります。
    ある is intransitive and never takes を. The が here is doing its normal job: marking what exists.

～たことがある · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 一度いちども見みたことがあります。 → ✅ 一度いちども見みたことがありません。
    一度いちども requires a negative to complete it, the way English "not once" does.

  • ❌ 学生がくせいことがあります。 → ✅ 学生がくせいだったことがあります。
    Nouns and な-adjectives need だった in front of ことがある. The pattern always wants a plain past form before it, whatever the word class.

～たことがある · Practice 1

1. 馬うまに乗の____ことがありますか。

馬うまに乗のったことがありますか。

The pattern is built on the た-form. 乗のる is a う-verb ending in る, so る → った: 乗のる → 乗のった.

～たことがある · Practice 2

2. "Trains around here are sometimes delayed." この辺へんの電車でんしゃは（遅おくれる・遅おくれた）ことがあります。

この辺へんの電車でんしゃは**遅おくれる**ことがあります。

Dictionary form + ことがある means "it happens from time to time" - a statement about how often something occurs. The た-form would have said "the trains have been late before", which is about experience rather than frequency.

～たことがある · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I've never been to Kyoto."

京都きょうとに**行いったことがありません**。 （casual: 京都きょうと、行いったことない。）

The negation goes on ある, not on the verb - the た-form stays fixed. Adding 一度いちども gives "never once": 京都きょうとには一度いちども行いったことがありません。

～たことがある · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 先週せんしゅう、あの映画えいがを見みたことがあります。

✅ 先週せんしゅう、あの映画えいがを**見みました**。

先週せんしゅう locates the event on a specific occasion, and ～たことがある cannot take a definite recent time - it deliberately leaves when open. Drop the time word and 見みたことがあります becomes fine again.

～たことがある · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 沖縄おきなわに行いきました。 (b) 沖縄おきなわに行いったことがあります。

(a) reports a particular trip. It belongs in a narrative - the listener will expect to hear when, or who with, or what happened there. On its own it can sound like the opening of a story.

(b) makes no claim about any particular trip. It says only that Okinawa is inside my experience, which is what you offer when someone asks "have you been?" or mentions Okinawa in passing. It is a fact about the speaker, not about a day.
