の - of; possessive

Meaning · links two nouns, with the first one describing or owning the second

の is the glue between nouns. 私わたしの本ほん is "my book", 日本語にほんごの本ほん is "a Japanese-language book", 木きの机つくえ is "a wooden desk" - ownership is only the most obvious of a much wider set of relationships.

の - of; possessive · Formation

The rule is one-directional and never varies: the modifier comes first, the head noun comes last. Whatever の points at, the thing being described is always on the right.

の - of; possessive · Formation (cont.)

Chained N1 の N2 の N3 友ともだちの車くるまのかぎ
Apposition ("N1, who is N2") N1 の N2 友ともだちのミラーさん
Material or origin N1 の N2 木きの机つくえ／イタリアのくつ
Standing in for the noun N1 の (nothing after) この傘かさは姉あねのです
After another particle N + particle + の + N 友人ゆうじんからの手紙てがみ
❌ な-adjective takes な, not の ❌きれいの花はな → ✅きれいな花はな
❌ い-adjective attaches bare ❌大おおきいの家いえ → ✅大おおきい家いえ

の - of; possessive · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - の joins a noun to a noun. If the word in front of it is an adjective, の is wrong: い-adjectives attach directly, な-adjectives take な.

の - of; possessive · Examples

これは私わたしの自転車じてんしゃです。
This is my bicycle.

田中たなかさんの会社かいしゃは東京とうきょうにあります。
Mr Tanaka's company is in Tokyo.

この傘かさは姉あねのです。
This umbrella is my sister's.

イタリア語ごの本ほんを三さん冊さつ借かりました。
I borrowed three Italian-language books.

の - of; possessive · Examples (cont.)

友ともだちのミラーさんが来週らいしゅう遊あそびに来きます。
My friend Miller is coming to visit next week.

会社かいしゃの近ちかくにおいしいラーメン屋やがあります。
There's a good ramen place near the office.

これは母ははが焼やいたパンで、あちらは店みせのです。
This is bread my mother baked; that one over there is from the shop.

大学だいがく時代じだいの友人ゆうじんからの手紙てがみが、久ひさしぶりに届とどきました。
A letter arrived from a university friend for the first time in ages.

の - of; possessive · Nuance & register

の is completely register-neutral - the single most common word in Japanese text, and invisible in the way that English "of" and "'s" are invisible.

Three things worth knowing beyond the basic rule:

の is vague on purpose. 山田やまださんの本ほん can mean the book Yamada owns, the book Yamada wrote, the book Yamada is holding, or the book about Yamada. Japanese does not distinguish these, and context does all the work. Do not go looking for a more precise particle; there isn't one.

の - of; possessive · Nuance & register (cont.)

の can stand in for a noun already understood. これは私わたしのです - "this is mine." The noun has been deleted, not the の. This is why 赤あかいの ("the red one") looks similar but is a different の: there, の is standing in for a whole noun after an adjective rather than marking a possessor.

Chains get heavy fast. 会社かいしゃの近ちかくの駅えきの前まえの店みせ is grammatical and no Japanese speaker would say it. Two の in a row is ordinary; three starts to strain; four means you should rebuild the sentence.

の - of; possessive · Nuance & register (cont.)

Contracted ん in speech. In fast casual speech の sometimes reduces: 俺おれんち ("my place", from 俺おれの家うち), 君きみんとこ. These are fixed, informal, and mostly limited to places and homes; do not generalise the pattern.

の - of; possessive · の vs な

Rule of thumb - の after a noun, な after a な-adjective. The words look alike in the dictionary, which is the whole problem.

Many な-adjectives - きれい, 有名ゆうめい, 元気げんき, 便利べんり - feel like nouns to a learner, and some of them genuinely can be nouns in other contexts. When they are describing the following noun, they take な.

有名ゆうめいな店みせに行いきました。
I went to a famous shop.

東京とうきょうの店みせに行いきました。
I went to a shop in Tokyo.

の - of; possessive · の vs な (cont.)

東京とうきょう is a noun, so it takes の; 有名ゆうめい is a な-adjective, so it takes な. ❌有名ゆうめいの店みせ and ❌東京とうきょうな店みせ are both wrong, and the second one is instantly noticeable to a native ear.

The awkward middle case is 病気びょうき and 元気げんき, which go both ways depending on what you mean: 元気げんきな子こ = "a lively child"; 病気びょうきの子こ = "a child who is ill" (the noun "illness" describing them).

の - of; possessive · の (linking nouns) vs の (the nominaliser)

Rule of thumb - if the word in front of の is a noun, it is this の. If it is a verb or a clause, it is the nominaliser - a different job entirely.

私わたしの趣味しゅみは写真しゃしんです。
My hobby is photography. (noun + の - possession)

写真しゃしんをとるのが好すきです。
I like taking photographs. (verb + の - the verb has been made into a noun)

The two never compete for the same slot, so once you can see whether the word in front is a noun or a verb, the ambiguity disappears. Both are extremely common and you will use them in the same conversation.

の - of; possessive · AのB vs BのA

Rule of thumb - the head noun goes last, always. English puts it first ("a map of Tokyo") and second ("Tokyo*'s* map"), which is exactly why learners get the order backwards.

東京とうきょうの地図ちずを買かいました。
I bought a map of Tokyo.

地図ちずの東京とうきょう
the Tokyo that belongs to the map - not a thing anyone would say

の - of; possessive · AのB vs BのA (cont.)

The test that always works: what am I actually talking about? In "a map of Tokyo" you are talking about a map, so 地図ちず goes at the end. In "the teacher's car" you are talking about a car, so 車くるま goes at the end: 先生せんせいの車くるま.

の - of; possessive · Common mistakes

  • ❌ きれいの花はなをもらいました。 → ✅ きれいな花はなをもらいました。
    きれい is a な-adjective despite ending in い. It takes な, never の.

  • ❌ 大おおきいの家いえに住すんでいます。 → ✅ 大おおきい家いえに住すんでいます。
    い-adjectives attach straight to the noun. Nothing goes in between.

  • ❌ 車くるまの私わたしです。 → ✅ 私わたしの車くるまです。
    The owner comes first and the thing owned comes last. Reversing them reverses the meaning, or produces nonsense.

の - of; possessive · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 日本語にほんご先生せんせいに聞ききました。 → ✅ 日本語にほんごの先生せんせいに聞ききました。
    Two nouns in a row need の to link them. (Some pairs have fused into single compound words over time, but you cannot invent new ones.)

  • ❌ これは私わたしのかばんのです。 → ✅ これは私わたしのかばんです。
    の stands in for a missing noun. When the noun is right there, a second の has nothing to do.

の - of; possessive · Practice 1

1. これは田中たなかさん____傘かさですか。

これは田中たなかさんの傘かさですか。

Two nouns side by side always need の between them. Note that the answer could also drop the second noun entirely - これは田中たなかさんのですか - because の can stand in for a noun both speakers already have in mind.

の - of; possessive · Practice 2

2. 静しずか（の・な）部屋へやをお願ねがいします。

静しずかな部屋へやをお願ねがいします。

静しずか is a な-adjective, so it links to the following noun with な. の is for nouns only, and the difference is audible - ❌静しずかの部屋へや marks a speaker as a beginner faster than almost any other error.

の - of; possessive · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "That's my mother's friend's dog."

あれは母ははの友ともだちの犬いぬです。

Chained の runs in the same order as the English possessives - mother, then friend, then dog - with the thing you are actually talking about at the very end. Two の in a row like this is perfectly ordinary.

の - of; possessive · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 有名ゆうめいの人ひとに会あいました。

✅ 有名ゆうめいな人ひとに会あいました。

有名ゆうめい is a な-adjective. The の here comes from thinking of it as a noun meaning "fame", which it is not in this sentence - it is describing the person, so it needs な.

の - of; possessive · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 日本にほんの車くるまを買かいました。 (b) 車くるまの日本にほんを買かいました。

(a) is a normal sentence: 日本にほん describes 車くるま, so this is "I bought a Japanese car." The head noun - the thing that was bought - sits at the end.

(b) reverses the roles, making 車くるま the description and 日本にほん the thing bought: "I bought the Japan that belongs to a car." It is not ambiguous or poetic, just broken. The lesson is that in Japanese the order carries all the meaning; there is no second particle to fall back on.

の - nominaliser

Meaning · turns a verb or a whole clause into a noun, so it can be a subject or an object

Japanese has no infinitive and no "-ing" noun. To say "swimming is fun" or "I forgot to lock the door", you take the clause you want and stick の on the end of it. What comes out behaves like an ordinary noun and can take が, を or は.

の - nominaliser · Formation

の attaches to the plain form of whatever is in front of it. Nouns and な-adjectives need な first - the same shape as ので and のに.

の - nominaliser · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (plain) dictionary form + の 泳およぐ → 泳およぐの
Verb (past) た-form + の 会あった → 会あったの
Verb (negative) ない-form + の 行いかない → 行いかないの
い-adjective plain form + の 安やすい → 安やすいの
な-adjective ～な + の 静しずかだ → 静しずかなの
Noun Noun + な + の 学生がくせいだ → 学生がくせいなの
A whole clause clause + の 彼かれが来こなかったの
Before です／だ の → ん 行いくのです → 行いくんです

の - nominaliser · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - never ❌だの. A noun or な-adjective takes な before the nominalising の, exactly as it does before ので.

Once nominalised, the phrase takes a case particle like any noun: 泳およぐのが好すきです、閉しめるのを忘わすれた、買かうのはやめた。

の - nominaliser · Examples

泳およぐのが好すきです。
I like swimming.

漢字かんじを覚おぼえるのは難むずかしい。
Memorising kanji is hard.

電気でんきを消けすのを忘わすれました。
I forgot to turn off the light.

子こどもが公園こうえんで遊あそんでいるのを見みました。
I saw children playing in the park.

の - nominaliser · Examples (cont.)

朝あさ早はやく起おきるのは苦手にがてです。
I'm no good at getting up early.

隣となりの部屋へやで誰だれかが歌うたっているのが聞きこえた。
I could hear someone singing in the next room.

彼女かのじょが会社かいしゃをやめたのを知しっていますか。
Did you know she'd quit the company?

雨あめの日ひに自転車じてんしゃで通勤つうきんするのは、思おもっていたより大変たいへんでした。
Commuting by bike on rainy days turned out to be harder than I'd expected.

の - nominaliser · Nuance & register

The nominaliser の is register-neutral, but it leans spoken and concrete. Its rival こと leans written and abstract, and the two are treated in the Contrast section below.

The thing worth real attention here is the ん contraction, because it is everywhere and it changes the feel of a sentence completely.

の becomes ん before です and だ. 行いくのです → 行いくんです. The のです form is the written or careful-speech version; んです is what you actually hear.

んです is not decoration. It frames the sentence as an explanation - the speaker is accounting for something, or asking the listener to account for something.

の - nominaliser · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 頭あたまが痛いたいです。 - "I have a headache." A fact, offered flat.
  • 頭あたまが痛いたいんです。 - "The thing is, I have a headache." Now it
    explains why you are leaving early.
  • どうしたんですか。 - "What's the matter?" Asking for an explanation. The bare
    ❌どうしましたか is possible but noticeably colder.

A learner who never uses んです sounds like they are reading a list; one who uses it in every sentence sounds like they are constantly justifying themselves. It belongs where there is a reason in the air.

の - nominaliser · Nuance & register (cont.)

The plain-form version is んだ: 明日あした、試験しけんなんだ。 Note the な survives the contraction - noun + な + んだ.

Finally, a sentence-final の with rising intonation is a soft question, and in Tokyo speech it is marked as feminine or as child-directed: どうしたの? 食たべないの? Men use it too, but usually with a following か or in a gentler register.

の - nominaliser · の vs こと

Rule of thumb - の for things you directly see, hear and experience; こと for abstractions, facts and reported information. Where either works, の is the spoken choice.

Three cases where the choice is not free:

Verbs of direct perception require の. 見みる、聞きく、感かんじる and friends are watching an event happen, not contemplating a fact.

猫ねこが走はしっているのを見みました。
I saw the cat running. (I watched it happen - の)

の - nominaliser · の vs こと (cont.)

Fixed expressions require こと. ことができる, ことがある, ことにする, ことになる are set phrases and admit no substitution: ❌泳およぐのができる.

X は Y です equations require こと. 趣味しゅみは本ほんを読よむことです。 With の this sounds like the hobby is the act happening in front of you.

Everywhere else - 泳およぐの／ことが好すきです - both are correct, and の is what you will hear.

の - nominaliser · の (nominaliser) vs の ("the one")

Rule of thumb - after a verb or clause, の makes an action into a noun. After an adjective, の usually stands in for a concrete thing: "the ~ one".

走はしるのが速はやいですね。
You're fast at running. (の = the act of running)

赤あかいのをください。
I'll take the red one. (の = the object itself)

の - nominaliser · の (nominaliser) vs の ("the one") (cont.)

In practice the difference is decided by what makes sense, and a clause can go either way: 昨日きのう買かったの can mean "the one I bought yesterday" or "the fact that I bought it yesterday". Context settles it, and native speakers do not experience this as ambiguous.

の - nominaliser · ～のです／んです vs the plain statement

Rule of thumb - んです says "here is the explanation". A plain です sentence just states the fact.

明日あした、東京とうきょうへ行いきます。
I'm going to Tokyo tomorrow.

明日あした、東京とうきょうへ行いくんです。
I'm going to Tokyo tomorrow, you see. (…which is why I can't come / why I'm packing)

の - nominaliser · ～のです／んです vs the plain statement (cont.)

Get this wrong in either direction and you sound odd. Answering 何なにをしていますか with a bare fact is fine; answering どうして来こなかったんですか without んです in your own reply sounds evasive, because the question explicitly asked for a reason.

の - nominaliser · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 日本語にほんごを話はなすのができます。 → ✅ 日本語にほんごを話はなすことができます。
    ことができる is a fixed expression. の cannot be substituted into it, however natural the rest of the sentence sounds.

  • ❌ 静しずかのが好すきです。 → ✅ 静しずかなのが好すきです。
    な-adjectives take な before the nominaliser, exactly as they do before ので and のに.

  • ❌ 猫ねこが鳴ないていることを聞ききました。 → ✅ 猫ねこが鳴ないているのを聞ききました。
    With こと this becomes "I heard that the cat was crying" - secondhand news. Direct perception needs の.

  • ❌ 学生がくせいのんです。 → ✅ 学生がくせいなんです。
    A noun keeps its な when の contracts to ん. ❌学生がくせいのんです and ❌学生がくせいだんです are both wrong.

の - nominaliser · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 窓まどを閉しめるの忘わすれた。 → ✅ 窓まどを閉しめるのを忘わすれた。
    Once you have made a noun, it needs a particle like any other noun. (を does drop in fast speech, but learn the full form first.)

の - nominaliser · Practice 1

1. 毎朝まいあさ走はしる____は気持きもちがいいです。

毎朝まいあさ走はしるのは気持きもちがいいです。

走はしる is a verb, and the sentence needs a subject for 気持きもちがいい - so the verb has to become a noun first. こと would also be grammatical here; の is the more spoken choice.

の - nominaliser · Practice 2

2. 私わたしはギターを弾ひく（の・こと）ができます。

私わたしはギターを弾ひくことができます。

ことができる is a set phrase for ability, and its こと is frozen in place. This is one of the small number of contexts where の and こと are not interchangeable at all.

の - nominaliser · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I saw him get on the train."

彼かれが電車でんしゃに乗のるのを見みました。

Direct perception - you watched it with your own eyes - so this must be の, not こと. Note that the subject inside the nominalised clause takes が, never は.

の - nominaliser · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 来週らいしゅう、試験しけんだのです。

✅ 来週らいしゅう、試験しけんなのです。 (spoken: 試験しけんなんです)

A noun takes な before the nominaliser, never だ. The same な is what survives into the contracted んです, which is why you hear 試験しけんなんです and never ❌試験しけんだんです.

の - nominaliser · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 田中たなかさんは来きません。 (b) 田中たなかさんは来こないんです。

(a) simply reports that Tanaka is not coming. It is the right sentence if someone asked you for the guest list.

(b) presents the same fact as the explanation for something - perhaps why there is a spare chair, or why the meeting is being moved. んです always points at a surrounding situation the fact is accounting for, and using it with no such situation in view makes the listener hunt for one.

Noun-modifying clauses

Meaning · put a whole clause in front of a noun to describe it - no relative pronoun, no word order change

English says "the book that I bought yesterday", moving the noun to the front and inserting a relative pronoun. Japanese says 昨日きのう買かった本ほん - clause first, noun last, nothing inserted. This one structure is the key to every sentence above N5.

Noun-modifying clauses · Formation

The clause goes in front of the noun, in the plain form. That is the whole rule.

Noun-modifying clauses · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + Noun 買かった本ほん／買かう本ほん
Verb (negative) ない-form + Noun 読よまない人ひと
Verb (continuous) ～ている + Noun 走はしっている人ひと
い-adjective plain form + Noun 高たかい店みせ／高たかかった店みせ
な-adjective ～な + Noun 有名ゆうめいな店みせ
Noun (describing) Noun + の + Noun 木きの机つくえ
Noun (as a predicate inside the clause) Noun + だった + Noun 先生せんせいだった人ひと

❌ Polite form inside never ❌買かいました本ほん

Noun-modifying clauses · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
❌ Topic は inside never ❌私わたしは買かった本ほん

Rule of thumb - three things Japanese does not do: it does not use a relative pronoun, it does not move the noun, and it does not use polite forms inside the clause. Everything you would express with "who / which / that" is expressed by position alone.

Note - the clause can be any length at all, and it keeps its own particles inside. 去年きょねん京都きょうとで買かった小ちいさい茶ちゃわん - every word before 茶ちゃわん is describing it. When reading, look for the noun at the end first, then work backwards.

Noun-modifying clauses · Examples

これは**母ははが作つくった**ケーキです。
This is a cake my mother made.

眼鏡めがねをかけている人ひとが中村なかむらさんです。
The person wearing glasses is Nakamura.

昨日きのう見みた映画えいがはとてもおもしろかったです。
The film I saw yesterday was really good.

去年きょねん泊とまったホテルは駅えきから遠とおかった。
The hotel we stayed at last year was a long way from the station.

Noun-modifying clauses · Examples (cont.)

父ちちの撮とった写真しゃしんが新聞しんぶんに載のりました。
A photograph my father took appeared in the newspaper.

甘あまい物ものが好すきな人ひとにこの店みせをおすすめします。
I'd recommend this shop to anyone who likes sweet things.

家族かぞくみんなで旅行りょこうに行いった思おもい出でを今いまでもよく覚おぼえています。
I still remember clearly the trip we all took as a family.

駅前えきまえに新あたらしくできた図書館としょかんは、夜よる九時くじまで開あいています。
The library that's just opened by the station stays open until nine at night.

Noun-modifying clauses · Nuance & register

The construction itself carries no register - it is simply how Japanese describes things, and it appears in every sentence type at every politeness level. What is register-marked is the fact that the clause is always plain: 買かった本ほんをお送おくりします is entirely polite, because politeness lives on the final verb of the sentence and nowhere else.

Three habits that separate fluent-sounding Japanese from translated English:

Long modifiers are normal. English resists front-loading and reaches for "which…" clauses instead. Japanese does not: a modifier of fifteen syllables sitting in front of a two-syllable noun is unremarkable in a newspaper. Learn to read to the end of the phrase before deciding what it means.

Noun-modifying clauses · Nuance & register (cont.)

が inside the clause often becomes の. 父ちちが撮とった写真しゃしん and 父ちちの撮とった写真しゃしん mean the same thing. The の version sounds slightly more compact and slightly more written; it is very common in headlines and titles. This substitution works only inside a modifying clause - never in a main sentence.

Ambiguity is tolerated. 私わたしが好すきな人ひと is "the person I like" in most contexts, but it can also be read as "the person who likes me". Japanese does not disambiguate this, and speakers rely on context rather than rebuilding the sentence.

Noun-modifying clauses · が vs の inside the clause

Rule of thumb - inside a noun-modifying clause, the subject may be marked が or の, with no change of meaning. Outside such a clause, only が works.

友ともだちが貸かしてくれた本ほんを読よんでいます。
I'm reading a book a friend lent me.

友ともだちの貸かしてくれた本ほんを読よんでいます。
I'm reading a book a friend lent me. (same meaning, slightly tighter)

Noun-modifying clauses · が vs の inside the clause (cont.)

Two guard rails. The の substitution fails as soon as the clause contains another の, because the string becomes unreadable. And it never applies to a main clause: ❌友ともだちの来きました is simply wrong, where 友ともだちが来きました is right.

Noun-modifying clauses · clause + Noun vs clause + の

Rule of thumb - a clause in front of a noun describes that noun. A clause in front of の turns the clause itself into a noun.

The two look identical until you notice what is at the end.

毎朝まいあさ走はしっている人ひとを見みました。
I saw a person who runs every morning.

毎朝まいあさ走はしっているのを見みました。
I saw them running every morning.

Noun-modifying clauses · clause + Noun vs clause + の (cont.)

In the first, the thing seen is a person. In the second, the thing seen is the event of running. Learners often produce the second when they meant the first, because の feels like a safe filler - it is not; it changes what the sentence is about.

Noun-modifying clauses · modifying clause vs ～という + Noun

Rule of thumb - if the noun could fill a slot inside the clause, modify it directly. If the clause is the content of the noun, you need という.

田中たなかさんが書かいた手紙てがみを読よみました。
I read a letter Tanaka wrote. (the letter is what he wrote - direct modification)

田中たなかさんが来こないという話はなしを聞ききました。
I heard that Tanaka isn't coming. (the news is the clause - という)

Noun-modifying clauses · modifying clause vs ～という + Noun (cont.)

The test: can you put the noun back into the clause? 田中たなかさんが手紙てがみを書かいた works, so 手紙てがみ modifies directly. ❌田中たなかさんが話はなしを来こない does not work, so 話はなし needs という. Content nouns like 話はなし、ニュース、約束やくそく and 予定よてい behave this way; you will meet the rule properly at N4.

Noun-modifying clauses · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 昨日きのう買かいました本ほんはこれです。 → ✅ 昨日きのう買かった本ほんはこれです。
    The clause is always plain. Politeness goes on the last verb of the sentence, which here is です.

  • ❌ 昨日きのう見みたの映画えいがはよかった。 → ✅ 昨日きのう見みた映画えいがはよかった。
    Nothing goes between the clause and the noun. The の is imported from 私わたしの本ほん, where it links two nouns - a verb needs no linker.

  • ❌ 人ひとが走はしっている、田中たなかさんです。 → ✅ 走はしっている人ひとが田中たなかさんです。
    The description goes before the noun. Japanese has no way to hang a clause off the back of a noun.

Noun-modifying clauses · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 私わたしは買かった本ほんをなくしました。 → ✅ 私わたしが買かった本ほんをなくしました。
    は marks the topic of a whole sentence and cannot appear inside a modifying clause. Inside, the subject takes が (or の).

  • ❌ 有名ゆうめい人ひとに会あいました。 → ✅ 有名ゆうめいな人ひとに会あいました。
    な-adjectives keep their な in front of the noun, just as they do outside a clause.

Noun-modifying clauses · Practice 1

1. ____人ひとは山田やまださんです。（あそこで新聞しんぶんを読よんでいます）

あそこで新聞しんぶんを読よんでいる人ひとは山田やまださんです。

Two changes: the clause moves in front of 人ひと, and the polite 読よんでいます becomes plain 読よんでいる. Nothing is inserted between the clause and the noun.

Noun-modifying clauses · Practice 2

2. 母はは（が・は）作つくった料理りょうりはおいしいです。

母ははが作つくった料理りょうりはおいしいです。

は belongs to the main sentence - here it is already marking 料理りょうり as the topic. Inside a modifying clause the subject takes が, and in this position の would also be acceptable: 母ははの作つくった料理りょうり.

Noun-modifying clauses · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The city where I was born is small."

私わたしが生うまれた町まちは小ちいさいです。

English needs "where"; Japanese needs nothing - the relationship between 町まち and the clause is carried by position alone. Note again that the clause verb is plain (生うまれた) while the sentence ends politely.

Noun-modifying clauses · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: きのう会あいましたの人ひとは先生せんせいでした。

✅ きのう会あった人ひとは先生せんせいでした。

Two errors in one phrase: the clause must be plain (会あった, not 会あいました), and there is no の between a clause and the noun it describes. The の is only for linking one noun to another.

Noun-modifying clauses · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 昨日きのう買かった本ほんを読よみました。 (b) 昨日きのう買かったのを読よみました。

(a) names the thing explicitly: a book, bought yesterday, was read. It works whether or not the listener already knows about any book.

(b) leaves the noun out and lets の stand in for it, so it only works when both speakers already know what is being talked about - "I read the one I bought yesterday." Grammatically the difference is whether there is a noun at the end of the clause at all; conversationally, (b) assumes shared context and (a) does not.

Counters

Meaning · you cannot count a Japanese noun with a bare number - every noun needs a counter word

English says "three apples". Japanese says りんごを三みっつ - number plus a counter chosen to fit the kind of thing being counted. The counters themselves are easy; the readings are not, because the number and the counter fuse and change sound. Those changes are the whole content of this page.

Counters · Formation

The counter attaches directly to the number, and the resulting phrase usually sits after the noun and its particle, behaving like an adverb: りんごを三みっつ買かいました。

Number ～つ ～人にん ～本ほん ～枚まい ～回かい

Rule of thumb - the sound changes are not random. A counter starting with h (本ほん、杯はい、匹ひき、分ふん) shifts to p after 1, 6, 8 and 10, and to b after 三さん (三本さんぼん、三杯さんばい、三匹さんびき - though 三分さんぷん keeps the p). A counter starting with k or s (回かい、個こ、冊さつ、歳さい) doubles its consonant after 1, 6, 8 and 10. Learn the pattern once and most counters fall out of it.

Counters · The three worst columns, 1 to 10 (cont.)

Note - a few readings have accepted alternatives. 七人しちにん is also said ななにん; 九人きゅうにん is also くにん; 十本じゅっぽん is also じっぽん (the older prescribed form); 三階さんがい is also さんかい. All are heard. The ones with no alternative - ひとり, ふたり, よにん, はたち - are the ones to drill.

Counters · Examples

りんごを**三みっつ**買かいました。
I bought three apples.

切手きってを**五枚ごまい**ください。
Five stamps, please.

教室きょうしつに学生がくせいが**八人はちにん**います。
There are eight students in the classroom.

ビールを**二本にほん**注文ちゅうもんしました。
I ordered two bottles of beer.

Counters · Examples (cont.)

この映画えいがはもう**三回さんかい**見みました。
I've already seen this film three times.

冷つめたい水みずを**一杯いっぱい**いただけますか。
Could I have a glass of cold water?

すみません、大人おとな二枚にまいと子こども一枚いちまい、お願ねがいします。
Excuse me - two adults and one child, please.

去年きょねんの夏なつ、北海道ほっかいどうへ**二回にかい**行いきました。
I went to Hokkaido twice last summer.

Counters · Nuance & register

Counters are not optional and not stylistic - they are part of the grammar, and a bare number in front of a noun is simply ungrammatical.

The ～つ series is your safety net. It is native Japanese vocabulary, it runs only from one to ten, and it counts almost any concrete object whose proper counter you have forgotten. Nobody will misunderstand りんごを四よっつ. Above ten, ～つ stops existing and you fall back on 個こ or the specific counter.

Counters · Nuance & register (cont.)

People are the one place you cannot improvise. ❌いちにん and ❌ににん do not exist. 一人ひとり and 二人ふたり are irregular native forms, and from three on the counter switches to the Sino-Japanese にん. This is worth over- learning: 一人ひとりで ("alone") and 二人ふたりで ("the two of us") are everyday words.

Register. In shops and restaurants you will hear the deferential 名めい様さま for people - 二名様にめいさま - and お一人ひとり様さま. You are not expected to produce these at N5, only to recognise them. In casual speech the counter can drop when context is obvious: コーヒー、二ふたつ。

Counters · Nuance & register (cont.)

Where the phrase sits. The neutral position is after the noun-plus-particle, as an adverb. Putting it in front with の (三みっつのりんご) is also correct but feels more written and slightly more emphatic - it is what you use when the quantity is the point.

Counters · ～つ vs ～個

Rule of thumb - both count generic objects. ～つ is native, softer, and stops at ten; 個こ is Sino-Japanese, has no upper limit, and leans towards small, discrete, roundish things.

たまごを**六むっつ**使つかいます。
I'll use six eggs.

たまごを**十二個じゅうにこ**買かいました。
I bought twelve eggs.

The second sentence cannot use ～つ, because the series has no word for twelve. Below ten the two are largely interchangeable for objects like eggs, apples and boxes, with ～つ sounding a shade more everyday.

Counters · ～つ vs ～個 (cont.)

個こ is also what a shop assistant will say, because it works for any quantity and does not need a decision about shape.

Counters · ～回 vs ～階

Rule of thumb - 回かい counts times; 階かい counts floors. They sound almost identical, and the one place they diverge is at three: 三回さんかい but 三階さんがい.

エレベーターで**三階さんがい**まで上あがってください。
Please take the lift up to the third floor.

このボタンを**三回さんかい**押おしてください。
Please press this button three times.

Both 何回なんかい ("how many times") and 何階なんがい ("which floor") exist, and the 階かい one is voiced. Getting 三階さんがい right is a small thing that immediately marks a careful learner.

Counters · りんごを三つ vs 三つのりんご

Rule of thumb - the counter phrase normally goes after the noun and its particle. Moving it in front with の is grammatical but shifts the emphasis onto the quantity.

テーブルにりんごを**三みっつ**置おきました。
I put three apples on the table.

テーブルに**三みっつの**りんごを置おきました。
I put three apples on the table. (with the "three" foregrounded)

The first is what people say. The second reads like a description in a catalogue or a maths problem, and a learner who defaults to it - because it matches English word order - will sound stilted without ever making an outright error.

Counters · Common mistakes

  • ❌ りんごを三さん買かいました。 → ✅ りんごを三みっつ買かいました。
    A bare number cannot count a noun. You must choose a counter, and ～つ is the safe default for objects up to ten.

  • ❌ いちにん、ににん → ✅ 一人ひとり、二人ふたり
    The first two people are native readings and do not follow the pattern. From 三人さんにん onwards the Sino-Japanese にん takes over.

  • ❌ よんにん → ✅ よにん
    四人よにん drops the ん. (よんにん is widely heard, but よにん is what you should say and what the JLPT expects.)

  • ❌ さんほん、いちほん → ✅ さんぼん、いっぽん
    本ほん voices to ぼん after 三さん and geminates to ぽん after 1, 6, 8 and 10. The same shift hits 杯はい、匹ひき and 分ふん.

Counters · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 二につ、三さんつ → ✅ 二ふたつ、三みっつ
    The ～つ series takes native numerals (ひと、ふた、みっ…), never the Sino-Japanese ones. Mixing the two systems is the most audible counter error there is.

Counters · Practice 1

1. ノートを____買かいました。（3）

ノートを三冊さんさつ買かいました。（also acceptable: 三みっつ）

Notebooks are bound volumes, so the proper counter is 冊さつ - read さんさつ, with no sound change at three. If the counter escapes you, 三みっつ is always understood.

Counters · Practice 2

2. 事務所じむしょは（三回さんかい・三階さんがい）にあります。

事務所じむしょは三階さんがいにあります。

A floor of a building is 階かい, and after 三さん it voices to がい. 回かい would be counting occurrences, which makes no sense with にあります.

Counters · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "There were two cats and one dog in the garden."

庭にわに猫ねこが二匹にひきと犬いぬが一匹いっぴきいました。

Small animals take 匹ひき, which geminates to ぴき after one: いっぴき, not ❌いちひき. Note also that animals are counted with いる, not ある.

Counters · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 部屋へやに人ひとが三みっついます。

✅ 部屋へやに人ひとが三人さんにんいます。

The ～つ series counts objects and never people. People always take 人にん, which is irregular at one and two (一人ひとり, 二人ふたり) but perfectly regular from three: さんにん.

Counters · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) みかんを四よっつ食たべました。 (b) 四よっつのみかんを食たべました。

(a) is the ordinary sentence. The counter phrase sits after the object and its particle and works adverbially - this is how quantities are normally expressed, and it is what you should default to.

(b) attaches the quantity to the noun with の, which makes "the four mandarins" a single described thing. It is correct, but it foregrounds the number in a way that suits a written description or a context where the count itself is the news. Learners produce (b) too often because it mirrors English word order.

ずつ

Meaning · ~ each; ~ at a time - the same amount, distributed or repeated

ずつ takes a quantity and spreads it evenly. Either across people (一ひとつずつ = one each) or across time (少すこしずつ = a little at a time). Both readings come from the same idea: the same portion, again and again.

ずつ · Formation

ずつ attaches to the end of a quantity expression - a number with its counter, or a word of amount. It never attaches to a bare noun.

ずつ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Number + counter Number+Counter + ずつ 一ひとつ → 一ひとつずつ
Number + counter (people) 二人ふたり + ずつ 二人ふたりずつ
Bare number Number + ずつ 三さんずつ
Amount word 少すこし + ずつ 少すこしずつ
Casual amount word ちょっと + ずつ ちょっとずつ
With an object Noun を Number+Counter ずつ あめを二ふたつずつ
Per unit ～に + Number+Counter ずつ 一日いちにちに三さんページずつ
❌ Bare noun not possible ❌りんごずつ

ずつ · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - count first, ずつ second. The quantity always comes in front: 一ひとつずつ, never ❌ずつ一ひとつ. And there must be a quantity - ずつ cannot hang off a noun on its own.

Note - ずつ is written in hiragana. You will occasionally meet the old spelling づつ in older books and on shop signs; it is the same word and it is not the modern standard.

ずつ · Examples

あめを一ひとつずつとってください。
Take one sweet each.

毎日まいにち漢字かんじを五いつつずつ覚おぼえます。
I learn five kanji a day.

少すこしずつ上手じょうずになっていますよ。
You're getting better bit by bit.

二人ふたりずつグループを作つくってください。
Please form groups of two.

ずつ · Examples (cont.)

一日いちにちに三さんページずつ読よめば、一いっか月げつで終おわります。
If you read three pages a day, you'll finish it in a month.

貯金ちょきんは毎月まいつき少すこしずつ増ふえている。
My savings are growing a little at a time.

新あたらしい仕事しごとにも、少すこしずつ慣なれてきました。
I'm gradually getting used to the new job too.

お土産みやげのクッキーは、一人ひとり二に枚まいずつ配くばってください。
Please hand out the souvenir cookies, two per person.

ずつ · Nuance & register

ずつ is register-neutral and unremarkable - it appears in classroom instructions, in recipes, in business email and in conversation without changing shape.

Its two senses are worth separating in your head, because they behave differently:

Distributive - "each". 一人ひとり二に枚まいずつ. There is a set of recipients and everyone gets the same amount. This is the sense you need when handing things out, forming groups, or dividing anything up. Japanese uses ずつ here where English often uses nothing at all: "take one" is ambiguous in English, 一ひとつずつとって is not.

ずつ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Incremental - "at a time". 少すこしずつ. There is no set of recipients; the repetition is over time. This is by far the most common use of the word, and 少すこしずつ is close to being a fixed adverb meaning "gradually".

The casual variant is ちょっとずつ, which is warmer and softer than 少すこしずつ and belongs to speech only. You will also hear the emphatic 一ひとつ一ひとつ ("one by one, individually"), which is not the same thing: that stresses handling each item separately rather than distributing equally.

One small pitfall: ずつ needs the sharing to be even. If two people get different amounts, ずつ is wrong and you should just name the amounts.

ずつ · 一つ vs 一つずつ

Rule of thumb - without ずつ, the number is the total. With ずつ, the number is the share per person or per occasion.

クッキーを**二ふたつ**買かいました。
I bought two cookies. (two in total)

クッキーを**二ふたつずつ**買かいました。
I bought two cookies each. (two per person, so more than two altogether)

The difference is not decoration - it changes the arithmetic. Leaving ずつ out of a sentence about handing things round is one of the errors most likely to produce an actual misunderstanding.

ずつ · 少しずつ vs だんだん

Rule of thumb - 少すこしずつ describes the size of the steps; だんだん describes the direction of the change. They overlap in English as "gradually".

スープに水みずを少すこしずつ足たしてください。
Add the water to the soup a little at a time.

だんだん暖あたたかくなってきました。
It's been getting warmer and warmer.

You cannot swap them here: ❌だんだん水みずを足たしてください gives no instruction about portions at all.

ずつ · 少しずつ vs だんだん (cont.)

少すこしずつ implies a series of small increments you could in principle count; だんだん implies a smooth trend and carries no notion of portions. They combine happily - 少すこしずつだんだん良よくなる is redundant, but 少すこしずつ覚おぼえる and だんだん覚おぼえる differ: the first is about the batches, the second about the trajectory.

ずつ · ずつ vs それぞれ

Rule of thumb - ずつ needs a quantity and says everyone gets the same amount. それぞれ needs no quantity and says only that each person has their own - possibly different - one.

学生がくせいに本ほんを一冊いっさつずつ渡わたしました。
I handed each student one book. (same number for everybody)

学生がくせいはそれぞれ違ちがう意見いけんを持もっています。
The students each have different opinions.

Because ずつ insists on equality, ❌それぞれ違ちがう数かずずつ is self-contradictory. And because it needs a quantity, ずつ cannot replace それぞれ in the second sentence at all.

ずつ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ ずつ一ひとつとってください。 → ✅ 一ひとつずつとってください。
    ずつ follows the quantity; it never leads. Word order here is fixed.

  • ❌ りんごずつ買かいました。 → ✅ りんごを一ひとつずつ買かいました。
    ずつ attaches to an amount, not to a noun. Without a number and counter in front of it there is nothing for it to distribute.

  • ❌ 私わたしにケーキを一ひとつずつください。 → ✅ 私わたしにケーキを一ひとつください。
    With a single recipient there is nothing to distribute across, so ずつ has no work to do and sounds strange.

  • ❌ 少すこしずつずつ良よくなる → ✅ 少すこしずつ良よくなる
    One ずつ per quantity. Doubling it for emphasis is not a thing; if you want emphasis, use 少すこしずつではあるが or simply repeat the verb.

ずつ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんは三さん枚まい、山田やまださんは二に枚まいずつもらいました。 → ✅ …山田やまださんは二に枚まいもらいました。
    ずつ promises that the shares are equal. If they are not, drop it and state the amounts.

ずつ · Practice 1

1. クラスを四人よにん____のグループに分わけます。

クラスを四人よにんずつのグループに分わけます。

The point is that every group has the same size, which is exactly what ずつ encodes. Without it the sentence would just describe one group of four rather than a whole class divided evenly.

ずつ · Practice 2

2. 学生がくせいに紙かみを一枚いちまい（ずつ・それぞれ）渡わたしてください。

学生がくせいに紙かみを一枚いちまいずつ渡わたしてください。

There is a quantity here - 一枚いちまい - and the instruction is that everybody gets that same quantity. それぞれ cannot follow a counter phrase; it would have to go earlier and would lose the "one apiece" meaning.

ずつ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Please add the sugar a little at a time."

砂糖さとうを少すこしずつ入いれてください。

This is the incremental sense: no recipients, just repeated small portions over time. 少すこしずつ is close to a fixed adverb and is the standard phrase in recipes.

ずつ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: みなさんにペンをずつ一本いっぽん配くばります。

✅ みなさんにペンを一本いっぽんずつ配くばります。

ずつ goes after the quantity, not before it. The counter phrase 一本いっぽん has to be complete before ずつ can attach - and note the reading いっぽん, not ❌いちほん.

ずつ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 子こどもたちにあめを三みっつあげました。 (b) 子こどもたちにあめを三みっつずつあげました。

(a) means three sweets were given to the children as a group - they will have to work out among themselves who gets what, and if there are four children someone is going without.

(b) means every child got three, so the total depends on how many children there were. The single word ずつ is doing all of that work, and English needs a whole phrase ("three each") to match it. When you are dividing anything up, leaving ずつ out changes the facts.
