や

Meaning · and - but only naming some of the things, not all of them

や joins nouns into a list the speaker is not finishing. You name two or three representative items and leave the rest implied, the way English does with "eggs, cheese, that sort of thing". If you want the list to be complete, や is the wrong particle.

や · Formation

や sits between nouns. It never attaches to a verb, an adjective or a clause, and the particle that governs the whole list (が, を, に…) goes after the last noun only.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (two items) Noun や Noun 卵たまご + チーズ → 卵たまごやチーズ
Noun (three or more) N や N や N ペンやノートや消けしゴム
Rounded off with など N や N など 野菜やさいや果物くだものなど
Followed by a case particle N や N + particle 机つくえや椅子いすを運はこぶ
❌ Verbs not possible - use ～たり～たり ❌読よむや書かく
❌ い-adjectives not possible - use ～くて ❌安やすいやおいしい

や · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - や is a sampler. Two items on the page, more items in the world. など at the end makes that explicit and is very common with や.

や · Examples

冷蔵庫れいぞうこに卵たまごやチーズがあります。
There are eggs, cheese and so on in the fridge.

週末しゅうまつは洗濯せんたくや掃除そうじをします。
On weekends I do laundry, cleaning, that kind of thing.

引ひき出だしの中なかにはさみやのりが入はいっています。
There are scissors, glue and suchlike in the drawer.

京都きょうとや奈良ならへ行いってみたいです。
I'd like to visit Kyoto, Nara and places like that.

や · Examples (cont.)

この店みせでは野菜やさいや果物くだものが安やすく買かえます。
At this shop you can buy vegetables, fruit and the like cheaply.

夏休なつやすみには海うみや山やまへ出でかける人ひとが多おおいです。
Over the summer holidays a lot of people head to the sea, the mountains and so on.

昼ひるごはんはおにぎりやサンドイッチで済すませることが多おおい。
I usually make do with a rice ball or a sandwich or something for lunch.

引ひっ越こしのとき、本棚ほんだなや古ふるいソファなどの大おおきい家具かぐは全部ぜんぶ捨すてました。
When I moved I threw out all the big furniture - the bookshelf, the old sofa and so on.

や · Nuance & register

や is register-neutral. It appears in newspapers, in textbooks and in ordinary conversation, and it does not sound stiff or casual either way.

What it does carry is a small amount of politeness-by-vagueness. Because や declines to close the list, it sounds less like a bureaucratic inventory. Asked what you did on holiday, 海うみや山やまに行いきました sounds relaxed; the same sentence with と sounds like you are filing a report.

In speech, とか does the same job and is much more common. 野菜やさいとか果物くだものとか買かってきて - that is what you would actually text a housemate. とか is casual, can repeat after the final item, and can (unlike や) also hang off verbs. In writing, prefer や.

や · Nuance & register (cont.)

など is the natural finisher. 野菜やさいや果物くだものなど makes the open-endedness explicit and is standard in written Japanese. In formal documents the same word is often written in kanji as 等など; write it in kana at N5.

や · や vs と

Rule of thumb - と closes the list; や leaves it open. と = "A and B, that's everything". や = "A and B, among other things".

This is the only difference, and it is a real one - a listener hears it.

かばんの中なかには、さいふとかぎがあります。
In my bag there's a wallet and a key. (Those two things. Nothing else.)

かばんの中なかには、さいふやかぎがあります。
In my bag there's a wallet, a key, stuff like that. (And other things besides.)

や · や vs と (cont.)

Two consequences worth remembering. First, when the set really is fixed, や is odd: 日本にほんの大おおきい島しまは北海道ほっかいどうと本州ほんしゅうと… - you are enumerating, so use と. Second, や needs at least two items; ❌水みずやください is not a sentence.

や · や vs とか

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register. や is neutral-to-written; とか is spoken.

とか also repeats after the last item, which や cannot do.

コーヒーとかお茶ちゃとか、何なにか飲のむ?
Coffee, tea, something - want a drink?

And とか will attach to verbs, where や is simply ungrammatical: 走はしるとか泳およぐとか、運動うんどうをしたほうがいいよ。

や · や vs ～たり～たり

Rule of thumb - や lists nouns. To give a non-exhaustive list of actions, you need ～たり～たり.

Learners reach for や here because English uses "and" for both. Japanese does not.

日曜日にちようびは音楽おんがくや映画えいがが好すきです。
On Sundays I like music, films, that sort of thing. (nouns - や is right)

日曜日にちようびは音楽おんがくを聞きいたり映画えいがを見みたりします。
On Sundays I listen to music, watch films, and so on. (actions - たり is required)

や · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 本ほんを読よむや音楽おんがくを聞ききます。 → ✅ 本ほんを読よんだり音楽おんがくを聞きいたりします。
    や joins nouns only. A list of activities needs ～たり～たり.

  • ❌ この部屋へやは明あかるいや広ひろいです。 → ✅ この部屋へやは明あかるくて広ひろいです。
    Adjectives chain with くて／で, never with や.

  • ❌ ペンやノートやを買かいました。 → ✅ ペンやノートを買かいました。
    や goes between nouns. Nothing follows the last item except the case particle (or など).

  • ❌ 父ちちや母ははは二人ふたりとも先生せんせいです。 → ✅ 父ちちと母ははは二人ふたりとも先生せんせいです。
    二人ふたりとも says the list is exactly two people, which contradicts the open-endedness of や.

や · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 野菜やさいなどや果物くだものを買かいます。 → ✅ 野菜やさいや果物くだものなどを買かいます。
    など closes the whole list, so it comes at the end - not in the middle.

や · Practice 1

1. テーブルの上うえにコップ____おさらがあります。

テーブルの上うえにコップやおさらがあります。

Either や or と is grammatical here, and they say different things. With や the cups and plates are examples of what is on the table; with と they are the complete contents. If you can imagine a fork sitting there too, use や.

や · Practice 2

2. 私わたしの家族かぞくは父ちち（や・と）母ははと私わたしの三人さんにんです。

私わたしの家族かぞくは父ちちと母ははと私わたしの三人さんにんです。

三人さんにんです states the exact size of the set, so the list has to be exhaustive. や would promise that there are other family members you have not mentioned, which flatly contradicts the rest of the sentence.

や · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I bought stamps, envelopes and things like that at the post office."

郵便局ゆうびんきょくで切手きってや封筒ふうとう（など）を買かいました。

"and things like that" is exactly what や encodes, so no extra word is needed - though など after the final noun makes it unmistakable. Note that を attaches once, to the end of the whole list.

や · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 朝あさはパンを食たべるや新聞しんぶんを読よみます。

✅ 朝あさはパンを食たべたり新聞しんぶんを読よんだりします。 "In the morning I eat toast, read the paper, and so on."

The two items being listed are actions, not nouns, so や cannot join them. The non-exhaustive list of actions is ～たり～たり, and the sentence then ends in します.

や · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 土曜日どようびはそうじと買かい物ものをしました。 (b) 土曜日どようびはそうじや買かい物ものをしました。

(a) presents cleaning and shopping as the whole of Saturday - those two jobs, and that is the account. It sounds like an answer to "what exactly did you do?"

(b) offers them as samples of a busier day: cleaning, shopping, and other errands you are not bothering to list. Japanese speakers reach for (b) far more often in small talk, because pinning the day down to precisely two activities sounds strangely precise.

ので

Meaning · because - presenting the reason as a plain fact, not as your opinion

ので links a cause to its result. The half before ので is the reason; the half after it is what follows from that reason. It frames the connection as something anyone would accept, which is why it is the polite way to explain yourself when you are asking for indulgence.

ので · Formation

ので attaches to a clause, not to a word. The trap is nouns and な-adjectives, which need な in front of it.

ので · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (plain) plain form + ので 降ふる → 降ふるので
Verb (past) た-form + ので 遅おくれた → 遅おくれたので
Verb (negative) ない-form + ので わからない → わからないので
い-adjective plain form + ので 寒さむい → 寒さむいので
い-adjective (past) かった + ので 寒さむかったので
な-adjective ～な + ので 静しずかだ → 静しずかなので
Noun Noun + な + ので 休やすみだ → 休やすみなので
Noun (past) ～だった + ので 休やすみだったので
Polite (formal) ます-form + ので 参まいります → 参まいりますので

ので · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ❌だので does not exist. A noun or な-adjective takes な before ので, and な only. The past forms だったので are fine, because there the だ has already become だった.

Politeness lives on the final verb of the sentence, not on the ので clause: 寒さむいので、まどを閉しめます. The ます-form before ので shown in the last row is a separate, extra-formal register - announcements and business email.

ので · Examples

寒さむいので、コートを着きます。
It's cold, so I'll put a coat on.

明日あしたは休やすみなので、ゆっくり寝ねます。
Tomorrow's a day off, so I'm going to sleep in.

電車でんしゃが遅おくれたので、会議かいぎに間まに合あいませんでした。
The train was delayed, so I didn't make the meeting.

図書館としょかんは静しずかなので、よく勉強べんきょうしに行いきます。
The library is quiet, so I often go there to study.

ので · Examples (cont.)

頭あたまが痛いたいので、先さきに失礼しつれいします。
I have a headache, so I'll head off before the rest of you.

まだ日本語にほんごが上手じょうずじゃないので、ゆっくり話はなしていただけませんか。
My Japanese still isn't very good, so could you speak slowly?

子こどもがまだ小ちいさいので、夜よるの集あつまりには参加さんかできません。
My children are still small, so I can't come to evening gatherings.

台風たいふうが近ちかづいておりますので、本日ほんじつは早はやめに閉店へいてんさせていただきます。
A typhoon is approaching, so we will be closing early today.

ので · Nuance & register

ので is polite-leaning but not itself a polite form - you will hear plain 眠ねむいので寝ねる among friends. What it does is present the reason as objective: this is how things are, and the conclusion follows by itself.

That objectivity is why ので is the excuse-making particle. Cancelling on someone, arriving late, asking to leave early - 急用きゅうようができましたので sounds like circumstances have decided, not like you have. Saying the same thing with から puts your own will in the frame and can land as "because I say so".

ので · Nuance & register (cont.)

The contraction is んで. 眠ねむいんで先さきに帰かえるね。 This is very common in casual speech and completely normal among friends, but it is markedly informal - it sounds rough in the mouth of a shop assistant and you should not write it. Learners often meet んで in dramas long before anyone tells them it is just ので.

At the other end, ますので (as in the last example above) is service and business register. It is not more grammatical, just more deferential, and it belongs to announcements, apologies and formal email.

ので · Nuance & register (cont.)

One more habit worth copying: ので frequently ends a sentence on its own, with the consequence left unsaid. 今いま手てが離はなせないので…。 The listener supplies "…so I can't right now", and nobody has had to refuse anything out loud.

ので · ので vs から

Rule of thumb - から is my reason; ので is the reason. から pushes, ので explains.

Both mean "because" and both are correct in most sentences. The difference is whose judgement the reason belongs to.

熱ねつがあるので、今日きょうは休やすませていただきます。
I have a fever, so I'll be taking today off. (Circumstances decide; I'm reporting them.)

熱ねつがあるから、今日きょうは休やすむ。
I've got a fever, so I'm taking today off. (My call, stated plainly.)

Practical consequences:

ので · ので vs から (cont.)

  • Apologising or asking a favour of someone senior → ので. から can sound like
    you are pressing your case.
  • Strong personal statements, warnings, invitations, imperatives → から.
    危あぶない*から、来くるな! An imperative with ので sounds mismatched, though polite requests with ので are completely normal and extremely common: 危あぶないので、下さがってください。*
  • Answering the question どうして? → からです. ❌のでです is not a form.

ので · ので vs のに

Rule of thumb - identical attachment, opposite meaning. ので = the result follows; のに = the result fails to follow, and the speaker is put out about it.

They are one syllable apart and both take な after a noun, so they are easy to mix up in production.

雨あめが降ふっているので、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしです。
It's raining, so the match is cancelled.

雨あめが降ふっているのに、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしになりませんでした。
It's raining, and yet the match wasn't cancelled.

のに always carries feeling - surprise, complaint, disappointment. ので is emotionally flat.

ので · ので vs the plain て-form

Rule of thumb - the て-form can imply cause, but only when the result is something outside your control. If the second clause is a decision, a request or an invitation, you need ので.

道みちが込こんでいたので、タクシーで行いきました。
The roads were busy, so I went by taxi. (a choice - ので)

道みちが込こんでいて、なかなか進すすめませんでした。
The roads were busy and we could hardly move. (no choice involved - て is fine)

So ❌寒さむくて、まどを閉しめてください is wrong; closing a window is a request. ✅寒さむいので、まどを閉しめてください。

ので · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 雨あめだので、出でかけません。 → ✅ 雨あめなので、出でかけません。
    A noun takes な before ので. The same goes for な-adjectives: ❌便利べんりだので → ✅便利べんりなので.

  • ❌ どうして来こなかったんですか。 - - 忙いそがしかったのでです。 → ✅ 忙いそがしかったからです。
    ので connects two clauses; it cannot stand as a predicate. The stand-alone answer form is からです.

  • ❌ 時間じかんがありませんので急いそげ! → ✅ 時間じかんがないから急いそげ!
    A blunt imperative wants the subjective から. ので with a barked order sounds like two different people talking.

ので · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 会議かいぎに間まに合あいませんでしたので電車でんしゃが遅おくれました。 → ✅ 電車でんしゃが遅おくれましたので、会議かいぎに間まに合あいませんでした。
    The reason comes first. Japanese never puts ので in front of the cause the way English puts "because" in front of it.

  • ❌ 安やすかったなので買かいました。 → ✅ 安やすかったので買かいました。
    The な belongs only to nouns and な-adjectives in their non-past form. Once a predicate is already かった or だった, ので attaches bare.

ので · Practice 1

1. 今日きょうは日曜日にちようび____、銀行ぎんこうは開あいていません。

今日きょうは日曜日にちようびなので、銀行ぎんこうは開あいていません。

日曜日にちようび is a noun, so ので needs な in front of it. ❌日曜日にちようびだので is the single most common ので error, and it comes from thinking of ので as attaching to a finished sentence.

ので · Practice 2

2. 山田やまださん、申もうし訳わけありません。急きゅうに用事ようじができました（ので・から）、今日きょうのパーティーは欠席けっせきさせていただきます。

…用事ようじができましたので、今日きょうのパーティーは欠席けっせきさせていただきます。

You are apologising to someone and asking to be excused, so you want the reason to look like circumstance rather than preference. から here would foreground your own decision and sound faintly self-justifying.

ので · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The room was noisy, so I couldn't sleep well."

部屋へやがうるさかったので、よく寝ねられませんでした。

うるさい is an い-adjective, so its past form うるさかった takes ので directly - no な. Note also that the whole sentence's tense sits on the final verb; you do not need to mark past twice in any special way.

ので · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 字じが小ちいさいので、もっと大おおきく書かけ。

✅ 字じが小ちいさいから、もっと大おおきく書かけ。 Or, keeping ので: 字じが小ちいさいので、もっと大おおきく書かいてください。

The grammar of the ので clause is fine; the mismatch is in register. A bare imperative is maximally subjective, and it pairs with the subjective から. Swap either half and the sentence settles.

ので · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 時間じかんがないから、また今度こんどにしましょう。 (b) 時間じかんがないので、また今度こんどにしましょう。

Both propose postponing, and both are perfectly natural. (a) puts the shortage of time forward as the speaker's own read on the situation - it is direct, it is what you say to a friend, and among strangers it can sound slightly brusque.

(b) presents the lack of time as an external fact that has made the decision for both of you. It is the softer, more deferential version, and it is what you would say to a client or a teacher. Nothing about the facts differs; only who is seen to be deciding.

けど / けれども

Meaning · but; although - and, very often, just a soft way to start talking

けど joins two clauses where the second is not what the first led you to expect. It is also, in everyday Japanese, the ordinary way to preface a request or a piece of news without landing on it too hard.

けど / けれども · Formation

けど clips onto the end of a finished clause, plain or polite. Nouns and な-adjectives need だ (plain) or です (polite) first.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (plain) plain form + けど 行いく → 行いくけど
Verb (polite) ます-form + けど 行いきます → 行いきますけど
Verb (past) た-form + けど 行いった → 行いったけど
い-adjective plain form + けど 高たかい → 高たかいけど
な-adjective ～だ + けど 便利べんりだ → 便利べんりだけど／便利べんりですけど
Noun Noun + だ + けど 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいだけど／学生がくせいですけど
Negative ない-form + けど 知しらないけど

けど / けれども · Formation (cont.)

The four spellings are one word at four levels of formality:

Attaches to Form Example
Casual speech けど 安やすいけど
Slightly softer けども 安やすいですけども
Neutral / written けれど 安やすいけれど
Formal / written けれども 安やすいけれども

Rule of thumb - longer means more formal. けど in conversation, けれども in a written report. が is one step more formal again.

けど / けれども · Examples

このケーキは甘あまいけど、おいしくない。
This cake is sweet but it isn't good.

日本語にほんごは難むずかしいけど、楽たのしいです。
Japanese is hard, but it's fun.

昨日きのう電話でんわしたけど、出でなかったね。
I called you yesterday, but you didn't pick up.

すみません、中村なかむらですけど、部長ぶちょうはいらっしゃいますか。
Excuse me, this is Nakamura - is the department head in?

けど / けれども · Examples (cont.)

夏なつの海うみは好すきだけど、人ひとが多おおすぎるのはちょっとね。
I love the sea in summer, but the crowds are a bit much.

去年きょねん買かった自転車じてんしゃは安やすかったけれども、ひと月つきで壊こわれてしまった。
The bike I bought last year was cheap, but it broke within a month.

一度いちどメールを送おくりましたけど、まだお返事へんじをいただいていません。
I did send an email once, but I haven't had a reply yet.

申もうし訳わけありませんけれども、明日あしたの会かいは欠席けっせきさせていただきます。
I'm very sorry, but I'll have to miss tomorrow's meeting.

けど / けれども · Nuance & register

けど is one of the most-used words in spoken Japanese, and most of the time it is not really contrastive at all.

The trailing けど. A sentence that stops on けど is a complete, polite utterance. 少すこし伺うかがいたいことがあるんですけど…。 Nothing has been left out by accident; the けど hands the floor to the listener instead of stating your business flatly. Requests, complaints, refusals and first approaches to a stranger nearly all arrive this way, and a learner who never uses trailing けど sounds blunt without knowing why.

けど / けれども · Nuance & register (cont.)

The preface けど. 映画えいがの話はなしなんですけど、今週こんしゅう見みに行いきませんか。 Here けど marks the first clause as background - "about the film" - with no contrast whatsoever. Translating it as "but" gives nonsense; it is closer to English "so,".

Register. けど is casual but not rude, and it is fine after です／ます: 高たかいですけど、いいですか。 is perfectly polite. けれども in speech sounds careful and slightly formal; in writing it is unremarkable. けども is a spoken middle ground you will hear a lot from shop staff.

けど is not gender- or age-marked. What is marked is stacking: two けど clauses in one sentence sounds rambling, and three sounds like you have lost the thread.

けど / けれども · けど vs が

Rule of thumb - same grammar, different formality. が is the written and formal member of the pair; けど is the spoken one.

They are interchangeable in structure - both attach to a finished clause, both can be contrastive or merely prefatory, both can trail off at the end.

値段ねだんは高たかいですが、品質ひんしつはたしかです。
It is expensive, but the quality is dependable. (a written product description)

値段ねだんは高たかいけど、ものはいいよ。
Pricey, but it's good stuff. (the same claim, said to a friend)

けど / けれども · けど vs が (cont.)

Two practical notes. が after a plain form (高たかいが) reads as literary, not casual - plain form plus が belongs to essays, not chat. And in a speech or a formal email, が is the safe default.

けど / けれども · けど vs でも

Rule of thumb - けど goes at the end of the first clause. でも goes at the start of the second sentence. They are never interchangeable in position.

この部屋へやは狭せまいけど、明あかるいです。
This room is small, but it's bright. (one sentence)

この部屋へやは狭せまいです。でも、明あかるいです。
This room is small. But it's bright. (two sentences)

けど / けれども · けど vs でも (cont.)

This is the mistake English speakers make most, because English "but" sits between the two halves and so looks like でも. It is not: ❌狭せまいでも is ungrammatical. If the word comes after the first clause, it must be けど.

けど / けれども · けど vs のに

Rule of thumb - のに is けど plus a grievance. Use のに only when the outcome genuinely disappoints or surprises the speaker.

三時間さんじかん待まったけど、来こなかった。
I waited three hours, but he didn't come.

三時間さんじかんも待まったのに、来こなかった。
I waited three whole hours and he still didn't come! (and I'm annoyed)

けど / けれども · けど vs のに (cont.)

Because のに carries emotion, it is wrong where you are simply setting two facts side by side: ❌弟おとうとは背せが低ひくいのに、兄あには高たかいです。 Nobody's expectation has been thwarted by how tall someone's brother is, so this needs けど.

けど / けれども · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 学生がくせいけど、働はたらいています。 → ✅ 学生がくせいだけど、働はたらいています。
    A noun cannot sit bare in front of けど. Insert だ (plain) or です (polite). Same for な-adjectives: ❌静しずかけど → ✅静しずかだけど.

  • ❌ 高たかいでも、買かいます。 → ✅ 高たかいけど、買かいます。
    でも cannot follow a clause. It opens a new sentence: 高たかいです。でも、買かいます。

  • ❌ 雨あめが降ふっているけど、傘かさを持もっているけど、ぬれました。 → ✅ 雨あめが降ふっていて傘かさも持もっていたけど、ぬれました。
    One けど per sentence. Chaining them reads as unfinished thinking.

けど / けれども · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 安やすいけれども。 as a full written sentence → ✅ 安やすいけれども、買かいませんでした。
    Trailing off is a spoken move. In writing, finish the sentence - a dangling けれども on the page just looks truncated.

  • ❌ けど、明日あしたは行いけません。 (at the start of a sentence) → ✅ でも、明日あしたは行いけません。
    けど is a clause-final connective. To begin a sentence with "but", use でも, しかし, or ですが.

けど / けれども · Practice 1

1. この辞書じしょは便利べんり____、重おもいです。

この辞書じしょは便利べんりだけど（polite: ですけど）、重おもいです。

便利べんり is a な-adjective, so it cannot touch けど directly - you need だ or です between them. ❌便利べんりけど and ❌便利べんりなけど are both wrong.

けど / けれども · Practice 2

2. 時間じかんがなかったです。（けど・でも）、全部ぜんぶ終おわりました。

時間じかんがなかったです。でも、全部ぜんぶ終おわりました。

The first clause has already been closed with です and a full stop, so the connector is starting a new sentence - that is でも's job. けど would have had to attach inside the first sentence: 時間じかんがなかったけど、全部ぜんぶ終おわりました。

けど / けれども · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I've read the book, but I don't really understand it."

その本ほんは読よみましたけど、あまりわかりません。

Polite ました plus けど is a completely normal combination - けど does not drag the register down. In casual speech: 読よんだけど、あまりわからない。

けど / けれども · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: すみません、道みちを聞ききたいでも、いいですか。

✅ すみません、道みちを聞ききたいんですけど、いいですか。

でも cannot follow a clause, and here the speaker is not contrasting anything anyway - this is the prefatory けど that softens an approach to a stranger. The んですけど shape is the standard opening for asking a favour.

けど / けれども · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうしたけど、試合しあいに負まけました。 (b) 毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうしたのに、試合しあいに負まけました。

(a) states two facts and marks the second as unexpected. It is neutral - a reasonable thing to write in a diary or say to a coach without making a scene.

(b) adds the speaker's feeling: after all that practice, losing is unfair or maddening. のに is never emotionally blank. Use it when you want the listener to hear the disappointment, and けど when you just want the facts joined.

～てから

Meaning · after doing X - and X has to happen first for Y to be possible

～てから puts two actions in order and says the first one clears the way for the second. It is not a neutral timestamp: the reason you mention washing your hands before eating is that eating comes after washing, and should.

～てから · Formation

から clips onto the plain て-form. It never conjugates and never carries tense - the tense of the whole sentence sits on the final verb.

～てから · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb て-form + から 食たべる → 食たべてから
Verb (voiced て-form) で-form + から 読よむ → 読よんでから
する して + から 勉強べんきょうする → 勉強べんきょうしてから
来くる 来きて + から 来くる → 来きてから
Tense of the sentence on the final verb only 洗あらってから食たべました
Politeness on the final verb only 洗あらってから食たべます
❌ Noun not possible - use Noun + のあとで ❌仕事しごとのてから
❌ Negative ❌ないでから does not exist - rephrase ❌見みないでから

～てから · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the verb in front of から is always bare て-form. ❌帰かえりますてから, ❌帰かえったから and ❌帰かえるから all mean something else or nothing at all.

Note - ～てから has a second, closely related job: measuring elapsed time from a starting point. 日本にほんに来きてから３年ねんになります - "it's been three years since I came to Japan." Same form, and the logic is the same (everything after that point rests on it), but here the second clause is a duration rather than an action.

～てから · Examples

手てを洗あらってから、ごはんを食たべます。
I'll wash my hands and then eat.

宿題しゅくだいをしてから、テレビを見みなさい。
Do your homework first, then watch television.

お金かねを入いれてから、ボタンを押おしてください。
Put the money in first, then press the button.

靴くつを脱ぬいでから、上あがってください。
Please take your shoes off before you come up.

～てから · Examples (cont.)

日本にほんに来きてから、３年ねんが経たちました。
Three years have passed since I came to Japan.

説明書せつめいしょをよく読よんでから、組くみ立たててください。
Read the instructions carefully before you assemble it.

大学だいがくを卒業そつぎょうしてから、ずっと同おなじ会社かいしゃで働はたらいています。
I've worked at the same company ever since I graduated.

会議かいぎの資料しりょうは、部長ぶちょうに確認かくにんしていただいてから、皆みなさんにお送おくりします。
I'll send the meeting materials round once the department head has checked them.

～てから · Nuance & register

～てから is register-neutral. It has no casual contraction and no formal variant; the politeness of the sentence is decided entirely by the verb at the end.

What it does carry is a faint sense of order being observed. That makes it the natural form for instructions, rules and procedures - machine displays, recipes, safety notices, and parents talking to children all use it. ボタンを押おしてから、レバーを下さげてください reads as a step-by-step procedure in a way that the plain て-form does not.

～てから · Nuance & register (cont.)

The flip side: because てから insists on the ordering, using it for two things that merely happened to follow one another sounds fussy. 起おきてから窓まどを開あけて、顔かおを洗あらってから服ふくを着きて… - nobody narrates a morning like that. One てから per sentence is the practical limit; when you need a longer chain, use plain て-forms and reserve てから for the one step that genuinely gates the rest.

For the elapsed-time use (来きてから３年ねん), the second half is very often なります, 経たちます or a state - not an action.

～てから · ～てから vs ～たあとで

Rule of thumb - てから says the first action is a precondition; たあとで just says it came earlier. Both translate as "after", and only てから claims a connection between the two.

くすりを飲のんでから、寝ねました。
I took the medicine and then went to bed. (I went to bed on the strength of having taken it.)

くすりを飲のんだあとで、寝ねました。
I went to bed after taking the medicine. (Simple sequence; the two are not necessarily linked.)

Three consequences you can act on:

～てから · ～てから vs ～たあとで (cont.)

  • If the second action depends on the first - permission, readiness,
    eligibility - てから is the right one: 十八歳じゅうはっさいになっ*てから運転うんてんできます。*
  • If you want a bare timeline, or the two events have nothing to do with each
    other, たあとで is safer.
  • Only てから measures elapsed time. ❌来きたあとで３年ねんになります is wrong;
    来きてから３年ねんになります is right.

Note that the forms differ too: てから takes the て-form, たあとで takes the た-form.

～てから · ～てから vs the plain て-form

Rule of thumb - plain ～て says "and then", loosely. ～てから says "and not until then".

駅えきまで歩あるいて、電車でんしゃに乗のりました。
I walked to the station and got on the train. (ordinary narration)

きっぷを買かってから、電車でんしゃに乗のってください。
Buy a ticket before boarding the train. (you may not board until you have)

Both are correct Japanese in most contexts, so the choice is about emphasis. Where the て-form is genuinely wrong is when the gating matters and the listener might miss it - a rule, a warning, an instruction.

～てから · ～てから vs ～前に

Rule of thumb - same two events, opposite anchor. AてからB puts A first and looks forward; BまえにA puts B first and looks back. Watch the form: てから wants the て-form, 前まえに wants the dictionary form.

手てを洗あらってから、料理りょうりを始はじめます。
I'll wash my hands, and then start cooking.

料理りょうりを始はじめる**前まえに**、手てを洗あらいます。
Before I start cooking, I'll wash my hands.

～てから · ～てから vs ～前に (cont.)

The facts are identical. What differs is which action is the topic of the sentence: with てから you are talking about the washing, with 前まえに you are talking about the cooking.

～てから · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 食たべるから、歯はをみがきます。 → ✅ 食たべてから、歯はをみがきます。
    Dictionary form + から is "because", not "after". The sequence meaning needs the て-form, and dropping the て changes the sentence entirely.

  • ❌ うちへ帰かえりますてから、晩ばんごはんを作つくります。 → ✅ うちへ帰かえってから、晩ばんごはんを作つくります。
    Politeness belongs to the last verb only. The てから clause is always plain.

  • ❌ 仕事しごとのてから、飲のみに行いきます。 → ✅ 仕事しごとのあとで、飲のみに行いきます。
    てから attaches to verbs, never to nouns. For a noun you need のあとで.

～てから · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 見みないでから買かいました。 → ✅ 見みないで買かいました。
    There is no negative てから. "Without doing X" is ～ないで; "not until X" is handled by rephrasing in the positive.

  • ❌ 起おきてから顔かおを洗あらってから服ふくを着きてから出でかけました。 → ✅ 起おきて顔かおを洗あらって、服ふくを着きてから出でかけました。
    Chained てから sounds mechanical. Use plain て-forms for the run of events and keep てから for the one step that actually gates what follows.

～てから · Practice 1

1. よく____から、はんこを押おしてください。（読よむ）

よく読よんでから、はんこを押おしてください。

読よむ is a む-verb, so its て-form is 読よんで - voiced. And since the signing is not supposed to happen until the reading is done, てから (rather than a plain て-form) is exactly right for an instruction like this.

～てから · Practice 2

2. 日本にほんに来き（てから・たあとで）、五年ごねんになります。

日本にほんに来きてから、五年ごねんになります。

Measuring elapsed time from a starting point is a job only てから can do. 来きたあとで would set up a sequence of two events, and "five years have become" is not an event - so the sentence would have nowhere to land.

～てから · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Please wait until the light turns green and then cross."

信号しんごうが青あおになってから、渡わたってください。

The crossing is conditional on the light changing, which is the core of てから. Japanese calls a green light 青あお ("blue"), and 信号しんごうが青あおになる is the fixed phrase.

～てから · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: シャワーを浴あびるてから、寝ねます。

✅ シャワーを浴あびてから、寝ねます。

浴あびる is a る-verb, so drop the る and add て: 浴あびて. The dictionary form can never sit in front of てから - ❌浴あびるてから mixes two different attachment rules.

～てから · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) お風呂ふろに入はいってから、ビールを飲のみます。 (b) お風呂ふろに入はいったあとで、ビールを飲のみます。

Both describe a bath followed by a beer, and in casual conversation either would pass unremarked.

(a) frames the bath as the thing that earns the beer - the beer is the reward, and drinking it beforehand would be doing it wrong. (b) simply reports the order of the evening: bath, then beer, no particular logic joining them. If someone asked "so when do you drink it?", (b) is the answer; if the point is that you never drink before bathing, (a) says that.

～前に

Meaning · before doing X - X hasn't happened yet at the moment Y takes place

前まえに puts one event in front of another on the timeline. The whole sentence can be past, present or future; the clause in front of 前まえに is always in the dictionary form, because from the viewpoint of the main clause that event still lies ahead.

～前に · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb dictionary form + 前まえに 寝ねる → 寝ねる前まえに
Verb (in a past sentence) still dictionary form 来くる前まえに習ならいました
Verb (in a future sentence) still dictionary form 来くる前まえに習ならいます
Noun Noun + の + 前まえに 食事しょくじ → 食事しょくじの前まえに
Duration ("ago") Quantity + 前まえに - no の 三日みっか前まえに、一週間いっしゅうかん前まえに
Politeness on the final verb only 寝ねる前まえに歯はをみがきます
❌ Negative ❌ない前まえに does not exist - use ～ないうちに ❌忘わすれない前まえに

～前に · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - 前まえに takes the dictionary form, and its mirror image ～たあとで takes the past form. That asymmetry is not a quirk of memorisation: "before eating" is a moment when the eating has not happened yet, "after eating" is a moment when it has. Japanese marks that literally. ❌食たべた前まえに and ❌食たべるあとで are both wrong, and for the same reason.

Note - the に can drop when the phrase is doing adverbial duty at the head of a sentence: 寝ねる前まえ、少すこし本ほんを読よみます。 Keep the に while you are learning; it is never wrong.

～前に · Examples

寝ねる**前まえに**、歯はをみがきます。
I brush my teeth before going to bed.

出でかける**前まえに**、かぎをかけてください。
Please lock up before you go out.

映画えいがが始はじまる**前まえに**、飲のみ物ものを買かっておきましょう。
Let's buy drinks before the film starts.

試験しけんの**前まえに**、トイレに行いっておいたほうがいいですよ。
You'd be wise to go to the toilet before the exam.

～前に · Examples (cont.)

日本にほんに来くる**前まえに**、２年ねんほど日本語にほんごを習ならいました。
I studied Japanese for about two years before coming to Japan.

暗くらくなる**前まえに**、家いえに帰かえりなさい。
Get home before it gets dark.

会議かいぎが始はじまる**前まえに**、コピーを人数にんずう分ぶん用意よういしておきました。
I got enough copies for everyone ready before the meeting started.

引ひっ越こす**前まえに**、市役所しやくしょで手続てつづきをしなければなりません。
You have to do the paperwork at the city office before you move.

～前に · Nuance & register

前まえに is register-neutral and has no contracted form. It appears in signs, in manuals and in ordinary chat without changing its shape.

The one thing to internalise is that the dictionary form here carries no tense. Look at the fifth example again: the studying is finished and gone, the main verb is 習ならいました, and yet the clause still reads 来くる前まえに. A learner who translates "before I came" word for word produces ❌来きた前まえに every time. The dictionary form is not "present tense" - it is simply the unmarked form the construction demands.

Two related patterns worth recognising when you meet them:

～前に · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • A bare 前まえ after a quantity means "ago". 三日みっか前まえに電話でんわしました - "I called three days ago." No の, and the sentence is
    about the past, not about an ordering.
  • 前まえ also means "in front of" in space, and then it takes で or に
    according to the usual place rules: 駅えきの前まえで待まっています。 Same kanji, entirely different job.

In more formal writing you will meet 以前いぜんに and ～に先立さきだち doing similar work. Neither is expected at N5.

～前に · ～前に vs ～たあとで

Rule of thumb - 前まえに demands the dictionary form; あとで demands the た-form. Neither one ever changes to match the tense of the sentence.

This is the single most useful table on the page, so here it is as a pair:

ごはんを食たべる**前まえに**、薬くすりを飲のみました。
I took the medicine before eating.

ごはんを食たべたあとで、薬くすりを飲のみました。
I took the medicine after eating.

～前に · ～前に vs ～たあとで (cont.)

Both main clauses are past. Only the first clause differs, and it differs in form, not in tense: 食たべる前まえに versus 食たべたあとで. Learners routinely write ❌食たべた前まえに by copying the English past tense across. Japanese does not.

～前に · 前に vs 前で

Rule of thumb - 前まえに is time; 前まえで is place, and it means physically in front of something.

授業じゅぎょうの**前まえに**質問しつもんしました。
I asked a question before the lesson.

教室きょうしつの**前まえで**質問しつもんしました。
I asked a question at the front of the classroom.

～前に · 前に vs 前で (cont.)

The two are told apart by the noun as much as by the particle: a time noun (授業じゅぎょう、食事しょくじ、試合しあい) makes it temporal, a place noun (教室きょうしつ、駅えき、店みせ) makes it spatial. With a place noun and a verb of existence you get 前まえに again, but locationally: 駅えきの前まえに銀行ぎんこうがあります。

～前に · ～前に vs ～までに

Rule of thumb - 前まえに orders two events; までに sets a deadline. If English would say "by", you want までに.

会議かいぎが始はじまる**前まえに**、資料しりょうを配くばります。
I'll hand out the documents before the meeting starts.

金曜日きんようびまでに、資料しりょうを出だしてください。
Please submit the documents by Friday.

～前に · ～前に vs ～までに (cont.)

The difference bites with a fixed time. 五時ごじの前まえに帰かえります sounds like you are arranging your afternoon around five o'clock; 五時ごじまでに帰かえります is a promise to be back no later than five. For appointments and obligations, までに.

～前に · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 寝ねた前まえに、電気でんきを消けしました。 → ✅ 寝ねる前まえに、電気でんきを消けしました。
    Always the dictionary form, no matter how far in the past the sentence is. The English "before I went to bed" is what leads you astray.

  • ❌ 三日みっかの前まえに着つきました。 → ✅ 三日みっか前まえに着つきました。
    A duration takes no の. The の appears only when 前まえに follows an ordinary noun: 食事しょくじの前まえに.

  • ❌ 食事しょくじ前まえに手てを洗あらいます。 → ✅ 食事しょくじの前まえに手てを洗あらいます。
    The mirror error: an ordinary noun does need の. (Some fixed compounds such as 食前しょくぜん exist, but they are separate words with their own readings.)

～前に · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 駅えきの前まえに待まっています。 → ✅ 駅えきの前まえで待まっています。
    Waiting is an action, and actions happen で. に in front of 前まえ marks location only with existence verbs.

  • ❌ 忘わすれない前まえに、メモしておきます。 → ✅ 忘わすれないうちに、メモしておきます。
    There is no negative 前まえに. "While it hasn't yet happened" is ～ないうちに.

～前に · Practice 1

1. 旅行りょこうに____前まえに、パスポートを確認かくにんしてください。（行いく）

旅行りょこうに行いく**前まえに**、パスポートを確認かくにんしてください。

Dictionary form, unchanged. Even though the trip is in the future and the check comes first, the verb in front of 前まえに is never conjugated - neither for tense nor for politeness.

～前に · Practice 2

2. 日本にほんに来くる前まえに、中国ちゅうごくに（住すむ・住すんでいました）。

日本にほんに来くる前まえに、中国ちゅうごくに**住すんでいました**。

The dictionary form belongs to the 前まえに clause and nowhere else. All the tense in the sentence is carried by the main verb at the end, which here has to be past - hence 住すんでいました.

～前に · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Wash the vegetables before you cut them."

野菜やさいは切きる**前まえに**洗あらってください。

切きる stays in the dictionary form; the instruction ("please wash") sits at the end. Note the Japanese order is the reverse of the English one - the 前まえに clause comes first, and the main verb last.

～前に · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 試験しけんの前まえで、よく勉強べんきょうしました。

✅ 試験しけんの前まえに、よく勉強べんきょうしました。

試験しけん is an event in time, not a place, so this is the temporal 前まえに. 前まえで would have to mean "physically in front of the exam", which is not a thing.

～前に · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 六時ろくじの前まえに駅えきに着つきます。 (b) 六時ろくじまでに駅えきに着つきます。

(a) simply places your arrival earlier on the clock than six - it describes the order of things and sounds a little odd on its own, because a bare clock time is not much of an event to be "before".

(b) treats six o'clock as a deadline and commits you to arriving at some point between now and then. That is what you say when someone is waiting for you or a train is leaving. With fixed times and obligations, Japanese overwhelmingly prefers までに.

～たあとで

Meaning · after X - plain chronological order, with no claim that X caused Y

～たあとで places one event later than another on the timeline and says nothing else about them. It is the neutral "after": useful precisely because it does not imply that the first thing had to happen for the second to be possible.

～たあとで · Formation

The verb in front of あとで is in the た-form, and it stays in the た-form whether the sentence is about the past, the present or the future.

～たあとで · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb た-form + あとで 食たべる → 食たべたあとで
Verb (voiced た-form) だ-form + あとで 飲のむ → 飲のんだあとで
Verb (in a future sentence) still た-form 終おわったあとで行いきます
Noun Noun + の + あとで 仕事しごと → 仕事しごとのあとで
Without で た-form + あと 食たべたあと、コーヒーを飲のむ
In kanji あと = 後あと 食たべた後あとで
Politeness on the final verb only 食たべたあとで飲のみます
❌ Negative ❌なかったあとで does not exist ❌見みなかったあとで

～たあとで · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - あとで takes the た-form; its mirror image 前まえに takes the dictionary form. ❌食たべるあとで and ❌食たべた前まえに are the two halves of the same mistake. The logic: after eating, the eating is done (た); before eating, it is not (dictionary).

Note - the で is optional and drops freely, especially in writing. 授業じゅぎょうが終おわったあと、質問しつもんしました is completely natural. There is also あとに, but at N5 treat it as a different word: it is about a thing being left in the place of another, not about time.

～たあとで · Examples

ごはんを食たべたあとで、コーヒーを飲のみます。
I have coffee after eating.

仕事しごとのあとで、買かい物ものに行いきます。
I'm going shopping after work.

映画えいがを見みたあとで、みんなで感想かんそうを話はなし合あいました。
After the film we all talked about what we thought of it.

授業じゅぎょうが終おわったあと、先生せんせいに質問しつもんしました。
I asked the teacher a question after the lesson finished.

～たあとで · Examples (cont.)

シャワーを浴あびたあとで、すぐに寝ねてしまった。
I fell straight asleep after taking a shower.

雨あめがやんだあとで、空そらに虹にじが出でました。
A rainbow appeared in the sky after the rain stopped.

夕ゆうごはんを作つくったあとで、台所だいどころをきれいに片付かたづけた。
After making dinner I tidied the kitchen up properly.

会議かいぎが終おわったあとで、少すこしお時間じかんをいただけませんか。
Could I have a few minutes of your time once the meeting is over?

～たあとで · Nuance & register

たあとで is register-neutral and does not contract. Whether the sentence is polite is settled entirely by the final verb, exactly as with 前まえに.

Its defining quality is neutrality. It reports a sequence and stops there. If you want to say that the first event made the second possible, or that you were waiting for it, たあとで is too flat and you want てから instead. This is why たあとで feels slightly odd in instructions: a sign that read 手てを洗あらったあとで食たべてください is grammatical but sounds like it is describing your habits rather than telling you what to do.

Two smaller habits:

～たあとで · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 後あと written in kanji is common in adult writing, and the reading is
    あと, not うしろ. Same character, two words: 後あとで = "later, afterwards"; 後うしろ = "behind". A learner who reads them the wrong way round produces sentences about standing behind a meeting.
  • あとで also stands alone as an adverb meaning "later", with no
    clause in front of it at all: あとで電話でんわするね。 That usage is extremely common in speech and is not the same grammar point, though it is obviously the same word.

～たあとで · ～たあとで vs ～てから

Rule of thumb - たあとで reports the order; てから claims the first event was a precondition for the second. If you could insert "and only then" in English, use てから.

宿題しゅくだいをしたあとで、友ともだちに電話でんわした。
I phoned my friend after doing my homework. (What I did, in order.)

宿題しゅくだいをしてから、友ともだちに電話でんわした。
I did my homework first, and then phoned my friend. (The homework had to come first.)

Three practical splits:

～たあとで · ～たあとで vs ～てから (cont.)

  • Rules, instructions and permissions want てから, because the whole point is
    the gate: お金かねを払はらってから入はいってください。
  • Elapsed time takes てから only: 結婚けっこんしてから十年じゅうねんになります。
    ❌結婚けっこんしたあとで十年じゅうねんになります is wrong.
  • A noun can only take のあとで. There is no ❌仕事しごとのてから.

And do not miss the form difference: てから needs the て-form, たあとで the た-form.

～たあとで · ～たあとで vs ～前に

Rule of thumb - mirror images, and the mirror includes the verb form. たあとで = た-form; 前まえに = dictionary form.

Put the two side by side on the same pair of events and the pattern is obvious.

会かいが終おわったあとで、写真しゃしんをとりました。
We took photos after the party ended.

会かいが終おわる**前まえに**、写真しゃしんをとりました。
We took photos before the party ended.

～たあとで · ～たあとで vs ～前に (cont.)

Both main clauses are past, and neither first clause is marked for tense at all - 終おわった and 終おわる are chosen by the construction, not by when things happened. English speakers reliably write ❌終おわった前まえに because English says "before it ended". Japanese never does.

There is one more asymmetry worth noticing: a noun takes の with both (会かいのあとで, 会かいの前まえに), so the noun pattern gives you no clue about which verb form to use. Learn the verb forms as a pair.

～たあとで · あとで on its own vs ～たあとで

Rule of thumb - with a clause in front, あとで means "after [that event]". With nothing in front, it means simply "later on".

電話でんわが終おわったあとで、返事へんじします。
I'll reply after I finish the phone call.

あとで返事へんじします。
I'll reply later.

～たあとで · あとで on its own vs ～たあとで (cont.)

The second sentence names no reference point at all - it just pushes the action into the unspecified future, which is why it is a favourite for putting things off. Note that 後あとでね。 on its own ("see you later", "in a bit") is a complete utterance in casual speech.

～たあとで · Common mistakes

  • ❌ ごはんを食たべるあとで、薬くすりを飲のみます。 → ✅ ごはんを食たべたあとで、薬くすりを飲のみます。
    あとで always takes the た-form, even when the whole sentence is about the future. Nothing here is being said about when - only about order.

  • ❌ 授業じゅぎょうあとで質問しつもんします。 → ✅ 授業じゅぎょうのあとで質問しつもんします。
    A noun needs の before あとで. Only a verb can attach directly, and only in the た-form.

  • ❌ 会議かいぎの後うしろで話はなしましょう。 → ✅ 会議かいぎの後あとで話はなしましょう。
    後うしろ is a place word meaning "behind". The time word is 後あと, and it is written without the ろ.

～たあとで · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 薬くすりを飲のんだあとで、運転うんてんしてもいいです。 → ✅ 薬くすりを飲のんでから、運転うんてんしてもいいです。
    Not a grammar error, a nuance error. Permission depends on the first event, so the sentence wants the precondition marker てから.

  • ❌ 日本にほんに来きたあとで、３年ねんになります。 → ✅ 日本にほんに来きてから、３年ねんになります。
    Only てから can measure elapsed time from a starting point. たあとで needs a second event, not a duration.

～たあとで · Practice 1

1. 昼ひるごはんを____あとで、散歩さんぽしませんか。（食たべる）

昼ひるごはんを食たべたあとで、散歩さんぽしませんか。

The た-form is required even though the lunch has not happened yet - the invitation is about the future. あとで fixes the form of the verb in front of it and leaves all the tense to the final verb.

～たあとで · Practice 2

2. 大学だいがくを出で（たあとで・てから）、もう十年じゅうねんになります。

大学だいがくを出でてから、もう十年じゅうねんになります。

Counting years from a starting point is a job てから does and たあとで cannot. たあとで needs a second event to place on the timeline, and "ten years have become" is a duration, not an event.

～たあとで · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'll do the dishes after the guests go home."

お客きゃくさんが帰かえったあとで、皿さらを洗あらいます。

The washing-up is not conditional on the guests leaving in any interesting way - it is just the next thing that happens - so the neutral たあとで fits better than てから. Note again the た-form on a future event.

～たあとで · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 仕事しごとたあとで、飲のみに行いきましょう。

✅ 仕事しごとのあとで、飲のみに行いきましょう。

仕事しごと is a noun, and a noun links to あとで with の. The た in たあとで is not part of the phrase - it is the past ending of a verb, and there is no verb here.

～たあとで · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 手てを洗あらったあとで、パンを食たべた。 (b) 手てを洗あらってから、パンを食たべた。

(a) is a report: two things happened, in that order. It would be a fine sentence in a diary, and it carries no suggestion that the hand-washing mattered.

(b) says the washing came first because it should - the bread was not going to be touched until the hands were clean. Parents, food-safety notices and anyone explaining a rule use (b). Same events, but (b) adds a reason for the order that (a) leaves out entirely.
