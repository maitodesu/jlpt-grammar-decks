くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately

Meaning · about, roughly - stuck onto an amount to say you are not being exact

くらい takes a number and loosens it. 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)ぐらい is "about half an hour", 千円(せんえん)ぐらい is "a thousand yen or so", これくらい is "about this much". Whatever it attaches to has to be a quantity or an extent - a length of time, a price, a headcount, a size - never a point on the clock or the calendar.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Formation

くらい and ぐらい are the same word, and nothing about what comes in front decides which one you say. It is a suffix: it goes straight onto the amount, with nothing in between.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Number + counter Amount + くらい／ぐらい 三十分(さんじゅっぷん) → 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)ぐらい
People Number + 人(にん) + ぐらい 十五人(じゅうごにん) → 十五人(じゅうごにん)ぐらい
Money Price + ぐらい 千円(せんえん) → 千円(せんえん)ぐらい
Amount word Noun + くらい 半分(はんぶん) → 半分(はんぶん)くらい
Demonstrative これ／それ／あれ + くらい これ → これくらい
Modifying a noun ～くらいの + Noun これ → これぐらいの大(おお)きさ
Asking an amount どのくらい／どれくらい どのくらいかかりますか
Followed by a particle ～くらい + で／から／まで 五分(ごふん) → 五分(ごふん)ぐらいで

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - くらい answers how much? how long? how many? If the phrase answers when?, you need ごろ instead: 三時間(さんじかん)ぐらい ("about three hours") but 三時(さんじ)ごろ ("around three o'clock").

Note - a quantity with くらい on it usually needs no particle at all. You say ビールを三本(さんぼん)ぐらい飲(の)みました, not ❌ビールを三本(さんぼん)ぐらいを飲(の)みました - the amount sits in front of the verb as an adverb, and を is already doing its work on ビール.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Examples

コーヒーを三杯(さんばい)ぐらい飲(の)みました。
I drank about three cups of coffee.

この本(ほん)は千円(せんえん)ぐらいです。
This book is about a thousand yen.

教室(きょうしつ)に学生(がくせい)が十五人(じゅうごにん)ぐらいいます。
There are about fifteen students in the classroom.

空港(くうこう)まで、どのくらいかかりますか。
How long does it take to get to the airport?

これぐらいの大(おお)きさの箱(はこ)はありますか。
Do you have a box about this big?

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Examples (cont.)

姉(あね)は毎日(まいにち)二時間(にじかん)ぐらいピアノを練習(れんしゅう)します。
My older sister practises the piano for about two hours a day.

すみません、あと十分(じゅっぷん)ぐらいで着(つ)きますので、先(さき)に注文(ちゅうもん)しておいてください。
Sorry - I'll be there in about ten minutes, so go ahead and order without me.

この店(みせ)のラーメンは、昼(ひる)に行(い)くと二十分(にじゅっぷん)ぐらい並(なら)ばなければなりません。
If you go to this ramen place at lunchtime you have to queue for twenty minutes or so.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Nuance & register

くらい is completely register-neutral. It is the same in casual speech and in a business email, it is not marked as male or female, and adding it to a number never makes you sound less serious - if anything the opposite, because Japanese conversation prefers a hedged number to a bare one. Answering どのくらいかかりますか with a flat 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)です claims a precision you probably do not have; 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)ぐらいです is what a native speaker says.

くらい and ぐらい are the same word. They mean the same thing, both are correct everywhere, and no listener will notice which one you chose. The variation is distributional rather than semantic: ぐらい is somewhat more common in speech and especially after the demonstratives - これぐらい, どれぐらい - while くらい turns up a little more often in careful writing. Those are tendencies, not rules. Pick whichever comes out of your mouth, and stay consistent inside a sentence.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Nuance & register (cont.)

In writing, and in announcements read aloud, you will also meet 約(やく), which does the same job from the other side of the number. It is treated in the Contrast section below.

One thing to expect later: the same word has a second job, measuring a degree rather than an amount - 泣(な)きたいくらいうれしい, "so happy I could cry" - along with a dismissive use that belittles a small task. That sense is a separate grammar point at N3. If you meet a くらい that is not sitting on a number, it is that other job, not this one.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · くらい／ぐらい vs ごろ

Rule of thumb - ごろ blurs a point in time; くらい blurs an amount. One character in the counter decides it: 六時(ろくじ) is a when, 六時間(ろくじかん) is a how-long.

夕方(ゆうがた)六時(ろくじ)ごろ帰(かえ)ります。
I'll be home around six in the evening.

昨日(きのう)は六時間(ろくじかん)ぐらい寝(ね)ました。
I slept about six hours last night.

Dates and days of the week are points in time too, so they take ごろ: 来週(らいしゅう)ごろ, 三月(さんがつ)ごろ. Anything that is counted rather than located - money, distance, weight, people, pages - can only take くらい: ❌五人(ごにん)ごろ is not Japanese.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · くらい vs 約(やく)

Rule of thumb - same meaning, opposite position, different register. 約(やく) is a prefix and belongs to written Japanese; ぐらい is a suffix and belongs to speech. Do not put both on one number.

会場(かいじょう)まで**約(やく)**三十分(さんじゅっぷん)かかります。
It is approximately thirty minutes to the venue.

会場(かいじょう)まで三十分(さんじゅっぷん)ぐらいかかります。
It takes about half an hour to get to the venue.

The first belongs on a printed leaflet or in a station announcement; the second is what you say to a friend on the phone. 約(やく) only ever attaches to a number, so there is no ❌約(やく)これ to match これぐらい, and stacking the two - ❌約(やく)三十分(さんじゅっぷん)ぐらい - hedges the same number twice.

Amount くらい vs degree くらい

Rule of thumb - if くらい sits on a number or an amount, it means "about". If it sits on a verb or an adjective, it is measuring a degree, and "about" is the wrong translation.

三十分(さんじゅっぷん)ぐらい待(ま)ちました。
I waited about thirty minutes.

立(た)てないぐらい疲(つか)れました。
I got so tired I couldn't stand up.

The second sentence has no number in it at all: the clause 立(た)てない is being used as a yardstick for how tired the speaker was. That degree use, together with a dismissive "it's only ～" reading, is a separate grammar point at N3. Here you only need to recognise the shape and not translate it as "approximately".

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 二週間(にしゅうかん)ごろ休(やす)みます。 → ✅ 二週間(にしゅうかん)ぐらい休(やす)みます。
    二週間(にしゅうかん) is a length of time, not a date. Only a point in time takes ごろ.

  • ❌ ぐらい三十分(さんじゅっぷん)かかります。 → ✅ 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)ぐらいかかります。
    English puts "about" in front of the number; Japanese puts ぐらい behind it. くらい is a suffix and can never lead.

  • ❌ 昨日(きのう)は三時間(さんじかん)ぐらいに勉強(べんきょう)しました。 → ✅ 昨日(きのう)は三時間(さんじかん)ぐらい勉強(べんきょう)しました。
    A duration goes in front of the verb with no particle. The に of 三時(さんじ)に marks a point in time, and a span has no point to mark.

  • ❌ 約(やく)五千円(ごせんえん)ぐらいでした。 → ✅ 約(やく)五千円(ごせんえん)でした。／五千円(ごせんえん)ぐらいでした。
    Both words already mean "about". Use one of them, and let the register decide which.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Practice 1

1. 「駅(えき)から会社(かいしゃ)まで、どの____かかりますか。」「歩(ある)いて十五分(じゅうごふん)____です。」

「どのくらいかかりますか。」「十五分(じゅうごふん)ぐらいです。」

Both blanks are about a length of time, so both take くらい／ぐらい. Note how the hedged answer is the natural one: a bare 十五分(じゅうごふん)です would sound as though you had timed it.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Practice 2

2. Choose: 明日(あした)は九時(くじ)（ごろ・ぐらい）会社(かいしゃ)に着(つ)いて、一時間(いちじかん)（ごろ・ぐらい）準備(じゅんび)をします。

九時(くじ)ごろ会社(かいしゃ)に着(つ)いて、一時間(いちじかん)ぐらい準備(じゅんび)をします。

九時(くじ) is a clock time - a point - so it takes ごろ. 一時間(いちじかん) is a span of time, so it takes ぐらい. The two counters differ by one character and choose different words.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "About twenty people came to the party."

パーティーに二十人(にじゅうにん)ぐらい来(き)ました。

The amount goes in front of the verb with ぐらい attached and no particle after it. 二十人(にじゅうにん)くらい is equally correct - the two forms are the same word.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 土曜日(どようび)ぐらいに電話(でんわ)をします。話(はなし)は十分(じゅっぷん)ごろで終(お)わると思(おも)います。

✅ 土曜日(どようび)ごろ電話(でんわ)をします。話(はなし)は十分(じゅっぷん)ぐらいで終(お)わると思(おも)います。

The two words have swapped places. A day of the week is a point in time and needs ごろ; ten minutes is a span and needs ぐらい. Note that に also drops away once ごろ is there.

くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference: (a) この町(まち)に十年(じゅうねん)ぐらい住(す)んでいます。 (b) この町(まち)に十年前(じゅうねんまえ)ごろ引(ひ)っ越(こ)してきました。

(a) measures how long the speaker has lived here: roughly ten years, and they are still here. 十年(じゅうねん) is a duration, so ぐらい is the only choice.

(b) locates when they arrived: at some point around ten years ago, as a single completed event. 十年前(じゅうねんまえ) is a moment on the calendar, which is ごろ territory. The same number, doing two different jobs.

だけ

Meaning · only; just - this much and nothing more

だけ draws a line around something and says that everything outside the line is excluded. It is a neutral limiter: it reports the boundary without complaining about it and without apologising for it.

だけ · Formation

だけ is a particle-like suffix. It goes after the word it limits, and the word keeps its own plain form.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + だけ 水(みず) → 水(みず)だけ
Number / counter Counter + だけ 三人(さんにん) → 三人(さんにん)だけ
Verb plain form + だけ 見(み)る → 見(み)るだけ
い-adjective plain form + だけ 安(やす)い → 安(やす)いだけ
な-adjective stem + な + だけ 静(しず)かだ → 静(しず)かなだけ
After a particle に／で／と + だけ 友(とも)だちにだけ／友(とも)だちだけに
Sentence-final だけ + です／だ 見(み)るだけです

だけ · Formation (cont.)

が and を are dropped after だけ, or moved behind it （水(みず)だけ飲(の)む／水(みず)だけを飲(の)む）. に, で, と, へ are kept, on either side of だけ.

だけ · Examples

コーヒーを一杯(いっぱい)だけ飲(の)みました。
I had just one cup of coffee.

うちには猫(ねこ)が一匹(いっぴき)だけいます。
We have only one cat.

土曜日(どようび)だけアルバイトをしています。
I only work my part-time job on Saturdays.

少(すこ)しだけ待(ま)ってください。
Please wait just a moment.

これは見(み)るだけで、買(か)いません。
I'm only looking - I'm not going to buy anything.

だけ · Examples (cont.)

名前(なまえ)をここに書(か)くだけでいいですよ。
All you have to do is write your name here.

その店(みせ)は高(たか)いだけで、あまりおいしくありませんでした。
That place was just expensive - it wasn't even very good.

部屋(へや)に残(のこ)っていたのは私(わたし)だけでしたから、電気(でんき)を消(け)して帰(かえ)りました。
I was the only one left in the room, so I turned off the lights and went home.

だけ · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. だけです in polite speech, だけだ or a bare だけ in plain speech, and nothing about it is marked for age or gender.

The important feeling is that だけ is factual, not emotional. It states the limit and stops. If you want the limit to sound insufficient - "only three people came, unfortunately" - Japanese reaches for しか～ない instead, which is the same fact wrapped in disappointment.

Two very common frames are worth memorising whole:

  • ～だけで - "just with / merely by". 名前(なまえ)を書(か)くだけでいいです
    means the writing is all that is required.
  • これだけ／それだけ - depending on the sentence these mean either
    "only this" or, with rising emphasis, "this much". これだけ勉強(べんきょう)したのに is "after studying this much…". Context decides.

だけ · Nuance & register (cont.)

In speech だけ often carries a small dismissive shrug when it follows an adjective: 高(たか)いだけ, 大(おお)きいだけ - "expensive and that's all it has going for it".

だけ · だけ vs しか～ない

Rule of thumb - same fact, opposite framing. だけ is neutral; しか～ない says the amount is less than you'd want, and forces the verb negative.

千円(せんえん)だけ持(も)っています。
I have a thousand yen. (that's the amount, stated flatly)

千円(せんえん)しか持(も)っていません。
I've only got a thousand yen. (and it isn't going to be enough)

Note the verb: だけ leaves it positive, しか requires the negative. Mixing them up - ❌千円(せんえん)しか持(も)っています - is the single most common error with this pair.

だけ · だけ vs ばかり

Rule of thumb - だけ counts; ばかり complains. ばかり means "nothing but", and usually implies the speaker thinks that is too much of one thing.

弟(おとうと)はゲームだけ好(す)きです。
Games are the one thing my little brother likes.

弟(おとうと)はゲームばかりしています。
My little brother does nothing but play games.

ばかり (an N4 pattern) does not really restrict the set - other things may happen - it says one thing dominates, and it usually sounds critical.

だけ · だけ vs のみ

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register. のみ is the written, formal twin of だけ and sounds stiff in conversation.

このドアは関係者(かんけいしゃ)だけ使(つか)えます。
Only staff can use this door.

関係者(かんけいしゃ)のみ入場(にゅうじょう)できます。
Staff only. (as printed on a sign)

You will meet のみ on notices, in contracts and in announcements. Say だけ when you are speaking to a person.

だけ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ りんごをだけ買(か)いました。 → ✅ りんごだけ買(か)いました。／りんごだけを買(か)いました。
    を cannot sit in front of だけ. Either drop it or move it behind. The same goes for が.

  • ❌ 水(みず)だけ飲(の)みません。（meaning "I only drink water"）→ ✅ 水(みず)だけ飲(の)みます。／水(みず)しか飲(の)みません。
    だけ plus a negative negates the limit itself: "it isn't only water that I drink." If you want the restriction to stand, keep the verb positive or switch to しか.

  • ❌ 静(しず)かだだけの町(まち) → ✅ 静(しず)かなだけの町(まち)
    な-adjectives take な before だけ, exactly as they do before a noun.

  • ❌ テレビを見(み)ますだけです。 → ✅ テレビを見(み)るだけです。
    Politeness is carried by the final です. The verb in front of だけ stays in its plain dictionary form.

だけ · Practice 1

1. ケーキを一(ひと)つ____食(た)べました。

ケーキを一(ひと)つだけ食(た)べました。

だけ follows the counter directly, and the を on ケーキ is unaffected because it belongs to a different word. "I ate just one piece of cake."

だけ · Practice 2

2. Choose: 時間(じかん)が十分(じゅっぷん)（だけ・しか）ありません。

時間(じかん)が十分(じゅっぷん)しかありません。

The verb is already negative (ありません), and only しか licenses that. With だけ you would have to write 十分(じゅっぷん)だけあります - same ten minutes, but reported as a plain fact rather than as "only ten minutes left".

だけ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I only studied for thirty minutes yesterday."

昨日(きのう)は三十分(さんじゅっぷん)だけ勉強(べんきょう)しました。

The limiter goes on the amount of time, not on the verb. If you want it to sound like an admission - only thirty minutes, which isn't enough - 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)しか勉強(べんきょう)しませんでした does that job.

だけ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 私(わたし)は野菜(やさい)をだけ食(た)べます。

✅ 私(わたし)は野菜(やさい)だけ食(た)べます。

を may not precede だけ. The natural fix is to delete it; 野菜(やさい)だけを食(た)べます is also grammatical and puts a little extra weight on the restriction.

だけ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 三人(さんにん)だけ来(き)ました。 (b) 三人(さんにん)しか来(き)ませんでした。

(a) simply reports the number: three people, no more. A host counting chairs would say it. There is no attitude attached.

(b) says three was disappointing. The speaker had expected more, and the negative verb carries that shortfall. Same three people, but (b) tells you how the speaker feels about them.

～すぎる

Meaning · too much; excessively - past the point where it is still all right

～すぎる is not an intensifier. 高(たか)すぎる does not mean "very expensive"; it means the price has crossed a line and become a problem. Whenever you attach it, you are passing judgement: this is more than it should be.

～すぎる · Formation

Cut the word back to its stem, then add すぎる. すぎる itself is a regular る-verb: すぎます, すぎない, すぎた, すぎて.

～すぎる · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + すぎる 飲(の)む → 飲(の)み → 飲(の)みすぎる
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + すぎる 食(た)べる → 食(た)べ → 食(た)べすぎる
Verb (する／来(く)る) し／来(き) + すぎる 心配(しんぱい)する → 心配(しんぱい)しすぎる
Verb (negative) ない → なさ + すぎる 食(た)べない → 食(た)べなさすぎる
い-adjective drop い + すぎる 高(たか)い → 高(たか)すぎる
な-adjective stem + すぎる 静(しず)かだ → 静(しず)かすぎる
いい／よい よ + すぎる（irregular） いい → よすぎる
ない なさ + すぎる ない → なさすぎる
Noun form すぎ 飲(の)みすぎ, 働(はたら)きすぎ

～すぎる · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - whatever goes in front of すぎる is the same shape you would use in front of ます (for verbs) or in front of ～くない (for adjectives, minus the く). If you can still see an い or a だ, you have cut too little.

～すぎる · Examples

このかばんは高(たか)すぎます。
This bag is too expensive.

昨日(きのう)のパーティーで飲(の)みすぎました。
I drank too much at the party yesterday.

このスープは私(わたし)にはからすぎる。
This soup is too spicy for me.

ごはんを食(た)べすぎて、おなかが痛(いた)いです。
I ate too much and now my stomach hurts.

この部屋(へや)は静(しず)かすぎて、なんだかおちつきません。
This room is too quiet - it makes me uneasy somehow.

～すぎる · Examples (cont.)

このシャツは息子(むすこ)には少(すこ)し大(おお)きすぎました。
This shirt turned out a bit too big for my son.

テレビを見(み)すぎると、目(め)が悪(わる)くなりますよ。
If you watch too much television, your eyes will suffer.

この問題(もんだい)は一年生(いちねんせい)には難(むずか)しすぎるので、もっとやさしいものに変(か)えましょう。
This question is too hard for first-years, so let's swap it for an easier one.

～すぎる · Nuance & register

Register-neutral in itself - 高(たか)すぎます is as polite as the ます on the end makes it - but the content is always a criticism, so it can land harder than you intend. Softening it with ちょっと or 少(すこ)し is extremely common: ちょっと高(たか)すぎますね sounds like a negotiation, while 高(たか)すぎます sounds like a verdict.

The すぎ noun form appears constantly in everyday health talk: 飲(の)みすぎ, 食(た)べすぎ, 働(はたら)きすぎ, 寝(ね)すぎ - often on its own as a diagnosis. 昨日(きのう)は飲(の)みすぎだ。

One modern exception to the "always negative" rule: in casual speech, especially among younger speakers, すぎる is used as pure hyperbole for praise - かわいすぎる, おいしすぎる, すごすぎる. This is slangy, it is written with a lot of exclamation, and it does not belong in a polite conversation or in writing. Learn to recognise it; do not build sentences on it yet.

～すぎる · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note also that すぎる is a real verb in its own right meaning "to pass by" （時間(じかん)が過(す)ぎる, 駅(えき)を通(とお)り過(す)ぎる）. Compounds are usually written in kana, the standalone verb in kanji.

～すぎる · ～すぎる vs とても／すごく

Rule of thumb - とても says the quality is high. すぎる says the quality has gone wrong. Praise never uses すぎる.

このコーヒーはとても熱(あつ)いです。
This coffee is very hot. (nice and hot)

このコーヒーは熱(あつ)すぎます。
This coffee is too hot. (I can't drink it)

So a compliment must not take すぎる in careful speech: if you tell a cook ❌ この料理(りょうり)はおいしすぎます in a formal setting, the literal reading is that the dish is unreasonably rich. Say とてもおいしいです.

～すぎる · ～すぎる vs ～すぎ

Rule of thumb - すぎる is a verb and conjugates; すぎ is a noun and takes だ／です.

昨日(きのう)は働(はたら)きすぎました。
I worked too much yesterday.

働(はたら)きすぎですよ。少(すこ)し休(やす)んでください。
You're overworking. Take a break.

The noun form is what you use to label a situation rather than narrate an action, and it is the form that appears in set warnings like 食(た)べすぎに注意(ちゅうい) - "don't overeat".

～すぎる · ～すぎる vs the standalone verb すぎる

Rule of thumb - attached to a stem, it means "too much". Standing alone with its own particle, the same verb means "to go past".

三時(さんじ)を過(す)ぎました。
It's gone three o'clock.

おかしを食(た)べすぎました。
I ate too many sweets.

In the first sentence を marks the point being passed and the verb is the main verb of the sentence. In the second, すぎる has no object of its own - it is riding on 食(た)べ. If you can find a を in front of it, it is the "pass by" verb.

～すぎる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 食(た)べるすぎる → ✅ 食(た)べすぎる
    Verbs attach at the ます-stem, never at the dictionary form. Ask yourself what goes before ます: 食(た)べます → 食(た)べ.

  • ❌ 高(たか)いすぎる → ✅ 高(たか)すぎる
    い-adjectives drop the final い first. The same cut you make for 高(たか)くない or 高(たか)かった.

  • ❌ 静(しず)かなすぎる → ✅ 静(しず)かすぎる
    な-adjectives use the bare stem. No な, and no だ.

  • ❌ いいすぎる（meaning "too good"）→ ✅ よすぎる
    いい becomes よ- in every conjugation, and this one is no exception. Careful: 言(い)いすぎる is a real and different word - "to say too much, to go too far".

  • ❌ 野菜(やさい)を食(た)べないすぎる → ✅ 野菜(やさい)を食(た)べなさすぎる
    Negatives insert さ before すぎる, because they behave like adjectives - the same さ you see in なさすぎる.

～すぎる · Practice 1

1. この靴(くつ)は小(ちい)____すぎます。

この靴(くつ)は小(ちい)さすぎます。

小(ちい)さい is an い-adjective, so you remove only the final い and keep the さ: 小(ちい)さい → 小(ちい)さ → 小(ちい)さすぎます. Learners often cut back to 小(ちい) by mistake.

～すぎる · Practice 2

2. Choose: 昨日(きのう)の映画(えいが)は（とてもおもしろかった・おもしろすぎた）です。 You loved it and want to recommend it.

昨日(きのう)の映画(えいが)はとてもおもしろかったです。

すぎる is a complaint. おもしろすぎた would suggest the film was somehow excessive - overwhelming, more than you wanted. To recommend something, use とても or すごく.

～すぎる · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I got up too late this morning."

今朝(けさ)は起(お)きるのが遅(おそ)すぎました。 （or simply: 今朝(けさ)は寝(ね)すぎました。）

Either the adjective takes すぎる（遅(おそ)い → 遅(おそ)すぎる）or you put it on the verb that caused the problem. 寝(ね)すぎる - "to oversleep" - is what a Japanese speaker would actually say.

～すぎる · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: このお茶(ちゃ)はあついいすぎます。

✅ このお茶(ちゃ)はあつすぎます。

The writer has attached すぎる without cutting the adjective, and doubled the い along the way. あつい → あつ → あつすぎます. Only the final い goes.

～すぎる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この町(まち)はとてもしずかです。 (b) この町(まち)はしずかすぎます。

(a) is a compliment, or at worst a neutral description. Quiet is being presented as a feature of the town.

(b) says the quiet has become a problem - nothing is open, nobody is about, the speaker is unsettled by it. The すぎる is what converts a pleasant quality into a complaint, and that conversion happens whatever the adjective is.

～と同じ

Meaning · the same as ～ - identical to something else, not merely similar

同(おな)じ is the word for sameness, and と is the particle that names what the sameness is measured against. English says "the same as"; Japanese says "と the same". Get that particle wrong and the sentence falls apart.

～と同じ · Formation

同(おな)じ is a strange word: it behaves like a な-adjective everywhere except directly in front of a noun, where the な disappears.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (predicate) Noun + と同(おな)じ + です／だ 私(わたし)と同(おな)じです
Noun (modifying) Noun + と同(おな)じ + Noun 私(わたし)と同(おな)じクラス
Negative と同(おな)じ + じゃない／ではありません 同(おな)じじゃありません
Past と同(おな)じ + でした／だった 同(おな)じでした
Degree Noun + と同(おな)じくらい + adjective 私(わたし)と同(おな)じくらい高(たか)い
Manner Noun + と同(おな)じように + verb 前(まえ)と同(おな)じように作(つく)る
Two things listed AとBは同(おな)じ このかばんとあのかばんは同(おな)じです

～と同じ · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - no な before a noun. ✅同(おな)じ学校(がっこう) ／ ❌同(おな)じな学校(がっこう). This one word is the exception that every learner trips over, because 静(しず)かな学校(がっこう) and 有名(ゆうめい)な学校(がっこう) behave normally.

～と同じ · Examples

このかばんは私(わたし)のと同(おな)じです。
This bag is the same as mine.

田中(たなか)さんは私(わたし)と同(おな)じ年(とし)です。
Tanaka is the same age as me.

この色(いろ)はあの色(いろ)と同(おな)じじゃありません。
This colour isn't the same as that one.

弟(おとうと)は兄(あに)と同(おな)じ学校(がっこう)に通(かよ)っています。
My younger brother goes to the same school as my older brother.

妹(いもうと)は私(わたし)と同(おな)じくらい背(せ)が高(たか)いです。
My little sister is about as tall as I am.

～と同じ · Examples (cont.)

去年(きょねん)と同(おな)じホテルに泊(と)まりました。
We stayed at the same hotel as last year.

毎朝(まいあさ)、父(ちち)と同(おな)じ電車(でんしゃ)に乗(の)って会社(かいしゃ)へ行(い)きます。
Every morning I take the same train as my father to the office.

昨日(きのう)説明(せつめい)したことと同(おな)じことを、もう一度(いちど)書(か)いておきます。
I'll write down the same thing I explained yesterday, one more time.

～と同じ · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and just as common in speech as in writing.

The と may be dropped when the two items are listed together as a pair: このペンとそのペンは同(おな)じです。 Here the と you can see is the "and" particle joining the two pens, and 同(おな)じ stands alone as the predicate. Both patterns are correct - what is not correct is having neither.

Watch the の when you compare possessions. 私(わたし)と同(おな)じ compares the other thing to me, the person. 私(わたし)のと同(おな)じ compares it to mine, the object. English hides this distinction; Japanese does not, and getting it wrong produces sentences like "this bag is the same as me."

Two fixed extensions are worth learning as units:

～と同じ · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 同(おな)じくらい - "about the same amount / to the same degree". This is
    how you say "as … as" for adjectives.
  • 同(おな)じように - "in the same way". It modifies a verb, not a noun.

In writing you may also meet 同様(どうよう)に and 同(おな)じく, both stiffer equivalents. Neither belongs in conversation at this level.

～と同じ · 同(おな)じ + noun vs 同(おな)じな + noun

Rule of thumb - 同(おな)じ is the one な-adjective that drops its な in front of a noun.

山田(やまだ)さんと同(おな)じ意見(いけん)です。
I'm of the same opinion as Yamada.

❌ 同(おな)じな意見(いけん) is simply wrong. But な does come back before ので and のに: 同(おな)じなので, 同(おな)じなのに. So the rule is narrower than "no な ever" - it is "no な before a noun".

～と同じ · ～と同(おな)じ vs ～に似(に)ている

Rule of thumb - 同(おな)じ means identical. 似(に)ている means resembling. And note the particle flips: と for same, に for similar.

この時計(とけい)は兄(あに)のと同(おな)じです。
This watch is the same one my brother has.

この時計(とけい)は兄(あに)のに似(に)ています。
This watch looks like my brother's.

The first says you could swap them and nobody would notice. The second says they merely look alike. For faces, 似(に)ている is the only option - 母(はは)に似(に)ています, never 母(はは)と同(おな)じです unless you mean something very odd.

～と同じ · ～と同(おな)じ vs ～と違(ちが)う

Rule of thumb - the same frame, negated. Both take と, and both put the comparison target in front of it.

この漢字(かんじ)はあの漢字(かんじ)と同(おな)じ読(よ)み方(かた)です。
This kanji has the same reading as that one.

この漢字(かんじ)はあの漢字(かんじ)と読(よ)み方(かた)が違(ちが)います。
This kanji is read differently from that one.

Note that 違(ちが)う is a verb, so it takes ます／ました and never です: ❌違(ちが)うです → ✅違(ちが)います. 同(おな)じ, being adjectival, does the opposite and needs です.

～と同じ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私(わたし)に同(おな)じです。 → ✅ 私(わたし)と同(おな)じです。
    The comparison target is marked with と. に belongs to 似(に)ている, not here.

  • ❌ 山田(やまだ)さんと同(おな)じな会社(かいしゃ)です。 → ✅ 山田(やまだ)さんと同(おな)じ会社(かいしゃ)です。
    No な in front of a noun. This is the single most frequent error with the word.

  • ❌ このかばんは私(わたし)と同(おな)じです。（meaning "the same as mine"）→ ✅ このかばんは私(わたし)のと同(おな)じです。
    Without の you are comparing a bag to a person. の turns 私(わたし) into "mine".

  • ❌ 去年(きょねん)と同(おな)じに作(つく)りました。 → ✅ 去年(きょねん)と同(おな)じように作(つく)りました。
    To modify a verb you need ように. Bare 同(おな)じに is not idiomatic here.

～と同じ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 二人(ふたり)の背(せ)は同(おな)じくらいと高(たか)いです。 → ✅ 二人(ふたり)は同(おな)じくらい背(せ)が高(たか)いです。
    同(おな)じくらい sits directly in front of the adjective, with no particle between them.

～と同じ · Practice 1

1. 林(はやし)さんは私(わたし)____同(おな)じアパートに住(す)んでいます。

林(はやし)さんは私(わたし)と同(おな)じアパートに住(す)んでいます。

The thing being matched always takes と. Note also that 同(おな)じ attaches straight to アパート with no な in between.

～と同じ · Practice 2

2. Choose: この写真(しゃしん)の人(ひと)は母(はは)（と同(おな)じ・に似(に)ている）です。 You mean the person resembles your mother.

この写真(しゃしん)の人(ひと)は母(はは)に似(に)ています。

Resemblance takes に似(に)ている, with に. 母(はは)と同(おな)じです would claim the two are the same person - or the same thing, which is stranger still. Note the answer ends in ています, not です, because 似(に)る is a verb.

～と同じ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I ordered the same thing as you."

あなたと同(おな)じものを注文(ちゅうもん)しました。

同(おな)じ modifies もの directly - no な. In real conversation the あなた would usually be replaced by the person's name, or dropped entirely: 同(おな)じものをください。

～と同じ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この傘(かさ)はあなたと同(おな)じなです。

✅ この傘(かさ)はあなたのと同(おな)じです。

Two problems. な cannot stand before です - 同(おな)じです is the predicate form. And the umbrella should be compared with yours, not with you, which is what the added の does.

～と同じ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 去年(きょねん)と同(おな)じ店(みせ)です。 (b) 去年(きょねん)の店(みせ)に似(に)ている店(みせ)です。

(a) claims identity: it is literally that shop, the one you went to last year, still in the same place under the same name.

(b) claims resemblance only: a different shop that reminds you of the old one - similar food, similar atmosphere. Japanese keeps these two claims strictly apart, where English "like last year's place" can be read either way.
