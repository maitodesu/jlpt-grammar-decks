お○○になる

Meaning · the general-purpose honorific frame - describes someone else's action while raising them

Japanese politeness runs on three separate systems. 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご) - です／ます - makes your speech polite to whoever is listening. 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) raises the person you are talking about. 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) lowers you. お○○になる is the workhorse of the second group: take almost any verb, and it becomes a respectful description of what your customer, your teacher or your boss did.

お○○になる · Formation

Take the ます-stem, put お in front and になる behind. になる then conjugates as an ordinary う-verb (五段(ごだん), Group 1).

お○○になる · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) お + ます-stem + になる 書(か)きます → お書(か)きになる
Verb (る-verb) お + ます-stem + になる 調(しら)べます → お調(しら)べになる
する-verb, Chinese-derived root ご + noun + になる 利用(りよう)する → ご利用(りよう)になる
する-verb, native root お + noun + になる 問(と)い合(あ)わせする → お問(と)い合(あ)わせになる
Polite / past お + stem + になります／になりました お使(つか)いになりました
Verb with its own honorific frame not used 食(た)べる → 召(め)し上(あ)がる
One-mora ます-stem frame not used 見(み)る → ご覧(らん)になる、寝(ね)る → お休(やす)みになる

お○○になる · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - if the ます-stem is only one mora long （見(み)、着(き)、寝(ね)、来(き)、し）, the frame will not carry it. Those verbs all have a dedicated honorific word instead: ご覧(らん)になる、お召(め)しになる、お休(やす)みになる、いらっしゃる、なさる.

お○○になる · Examples

先生(せんせい)は何時(なんじ)にお帰(かえ)りになりますか。
What time will you be leaving, sensei?

この本(ほん)をもうお読(よ)みになりましたか。
Have you read this book already?

部長(ぶちょう)は昼(ひる)すぎにお出(で)かけになりました。
The department head went out in the early afternoon.

お客(きゃく)様(さま)がお選(えら)びになったのは、こちらの色(いろ)でよろしいでしょうか。
Is this the colour you chose, madam?

課長(かちょう)がお決(き)めになった方法(ほうほう)で進(すす)めます。
We'll go ahead with the method the section chief settled on.

お○○になる · Examples (cont.)

エレベーターをご利用(りよう)になる方(かた)は、右(みぎ)の通路(つうろ)へお回(まわ)りください。
Those using the lift, please go round to the right-hand corridor.

皆(みな)様(さま)がお集(あつ)まりになる時間(じかん)は午後(ごご)二時(にじ)でよろしいでしょうか。
Would two o'clock suit as the time for everyone to gather?

新(あたら)しい計画(けいかく)について、社長(しゃちょう)はどうお考(かんが)えになっているのでしょうか。
I wonder what the president makes of the new plan.

お○○になる · Nuance & register

This is business and service language. In a shop, a hospital, a hotel, a 会議(かいぎ)室(しつ) or a classroom it is completely ordinary; among friends it is not. Saying お食(た)べになりますか to a classmate is not "extra polite", it is strange - it puts a counter between the two of you. Overusing 敬語(けいご) socially is a real mistake, not a safe default.

Three things follow from the fact that お○○になる is 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご):

  • Never about yourself. ❌私(わたし)がお作(つく)りになりました is the
    single commonest 敬語(けいご) error learners make. You cannot raise your own head. Your own actions take 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) - お作(つく)りしました

  • or plain ます.

お○○になる · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • Never doubled. ❌お読(よ)みになられる stacks the frame on top of the
    られる honorific. That is 二重(にじゅう)敬語(けいご), and it sounds overcooked rather than extra respectful. One layer is enough.
  • Not for your own side. Speaking to a customer about a colleague, even your
    own boss, you drop the honorific: 田中(たなか)はただいま席(せき)をはずしております - never お席(せき)をはずしになっています.

On the politeness ladder, お○○になる sits above れる／られる and below the set honorific verbs. Treat that as a tendency rather than a measurement: in most offices お書(か)きになる and 書(か)かれる are simply two acceptable choices, and which one a person reaches for depends on the workplace and on how far above them the other person stands.

お○○になる · お○○になる vs ～れる／～られる

Rule of thumb - both are honorific. られる is the light, everyday office form; お○○になる is heavier, more deferential, and unambiguous.

社長(しゃちょう)はもう帰(かえ)られました。
The president has already left for the day.

社長(しゃちょう)はもうお帰(かえ)りになりました。
The president has already left for the day.

The second is the more respectful of the two, and it is what you would say to an outside visitor; the first is what one colleague says to another. The practical advantage of お○○になる is that it cannot be misread. 帰(かえ)られる is also the passive and the potential, so 先生(せんせい)に来(こ)られました can be read two ways, while 先生(せんせい)がお越(こ)しになりました cannot.

お○○になる · お○○になる vs お○○する

Rule of thumb - same stem, opposite direction. になる raises the other person; する lowers you.

先生(せんせい)が荷物(にもつ)をお持(も)ちになります。
The teacher is going to carry the bags himself.

先生(せんせい)の荷物(にもつ)をお持(も)ちします。
I'll carry your bags for you, sensei.

One says the teacher does the carrying; the other offers to do it for him. Swapping them reverses who is being honoured, which is why the pair is worth drilling until it is automatic.

お○○になる · お○○になる vs the set honorific verbs

Rule of thumb - if a verb owns a dedicated honorific word, the frame is closed to it. Learn that short list; use the frame for everything else.

先生(せんせい)はもうお昼(ひる)を召(め)し上(あ)がりましたか。
Has sensei had lunch yet?

資料(しりょう)はもうご覧(らん)になりましたか。
Have you had a look at the documents yet?

食(た)べる, 見(み)る, 言(い)う, する, 行(い)く, 来(く)る, いる and 寝(ね)る all belong to that closed set. Note that ご覧(らん)になる ends in になる but is not built from a ます-stem - ご覧(らん) is a noun, and the word has to be memorised whole.

お○○になる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私(わたし)が資料(しりょう)をお作(つく)りになりました。 → ✅ 私(わたし)が資料(しりょう)をお作(つく)りしました。
    Honorific language points away from the speaker. Describing your own work with お○○になる is the error a Japanese listener notices fastest.

  • ❌ 地図(ちず)をお見(み)になりましたか。 → ✅ 地図(ちず)をご覧(らん)になりましたか。
    見(み)る has a one-mora stem and its own honorific verb. The frame does not build on it.

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)がお話(はな)しになられました。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)がお話(はな)しになりました。
    Two honorific layers on one verb is 二重(にじゅう)敬語(けいご). Pick one.

  • ❌ 課長(かちょう)がお説明(せつめい)になりました。 → ✅ 課長(かちょう)がご説明(せつめい)になりました。
    A Chinese-derived する-verb root takes ご: ご利用(りよう)、 ご出発(しゅっぱつ)、ご記入(きにゅう).

お○○になる · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ ねえ、この漫画(まんが)お読(よ)みになった? → ✅ ねえ、この漫画(まんが)読(よ)んだ?
    Grammatical, wrong for the situation. Full 敬語(けいご) aimed at a friend reads as sarcasm or as sudden distance.

お○○になる · Practice 1

1. 先生(せんせい)は毎朝(まいあさ)新聞(しんぶん)を____読(よ)____になります。

先生(せんせい)は毎朝(まいあさ)新聞(しんぶん)をお読(よ)みになります。

The frame is お + ます-stem + になる. The stem of 読(よ)みます is 読(よ)み, so お goes in front of it and になります behind.

お○○になる · Practice 2

2. Choose: 係(かかり)の者(もの)が（ご案内(あんない)になります・ご案内(あんない)します）。

係(かかり)の者(もの)がご案内(あんない)します。

係(かかり)の者(もの) is a member of your own side, so the sentence has to lower rather than raise. ご○○になる would honour your own staff in front of a customer, which is exactly backwards.

お○○になる · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "What time will the president be arriving?"

社長(しゃちょう)は何時(なんじ)にお着(つ)きになりますか。

着(つ)きます gives the stem 着(つ)き, hence お着(つ)きになります. ご到着(とうちゃく)になりますか works too, since 到着(とうちゃく) is a Chinese-derived root and takes ご.

お○○になる · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 明日(あした)、私(わたし)は十時(じゅうじ)にお越(こ)しになります。

✅ 明日(あした)、私(わたし)は十時(じゅうじ)に参(まい)ります。

Two faults in one clause. The verb describes you, so it needs 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご), not 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご). And 来(く)る is outside the frame anyway: the honorific for someone else coming is お越(こ)しになる or いらっしゃる, while the humble word for your own coming is 参(まい)る.

お○○になる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 田中(たなか)様(さま)はいつ出発(しゅっぱつ)されますか。 (b) 田中(たなか)様(さま)はいつご出発(しゅっぱつ)になりますか。

Both are honorific and both are correct. (a) uses the される form, which is light and quick - the register of a colleague, or of a routine business email.

(b) is the heavier frame, and it is what a hotel clerk or a receptionist would say to a guest. It sounds more careful and more deliberate. The gap is one of degree, not of grammar: choosing (a) with a customer would not offend anyone, it would only sound a little brisk.

お○○ください

Meaning · "please do ○○" - an honorific request, the form used to customers, guests and audiences

お○○ください asks the listener to do something while raising them. It is the 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) counterpart of ～てください: same job, but the listener is honoured rather than simply addressed politely. Every announcement you hear on a Japanese train, in a lift and in a shop is built out of this frame.

お○○ください · Formation

ください is the imperative of くださる, the honorific of くれる. Put it after お + the ます-stem.

お○○ください · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) お + ます-stem + ください 待(ま)ちます → お待(ま)ちください
Verb (る-verb) お + ます-stem + ください 調(しら)べます → お調(しら)べください
する-verb, Chinese-derived root ご + noun + ください 確認(かくにん)する → ご確認(かくにん)ください
する-verb, native root お + noun + ください 申(もう)し込(こ)みする → お申(もう)し込(こ)みください
Set honorific verb fixed form 見(み)る → ご覧(らん)ください
Extra-polite service form + ませ お待(ま)ちくださいませ
Negative ("please don't") frame not used ご遠慮(えんりょ)ください／～ないでください

お○○ください · Formation (cont.)

Note - one-mora stems are shut out here for the same reason as in お○○になる: ❌お見(み)ください、❌お来(き)ください do not exist. Use ご覧(らん)ください and お越(こ)しください.

お○○ください · Examples

こちらでお待(ま)ちください。
Please wait here.

どうぞおかけください。
Please have a seat.

この線(せん)の内側(うちがわ)までお下(さ)がりください。
Please step back behind this line.

温(あたた)かいうちにお召(め)し上(あ)がりください。
Please eat it while it's still warm.

足元(あしもと)にご注意(ちゅうい)ください。
Please mind your step.

お○○ください · Examples (cont.)

順番(じゅんばん)にご案内(あんない)しますので、こちらにお並(なら)びください。
We'll show you through in turn, so please queue here.

ご不明(ふめい)な点(てん)がございましたら、いつでもお問(と)い合(あ)わせください。
If anything is unclear, please get in touch at any time.

会場(かいじょう)にお入(はい)りの際(さい)は、受付(うけつけ)でこちらの用紙(ようし)をお出(だ)しください。
When you enter the hall, please hand this form in at reception.

お○○ください · Nuance & register

This frame is service and announcement language, and it carries a faint flavour of instruction. A station guard, a shop assistant, a nurse calling a patient and a website's help page all use it. Between friends it is bizarre - you do not tell a classmate ❌ちょっとお待(ま)ちください unless you are joking.

The instructional flavour matters when you want a personal favour. ください is still an imperative underneath, so a bare お書(か)きください to your boss can land like a directive from below. For asking a superior for something, shift to a question form:

  • お書(か)きいただけますか - "could you write it?"
  • お書(か)きいただけませんでしょうか - the softest, and standard in email.
  • 書(か)いていただけると助(たす)かります - "it would help me if you would."

お○○ください · Nuance & register (cont.)

ませ on the end（お待(ま)ちくださいませ、いらっしゃいませ）belongs to shop and hotel speech specifically. It is warm and formal at once, and it is heard far more from staff than from anyone else.

Two words about お召(め)し上(あ)がりください and お求(もと)めください. Strictly, 召(め)し上(あ)がる is already honorific, so adding お makes a double layer; usage guides nonetheless list these as established by custom, and you will hear them constantly. Treat them as fixed phrases rather than as models for building new ones.

One more tendency worth knowing: many speakers avoid お座(すわ)りください in favour of おかけください, because お座(すわ)り on its own is what you say to a dog. Plenty of hospitals and offices say お座(すわ)りください anyway, so it is a preference rather than a rule - but おかけください is never the wrong choice.

お○○ください · お○○ください vs ～てください

Rule of thumb - ～てください is polite; お○○ください is polite and honorific. The first addresses the listener courteously, the second raises them.

ここに名前(なまえ)を書(か)いてください。
Please write your name here.

こちらにお名前(なまえ)をお書(か)きください。
Please write your name here.

The first is what a teacher says to a class, or a friend to a friend. The second is what a bank clerk says to a customer - notice how ここ becomes こちら and 名前(なまえ) takes its prefix. 敬語(けいご) rarely arrives alone; the whole sentence shifts register together.

お○○ください · お○○ください vs お○○いただけますか

Rule of thumb - ください tells; いただけますか asks. When the favour is for your benefit, ask.

明日(あした)までにご返事(へんじ)ください。
Please reply by tomorrow.

明日(あした)までにご返事(へんじ)いただけますでしょうか。
Might I ask you to reply by tomorrow?

Both are polite. The first states what needs to happen and suits a notice or a form; the second leaves the decision with the listener and suits a request to a client or a superior. いただく is 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご), so the second sentence lowers you at the same time as it raises them - which is why it feels so much softer.

お○○ください · お○○ください vs ご遠慮(えんりょ)ください

Rule of thumb - the frame has no negative. To ask someone not to do something, use a different verb: ご遠慮(えんりょ)ください or お控(ひか)えください.

車内(しゃない)でのお電話(でんわ)はご遠慮(えんりょ)ください。
Please refrain from making phone calls on board.

写真(しゃしん)の撮影(さつえい)はお控(ひか)えください。
Please refrain from taking photographs.

There is no ❌お話(はな)しにならないでください in ordinary use. Japanese public notices prefer to name a polite verb of refraining rather than to negate the request - it reads as an appeal to the listener's discretion instead of a ban.

お○○ください · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日(あした)会社(かいしゃ)にお来(き)ください。 → ✅ 明日(あした)会社(かいしゃ)にお越(こ)しください。
    来(く)る has no place in the frame. Use お越(こ)しください, おいでください or いらしてください.

  • ❌ こちらのボタンをご確認(かくにん)してください。 → ✅ こちらのボタンをご確認(かくにん)ください。
    ご○○する is the humble frame, so ご確認(かくにん)してください tells the listener to humble themselves. Drop the して.

  • ❌ メニューをお見(み)ください。 → ✅ メニューをご覧(らん)ください。
    One-mora stem, dedicated honorific verb. The same goes for お食(た)べください → お召(め)し上(あ)がりください.

お○○ください · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、この書類(しょるい)におサインください。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、この書類(しょるい)にご署名(しょめい)いただけますでしょうか。
    Two problems: loanwords do not take the prefix, and a direct ください to your own boss for your own paperwork is too flat. Ask with いただけますか.

お○○ください · Practice 1

1. 危(あぶ)ないですので、白線(はくせん)の内側(うちがわ)まで____下(さ)がり____。

危(あぶ)ないですので、白線(はくせん)の内側(うちがわ)までお下(さ)がりください。

お + ます-stem + ください. 下(さ)がります gives the stem 下(さ)がり. This is the standard platform announcement, and it is honorific because it is addressed to passengers.

お○○ください · Practice 2

2. Choose: 資料(しりょう)を（ご覧(らん)ください・拝見(はいけん)ください）。

資料(しりょう)をご覧(らん)ください。

拝見(はいけん)する is 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) - it lowers the person doing the looking. Telling the listener to 拝見(はいけん) orders them to be humble towards you, which is the opposite of what a request should do.

お○○ください · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Please use the lift on the left."

左(ひだり)のエレベーターをご利用(りよう)ください。

利用(りよう) is a Chinese-derived する-verb root, so it takes ご and the する disappears entirely: ご利用(りよう)ください, not ❌ご利用(りよう)してください.

お○○ください · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 館内(かんない)ではおタバコをお吸(す)いにならないでください。

✅ 館内(かんない)での喫煙(きつえん)はご遠慮(えんりょ)ください。

Two faults. Loanwords do not take the polite prefix, and the honorific frame has no natural negative - Japanese notices name a verb of refraining instead. 館内(かんない)ではタバコをお吸(す)いにならないでください is understandable but sounds clumsy next to the fixed phrase.

お○○ください · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 少(すこ)々お待(ま)ちください。 (b) 少(すこ)々お待(ま)ちいただけますか。

(a) is the standard service line - a receptionist, a phone operator, a waiter. It is honorific and perfectly courteous, but it tells the listener what will happen, which is fine when the waiting is part of the process.

(b) turns the same content into a question, so the listener is free to say no. It is what you use when the delay is your fault, or when you are asking a favour of someone senior. Between colleagues (a) would sound oddly official; from a shop assistant (b) can sound over-apologetic. Match the form to who owns the delay.

いらっしゃる

Meaning · the honorific verb covering いる, 行(い)く and 来(く)る all at once

いらっしゃる is 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご): it raises the person it describes, so it is used about your teacher, your client, your guest - never about yourself. Three ordinary verbs collapse into it, and Japanese speakers do not find that ambiguous, because the situation always says which one is meant.

いらっしゃる · Formation

Plain verb Honorific Example
いる いらっしゃる 先生(せんせい)は部屋(へや)にいらっしゃいます
行(い)く いらっしゃる どちらへいらっしゃいますか
来(く)る いらっしゃる よくいらっしゃいました
～ている ～ていらっしゃる 待(ま)っていらっしゃいます
Noun + です (about a person) ～でいらっしゃる 社長(しゃちょう)でいらっしゃいます

It is a う-verb, but its ます-form is irregular - the り you expect becomes い. Four verbs share this quirk, and they are all 敬語(けいご):

いらっしゃる · Examples

先生(せんせい)は教室(きょうしつ)にいらっしゃいますか。
Is the teacher in the classroom?

どちらへいらっしゃいますか。
Where are you off to?

遠(とお)いところ、よくいらっしゃいました。
Thank you for coming all this way.

明日(あした)の会(かい)には何名(なんめい)ほどいらっしゃいますか。
Roughly how many people will be coming to tomorrow's gathering?

部長(ぶちょう)は今(いま)、会議室(かいぎしつ)にはいらっしゃいません。
The department head isn't in the meeting room at the moment.

いらっしゃる · Examples (cont.)

山田(やまだ)先生(せんせい)は来週(らいしゅう)大阪(おおさか)からいらっしゃるそうです。
I hear Professor Yamada is coming up from Osaka next week.

お客(きゃく)様(さま)が先(さき)ほどから受付(うけつけ)で待(ま)っていらっしゃいます。
The customer has been waiting at reception for a little while now.

失礼(しつれい)ですが、田中(たなか)様(さま)でいらっしゃいますか。
Excuse me - would you be Mr Tanaka?

いらっしゃる · Nuance & register

いらっしゃる is the first 敬語(けいご) verb most learners can actually use, because it turns up everywhere: shop doorways, phone calls, introductions, reception desks. It belongs to business, service and formal social speech. Saying it about a friend - ❌ゆうべ友(とも)だちが家(いえ)にいらっしゃった - is not extra-polite, it is odd, because you are honouring someone who is on your own level.

Some texture:

いらっしゃる · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • いらっしゃいませ is not an invitation to come. It is a shop and restaurant
    greeting, roughly "welcome", and it expects no reply at all.
  • いらっしゃい on its own is warm and informal - a host to a guest, an adult to
    a child, a market stall to passers-by.
  • いらして／いらしてください is the contracted て-form. It is softer and a
    little lighter than いらっしゃって, and it leans towards polite conversational speech rather than announcements.
  • お越(こ)しになる, おいでになる, お見(み)えになる are alternatives for
    the "come" sense, each a shade more formal than plain いらっしゃる.

いらっしゃる · Nuance & register (cont.)

The trap that catches every English speaker is the in-group rule. Japanese politeness is not about rank in the abstract; it is about which side of a line the person stands on. Inside your company, 部長(ぶちょう) outranks you and takes 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご). The moment you speak to somebody outside the company - a client, a supplier, a caller - your whole company becomes your in-group, and your boss is lowered along with you:

  • To a colleague: 部長(ぶちょう)はもうお帰(かえ)りになりました。
  • To a client: 田中(たなか)は本日(ほんじつ)外出(がいしゅつ)しております。

No title, no さん, and おります rather than いらっしゃいます. It feels disloyal to an English speaker; in Japanese it is exactly the loyal thing to do, because lowering your own side is how you raise the customer.

いらっしゃる · いらっしゃる vs おる

Rule of thumb - いらっしゃる for people you are raising; おる for yourself and for anyone on your own side of the line.

社長(しゃちょう)は三階(さんがい)にいらっしゃいます。
The president is on the third floor.

私(わたし)どもは明日(あした)も店(みせ)におります。
We'll be at the shop tomorrow as well.

The same phone call can need both, one clause apart. An outside caller asking 田中(たなか)さんはいらっしゃいますか is right - from where they stand, 田中(たなか) is an outsider and deserves the honorific. Your reply, 田中(たなか)はただいま席(せき)をはずしております, is also right, because from where you stand he is family.

いらっしゃる · いらっしゃる vs 参(まい)る・伺(うかが)う

Rule of thumb - いらっしゃる is them going or coming; 参(まい)る and 伺(うかが)う are you going or coming.

先生(せんせい)は三時(さんじ)にいらっしゃいます。
Sensei will be arriving at three.

三時(さんじ)に研究室(けんきゅうしつ)へ伺(うかが)います。
I'll come to your office at three.

伺(うかが)う is used when the place you are going belongs to somebody you respect; 参(まい)る is the plainer humble word and works even when nobody in particular is being honoured - 電車(でんしゃ)が参(まい)ります on a platform announcement is about a train, not a person.

いらっしゃる · いらっしゃる vs おいでになる・お見(み)えになる

Rule of thumb - all three are honorific. いらっしゃる is the default; おいでになる is more formal; お見(み)えになる only ever means "come".

会長(かいちょう)が会場(かいじょう)においでになりました。
The chairman has arrived at the venue.

お約束(やくそく)の方(かた)がお見(み)えになりました。
Your appointment has arrived.

Because いらっしゃる covers three meanings, the alternatives are sometimes chosen simply for clarity: お見(み)えになりました can only be an arrival, so nobody has to work out whether the person came, went or is present.

いらっしゃる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)は明日(あした)いらっしゃります。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)は明日(あした)いらっしゃいます。
    The り becomes い in the ます-form. The same applies to おっしゃいます, なさいます and くださいます.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)、私(わたし)が会社(かいしゃ)にいらっしゃいます。 → ✅ 明日(あした)、私(わたし)が会社(かいしゃ)に参(まい)ります。
    尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) about yourself is the classic error. You cannot raise your own head - use the humble verb.

  • ❌ （to a client）弊社(へいしゃ)の社長(しゃちょう)は今(いま)いらっしゃいません。 → ✅ 社長(しゃちょう)の佐藤(さとう)は本日(ほんじつ)おりません。
    The in-group rule. Talking to an outsider, your own president is lowered like everyone else on your side - and loses the さん.

いらっしゃる · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ こちらの資料(しりょう)はいらっしゃいますか。 → ✅ こちらの資料(しりょう)はございますか。
    いらっしゃる honours a person. Objects take ある and its polite form ございます; there is nobody there to raise.

いらっしゃる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) いらっしゃいませ。 (b) いらっしゃい。

(a) is service language. A shop assistant, a waiter or a hotel receptionist says it as you walk in, and it is not really addressed to you as an individual - no reply is expected.

(b) is the same word without the ませ, and dropping ませ drops the formality. It is what a host says opening the door to a guest, or a parent to a child's friend: friendly and personal. A market trader shouting いらっしゃい, いらっしゃい is using it as a call to passers-by, which is friendlier still.

なさる

Meaning · the honorific of する - "to do", said about someone you are raising

なさる is 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご). Because する combines with hundreds of nouns, なさる gives you an honorific for every one of them at a stroke: 勉強(べんきょう)なさる、運転(うんてん)なさる、ご結婚(けっこん)なさる. It is also the verb behind the two most useful service questions in Japanese, どうなさいますか and いかがなさいましたか.

なさる · Nuance & register

なさる is squarely business, service and formal-social language. Shop staff, receptionists, doctors, flight attendants and hotel clerks use it constantly, mostly in the fixed openers どうなさいますか（"what would you like to do?"）, いかがなさいますか（the more formal version）and どうなさいましたか（"what's wrong?"）. To a friend, ❌何(なに)をなさっているんですか would be comic.

Because it is 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) it obeys the same two hard limits as the rest of the system. It never describes your own action - that is いたす - and it never describes your own side when you are speaking to an outsider. On the phone to a customer, ❌担当(たんとう)の者(もの)が確認(かくにん)なさいます honours your own colleague; the sentence you want is 担当(たんとう)の者(もの)が確認(かくにん)いたします.

なさる · Nuance & register (cont.)

される is the lighter alternative: 部長(ぶちょう)はどうされますか is perfectly polite and rather more everyday than 部長(ぶちょう)はどうなさいますか. As a tendency, なさる is felt as warmer and more attentive, and it is heard more often from service staff and in careful formal speech; される is the workhorse of ordinary office Japanese. Neither is wrong in the other's setting.

Do not stack layers. ❌おなさりになる and ❌なさられる are 二重(にじゅう)敬語(けいご); なさる on its own is already the honorific.

なさる · なさる vs いたす

Rule of thumb - なさる for what they do; いたす for what you do. Same verb, opposite directions.

お客(きゃく)様(さま)はどちらになさいますか。
Which will you have, madam?

私(わたし)どもがご用意(ようい)いたします。
We'll get it ready for you.

This pair is the cleanest illustration of the whole three-way system. Both sentences are polite to the customer; the first gets there by lifting her, the second by lowering the speaker. Using なさる about yourself does not merely sound odd - it claims a status you have just given to somebody else.

なさる · なさる vs される

Rule of thumb - both are honorific. される is lighter and unmarked; なさる is more attentive and more distinctly 敬語(けいご).

課長(かちょう)はいつ出張(しゅっちょう)されますか。
When are you going on the business trip, 課長(かちょう)?

会長(かいちょう)はいつ出張(しゅっちょう)なさいますか。
When will the chairman be going on his trip?

There is one practical reason to reach for なさる: される is also the passive. 注意(ちゅうい)されました can mean "you kindly took care" or "I got told off", and 注意(ちゅうい)なさいました can only mean the first.

なさる · なさる vs ～なさい

Rule of thumb - なさる looks up; ～なさい looks down. They come from the same root and point in opposite social directions.

宿題(しゅくだい)を先(さき)にしなさい。
Do your homework first.

宿題(しゅくだい)はもうなさいましたか。
Have you done your homework yet? (said respectfully)

～なさい is softer than the bare imperative しろ, which is why parents and teachers like it - but softness is not respect. Aimed upward it is an insult, so ❌先生(せんせい)、こちらに書(か)きなさい must be 先生(せんせい)、こちらにお書(か)きください.

なさる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 社長(しゃちょう)は何時(なんじ)に出発(しゅっぱつ)なさりますか。 → ✅ 社長(しゃちょう)は何時(なんじ)に出発(しゅっぱつ)なさいますか。
    The り turns into い before ます. Same irregularity as いらっしゃいます and くださいます.

  • ❌ 私(わたし)がご説明(せつめい)なさいます。 → ✅ 私(わたし)がご説明(せつめい)いたします。
    尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) pointed at yourself. Your own actions take 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご).

  • ❌ お客(きゃく)様(さま)、どうしましたか。 → ✅ お客(きゃく)様(さま)、どうなさいましたか。
    Grammatically fine, socially thin. しましたか is only 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご) - polite speech with no honorific in it - and a customer expects to be raised, not merely addressed politely.

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)が説明(せつめい)なさられました。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)が説明(せつめい)なさいました。
    なさる plus られる is two honorifics on one verb. One is the ceiling.

なさる · Practice 5

敬語の動詞 · Nuance & register

Hold the three-way system in mind and none of this is guesswork. 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご)（です・ます）makes your speech polite to the listener. 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) raises the subject of the verb. 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) lowers the subject of the verb, which raises whoever the action is aimed at. Choosing a form is therefore a question about who is doing the verb, not about how polite you feel.

Two refinements worth having early:

The humble column has two halves. 伺(うかが)う, 申(もう)し上(あ)げる, 拝見(はいけん)する and お目(め)にかかる need a specific person on the receiving end who deserves the respect. 参(まい)る, 申(もう)す and おる do not - they simply lower and formalise, which is why 電車(でんしゃ)が参(まい)ります works on a platform announcement even though a train respects nobody. That second group is sometimes labelled 丁重(ていちょう)語(ご), and it is the group you use for your own side in front of an outsider.

敬語の動詞 · Nuance & register (cont.)

In-group before rank. English speakers expect politeness to track seniority. Japanese tracks the boundary of your group first. Talking to a client, your own 部長(ぶちょう) is lowered exactly like you: not ❌部長(ぶちょう)がおっしゃっていました but 部長(ぶちょう)の中村(なかむら)が申(もう)しておりました - no さん, humble verb, and the title placed before the surname rather than after it. Inside the office, with only colleagues listening, おっしゃいました is right again. Nothing about the boss changed; the audience did.

Some smaller points:

敬語の動詞 · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • いただく does double duty - humble "eat/drink" and humble "receive". The
    context separates them.
  • 存(ぞん)じ上(あ)げる is used about people
    （お名前(なまえ)は存(ぞん)じ上(あ)げております）, and 存(ぞん)じる about facts.
  • 承(うけたまわ)る is heavy formal business language - order-taking, phone
    messages - and is rare in speech otherwise.
  • お伺(うかが)いします is strictly a double layer, but it is so established that
    usage guides let it stand. Do not extend the pattern to new verbs.

And the honest register note: this is the language of work and of service. It is not a politer version of everyday Japanese that you can switch on at will. Using it with friends puts a formal wall between you, and Japanese speakers read that wall as coldness or as mockery.

敬語の動詞 · 召(め)し上(あ)がる vs いただく

Rule of thumb - 召(め)し上(あ)がる is them eating; いただく is you eating. Both mean "eat", and they are never interchangeable.

冷(さ)めないうちに召(め)し上(あ)がってください。
Do please eat before it gets cold.

おいしいお菓子(かし)をいただきました。
I had some lovely cakes.

The commonest single 敬語(けいご) error learners make is turning the honorific on themselves, and this is where it happens most: ❌私(わたし)はもう召(め)し上(あ)がりました. You have just told the room you consider yourself the honoured guest. The word for your own eating is いただきました - the same word as in the いただきます said before a meal.

敬語の動詞 · おっしゃる vs 申(もう)す・申(もう)し上(あ)げる

Rule of thumb - おっしゃる is their speaking; 申(もう)す is yours. 申(もう)し上(あ)げる adds a person you are speaking to and deserves.

先生(せんせい)は何(なに)かおっしゃいましたか。
Did sensei say anything?

はじめまして、鈴木(すずき)と申(もう)します。
How do you do - my name is Suzuki.

Introducing yourself with ❌鈴木(すずき)とおっしゃいます would mean "I am called Suzuki, the honourable gentleman". The reverse error is just as common: asking a customer ❌何(なん)と申(もう)しますか lowers the customer.

敬語の動詞 · 伺(うかが)う vs 参(まい)る

Rule of thumb - both are humble "go/come". 伺(うかが)う requires a person at the destination whom you respect; 参(まい)る does not.

来週(らいしゅう)、先生(せんせい)のお宅(たく)へ伺(うかが)います。
I'll visit your home next week, sensei.

三番(さんばん)線(せん)に電車(でんしゃ)が参(まい)ります。
A train is arriving at platform three.

Announcements, weather reports and formal self-description use 参(まい)る freely; you cannot substitute 伺(うかが)う into any of them, because there is nobody to honour. Note too that 伺(うかが)う has a second job - humble "ask" - so ちょっと伺(うかが)いますが is "may I ask you something?"

敬語の動詞 · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私(わたし)は会議(かいぎ)の資料(しりょう)をご覧(らん)になりました。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は会議(かいぎ)の資料(しりょう)を拝見(はいけん)しました。
    尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) about yourself. Looking at somebody's documents is your own action, so it takes the humble word.

  • ❌ （to a customer）社長(しゃちょう)さんがそうおっしゃいました。 → ✅ 社長(しゃちょう)の佐藤(さとう)がそう申(もう)しておりました。
    The in-group rule. Your own president is lowered in front of an outsider, referred to by bare surname, with the title in front.

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)のお話(はなし)をお聞(き)きになりました。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)のお話(はなし)を伺(うかが)いました。
    Same error with a different verb. When you are the listener, the humble verb is 伺(うかが)う.

敬語の動詞 · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ その方(かた)のお名前(なまえ)は存(ぞん)じております。 → ✅ その方(かた)のお名前(なまえ)は存(ぞん)じ上(あ)げております。
    Knowing a person takes 存(ぞん)じ上(あ)げる; 存(ぞん)じる on its own is for facts and things.

  • ❌ ねえ、もう召(め)し上(あ)がった? → ✅ ねえ、もう食(た)べた?
    Correct grammar, wrong world. Full 敬語(けいご) between friends reads as a joke at the listener's expense.

敬語の動詞 · Practice 1

The person eating is your guest, so the verb must raise them. There is no such form as ❌お食(た)べください, and お召(め)し上(あ)がりください is the fixed extra-polite variant of the same thing.

お○○する

Meaning · the general-purpose humble frame - describes your own action while lowering you

お○○する is the mirror image of お○○になる. Same prefix, same ます-stem, but する on the end instead of になる, and the whole thing points the other way: it lowers the speaker, and by lowering the speaker it raises the person the action is done for. This is 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご), and it is the half of 敬語(けいご) that learners most often leave out.

お○○する · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) お + ます-stem + する 持(も)ちます → お持(も)ちする
Verb (る-verb) お + ます-stem + する 届(とど)けます → お届(とど)けする
する-verb, Chinese-derived root ご + noun + する 説明(せつめい)する → ご説明(せつめい)する
する-verb, native root お + noun + する 手伝(てつだ)いする → お手伝(てつだ)いする
More humble お／ご + stem + いたす お持(も)ちいたします
Most formal (written) お／ご + stem + 申(もう)し上(あ)げる ご案内(あんない)申(もう)し上(あ)げます
Ongoing / standing state お／ご + stem + しております お待(ま)ちしております
Verb with a set humble word frame not used 見(み)る → 拝見(はいけん)する

お○○する · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the frame needs a recipient: somebody the action is done to, for, or in front of, whom you respect. お持(も)ちします works because you carry it for them. ❌お寝(ね)します and ❌お散歩(さんぽ)します do not, because nobody is on the receiving end.

お○○する · Examples

お荷物(にもつ)をお持(も)ちします。
Let me take your bag.

この傘(かさ)をお貸(か)しします。
I'll lend you this umbrella.

使(つか)い方(かた)をご説明(せつめい)します。
I'll explain how it works.

ロビーでお待(ま)ちしております。
I'll be waiting in the lobby.

書類(しょるい)は明日(あした)までにお届(とど)けします。
I'll get the documents to you by tomorrow.

お○○する · Examples (cont.)

少(すこ)しお尋(たず)ねしたいことがあるのですが、よろしいでしょうか。
There's something I'd like to ask you, if I may.

受付(うけつけ)から会場(かいじょう)まで、係(かかり)の者(もの)がご案内(あんない)します。
A member of staff will show you from reception through to the hall.

新(あたら)しいサービスにつきましては、あらためて資料(しりょう)をお送(おく)りしますので、ご確認(かくにん)いただければ幸(さいわ)いです。
I'll send you the material on the new service separately, so I'd be grateful if you would take a look.

お○○する · Nuance & register

Everything here belongs to work, service and formal dealings with strangers. In a shop, on a client call, in a business email it is the normal way to describe what you are about to do; between friends it is not, and お貸(か)ししようか to a classmate sounds like you are role-playing a hotel clerk.

The ladder inside the frame is worth knowing:

  • お送(おく)りします - standard humble. Fine for almost everything.
  • お送(おく)りいたします - いたす is itself the humble する, so this is
    one notch lower and one notch more formal. Common in business speech.
  • お送(おく)り申(もう)し上(あ)げます - ceremonial and written. Notices,
    formal letters, wedding invitations.

お○○する · Nuance & register (cont.)

Treat that as a gradient rather than three fixed rungs: most speakers move between the first two without thinking about it, and the third is rare enough that using it in conversation would draw attention.

しております is the humble ～ている, and it is everywhere in service Japanese because so much of that language describes standing states rather than single acts - お待(ま)ちしております, ご連絡(れんらく)をお待(ま)ちしております, お問(と)い合(あ)わせをお受(う)けしております.

Two limits to keep straight. First, the frame never describes somebody else's action: ❌先生(せんせい)がお話(はな)しします turns your teacher into your servant. Second, an action with no human on the other end will not take it, which is why "I'll go home early today" cannot be ❌早(はや)くお帰(かえ)りします - say 失礼(しつれい)します or 早(はや)く帰(かえ)らせていただきます instead.

お○○する · お○○する vs お○○になる

Rule of thumb - identical up to the last word. する = my action, lowered. になる = their action, raised.

先生(せんせい)に新(あたら)しい住所(じゅうしょ)をお知(し)らせします。
I'll let sensei know my new address.

先生(せんせい)が新(あたら)しい住所(じゅうしょ)をお知(し)らせになりました。
Sensei let us know his new address.

Get the pair backwards and you say the opposite of what you mean. It is worth practising them as a drill: pick a verb, build both, and say out loud who is doing which.

お○○する · お○○する vs ～させていただく

Rule of thumb - お○○する says "I'll do it for you"; ～させていただく says "I'll take the liberty, with your permission".

来週(らいしゅう)の予定(よてい)をご連絡(れんらく)します。
I'll let you know next week's schedule.

本日(ほんじつ)は早(はや)めに閉(し)めさせていただきます。
We'll be closing early today.

させていただく frames the action as something you are permitted to do, so it fits things that affect the listener without being for them - closing early, taking leave, declining. It has spread so far in recent business Japanese that it is regularly criticised as overused; where the action really is a service you are performing, お○○する is the cleaner choice.

お○○する · お○○する vs plain ます

Rule of thumb - ます is 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご) only: polite speech, no humility. お○○する adds the humility.

明日(あした)資料(しりょう)を送(おく)ります。
I'll send the material tomorrow.

明日(あした)資料(しりょう)をお送(おく)りします。
I'll send the material to you tomorrow.

Both are polite; neither is rude. The first is what you write to a colleague, the second to a customer. This is the distinction to keep hold of when people say 敬語(けいご) is "polite Japanese": です／ます politeness and 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) humility are separate dials, and you can turn one without the other.

お○○する · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)が会議(かいぎ)の内容(ないよう)をご説明(せつめい)します。（to a colleague）→ ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)が会議(かいぎ)の内容(ないよう)をご説明(せつめい)になります。
    The frame lowers whoever is doing the verb. Applied to your department head in front of colleagues, it demotes him.

  • ❌ 私(わたし)がお説明(せつめい)します。 → ✅ 私(わたし)がご説明(せつめい)します。
    説明(せつめい) is a Chinese-derived root, so the prefix is ご. Compare ご連絡(れんらく)します, ご報告(ほうこく)します, ご案内(あんない)します.

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)の論文(ろんぶん)をお見(み)しました。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)の論文(ろんぶん)を拝見(はいけん)しました。
    One-mora stems are shut out of the frame, and 見(み)る has its own humble verb.

お○○する · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 今日(きょう)は用事(ようじ)があるので、早(はや)くお帰(かえ)りします。 → ✅ 今日(きょう)は用事(ようじ)があるので、お先(さき)に失礼(しつれい)します。
    Going home is not done for anybody, so there is no recipient for the humility to reach. Worse, お帰(かえ)り is strongly associated with the honorific, so the sentence sounds as if you are honouring yourself.

いたす

Meaning · the humble of する - "to do", said about your own action

いたす is 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご). It replaces する when the person doing the verb is you, or anyone on your side of the group boundary, and by lowering that person it raises whoever you are speaking to. It is the direct opposite number of なさる, and between them the two cover every する verb in the language.

いたす · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
する いたす する → いたす
Noun + する Noun + いたす 失礼(しつれい)する → 失礼(しつれい)いたす
Noun + する, with prefix ご／お + Noun + いたす 案内(あんない)する → ご案内(あんない)いたす
Verb (humble frame) お + ます-stem + いたす 届(とど)けます → お届(とど)けいたす
～ている いたしております 準備(じゅんび)している → 準備(じゅんび)いたしております

Unlike なさる and いらっしゃる, いたす is a completely regular う-verb:

いたす · Formation (cont.)

Form Shape
Polite いたします
Past polite いたしました
Negative polite いたしません
Plain negative いたさない
て-form いたして／いたしまして

Note - in practice you will almost never meet the plain form in speech. いたす lives in the ます-form; a plain いたす turns up mainly in written notices and in fixed compounds.

いたす · Examples

その仕事(しごと)は私(わたし)がいたします。
I'll take care of that job.

失礼(しつれい)いたします。
Excuse me. / I'll be on my way.

どういたしまして。
Not at all - you're very welcome.

準備(じゅんび)は私(わたし)どもがいたしますので、ご安心(あんしん)ください。
We'll handle the preparations, so please don't worry.

ただいま担当(たんとう)の者(もの)が確認(かくにん)いたします。
Someone from the relevant team will check that right now.

いたす · Examples (cont.)

承知(しょうち)いたしました。すぐに手配(てはい)いたします。
Understood. I'll arrange it straight away.

本日(ほんじつ)は貴重(きちょう)なお時間(じかん)をいただき、感謝(かんしゃ)いたします。
Thank you for giving us your valuable time today.

来週(らいしゅう)の打(う)ち合(あ)わせにつきましては、あらためてメールでご連絡(れんらく)いたします。
As for next week's meeting, I'll be in touch again by email.

いたす · Nuance & register

いたします is one of the most-used words in Japanese working life, largely because it is welded into a handful of phrases you will say every day:

  • よろしくお願(ねが)いいたします - closes almost every business email.
  • 失礼(しつれい)いたします - entering a room, leaving a room, ending a call.
  • 承知(しょうち)いたしました - "understood", to a customer or a superior.
  • どういたしまして - a frozen phrase built on this verb, used to wave away
    thanks.

There is a useful register ordering for acknowledging an instruction, though it is a tendency rather than a rule: かしこまりました is the most deferential and belongs to service staff, 承知(しょうち)いたしました is the business standard, わかりました is neutral, and 了解(りょうかい)しました is felt by many people to be too casual to send upward. Plenty of workplaces use 了解(りょうかい)です internally without anyone minding; to a client it is worth avoiding.

いたす · Nuance & register (cont.)

いたす also has a slightly different flavour depending on what it is attached to. In ご連絡(れんらく)いたします it is aimed at a specific person you are serving. In 失礼(しつれい)いたします or 開店(かいてん)時間(じかん)を変更(へんこう)いたしました it simply makes the statement formal and self-effacing, with nobody in particular being honoured - the same job that 参(まい)る and 申(もう)す do. Both uses are humble; only the second works in an announcement.

Finally, the limit that defines the word: いたす never describes somebody else's action when you are honouring them. ❌先生(せんせい)が説明(せつめい)いたしました lowers your teacher. The exception is your own in-group in front of an outsider, where lowering is exactly right: 部長(ぶちょう)の中村(なかむら)がご説明(せつめい)いたします.

いたす · いたす vs なさる

Rule of thumb - いたす for what I do, なさる for what you or they do. One verb, two directions.

お席(せき)はどちらになさいますか。
Which table would you like?

それでは、三十分(さんじゅっぷん)ほどでお持(も)ちいたします。
In that case, I'll bring it to you in about thirty minutes.

One restaurant exchange, two verbs. Everything the customer does takes なさる; everything the staff do takes いたす. A single conversation swings between them constantly, and the swing follows the subject of each verb, not the overall tone of the conversation.

いたす · いたす vs する

Rule of thumb - します is polite speech. いたします is polite speech plus humility. They are not two levels of the same dial.

明日(あした)の朝(あさ)、電話(でんわ)します。
I'll phone tomorrow morning.

明日(あした)の朝(あさ)、お電話(でんわ)いたします。
I'll telephone you tomorrow morning.

The first is perfectly polite and entirely normal to a colleague or a friend's parent. The second lowers you in front of the listener and belongs to a customer, a client or a professor. Reaching for いたします with a friend does not make you sound respectful; it makes you sound like a call centre.

いたす · お○○いたす vs お○○する

Rule of thumb - both are humble. いたす is one notch more formal, because the humble frame and the humble verb are working together.

資料(しりょう)をお渡(わた)しします。
I'll hand you the material.

資料(しりょう)をお渡(わた)しいたします。
I'll hand you the material.

Strictly this doubles up, but it is long-established and nobody objects to it - unlike ❌お読(よ)みになられる on the honorific side, which does draw comment. Use お○○します in ordinary business speech and お○○いたします when you are being formal: a first meeting, a customer, a public announcement.

いたす · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)が説明(せつめい)いたしました。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)が説明(せつめい)なさいました。
    A humble verb applied to the person you mean to honour. It says your teacher demeaned himself in order to explain.

  • ❌ お客(きゃく)様(さま)、どういたしますか。 → ✅ お客(きゃく)様(さま)、どうなさいますか。
    The customer is doing the deciding, so the verb must raise. どういたしましょうか is fine, because there the speaker is offering to act.

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、会議(かいぎ)に出席(しゅっせき)いたしますか。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、会議(かいぎ)に出席(しゅっせき)なさいますか。
    Inside the company your department head is above you and takes 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご). Only in front of an outsider does he get lowered.

いたす · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ （to a client）了解(りょうかい)しました。 → ✅ 承知(しょうち)いたしました。
    Not a grammar error, a register one. 了解(りょうかい) reads as flat and slightly casual coming from the serving side of a business relationship.

いたす · Practice 1

1. ご質問(しつもん)には私(わたし)どもが責任(せきにん)をもってお答(こた)え____。

ご質問(しつもん)には私(わたし)どもが責任(せきにん)をもってお答(こた)えいたします。

The subject is 私(わたし)ども, so the verb is humble. お + ます-stem + いたす is the more formal of the two humble frames, which suits a written undertaking like this one. お答(こた)えします would also be correct.

いたす · Practice 2

2. Choose: 社長(しゃちょう)は明日(あした)大阪(おおさか)へ（出張(しゅっちょう)いたします・出張(しゅっちょう)なさいます） - said to a colleague.

社長(しゃちょう)は明日(あした)大阪(おおさか)へ出張(しゅっちょう)なさいます。

The listener is inside the company, so the president is honoured normally. 出張(しゅっちょう)いたします would lower him. Said to a client, however, the humble version becomes correct - and he would lose his さん too.

いたす · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, as an email to a client: "I'll send you the details tomorrow."

詳(くわ)しい内容(ないよう)は明日(あした)ご連絡(れんらく)いたします。

連絡(れんらく) is a Chinese-derived root, so it takes ご, and the humble いたします replaces します because the sender is the one acting. お知(し)らせいたします is an equally good alternative.

いたす · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 恐(おそ)れ入(い)りますが、こちらの用紙(ようし)にご記入(きにゅう)いたしてください。

✅ 恐(おそ)れ入(い)りますが、こちらの用紙(ようし)にご記入(きにゅう)ください。

A request tells the listener to act, so it cannot contain a humble verb - ご記入(きにゅう)いたしてください orders them to lower themselves. Drop the いたして and the honorific request form appears on its own.

いたす · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 明日(あした)伺(うかが)います。 (b) 明日(あした)訪問(ほうもん)いたします。

Both are humble, and either would be understood as "I'll visit you tomorrow". (a) uses the dedicated humble verb for visiting a person you respect. It is warmer and more personal, and it implies the visit is to you.

(b) is built on a Chinese-derived noun plus いたす, which makes it flatter and more procedural - the register of a written schedule or a formal notification. Neither is more polite than the other; (a) sounds like a person speaking, (b) like a company writing.

ございます

Meaning · the very polite form of ある - "there is", "we have"

ございます does the same work as あります, one register higher. It is 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご): it lifts the tone of your speech, not the subject of the sentence. That is why you can use it about the listener's questions and about your own shop's stock without contradicting yourself - nobody is being raised or lowered, only the language.

ございます · Formation

Attaches to Form Example

ない ございません 席(せき)がない → 席(せき)がございません
Past ございました／ございませんでした 連絡(れんらく)がございました
Conditional ございましたら ご希望(きぼう)がございましたら

Noun + です でございます 受付(うけつけ)でございます

The い-adjective pattern is worth seeing once, because three of the commonest greetings in the language come out of it:

Adjective く-form Sound change Result
ありがたい ありがたく ～あく → ～おう ありがとうございます
おめでたい おめでたく ～あく → ～おう おめでとうございます

ございます · Examples

お手洗(てあら)いは二階(にかい)にございます。
The washrooms are on the second floor.

時間(じかん)はまだございます。
There is still time.

ご質問(しつもん)はございませんか。
Are there any questions?

あいにく在庫(ざいこ)がございません。
I'm afraid we're out of stock.

会員(かいいん)の方(かた)には特別(とくべつ)な割引(わりびき)がございます。
There is a special discount for members.

ございます · Examples (cont.)

恐(おそ)れ入(い)りますが、喫煙(きつえん)できるお席(せき)はございません。
I'm sorry, but we have no seats where smoking is allowed.

何(なに)かご要望(ようぼう)がございましたら、フロントまでお申(もう)し付(つ)けください。
If there is anything you need, please let the front desk know.

車内(しゃない)にお忘(わす)れ物(もの)のございませんよう、ご注意(ちゅうい)ください。
Please take care not to leave anything behind on the train.

ございます · Nuance & register

This is service language above all. Shops, hotels, restaurants, station announcements, formal speeches and business correspondence run on ございます; a conversation between friends does not. Saying ❌冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)にビールがございます to a flatmate is not politer, it is theatrical.

Because it is 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご) and not 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご), ございます never honours anybody by itself. That has one practical consequence worth knowing: strictly, asking about something the listener possesses calls for the honorific おありです - ご質問(しつもん)はおありでしょうか. In real service Japanese, ご質問(しつもん)はございませんか is what you will hear almost every time, and it is not treated as an error. Use whichever you like; recognise both.

Three details:

ございます · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ありがとうございます／ありがとうございました. The present form thanks for
    something still going on or just now done; the past form closes it off. A shop says ありがとうございました as you leave with the bag.
  • 申(もう)し訳(わけ)ございません is the standard business apology. A
    minority of usage guides object to it on the grounds that 申(もう)し訳(わけ)ない is a single adjective that should not be split, and prefer 申(もう)し訳(わけ)なく存(ぞん)じます. In practice the objection has lost; you will hear ございません everywhere, and using it will not mark you.
  • The い-adjective form is dated. よろしゅうございます, うれしゅうございます
    and 大(おお)きゅうございます are grammatical, but they belong to older, very formal or self-consciously refined speech. The frozen greetings survive; the productive pattern has largely gone.

ございます · ございます vs あります

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different altitude. あります is polite; ございます is polite and formal, and it announces that you are in a service or ceremonial situation.

地下(ちか)に駐車場(ちゅうしゃじょう)があります。
There's a car park in the basement.

地下(ちか)に駐車場(ちゅうしゃじょう)がございます。
There is a car park in the basement.

Nothing about the car park has changed. The second sentence tells you the speaker is behind a counter. Reach for あります in ordinary conversation, in class and with colleagues, and switch to ございます when you are serving somebody or speaking formally to a group.

ございます · ございます vs いらっしゃいます

Rule of thumb - ございます is for things and facts; いらっしゃいます is for people you are honouring.

受付(うけつけ)に案内図(あんないず)がございます。
There is a floor plan at reception.

受付(うけつけ)に係(かかり)の方(かた)がいらっしゃいます。
There is a member of staff at reception.

The mistake goes one way: learners reach for ございます about a person because it feels like the most polite word available. It is not more polite about a person - it is wrong, because it treats them as an object. Note that your own side does take a non-honorific verb, but the word is おります, not ございます: 担当(たんとう)の者(もの)が受付(うけつけ)におります.

ございます · ございます vs でございます

Rule of thumb - ございます replaces あります; でございます replaces です. Existence versus identity.

三階(さんがい)に喫茶店(きっさてん)がございます。
There is a coffee shop on the third floor.

こちらが喫茶店(きっさてん)でございます。
This is the coffee shop.

The で is the giveaway. If the English is "there is a…" or "we have a…", you want the bare form; if it is "X is Y", you want でございます. Mixing them produces ❌こちらが喫茶店(きっさてん)ございます, which is simply ungrammatical.

ございます · Common mistakes

  • ❌ ご用件(ようけん)は何(なに)かござりますか。 → ✅ ご用件(ようけん)は何(なに)かございますか。
    The り becomes い before ます - the same irregularity as いらっしゃいます, おっしゃいます, なさいます and くださいます.

  • ❌ お客(きゃく)様(さま)は二階(にかい)にございます。 → ✅ お客(きゃく)様(さま)は二階(にかい)にいらっしゃいます。
    A person is not a thing. ございます about a customer sounds as though they are stock on a shelf.

  • ❌ ねえ、消(け)しゴムございます? → ✅ ねえ、消(け)しゴムある?
    A register error rather than a grammar one, and the kind that makes people laugh. Service Japanese aimed at a classmate is comic.

  • ❌ ありがとうでございます。 → ✅ ありがとうございます。
    ございます follows the adjective form directly. The で belongs only after a noun.

ございます · Practice 1

1. 申(もう)し訳(わけ)ありません、本日(ほんじつ)のチケットは売(う)り切(き)れて席(せき)が____。

申(もう)し訳(わけ)ありません、本日(ほんじつ)のチケットは売(う)り切(き)れて席(せき)がございません。

The negative of ございます is ございません - there is no ❌ございないです. The whole sentence is service register, so ありません would sit a step lower than the rest of it.

ございます · Practice 2

2. Choose: 田中(たなか)様(さま)は会議室(かいぎしつ)に（ございます・いらっしゃいます）。

田中(たなか)様(さま)は会議室(かいぎしつ)にいらっしゃいます。

ございます is the polite form of ある, which is used for things. A person you are honouring takes the honorific of いる. If 田中(たなか) were your own colleague and you were speaking to a client, the answer would change again - to おります.

ございます · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, as a hotel receptionist: "There is a convenience store next door."

隣(となり)にコンビニがございます。

The subject is a shop, so the verb of existence is ある, raised to service register as ございます. Note that no honorific prefix is needed on コンビニ - loanwords do not take one.

ございます · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: こちらが新(あたら)しいパンフレットございます。

✅ こちらが新(あたら)しいパンフレットでございます。

This sentence identifies one thing as another, so it needs the polite copula, not the polite verb of existence. Without the で there is nothing linking the noun to the sentence at all.

ございます · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) ご意見(いけん)はありませんか。 (b) ご意見(いけん)はございませんか。

Both are polite and both are correct. (a) is ordinary polite Japanese - a chair asking a familiar committee, or a teacher asking a class. (b) lifts the whole utterance into formal register, and suits a public meeting, a shareholders' briefing or a customer.

Neither honours the listener grammatically, since ございます is 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご). If you want to raise the listener as well, the honorific of ある is おありです: ご意見(いけん)はおありでしょうか. That is the strictly correct form, though (b) is far more often heard.

でございます

Meaning · the formal copula - "is", at the register of a hotel desk or a station announcement

でございます replaces です. Like ございます, it is 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご): it raises the formality of the sentence without honouring anybody in it. That is why you can say 山田(やまだ)でございます about yourself, and why you must not say it about the person you are talking to.

でございます · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + でございます 受付(うけつけ)です → 受付(うけつけ)でございます
な-adjective な-adj + でございます 結構(けっこう)です → 結構(けっこう)でございます
Negative ではございません ちがいます → ではございません
Past でございました 以上(いじょう)でございました
Tentative でございましょう そのとおりでございましょう
い-adjective frame not used よろしいです → よろしゅうございます
About a person you honour でいらっしゃいます 社長(しゃちょう)でいらっしゃいます

でございます · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - でございます for things, places, prices, facts, and for yourself. でいらっしゃいます for the listener and for anyone else you are raising.

でございます · Examples

こちらが会議室(かいぎしつ)でございます。
This is the meeting room.

はい、山田(やまだ)でございます。
Yes, Yamada speaking.

お会計(かいけい)は三千(さんぜん)円(えん)でございます。
That comes to three thousand yen.

次(つぎ)は終点(しゅうてん)、東京(とうきょう)でございます。
The next stop is the last one, Tokyo.

こちらのお席(せき)はすべて禁煙(きんえん)でございます。
All of the seats in this section are non-smoking.

でございます · Examples (cont.)

申(もう)し訳(わけ)ございませんが、その商品(しょうひん)は当店(とうてん)の取(と)り扱(あつか)いではございません。
I'm sorry, but that isn't a product we carry.

担当(たんとう)の者(もの)は本日(ほんじつ)休(やす)みでございますので、明日(あした)あらためてご連絡(れんらく)いたします。
The person handling this is off today, so we'll be in touch again tomorrow.

本日(ほんじつ)はお忙(いそが)しいところお越(こ)しいただき、ありがとうございます。司会(しかい)を務(つと)めます佐藤(さとう)でございます。
Thank you for coming today despite your busy schedules. I'm Sato, and I'll be chairing.

でございます · Nuance & register

Announcements, counters, telephones and formal speeches. In a shop or a hotel it is the default copula and nobody notices it; in a conversation with a friend it would be as odd as answering the door in a uniform. Between those extremes, a business meeting will use it for set pieces - introductions, figures, conclusions - and drop back to です for ordinary discussion.

The single most useful thing to keep straight is that でございます does not honour. Saying ❌田中(たなか)様(さま)でございますか to a visitor is a real error, not merely a stiff one: it equates the guest with a price or a room number. The honorific copula is でいらっしゃいます, and it is used the same way for descriptions: お元気(げんき)でいらっしゃいますか, 社長(しゃちょう)の奥様(おくさま)でいらっしゃいます.

Some notes on the edges:

でございます · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • Negatives. ではございません is the formal negative. In quick speech people
    reduce it to じゃございません, which is a mixed register and is best avoided in writing.
  • い-adjectives. There is no ❌よろしいでございますか. The forms that exist are
    よろしゅうございますか, which sounds old-fashioned, and よろしいでしょうか, which is what you should actually say.
  • Placement. でございます closes the sentence. Inside a sentence you use the
    て-form でございまして, which is heavy and almost entirely a written or speech-making form.
  • Do not stack it on 敬語(けいご) you have already used.
    ご覧(らん)になるお品(しな)でございます is fine, but ❌ご覧(らん)になられるお品(しな)でございます piles two honorific layers under the formal copula.

でございます · でございます vs です

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different height. です is polite; でございます is polite and formal, and it signals a service or ceremonial setting.

これは去年(きょねん)の資料(しりょう)です。
This is last year's material.

こちらが昨年度(さくねんど)の資料(しりょう)でございます。
This is last year's material.

Notice how much moves with the copula. これ becomes こちら, 去年(きょねん) becomes 昨年度(さくねんど). Register is carried by the whole sentence, and a lone でございます on top of casual vocabulary sounds like a joke.

でございます · でございます vs でいらっしゃいます

Rule of thumb - でございます about yourself and about things; でいらっしゃいます about the person you are honouring. Getting this backwards is the classic 敬語(けいご) error.

失礼(しつれい)ですが、ご予約(よやく)の方(かた)でいらっしゃいますか。
Excuse me - do you have a reservation with us?

受付(うけつけ)の木村(きむら)でございます。
I'm Kimura from reception.

The pair shows the whole three-way system in two lines. The first raises the guest; the second simply formalises a statement about the speaker. Swap them and you have honoured yourself while treating the guest as an object - which is why ❌私(わたし)が担当(たんとう)でいらっしゃいます is such a jarring sentence.

でございます · でございます vs ～になります

Rule of thumb - になる means "becomes". For "is", the formal copula is でございます.

こちらがお飲(の)み物(もの)のメニューでございます。
Here is the drinks menu.

合計(ごうけい)で二千(にせん)円(えん)でございます。
That's two thousand yen altogether.

You will hear こちらメニューになります and 二千(にせん)円(えん)になります constantly in Japanese shops. It is common enough to have its own nickname - マニュアル敬語(けいご), "manual keigo" - and a large number of speakers consider it wrong, on the grounds that nothing is turning into anything. になります is correct when there really is a change （四月(しがつ)から新(あたら)しい料金(りょうきん)になります）. For a plain identification, でございます is the safe choice.

でございます · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 田中(たなか)様(さま)でございますか。 → ✅ 田中(たなか)様(さま)でいらっしゃいますか。
    でございます does not raise anybody. Asked of a guest, it puts them in the same grammatical box as the room key.

  • ❌ 私(わたし)は鈴木(すずき)でいらっしゃいます。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は鈴木(すずき)でございます。
    The mirror error, and the one learners make when they decide でいらっしゃいます is simply "the politest version".

  • ❌ お席(せき)はこちらでよろしいでございますか。 → ✅ お席(せき)はこちらでよろしいでしょうか。
    An い-adjective cannot take でございます. Either use でしょうか, or the old-fashioned よろしゅうございますか.

  • ❌ こちら、お釣(つ)りになります。 → ✅ こちら、お釣(つ)りでございます。
    The change is nothing to do with becoming. Very widely heard, and very widely disliked.

でございます · Practice 1

1. お電話(でんわ)ありがとうございます。山(やま)の上(うえ)ホテル、予約(よやく)係(がかり)____。

お電話(でんわ)ありがとうございます。山(やま)の上(うえ)ホテル、予約(よやく)係(がかり)でございます。

You are identifying yourself and your own department, so the formal copula is right. でいらっしゃいます here would honour yourself, which is the one thing 敬語(けいご) never lets you do.

でございます · Practice 2

2. Choose: 失礼(しつれい)ですが、部長(ぶちょう)の奥様(おくさま)（でございます・でいらっしゃいます）か。

失礼(しつれい)ですが、部長(ぶちょう)の奥様(おくさま)でいらっしゃいますか。

The sentence identifies the person in front of you, so it needs the honorific copula. でございます would be merely formal, and asking a guest ❌奥様(おくさま)でございますか sounds as though you are reading a label.

でございます · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, as a shop assistant: "This one is this year's newest model."

こちらが今年(ことし)の最新(さいしん)モデルでございます。

A product is a thing, so the formal copula is the right choice, and こちら rather than これ keeps the whole sentence at the same height. ❌モデルになります would say the item is turning into a model.

でございます · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 田中(たなか)様(さま)、こちらがご注文(ちゅうもん)の品(しな)になります。合計(ごうけい)で千五百(せんごひゃく)円(えん)になります。

✅ 田中(たなか)様(さま)、こちらがご注文(ちゅうもん)の品(しな)でございます。合計(ごうけい)で千五百(せんごひゃく)円(えん)でございます。

Neither the goods nor the total is becoming anything, so になります has no work to do. Both clauses are plain identifications and take the formal copula.

でございます · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 受付(うけつけ)は一階(いっかい)です。 (b) 受付(うけつけ)は一階(いっかい)でございます。
