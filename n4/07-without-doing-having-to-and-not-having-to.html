～ないで

Meaning · without doing something - and, in speech, a bare "don't"

～ないで is the negative て-form of a verb. Like the ordinary て-form it hooks one clause onto the next, but it hooks on a non-event: the thing that did not happen while the main action went ahead. You met it at N5 inside ～ないでください; that で was never decoration, and the form stands on its own.

～ないで · Formation

Build the ない-form, then add で. Nothing else changes.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ない-form + で 急(いそ)ぐ → 急(いそ)がないで
Verb (る-verb) ない-form + で 閉(し)める → 閉(し)めないで
Verb (する) しないで 運動(うんどう)する → 運動(うんどう)しないで
Verb (来(く)る) 来(こ)ないで 来(く)る → 来(こ)ないで
Written / formal equivalent ない-form − ない + ずに 急(いそ)ぐ → 急(いそ)がずに
With a following ending ないで + ください／ほしい／くれる 急(いそ)がないでください
い-adjective not available - use くなくて 高(たか)い → ❌高(たか)くないで
な-adjective / Noun not available - use でなくて 静(しず)かだ → ❌静(しず)かじゃないで

～ないで · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ないで is verb-only. If the negated word is an adjective or a noun, the joining form is なくて, and no で version exists.

Note - the same string does three jobs, and context separates them cleanly. (1) Without doing: 地図(ちず)を見(み)ないで歩(ある)いた. (2) Instead of doing: 勉強(べんきょう)しないでゲームをした. (3) A negative request, with ください or with nothing at all: 開(あ)けないでください／開(あ)けないで.

～ないで · Examples

けさは食(た)べないで家(いえ)を出(で)た。
I left the house this morning without eating.

何(なに)も買(か)わないで帰(かえ)りました。
I came home without buying anything.

靴(くつ)を脱(ぬ)がないで入(はい)ってください。
Please come in without taking your shoes off.

辞書(じしょ)を使(つか)わないで読(よ)んでみましょう。
Let's try reading it without using a dictionary.

日曜日(にちようび)はどこにも行(い)かないで、一日中(いちにちじゅう)ゲームをしていた。
On Sunday I didn't go anywhere - I played games all day.

～ないで · Examples (cont.)

お願(ねが)い、この話(はなし)はまだ言(い)わないでね。
Please - don't tell anyone about this yet.

昨日(きのう)は疲(つか)れていたので、晩(ばん)ごはんも作(つく)らないでそのまま寝(ね)てしまいました。
I was so tired yesterday that I fell asleep without even making dinner.

説明書(せつめいしょ)を読(よ)まないで組(く)み立(た)てたら、ねじが二本(にほん)余(あま)ってしまった。
I assembled it without reading the instructions and ended up with two screws left over.

～ないで · Nuance & register

The joining ないで is register-neutral. It appears in casual speech and in ordinary polite writing alike, and the politeness of the sentence is decided at the end, by the main verb: 傘(かさ)を持(も)たないで出(で)かけた and 傘(かさ)を持(も)たないで出(で)かけました differ only there.

The request use is different, and it is the one to watch. Bare ないで with nothing after it - 見(み)ないで, 行(い)かないで - is casual and emotionally direct. It is what you say to a friend, a partner, a child or a pet, and it can sound pleading rather than commanding. ね softens it further (忘(わす)れないでね); よ presses. Said to a stranger or a superior it is far too blunt, and you want ないでください at minimum, or ないでいただけますか if you are genuinely asking a favour.

Two neighbouring endings live on the same stem and are worth having ready: ～ないでほしい「I'd rather you didn't」, which reports a wish instead of issuing an order, and ～ないでくれる？／～ないでくれない？「could you not…?」, casual but softer than the bare form because it is a question.

～ないで · Nuance & register (cont.)

In writing - reports, instructions, signage, anything with a formal voice - the joining ないで is routinely replaced by ずに. That swap is not optional in a stiff register; 確認(かくにん)しないで送(おく)りました in a written apology reads as spoken language.

～ないで · ～ないで vs ～ずに

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register. ないで is spoken and neutral; ずに is written and formal. Only ずに has an irregular form: する becomes せずに, never ❌しずに.

ずに is a survival from classical Japanese, and it attaches to the same negative stem: 書(か)かない → 書(か)かずに, 食(た)べない → 食(た)べずに, 来(く)る → 来(こ)ずに.

彼(かれ)は何(なに)も言(い)わずに部屋(へや)を出(で)て行(い)った。
He left the room without saying a word.

無理(むり)をせずにゆっくり休(やす)んでください。
Please rest properly and don't push yourself.

～ないで · ～ないで vs ～ずに (cont.)

Two limits on ずに. It cannot carry a request: there is no ❌見(み)ずにください - requests are ないで territory. And the instead of reading is weaker with ずに; for that sense ないで is the natural choice in speech.

You will also meet bare ず with no に, joining clauses in written Japanese: 誰(だれ)にも相談(そうだん)せず、一人(ひとり)で決(き)めた. Treat it as the same word wearing its most formal clothes.

～ないで · ～ないで vs ～なくて

Rule of thumb - ないで tells you how the next thing happened; なくて tells you why it happened.

朝(あさ)ごはんを食(た)べないで会社(かいしゃ)へ行(い)った。
I went to work without eating breakfast.

朝(あさ)ごはんを食(た)べなくて、おなかがすいた。
I didn't eat breakfast, so I got hungry.

The first sentence is about the manner of going; the second gives the cause of the hunger. The practical consequence is that なくて cannot introduce a request or a command - ❌食(た)べなくてください is not Japanese - and that a ないで clause must be followed by something the same subject deliberately did. This distinction has its own page at this level; it repays the time.

～ないで · ～ないで vs ～ないように

Rule of thumb - ないで is without doing it; ないように is so that it does not happen. One reports, the other aims.

窓(まど)を閉(し)めないで出(で)かけた。
I went out without closing the window.

風邪(かぜ)をひかないように、上着(うわぎ)を持(も)って行(い)きます。
I'll take a jacket so I don't catch a cold.

The difference matters most in requests. 遅(おく)れないでください is a flat "please don't be late"; 遅(おく)れないようにしてください is "please make sure you're not late", which frames the same content as advice and lands far more gently. ように also survives being said upward, where the bare negative request does not.

～ないで · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 見(み)ないて → ✅ 見(み)ないで
    The negative て-form is ないで. There is no form ❌ないて anywhere in the language.

  • ❌ 静(しず)かじゃないで集中(しゅうちゅう)できない。 → ✅ 静(しず)かじゃなくて集中(しゅうちゅう)できない。
    ないで attaches only to verbs. Adjectives and nouns join with なくて / でなくて.

  • ❌ 準備(じゅんび)をしずに出(で)かけた。 → ✅ 準備(じゅんび)をせずに出(で)かけた。
    する is irregular before ずに: the stem is せ, not し.

  • ❌ 朝(あさ)ごはんを食(た)べなくて出(で)かけた。 → ✅ 朝(あさ)ごはんを食(た)べないで出(で)かけた。
    Going out is not caused by not eating. When the negative describes the manner of the main action, it must be ないで.

～ないで · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、そこに座(すわ)らないで。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、そちらには座(すわ)らないでください。
    Bare ないで is intimate and blunt. Upward it needs ください, or better, a question form.

～ないで · Practice 1

1. 傘(かさ)を持(も)た____出(で)かけたので、ぬれてしまった。

傘(かさ)を持(も)たないで出(で)かけたので、ぬれてしまった。

Not taking an umbrella is the manner in which the going-out happened, so the joining form is ないで. Writing 持(も)たなくて would claim that going out was caused by the lack of an umbrella.

～ないで · Practice 2

2. 会議(かいぎ)の資料(しりょう)を確認(かくにん)（しないで・しなくて）送(おく)ってしまいました。

資料(しりょう)を確認(かくにん)しないで送(おく)ってしまいました。

The sending is the main event and the not-checking describes how it was done - carelessly, in one motion. しなくて would need a consequence after it, such as 確認(かくにん)しなくて、あとで困(こま)った.

～ないで · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, in a formal written register: "He signed the contract without reading it."

彼(かれ)は契約書(けいやくしょ)を読(よ)まずにサインした。

ずに is the written counterpart of ないで, and a sentence about contracts is exactly where it belongs. 読(よ)まないで is not wrong, but it sounds spoken.

～ないで · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昨日(きのう)は宿題(しゅくだい)をしずにねてしまった。

✅ 昨日(きのう)は宿題(しゅくだい)をせずに寝(ね)てしまった。

The ず ending takes an irregular stem from する - せ. Every other verb uses its ordinary ない-stem, which is why this one is so easy to get wrong.

～ないで · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 電気(でんき)を消(け)さないで。 (b) 電気(でんき)を消(け)さないでください。

(a) is bare and intimate. With no ending after it, it reads as an immediate, emotional "don't!" - the kind of thing said to a family member or a close friend, and depending on the tone it can sound closer to pleading than to ordering.

(b) adds the standard polite request ending. It is neutral, usable with a colleague or a stranger, and it sounds like an instruction rather than a reaction. Between the two sit 消(け)さないでね (softer) and 消(け)さないでくれる？ (casual but framed as a question).

～ないで vs ～なくて

Meaning · the two negative て-forms - without doing versus because … not

Japanese has one positive て-form and two negative ones. ～ないで joins a non-event to an action and says how that action happened. ～なくて joins a non-event to a consequence and says why it happened. They are not stylistic variants, and swapping them usually produces a sentence that means nothing.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Formation

Both start from the ない-form. The split is in the ending, and in what the word being negated is.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb → without doing ない-form + で 言(い)う → 言(い)わないで
Verb → because not ない-form − い + くて 言(い)う → 言(い)わなくて
Verb (する) しないで／しなくて 練習(れんしゅう)する → 練習(れんしゅう)しないで／しなくて
Verb (来(く)る) 来(こ)ないで／来(こ)なくて 来(く)る → 来(こ)ないで／来(こ)なくて
い-adjective ～くない → ～くなくて (no ないで) 安(やす)い → 安(やす)くなくて
な-adjective じゃない → じゃなくて (no ないで) 便利(べんり)だ → 便利(べんり)じゃなくて
Noun じゃない → じゃなくて (no ないで) 学生(がくせい) → 学生(がくせい)じゃなくて
Written equivalent of ないで ない-form − ない + ずに 言(い)う → 言(い)わずに

～ないで vs ～なくて · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - なくて can negate anything; ないで only ever negates a verb. So if the word before the blank is an adjective or a noun, the answer is なくて before you have thought about meaning at all.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Examples

上着(うわぎ)を着(き)ないで外(そと)に出(で)た。
I went outside without putting a jacket on.

返事(へんじ)が来(こ)なくて、心配(しんぱい)です。
No reply has come, and I'm worried.

席(せき)を立(た)たないで待(ま)っていてください。
Please wait without leaving your seat.

意味(いみ)がわからなくて、先生(せんせい)に聞(き)きました。
I didn't understand what it meant, so I asked the teacher.

息子(むすこ)は野菜(やさい)を食(た)べないで肉(にく)ばかり食(た)べる。
My son leaves the vegetables and eats nothing but meat.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Examples (cont.)

部屋(へや)が広(ひろ)くなくて、ベッドが入(はい)りませんでした。
The room wasn't big enough, so the bed wouldn't fit.

原因(げんいん)は雨(あめ)じゃなくて、工事(こうじ)でした。
The cause wasn't the rain - it was the roadworks.

電車(でんしゃ)が動(うご)かなくて、面接(めんせつ)に三十分(さんじゅっぷん)も遅(おく)れてしまいました。
The trains weren't running, and I ended up half an hour late for my interview.

誰(だれ)にも相談(そうだん)しないで会社(かいしゃ)を辞(や)めたので、親(おや)にひどく怒(おこ)られました。
I quit my job without talking to anyone about it, and my parents were furious.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Nuance & register

Neither form is marked for politeness. Both sit in the middle of the sentence, and the ending decides the register.

What なくて is marked for is control. Causal なくて is at its most natural when the thing that failed to happen was outside the speaker's hands - the bus, the weather, an email, your own memory or ability: 見(み)つからなくて, 間(ま)に合(あ)わなくて, 来(こ)なくて, 思(おも)い出(だ)せなくて. When the non-action was a choice you made, native speakers reach for なかったので／なかったから instead: 勉強(べんきょう)しなかったので、試験(しけん)に落(お)ちた rather than ❌勉強(べんきょう)しなくて、試験(しけん)に落(お)ちた.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Nuance & register (cont.)

The other half of that restriction is what comes after なくて. It wants a state, a feeling or an involuntary result - 困(こま)った, 寂(さび)しい, 安心(あんしん)した, よかった, 大変(たいへん)だった. It does not want a deliberate next action, and it flatly cannot take a request, an invitation or an intention. That single fact resolves most of the exam questions on this pair.

Two frozen expressions are worth memorising whole, because you will use them constantly and both take なくて: 来(こ)られなくてすみません「sorry I couldn't come」 and 大(おお)きい事故(じこ)にならなくてよかった「thank goodness it wasn't a serious accident」.

Finally, ないで has a written twin, ずに, and なくて does not. In a formal report 確認(かくにん)せずに replaces 確認(かくにん)しないで, while a causal なくて in the same document would normally be rewritten as ないため - 届(とど)かないため、再送(さいそう)いたします.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Manner vs cause

Rule of thumb - ask what the second clause is. An action performed → ないで. A result suffered → なくて.

めがねをかけないで運転(うんてん)した。
I drove without wearing my glasses.

めがねがなくて運転(うんてん)できなかった。
I didn't have my glasses, so I couldn't drive.

In the first, the driving happened and the not-wearing describes the state I was in while doing it - one event, described two ways. In the second there are two facts in a chain: no glasses, therefore no driving. English blurs this because both come out as "without" or "didn't … so"; Japanese does not.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Manner vs cause (cont.)

A useful test: if you can insert 「そのまま」 or 「その状態(じょうたい)で」 before the main verb and the sentence still works, you want ないで.

～ないで vs ～なくて · What can follow each

Rule of thumb - requests, commands, invitations and intentions can only follow ないで. なくて is followed by things that happen to you.

砂糖(さとう)を入(い)れないでください。
Please don't put sugar in it.

砂糖(さとう)が入(はい)っていなくて、安心(あんしん)しました。
There was no sugar in it, which was a relief.

There is no ❌入(い)れなくてください, no ❌行(い)かなくて、帰(かえ)ろう, no ❌待(ま)たなくて、先(さき)に始(はじ)めます. If the sentence ends in ください, ましょう, つもり, たい or an imperative, the negative in front of it is ないで.

～ないで vs ～なくて · What can follow each (cont.)

The mirror of that rule: an emotion word after the negative almost forces なくて. 眠(ねむ)れなくて辛(つら)かった, 会(あ)えなくて残念(ざんねん)です, 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)らなくてよかった.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Adjectives and nouns: only なくて exists

Rule of thumb - ないで is verb-only. Adjectives and nouns have exactly one negative て-form, and it is なくて / でなくて.

この部屋(へや)は静(しず)かじゃなくて、勉強(べんきょう)できません。
This room isn't quiet, so I can't study.

値段(ねだん)は高(たか)くなくて、味(あじ)もよかったです。
It wasn't expensive, and it tasted good too.

Note the second sentence: with adjectives, なくて is not always causal. It can simply be the and of a list - "not A, and B" - exactly as the positive て-form links two descriptions. The same neutral linking shows up in corrections, where じゃなくて means "not A but B": 明日(あした)じゃなくて、あさってです.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Adjectives and nouns: only なくて exists (cont.)

For nouns the formal written variant is ではなくて, and in the stiffest registers ではなく with no て at all: 対象(たいしょう)は学生(がくせい)ではなく、社会人(しゃかいじん)です.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ ここに荷物(にもつ)を置(お)かなくてください。 → ✅ ここに荷物(にもつ)を置(お)かないでください。
    A request can only follow ないで. This is the single most common error with the pair, and it survives well past N4 if it is not fixed early.

  • ❌ 部屋(へや)が明(あか)るくないで、本(ほん)が読(よ)めない。 → ✅ 部屋(へや)が明(あか)るくなくて、本(ほん)が読(よ)めない。
    明(あか)るい is an adjective, and adjectives have no ないで form at all.

  • ❌ 鍵(かぎ)が見(み)つからないで、困(こま)りました。 → ✅ 鍵(かぎ)が見(み)つからなくて、困(こま)りました。
    Being stuck is a result, not an action performed while not finding the key. Causal chain → なくて.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 練習(れんしゅう)しなくて、試合(しあい)に負(ま)けた。 → ✅ 練習(れんしゅう)しなかったので、試合(しあい)に負(ま)けた。
    The non-action was a choice. Causal なくて prefers things you could not help; for deliberate causes use なかったので／なかったから.

  • ❌ 朝(あさ)ごはんを食(た)べなくて会社(かいしゃ)に行(い)きます。 → ✅ 朝(あさ)ごはんを食(た)べないで会社(かいしゃ)に行(い)きます。
    Going to work is a deliberate act, and skipping breakfast is the manner of doing it, not its cause.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 1

1. 昨日(きのう)は暑(あつ)くて、エアコンを消(け)さ____寝(ね)てしまった。

エアコンを消(け)さないで寝(ね)てしまった。

Sleeping is something the speaker did, and leaving the air conditioner on describes the state they were in while doing it. That is manner, so ないで.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 2

2. バスが来(こ)（ないで・なくて）、一時間(いちじかん)も待(ま)ちました。

バスが来(こ)なくて、一時間(いちじかん)も待(ま)ちました。

The waiting is a consequence the speaker suffered, and the bus not coming was outside their control - the textbook profile for causal なくて.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Please don't decide anything without asking me."

私(わたし)に聞(き)かないで決(き)めないでください。

Two negatives, both ないで, and for different reasons: the first is without asking (manner), the second is the negative request. Neither slot can take なくて.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この店(みせ)は安(やす)くないで、あまり人気(にんき)がない。

✅ この店(みせ)は安(やす)くなくて、あまり人気(にんき)がない。

安(やす)い is an い-adjective. Only verbs form ないで; every other word class negates and joins with なくて, built from the くない form.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 連絡(れんらく)しないで帰(かえ)りました。 (b) 連絡(れんらく)がなくて心配(しんぱい)しました。

(a) is about the speaker's own behaviour: they went home, and the going home was done without getting in touch first. The focus is on the manner of one action, and it carries a faint sense of something skipped that should have been done.

(b) is about what happened to the speaker as a result of someone else's silence. There are two linked facts - no contact, therefore worry - and the second half is a feeling rather than a deliberate act, which is exactly the environment causal なくて wants.

～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to

Meaning · you have to do it; there is no option of not doing it

This is the same double-negative machine you met at N5 in ～なくてはいけない: if you don't do it, that won't do. What changes here is the conditional in front. ～なければ is the ば-conditional of ない, and it is the tidier, more formal of the two ways in. なければならない is the wording of rules, contracts and careful written Japanese.

～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Formation

ない is an い-adjective, so it conditionalises exactly like one: 高(たか)い → 高(たか)ければ, therefore ない → なければ. Build the ない-form, drop the final い, add ければ.

Verb (する) しなければならない 提出(ていしゅつ)する → 提出(ていしゅつ)しなければならない

Casual contraction なければ → なきゃ 払(はら)わなきゃ

Rule of thumb - the ending ならない is a verb, so its polite form is なりません, never ❌ならないです. Same for いけない → いけません.

Note - なければ on its own is just an ordinary conditional and has nothing to do with obligation: 時間(じかん)がなければ、また今度(こんど)でいいですよ 「if you haven't got time, another day is fine」. The obligation only appears when いけない or ならない closes the sentence.

もう出(で)なければなりません。
I have to leave now.

宿題(しゅくだい)をしなければいけない。
I have to do my homework.

Do I have to come in tomorrow as well?

The application must be made by the person themselves.

I know I ought to eat more vegetables for my health.

A typhoon is on the way, so we may have to call tomorrow's match off.

Nuance & register

There is only one meaning in this family, and five common ways to say it. Put them on a single line and the choice becomes mechanical:

  1. ～なければならない - the most formal. Laws, contracts, manuals, academic writing, official announcements. Impersonal: it states a requirement that holds for anyone, not a chore facing one person. 2. ～なければいけません - formal-polite speech and ordinary business writing. Still stiff, but it can be about you. 3. ～なくてはいけません - the everyday polite form (this is the N5 branch). What you say to a colleague or in a classroom. 4. ～ないといけない - the everyday spoken form, and the most frequent of all in conversation. 5. ～なきゃ／～なくちゃ - casual, contracted, usually with the ending dropped entirely. What people actually say to themselves and to friends.

Nuance & register (cont.)

Two axes are hiding inside that list. なければ versus なくては is a matter of tidiness: なければ is the written-feeling conditional, なくては the more conversational one. ならない versus いけない is a matter of who the requirement belongs to: ならない states a standing rule with no addressee, while いけない points at a situation and often at a person.

Because なければならない is impersonal, it is a safe thing to say upward. You cannot comfortably tell your boss 確認(かくにん)しなければいけませんよ, but you can report the rule - 規則(きそく)では部長(ぶちょう)のサインが必要(ひつよう)です - and let the rule do the pushing.

Do not use it about a mild preference. なければならない is heavy; if what you mean is "you'd be better off", the pattern is ～たほうがいい, and reaching for the obligation form instead sounds alarming.

Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, the polite negative-past forms come out longer than learners expect: 待(ま)たなければなりませんでした「I had to wait」. There is no shortcut, and in speech people simply say 待(ま)たなきゃいけなかった.

～なければ vs ～なくては

Rule of thumb - identical meaning. なければ is the neater, more written conditional; なくては is a shade more conversational.

会費(かいひ)は三月(さんがつ)までに納(おさ)めなければなりません。
Membership fees must be paid by March.

会費(かいひ)、三月(さんがつ)までに納(おさ)めなくてはいけませんね。
We've got to get the membership fee in by March, haven't we.

The requirement is the same; the voice is not. The first could be printed on a form. The second is two people discussing the form.

One structural difference worth knowing: なくては contracts to なくちゃ and なければ contracts to なきゃ, and the contractions do not cross. There is no ❌なくきゃ.

いけない vs ならない

Rule of thumb - ならない for a rule that stands on its own; いけない for something a particular person has to do about a particular situation.

受験生(じゅけんせい)は九時(くじ)までに入室(にゅうしつ)しなければならない。
Candidates must be seated by nine o'clock.

あ、九時(くじ)までに着(つ)かなければいけないんだった。
Oh - I have to be there by nine.

ならない is faceless, which is why it dominates in writing and why it sounds odd when someone uses it about their own small errands. いけない is the workhorse of spoken obligation above the casual line. In a ～んです explanation, in a question, in an apology, いけない is almost always the one.

～なければならない vs ～たほうがいい

Rule of thumb - obligation versus advice. なければならない says there is no alternative; たほうがいい says one alternative is better.

薬(くすり)は食後(しょくご)に飲(の)まなければなりません。
The medicine must be taken after meals.

薬(くすり)は食後(しょくご)に飲(の)んだほうがいいですよ。
You're better off taking the medicine after meals.

The first is the pharmacist reading the instruction; the second is a friend passing on a tip. Learners routinely over-use the obligation form because English "you have to" is so mild - but Japanese なければならない is not mild, and using it for a suggestion makes a recommendation sound like a regulation.

Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行(い)くなければなりません。 → ✅ 行(い)かなければなりません。
    The pattern builds on the ない-form, never on the dictionary form: 行(い)かない → 行(い)かなければ.

  • ❌ 学生(がくせい)だなければいけません。 → ✅ 学生(がくせい)でなければいけません。
    Nouns and な-adjectives negate through で(は)ない, so the conditional is でなければ.

  • ❌ 急(いそ)がなければならないです。 → ✅ 急(いそ)がなければなりません。
    ならない is the negative of the verb なる. Verbs do not take です to become polite.

  • ❌ 部屋(へや)が明(あか)るいなければいけません。 → ✅ 部屋(へや)が明(あか)るくなければいけません。
    い-adjectives negate to くない first, and only then take ければ.

  • ❌ 来(こ)なければならなくないです。（intending "you don't have to come"）→ ✅ 来(こ)なくてもいいです。
    You cannot negate the obligation form to release someone from it. The opposite of なければならない is a different pattern: なくてもいい.

～ないといけない

Meaning · have to; must - the version people actually say out loud

Third conditional, same machine. ～なくては and ～なければ build obligation out of if you don't … that won't do; ～ないと does the same with the と conditional. What makes it worth a page of its own is frequency: in conversation this form outnumbers the textbook ones, and it has a trick the others do not - it can stop in the middle and still be a complete sentence.

～ないといけない · Formation

Take the ない-form and add と. Then close with いけない, だめだ, or nothing at all.

Before next week's business trip I have to book the hotel and the tickets myself.

～ないといけない · Nuance & register

～ないといけない sits in the middle of the obligation ladder and leans casual. In plain form it is what friends, family and colleagues use with each other all day. In いけません it is perfectly usable at work, though it still sounds like speech rather than writing - a report or a notice would say なければならない.

The ending falls off constantly. 急(いそ)がないと, 買(か)わないとね, そろそろ寝(ね)ないとな are not fragments; the trailing と carries the whole meaning, and adding いけない would sound oddly complete. Learners consistently under-produce this and it is one of the quickest wins in sounding natural. ね invites agreement, な is a masculine-leaning mutter to oneself.

～ないとだめ swaps a softer ending for a blunter one. だめ is flat and physical - "no good" - so 出(だ)さないとだめだよ presses harder than 出(だ)さないといけないよ. Said to a child or a close friend it is normal; said to a colleague it can sting.

～ないといけない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two things to file away. First, ならない almost never follows と: you will occasionally hear ～ないとならない, but it is uncommon and いけない is the safe choice. Second, in Kansai the whole family is replaced by ～なあかん (行(い)かなあかん, せなあかん), which you will hear constantly in the west and in comedy, and which no textbook prepares you for.

As with every obligation form, aiming it at someone above you is strong. Reporting your own obligation is always safe; telling your boss 確認(かくにん)しないといけませんよ is not.

～ないといけない · ～ないといけない vs ～なければならない

Rule of thumb - same requirement, different room. ないと belongs in the kitchen; なければならない belongs on the noticeboard.

申込書(もうしこみしょ)は窓口(まどぐち)に提出(ていしゅつ)しなければならない。
Application forms must be submitted at the counter.

申込書(もうしこみしょ)、窓口(まどぐち)に出(だ)さないといけないんだって。
Apparently you have to hand the form in at the counter.

The information is identical. The first is impersonal and could be printed; the second is one person relaying it to another, complete with んだ and hearsay って. Mixing them up is not a grammar error, it is a costume error - and it is just as noticeable.

～ないといけない · ～ないと, and stopping there

Rule of thumb - a bare ～ないと at the end of an utterance is a finished sentence, not a broken one. The unspoken いけない is understood.

やばい、電車(でんしゃ)来(く)る。走(はし)らないと。
Uh-oh, the train's coming. We've got to run.

明日(あした)試験(しけん)だから、今日(きょう)は寝(ね)ないとね。
I've got an exam tomorrow, so I'd better get some sleep.

This works because Japanese lets a conditional trail off when the consequence is obvious, and "that would be bad" is always obvious. The same trick is available to なきゃ and なくちゃ, which is why the casual end of this family so often sounds unfinished to a learner's ear. It is not.

Obligation ～ないと vs the plain conditional ～ないと

Rule of thumb - if a consequence follows, と is just "if". Obligation only appears when the consequence is いけない/だめ, or is left unsaid.

早(はや)く出(で)ないと、電車(でんしゃ)に間(ま)に合(あ)わないよ。
If we don't leave soon we won't make the train.

早(はや)く出(で)ないと。
We've got to get going.

Same five words, two different sentences. In the first, と behaves exactly as it does in any conditional - the second half spells out what goes wrong. In the second there is no second half, so the listener supplies the standard one and the whole thing reads as obligation.

Obligation ～ないと vs the plain conditional ～ないと (cont.)

This is also why ないと cannot be used for a rule stated in the abstract. A conditional needs a situation; a regulation with no situation attached wants なければならない.

～ないといけない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日(あした)は早(はや)く起(お)きるといけない。 → ✅ 明日(あした)は早(はや)く起(お)きないといけない。
    The obligation lives in the negative. 起(お)きるといけない means "it would be a problem if I got up", which is the opposite of the intended sentence.

  • ❌ 三人以上(さんにんいじょう)だといけません。（intending "there must be three or more people"）→ ✅ 三人以上(さんにんいじょう)でないといけません。
    Nouns negate with でない before と. だと is the affirmative conditional and flips the meaning.

  • ❌ 待(ま)たないといけないでした。 → ✅ 待(ま)たないといけませんでした。／待(ま)たないといけなかった。
    いけない is a negative predicate in its own right; make it past by conjugating it (いけなかった) or polite-past (いけませんでした), not by hanging でした on the plain form.

～ないといけない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 急(いそ)がないとならないです。 → ✅ 急(いそ)がないといけません。／急(いそ)がなければなりません。
    ならない is at home after なければ/なくては, not after と, and it never takes です.

  • ❌（in a written report）至急(しきゅう)対応(たいおう)しないといけない。 → ✅ 至急(しきゅう)対応(たいおう)しなければならない。
    Not ungrammatical, just spoken. In documents the と branch of the family reads as if someone forgot they were writing.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ

Meaning · gotta - the same obligation, squeezed down for speech

These are not new grammar. なくちゃ is なくては with the syllables worn off, and なきゃ is なければ with the same treatment. What they add is register: they are what obligation sounds like in an actual conversation, and they usually appear with the いけない/ならない tail chopped off entirely. Learners are blindsided by なきゃ because no textbook sentence ever contains it and every real one does.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Formation

Japanese contracts ては to ちゃ everywhere (見(み)ては → 見(み)ちゃ), and ければ to きゃ. Apply that to the two obligation conditionals and you get these.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Nuance & register

Casual. That is the whole point, and it is also the whole restriction. なくちゃ and なきゃ belong in speech among friends, family and close colleagues, and in writing only where writing imitates speech - messages, chat, blogs, dialogue in manga and novels. They do not appear in reports, emails to clients, exam answers or announcements.

They are not sloppy or uneducated, and they are not gender-marked: adults of every age and both sexes use them constantly. What they are is unguarded. Saying 帰(かえ)らなきゃ lets the listener see you making a decision, which is exactly right with a friend and wrong in front of a customer.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Nuance & register (cont.)

なきゃ is the more frequent of the two in everyday Tokyo speech. なくちゃ is a touch lighter and rounder, and it turns up often when someone is talking to a child or being deliberately breezy; a few speakers hear it as slightly cuter than なきゃ, but nobody would call it a different level of politeness. The stretched なくっちゃ adds emphasis - これは見(み)なくっちゃ「this I've got to see」.

The tail is normally dropped. When it is kept, it stays in plain form: なきゃいけない, なきゃだめ, and in very casual Tokyo speech なきゃなんない (from ならない). Attaching a polite tail is the classic learner error - you cannot half-contract a sentence, and ❌行(い)かなきゃいけません mixes two registers inside one predicate. If you need politeness, expand the whole thing back out to なければなりません.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Nuance & register (cont.)

One more thing that catches people out in listening: because the ending is missing, these forms often end an utterance on a rising or trailing intonation and sound unfinished. あ、洗濯(せんたく)しなきゃ… is a complete thought. Do not wait for the rest of it.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · ～なきゃ vs ～なくちゃ

Rule of thumb - the same word twice over. Choose by ear, not by meaning; なきゃ is the more common default.

そろそろ出(で)なきゃ。
I'd better get going.

そろそろ出(で)なくちゃ。
I'd better get going.

There is no difference of meaning, obligation strength or politeness to report, and a page that invented one would be lying. The only observable tendencies are that なきゃ is heard more often overall, なくちゃ sounds slightly softer and is common in speech directed at children, and only なくちゃ has the emphatic stretched form なくっちゃ.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · ～なきゃ vs ～ないと

Rule of thumb - both are casual spoken obligation. ないと is neutral and usable slightly further up the politeness scale; なきゃ is more informal and more self-directed.

会議(かいぎ)の資料(しりょう)、作(つく)らないといけませんね。
We'll need to put the meeting materials together, won't we.

うわ、資料(しりょう)作(つく)らなきゃ。
Ugh - I've got to make the handouts.

ないと survives being made polite: 行(い)かないといけません is fine at work. なきゃ does not. And なきゃ leans towards things the speaker is telling themselves, which is why it so often follows あ or うわ and precedes nothing at all.

～なきゃ as obligation vs ～なきゃ as plain "unless"

Rule of thumb - なきゃ is just contracted なければ. If a consequence follows it, it is an ordinary conditional and has nothing to do with obligation.

自分(じぶん)で見(み)なきゃわからないよ。
You won't know unless you look for yourself.

自分(じぶん)で見(み)なきゃ。
I've got to go and look for myself.

The first is "if you don't look, you won't understand" - the いけない is not implied and never was. The second has nothing after it, so the listener fills in the missing "that won't do" and hears an obligation.

～なきゃ as obligation vs ～なきゃ as plain "unless" (cont.)

The same holds for なくちゃ, and it explains a shape you will meet often: 安(やす)くなきゃ買(か)わない「I won't buy it unless it's cheap」, 君(きみ)じゃなきゃだめだ「it has to be you」.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行(い)かなきゃです。 → ✅ 行(い)かなきゃ。／行(い)かなければなりません。
    There is no polite version of a contraction. です cannot be bolted onto なきゃ; if you need politeness, expand the form.

  • ❌ 行(い)くなきゃ → ✅ 行(い)かなきゃ
    The contraction still starts from the ない-form. Build 行(い)かない first, then squeeze.

  • ❌ 急(いそ)がなくきゃ → ✅ 急(いそ)がなくちゃ／急(いそ)がなきゃ
    The two contractions have different parents and cannot be blended. なくては→なくちゃ, なければ→なきゃ.

  • ❌ 会員(かいいん)だなきゃだめです。 → ✅ 会員(かいいん)じゃなきゃだめだよ。
    Nouns negate to じゃない, so the contracted conditional is じゃなきゃ. Note the ending changed too - だめです after じゃなきゃ is a register mismatch.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌（in an email to a client）明日(あした)までに送(おく)らなきゃいけません。 → ✅ 明日(あした)までに送(おく)らなければなりません。
    Contractions do not belong in written business Japanese, however polite the tail you attach to them.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 1

1. やばい、電車(でんしゃ)行(い)っちゃう。走(はし)ら____。

走(はし)らなきゃ。（also fine: 走(はし)らなくちゃ。）

The situation is urgent, spoken and self-directed, which is the natural home of the contracted forms with the ending dropped. Anything longer would sound like the speaker had time to think.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 2

2. 部長(ぶちょう)に（報告(ほうこく)しなきゃいけません・報告(ほうこく)しなければなりません）。

なきゃ is a casual contraction and いけません is a polite ending; putting them in one predicate mixes registers inside a single word. Pick one level - fully contracted for speech among friends, fully expanded when you are being polite.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, as something you say to yourself on remembering it: "Oh no, I have to return the library books today."

Muttered self-obligation drops the いけない and drops the particles too. The interjection at the front is part of the pattern: these forms almost always follow a moment of realisation.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この映画(えいが)、絶対(ぜったい)見(み)るなきゃ。

✅ この映画(えいが)、絶対(ぜったい)見(み)なきゃ。（emphatic: 見(み)なくっちゃ。）

なきゃ replaces なければ, which is built on the ない-form - 見(み)ない → 見(み)なければ → 見(み)なきゃ. The dictionary form never appears in front of it.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 早(はや)く予約(よやく)しなきゃ席(せき)がなくなるよ。 (b) 早(はや)く予約(よやく)しなきゃ。

(a) is not an obligation sentence at all. なきゃ here is simply contracted なければ working as an ordinary conditional, and the second half states the consequence: book early, or the seats will be gone.

(b) leaves the consequence out. Because the missing half is understood to be "that won't do", the whole utterance is heard as the speaker realising they have to act. The contraction is identical in both; what decides the reading is whether anything follows it.

～なくてもいい

Meaning · you don't have to; it's fine not to

～なくてもいい is the exact negative counterpart of ～てもいい. Same shape, same も of concession, same verdict いい - only the thing being conceded is now a non-action: "even if you don't do it, that's fine." It does not tell anyone to stop. It removes a requirement and leaves the choice open.

～なくてもいい · Formation

Negate first, swap the final い of ない for くても, then finish with いい.

～なくてもいい · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the particle carries the whole meaning. ～なくて**は**いけない = must; ～なくて**も**いい = needn't. Everything else in the two patterns is shared scaffolding.

～なくてもいい · Examples

靴(くつ)は脱(ぬ)がなくてもいいです。
You don't have to take your shoes off.

急(いそ)がなくてもいいよ。
There's no need to rush.

返事(へんじ)は書(か)かなくてもいいですか。
Is it all right if I don't write back?

服装(ふくそう)はスーツでなくてもいいそうです。
I hear it doesn't have to be a suit.

部屋(へや)は広(ひろ)くなくてもいいけど、駅(えき)に近(ちか)いほうがいい。
The flat doesn't have to be big, but I'd rather it were near a station.

～なくてもいい · Examples (cont.)

そんなに心配(しんぱい)しなくてもいいんじゃない？
Don't you think you're worrying more than you need to?

今日(きょう)は仕事(しごと)が早(はや)く終(お)わったので、残業(ざんぎょう)はしなくてもいいことになりました。
Work finished early today, so it turns out we don't have to stay late.

レポートは手書(てが)きでも印刷(いんさつ)でもよくて、表紙(ひょうし)はつけなくてもかまいません。
The report may be handwritten or printed, and you needn't add a cover page.

～なくてもいい · Nuance & register

The pattern itself is neutral; いいです makes it polite in form. What is not neutral is the social act. Saying "you don't have to" means you are in a position to lift the requirement, and that is a position of authority. Between equals it is generous. Aimed upward it is presumptuous: telling your manager 部長(ぶちょう)は来(こ)なくてもいいですよ sounds like you are giving them permission to skip work.

Upward, the same content goes through different wording: ご無理(むり)なさらないでください, お気(き)になさらないでください, そのままで結構(けっこう)です. Note that 結構(けっこう)です is genuinely ambiguous - it can mean "that's fine as it is" or "no thank you" - and tone and context are the only things separating them.

～なくてもいい · Nuance & register (cont.)

In speech the も disappears constantly: 行(い)かなくていいよ, 待(ま)たなくていいから. This is not a different pattern and carries no change in meaning; it is simply what fast Japanese does to the phrase. Learners who have only seen なくてもいい in a textbook often fail to recognise なくていい in conversation. The more emphatic casual variant is なくたっていい (謝(あやま)らなくたっていいよ).

Three neighbours worth knowing, in rising order of formality: なくても大丈夫(だいじょうぶ) (warm, spoken), なくてもかまいません「it doesn't matter if you don't」(neutral, business), なくても結構(けっこう)です「it is not required」(formal, institutional). かまわない is the one to reach for when you want to sound like you truly do not mind.

Finally, the pattern is not only about other people. ～なくてもいい about yourself reports relief or a decision: 明日(あした)は早(はや)く起(お)きなくてもいいから、映画(えいが)でも見(み)よう.

～なくてもいい · ～なくてもいい vs ～てもいい

Rule of thumb - ～てもいい opens a door: you may do it. ～なくてもいい closes a duty: you needn't do it. Neither one pushes you in either direction.

写真(しゃしん)を撮(と)ってもいいですか。
Is it okay if I take photos?

写真(しゃしん)を撮(と)らなくてもいいですか。
Is it okay if I don't take photos?

The first asks whether the action is permitted; the second asks whether the action is required. In a museum you would ask the first; on a job where photographing is part of your task, you would ask the second.

～なくてもいい · ～なくてもいい vs ～てもいい (cont.)

They pair up into a neat square with the obligation forms. てもいい may, てはいけない must not, なくてはいけない must, なくてもいい needn't. Permission and prohibition are built on the て-form; obligation and exemption on the negative stem.

～なくてもいい · ～なくてもいい vs ～ないでください

Rule of thumb - "you don't have to" is not "please don't". なくてもいい leaves the action available; ないでください takes it away.

手伝(てつだ)わなくてもいいですよ。
You don't have to help.

手伝(てつだ)わないでください。
Please don't help.

English blurs these - "you don't have to help" is often meant as a polite refusal - but Japanese does not. The first sentence releases someone who was offering; the second turns them away, and would be startling from a colleague. When you want to decline an offer kindly, the natural wording is 大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)です、ありがとうございます, not either of these.

～なくてもいい vs ～なくてもかまいません・～なくても大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)

Rule of thumb - same exemption, three temperatures. 大丈夫(だいじょうぶ) is warm, いい is neutral, かまいません is businesslike.

無理(むり)に全部(ぜんぶ)食(た)べなくても大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)だよ。
Honestly, you don't have to finish it all.

当日(とうじつ)は印鑑(いんかん)をお持(も)ちにならなくてもかまいません。
You do not need to bring a personal seal on the day.

大丈夫(だいじょうぶ) says don't worry about it and belongs in speech between people who know each other. かまいません says it makes no difference to us and belongs in counters, forms and customer service. Plain いい sits between them and is never wrong, which is why it is the form to learn first.

～なくてもいい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 払(はら)わなくてはいいです。 → ✅ 払(はら)わなくてもいいです。
    なくては pairs with いけない; なくても pairs with いい. Mixing them produces a pattern that does not exist.

  • ❌ 学生(がくせい)だなくてもいいです。 → ✅ 学生(がくせい)でなくてもいいです。
    Nouns and な-adjectives negate through で(は)ない, so the join is でなくても.

  • ❌ 新(あたら)しいじゃなくてもいい。 → ✅ 新(あたら)しくなくてもいい。
    新(あたら)しい is an い-adjective, so its negative is くない and the join is くなくても.

  • ❌（meaning "you don't have to come tomorrow"）明日(あした)は来(こ)ないでください。 → ✅ 明日(あした)は来(こ)なくてもいいですよ。
    Straight from English. The ないでください version instructs the person to stay away, which is a completely different message.

～なくてもいい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 課長(かちょう)は出席(しゅっせき)しなくてもいいですよ。 → ✅ 課長(かちょう)、ご都合(つごう)が悪(わる)ければ、ご欠席(けっせき)でも問題(もんだい)ございません。
    Releasing someone from a duty implies you had the authority to impose it. Upward, state the situation instead of granting an exemption.

～なくてもいい · Practice 1

1. このレストランは予約(よやく)し____いいですよ。いつも空(す)いていますから。

予約(よやく)しなくてもいいですよ。

The restaurant is always empty, so booking is not required - the exemption reading. Note the particle: なくても with いい, never なくては.

～なくてもいい · Practice 2

2. 会議(かいぎ)のとき、ネクタイを（しなくてもいいです・しないでください）。

Either is grammatical, but they say different things. しなくてもいいです means a tie is optional; しないでください instructs people not to wear one. For a normal dress-code announcement the first is what is meant.

～なくてもいい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, politely: "You don't need to bring anything."

何(なに)も持(も)って来(こ)なくてもいいですよ。 （more formal: 何(なに)もお持(も)ちいただかなくてもかまいません。）

The よ matters: without it the sentence can read as a flat instruction, whereas telling a guest not to trouble themselves should sound warm. 来(く)る takes the irregular negative stem 来(こ).

～なくてもいい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 明日(あした)のパーティーは正式(せいしき)な服(ふく)だなくてもいいです。

✅ 明日(あした)のパーティーは正式(せいしき)な服(ふく)でなくてもいいです。

だ never survives in front of a negative join. A noun negates as 服(ふく)ではない, and the exemption form built on that is 服(ふく)でなくてもいい.

～なくてもいい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 全部(ぜんぶ)読(よ)まなくてもいいです。 (b) 全部(ぜんぶ)読(よ)まなくてはいけません。

(a) removes a requirement: reading all of it is optional, and the listener may read as much or as little as they like. One particle - も - is doing that work.

(b) imposes one: all of it has to be read, and there is no choice in the matter. The は closes off the alternative that も opens. Because the two sentences differ by a single kana in the middle of a long predicate, they are easy to mishear and worth saying aloud a few times.

～必要がある

Meaning · there is a need to do it; it is necessary to do it

Read 必要(ひつよう). 必要(ひつよう) is a noun meaning necessity, and ある is the ordinary verb of existence - so the pattern literally says a need to do X exists. That is why it feels cooler than the obligation forms: it does not push anybody, it states a fact about the situation.

～必要がある · Formation

The verb in front stays in the dictionary form. Nouns join with の.

Note - the negation goes on ある, never on the verb in front. "You don't need to go" is 行(い)く必要(ひつよう)はない, not ❌行(い)かない必要(ひつよう)がある, which would mean something like "there is a need for you not to go."

～必要がある · Examples

パスポートを見(み)せる必要(ひつよう)があります。
You need to show your passport.

急(いそ)ぐ必要(ひつよう)はありません。
There's no need to hurry.

もう一度(いちど)確認(かくにん)する必要(ひつよう)がある。
We need to check it one more time.

この書類(しょるい)は翻訳(ほんやく)の必要(ひつよう)がありますか。
Does this document need translating?

心配(しんぱい)する必要(ひつよう)はないよ。全部(ぜんぶ)うまくいっているから。
You've no need to worry - everything's going fine.

～必要がある · Examples (cont.)

海外(かいがい)で働(はたら)くには、ビザを申請(しんせい)する必要(ひつよう)があります。
To work abroad, you need to apply for a visa.

去年(きょねん)作(つく)った資料(しりょう)なので、数字(すうじ)を新(あたら)しいものに直(なお)す必要(ひつよう)があります。
These materials are from last year, so the figures need updating.

会員(かいいん)カードをお持(も)ちの方(かた)は、受付(うけつけ)で住所(じゅうしょ)を書(か)く必要(ひつよう)はございません。
Members do not need to write their address at reception.

～必要がある · Nuance & register

～必要(ひつよう)がある is the objective member of the family. It describes a requirement as a property of the situation, with nobody's authority behind it, which makes it the default in manuals, instructions, application guidance, business email and academic writing. If you have ever read a Japanese form, you have read this pattern.

That neutrality has a practical use: it lets you tell someone what has to happen without telling them what to do. ご確認(かくにん)いただく必要(ひつよう)がございます is still direct, but 三月(さんがつ)までに提出(ていしゅつ)する必要(ひつよう)があります said about a shared deadline points at the deadline, not at the listener.

The flip side is that it sounds stiff about small personal errands. Nobody says 今日(きょう)は買(か)い物(もの)をする必要(ひつよう)がある to a friend - that is 買(か)い物(もの)行(い)かないと territory. Using the formal pattern for a trip to the supermarket sounds like reading from a report.

～必要がある · Nuance & register (cont.)

The negative is the half you will use most, and it is unusually warm for such a formal pattern. 謝(あやま)る必要(ひつよう)はないよ「you don't need to apologise」 and 気(き)にする必要(ひつよう)はありません「there's no need to worry about it」 are everyday reassurances. In casual speech が drops out altogether: そんなに急(いそ)ぐ必要(ひつよう)ない.

At the formal end, ある is replaced by its humble-polite equivalents: 必要(ひつよう)がございます and 必要(ひつよう)はございません, which is what shop staff and official notices use.

～必要がある · ～必要(ひつよう)がある vs ～なければならない

Rule of thumb - 必要(ひつよう)がある reports that a need exists; なければならない says the alternative is unacceptable. The first describes, the second compels.

この薬(くすり)は毎日(まいにち)飲(の)む必要(ひつよう)があります。
This medicine needs to be taken every day.

この薬(くすり)は毎日(まいにち)飲(の)まなければなりません。
You must take this medicine every day.

Both are correct and a doctor might say either. The first sounds like an explanation of how the treatment works; the second sounds like an instruction being issued to you. When the requirement comes from a rule with force behind it - a law, a contract, a school regulation - なければならない is the stronger fit. When it comes from the nature of the task, 必要(ひつよう)がある is.

～必要がある · ～必要(ひつよう)がある vs ～なければならない (cont.)

There is also a grammatical difference worth noticing: なければならない attaches to a negative stem, while 必要(ひつよう)がある attaches to a plain dictionary form. That makes the second much easier to build under time pressure.

～必要がある · ～必要(ひつよう)はない vs ～なくてもいい

Rule of thumb - なくてもいい grants an exemption; 必要(ひつよう)はない denies that a requirement ever existed.

明日(あした)は来(こ)なくてもいいですよ。
You don't have to come in tomorrow.

明日(あした)は来(く)る必要(ひつよう)はありません。
There is no need for you to come in tomorrow.

The first is a person releasing another person, which is why it needs よ or a smile to sound kind. The second is a statement about the schedule, and because it does not position the speaker as the one granting permission, it is often the safer thing to say upward or to a stranger.

～必要がある · ～必要(ひつよう)はない vs ～なくてもいい (cont.)

In their negative-reassurance use they come very close: 心配(しんぱい)しなくてもいい and 心配(しんぱい)する必要(ひつよう)はない are near-synonyms, the second slightly firmer, as if arguing the worry away rather than waving it off.

～必要がある · ～必要(ひつよう)がある vs ～が必要(ひつよう)だ

Rule of thumb - する必要(ひつよう)がある needs an action; が必要(ひつよう)だ needs a thing.

ここで靴(くつ)を脱(ぬ)ぐ必要(ひつよう)があります。
You need to take your shoes off here.

ここでは上(うわ)ばきが必要(ひつよう)です。
You need indoor shoes here.

The two are often interchangeable in translation and never in grammar. A verb before 必要(ひつよう) requires the dictionary form and the が…ある frame; a noun before it takes が and the plain な-adjective predicate 必要(ひつよう)だ／です. A noun can also be forced into the ある frame with の - 修理(しゅうり)の必要(ひつよう)がある - which sounds distinctly more formal than 修理(しゅうり)が必要(ひつよう)だ.

～必要がある · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行(い)きます必要(ひつよう)があります。 → ✅ 行(い)く必要(ひつよう)があります。
    The verb in front of 必要(ひつよう) is plain and non-past. ます cannot appear inside the sentence; politeness goes on あります at the end.

  • ❌ 行(い)かない必要(ひつよう)があります。 → ✅ 行(い)く必要(ひつよう)はありません。
    To say there is no need, negate ある, not the verb. Negating the verb creates a need not to go, which is a different and much rarer idea.

  • ❌ ビザ必要(ひつよう)があります。 → ✅ ビザの必要(ひつよう)があります。／ビザが必要(ひつよう)です。
    A noun cannot sit straight against 必要(ひつよう). Either link it with の or switch to the が必要(ひつよう)だ pattern.

～必要がある · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部屋(へや)が静(しず)かな必要(ひつよう)があります。 → ✅ 部屋(へや)は静(しず)かでなければなりません。
    Adjectives do not take this pattern at all. Requirements about qualities go through the obligation forms.

  • ❌（to a friend）そろそろ帰(かえ)る必要(ひつよう)がある。 → ✅ そろそろ帰(かえ)らないと。
    Not wrong, just wrongly dressed. For your own small obligations in conversation, the ないと / なきゃ end of the family is what people actually use.

～必要がある · Practice 1

1. この申込書(もうしこみしょ)には、はんこを押(お)す____ありますか。

はんこを押(お)す必要(ひつよう)がありますか。

The verb stays in the dictionary form, and the question is about whether a requirement exists - exactly what this pattern is for. On a form, it is more natural than asking 押(お)さなくてもいいですか.

～必要がある · Practice 2

2. 課長(かちょう)に（報告(ほうこく)する必要(ひつよう)があります・報告(ほうこく)しなきゃ）と会議(かいぎ)で言(い)った。

報告(ほうこく)する必要(ひつよう)があります。

A meeting is a formal setting and the sentence is stating what the situation requires, not muttering a personal chore. なきゃ is a casual contraction and would sound out of place in front of colleagues.

～必要がある · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, as a line of written guidance: "Applicants need to submit two photographs."

申込者(もうしこみしゃ)は写真(しゃしん)を二枚(にまい)提出(ていしゅつ)する必要(ひつよう)があります。

Written guidance wants the impersonal pattern: it states what the process requires without addressing anyone. 提出(ていしゅつ)しなければならない would also fit a rulebook, but it sounds more like a command than an instruction sheet.

～必要がある · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 明日(あした)は来(こ)ない必要(ひつよう)がありません。

✅ 明日(あした)は来(く)る必要(ひつよう)はありません。

Two things went wrong. The verb must be affirmative and in the dictionary form, and the negation belongs on ある. Note also the particle flip: negatives of this pattern normally take は.

～必要がある · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 返事(へんじ)を書(か)く必要(ひつよう)はありません。 (b) 返事(へんじ)を書(か)かなくてもいいです。

(a) states that the process does not call for a reply. The speaker is describing how things work, which makes it neutral and slightly formal - the wording of a notice or of someone explaining a procedure.

(b) tells the listener they are released from writing one. It implies the speaker is in a position to decide, so it sounds more personal and, depending on who is speaking to whom, either kind or presumptuous.

必要

Meaning · necessary; needed - the word itself, not the pattern

Read 必要(ひつよう). It is a noun that behaves as a な-adjective: something が必要(ひつよう)だ「is needed」, and a 必要(ひつよう)な thing is a required one. The verb pattern ～必要(ひつよう)がある is built out of this word, but the word does plenty of work on its own, and getting its particles right is what stops learners writing English with Japanese vocabulary in it.

必要 · Formation

The thing that is needed takes が, not を - 必要(ひつよう) is not a verb and has no object.

必要 · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (predicate) Noun + が + 必要(ひつよう)だ／です ビザ → ビザが必要(ひつよう)です
Before a noun 必要(ひつよう) + な + Noun 道具(どうぐ) → 必要(ひつよう)な道具(どうぐ)
Purpose Noun + に + 必要(ひつよう)な + Noun 生活(せいかつ) → 生活(せいかつ)に必要(ひつよう)なもの
Negative (casual) 必要(ひつよう)ない／必要(ひつよう)じゃない それは必要(ひつよう)ない
Negative (polite) 必要(ひつよう)ありません／必要(ひつよう)ではありません それは必要(ひつよう)ありません
Past 必要(ひつよう)だった／必要(ひつよう)なかった 時間(じかん)が必要(ひつよう)だった
Becoming 必要(ひつよう)になる お金(かね)が必要(ひつよう)になった

必要 · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb instead of noun dictionary form + 必要(ひつよう)がある 申(もう)し込(こ)む必要(ひつよう)がある
Formal verb Noun + を + 必要(ひつよう)とする 助(たす)けを必要(ひつよう)とする人(ひと)

Rule of thumb - 必要(ひつよう) is a な-adjective, so it never takes くない or かった. The negative is 必要(ひつよう)じゃない／必要(ひつよう)ない, and the past is 必要(ひつよう)だった.

Note - the written antonym is 不要(ふよう), which you will meet on forms and labels rather than in speech: 記入(きにゅう)不要(ふよう)「no entry required」, 返却(へんきゃく)不要(ふよう)「no need to return this」.

必要 · Examples

パスポートが必要(ひつよう)です。
You'll need your passport.

必要(ひつよう)なものだけ持(も)って来(き)てください。
Please bring only what you need.

その説明(せつめい)は必要(ひつよう)ないよ。
That explanation isn't necessary.

申(もう)し込(こ)みに必要(ひつよう)な書類(しょるい)は三(みっ)つです。
There are three documents required for the application.

急(きゅう)に現金(げんきん)が必要(ひつよう)になった。
I suddenly found myself needing cash.

必要 · Examples (cont.)

この仕事(しごと)に経験(けいけん)は必要(ひつよう)ではありません。
No experience is required for this job.

一人(ひとり)暮(ぐ)らしを始(はじ)めるとき、思(おも)っていたより多(おお)くのものが必要(ひつよう)だった。
When I started living on my own, I needed more things than I had expected.

災害(さいがい)のときに本当(ほんとう)に必要(ひつよう)なのは、水(みず)と情報(じょうほう)だと言(い)われています。
What you really need in a disaster, they say, is water and information.

必要 · Nuance & register

必要(ひつよう) is a Sino-Japanese word, and like most of them it leans formal and slightly abstract. It is completely ordinary in polite speech, business email, notices and writing. In casual conversation, though, Japanese usually reaches for the plain verb いる instead: a friend asks 袋(ふくろ)いる？, not 袋(ふくろ)が必要(ひつよう)ですか. At a convenience store counter you will hear both, because staff speech is formal.

The negatives are worth separating. 必要(ひつよう)ない is short, everyday and can sound abrupt - そんなの必要(ひつよう)ない is close to "we don't need that rubbish." 必要(ひつよう)ありません is the neutral polite version and the one to use with strangers. 必要(ひつよう)ではありません is stiffer again and belongs in writing. As a refusal at a shop counter, the standard phrase is not any of these but 大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)です or 結構(けっこう)です.

必要 · Nuance & register (cont.)

必要(ひつよう)とする is a genuine verb and a register marker: it appears in journalism, official documents and formal description - 支援(しえん)を必要(ひつよう)としている地域(ちいき)「areas that require assistance」. Using it in conversation would sound like reading a press release.

One more thing to notice: 必要(ひつよう) describes what a purpose demands. It carries no warmth. That is why it is the right word for documents, tools, time and money, and the wrong word for people - see the contrast below.

必要 · 必要(ひつよう) vs いる

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different word class and different register. いる is a verb and casual; 必要(ひつよう) is a な-adjective and formal.

レシート、いる？
D'you want the receipt?

レシートは必要(ひつよう)ですか。
Would you like a receipt?

Both take their object with が (or with no particle at all in speech), which is the one thing they share structurally. いる - written 要(い)る, though usually left in kana - is a う-verb: いります, いらない, いった. Beware that its past and て-forms collide with 入(はい)る, 行(い)く and the existence verb いる, which is why it is far more common in the non-past.

必要 · 必要(ひつよう) vs いる (cont.)

いる is also limited to things. For an action you need ～必要(ひつよう)がある or one of the obligation forms: there is no ❌行(い)くがいる.

必要 · 必要(ひつよう) vs 大切(たいせつ)・大事(だいじ)

Rule of thumb - 必要(ひつよう) = required for a purpose. 大切(たいせつ)／大事(だいじ) = important, precious, valued. Do not use 必要(ひつよう) about people.

家族(かぞく)はとても大切(たいせつ)です。
My family means a great deal to me.

この仕事(しごと)には集中力(しゅうちゅうりょく)が必要(ひつよう)です。
This job requires concentration.

Swap them and both sentences go wrong in interesting ways. 家族(かぞく)が必要(ひつよう)です sounds like you are shopping for one - the word treats its object as a resource. And 集中力(しゅうちゅうりょく)が大切(たいせつ)です is fine but says something milder: concentration matters, rather than the job being impossible without it.

必要 · 必要(ひつよう) vs 大切(たいせつ)・大事(だいじ) (cont.)

大事(だいじ) is the more conversational of the two importance words; 大切(たいせつ) is a shade warmer and more careful. Both are な-adjectives, like 必要(ひつよう) itself.

必要 · 必要(ひつよう)だ vs 必要(ひつよう)がある

Rule of thumb - a noun is needed → が必要(ひつよう)だ. An action is needed → ～する必要(ひつよう)がある.

予約(よやく)が必要(ひつよう)です。
A reservation is required.

予約(よやく)する必要(ひつよう)があります。
You need to make a reservation.

The information is the same and the framing is not. The first names a thing the situation demands, and would suit a sign on a door. The second describes something the listener has to do, and suits an explanation.

必要 · 必要(ひつよう)だ vs 必要(ひつよう)がある (cont.)

A noun can be pushed into the ある frame with の (予約(よやく)の必要(ひつよう)があります), which sounds noticeably more bureaucratic. A verb, on the other hand, cannot use the だ frame at all - there is no ❌予約(よやく)するが必要(ひつよう)です.

必要 · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 必要(ひつよう)の書類(しょるい) → ✅ 必要(ひつよう)な書類(しょるい)
    必要(ひつよう) is a な-adjective when it modifies a noun. の looks tempting because the word is also a noun, but it is not how the phrase is built.

  • ❌ パスポートを必要(ひつよう)です。 → ✅ パスポートが必要(ひつよう)です。
    There is no verb here, so there is no object. The needed thing is the subject and takes が.

  • ❌ この靴(くつ)は必要(ひつよう)くない。 → ✅ この靴(くつ)は必要(ひつよう)じゃない。
    Only い-adjectives negate with くない. 必要(ひつよう) takes じゃない, ではない, or bare ない.

  • ❌ 病院(びょういん)に行(い)くが必要(ひつよう)です。 → ✅ 病院(びょういん)に行(い)く必要(ひつよう)があります。
    A verb cannot be marked with が. To say an action is needed, switch to the 必要(ひつよう)がある frame.

必要 · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌（to a friend, about a plastic bag）袋(ふくろ)が必要(ひつよう)ですか。 → ✅ 袋(ふくろ)いる？
    Grammatical and oddly formal. Among friends the everyday word for needing a thing is いる.

必要 · Practice 1

1. この手続(てつづ)きに____な物(もの)を全部(ぜんぶ)持(も)って来(き)ました。

この手続(てつづ)きに**必要(ひつよう)**な物(もの)を全部(ぜんぶ)持(も)って来(き)ました。

Modifying a noun, 必要(ひつよう) behaves as a な-adjective, so the connector is な. The に marks the purpose the things are needed for.

必要 · Practice 2

2. ペン、（いる・必要(ひつよう)でいらっしゃいますか）？ - said to a close friend.

ペン、いる？

Between friends the plain verb is what people use; the honorific version is not merely over-polite, it is comic. 必要(ひつよう) itself is not rude here, but it still sounds like a form rather than a question about a pen.

必要 · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, as a line on a website: "A credit card is not required."

クレジットカードは必要(ひつよう)ありません。

必要(ひつよう)ありません is the standard polite negative and the usual wording on notices. 必要(ひつよう)ない would be too blunt for a public-facing page, and 必要(ひつよう)ではございません would be stiffer than most sites go.

必要 · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 明日(あした)は早(はや)く起(お)きるが必要(ひつよう)です。

✅ 明日(あした)は早(はや)く起(お)きる必要(ひつよう)があります。

が can only mark a noun. When what is needed is an action, the dictionary form attaches straight to 必要(ひつよう) and the sentence closes with ある.

必要 · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この会社(かいしゃ)には君(きみ)が必要(ひつよう)だ。 (b) この会社(かいしゃ)にとって君(きみ)は大切(たいせつ)な人(ひと)だ。

(a) says the company cannot function without you. 必要(ひつよう) frames a person as something the organisation requires, so the compliment is real but cool - it is about what you produce. In a film this is the line a boss says to stop someone resigning.

(b) says you are valued. 大切(たいせつ) is about worth rather than utility, and it is the word you would use about a person if you wanted the sentence to sound warm. Both are natural Japanese; only one of them is affectionate.
