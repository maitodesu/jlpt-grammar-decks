～ないで

Meaning · without doing something - and, in speech, a bare "don't"

～ないで is the negative て-form of a verb. Like the ordinary て-form it hooks one clause onto the next, but it hooks on a non-event: the thing that did not happen while the main action went ahead. You met it at N5 inside ～ないでください; that で was never decoration, and the form stands on its own.

～ないで · Formation

Build the ない-form, then add で. Nothing else changes.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ない-form + で 急いそぐ → 急いそがないで
Verb (る-verb) ない-form + で 閉しめる → 閉しめないで
Verb (する) しないで 運動うんどうする → 運動うんどうしないで
Verb (来くる) 来こないで 来くる → 来こないで
Written / formal equivalent ない-form − ない + ずに 急いそぐ → 急いそがずに
With a following ending ないで + ください／ほしい／くれる 急いそがないでください
い-adjective not available - use くなくて 高たかい → ❌高たかくないで
な-adjective / Noun not available - use でなくて 静しずかだ → ❌静しずかじゃないで

～ないで · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ないで is verb-only. If the negated word is an adjective or a noun, the joining form is なくて, and no で version exists.

Note - the same string does three jobs, and context separates them cleanly. (1) Without doing: 地図ちずを見みないで歩あるいた. (2) Instead of doing: 勉強べんきょうしないでゲームをした. (3) A negative request, with ください or with nothing at all: 開あけないでください／開あけないで.

～ないで · Examples

けさは**食たべないで**家いえを出でた。
I left the house this morning without eating.

何なにも**買かわないで**帰かえりました。
I came home without buying anything.

靴くつを**脱ぬがないで**入はいってください。
Please come in without taking your shoes off.

辞書じしょを**使つかわないで**読よんでみましょう。
Let's try reading it without using a dictionary.

～ないで · Examples (cont.)

日曜日にちようびはどこにも**行いかないで**、一日中いちにちじゅうゲームをしていた。
On Sunday I didn't go anywhere - I played games all day.

お願ねがい、この話はなしはまだ**言いわないで**ね。
Please - don't tell anyone about this yet.

昨日きのうは疲つかれていたので、晩ばんごはんも**作つくらないで**そのまま寝ねてしまいました。
I was so tired yesterday that I fell asleep without even making dinner.

説明書せつめいしょを**読よまないで**組くみ立たてたら、ねじが二本にほん余あまってしまった。
I assembled it without reading the instructions and ended up with two screws left over.

～ないで · Nuance & register

The joining ないで is register-neutral. It appears in casual speech and in ordinary polite writing alike, and the politeness of the sentence is decided at the end, by the main verb: 傘かさを持もたないで出でかけた and 傘かさを持もたないで出でかけました differ only there.

The request use is different, and it is the one to watch. Bare ないで with nothing after it - 見みないで, 行いかないで - is casual and emotionally direct. It is what you say to a friend, a partner, a child or a pet, and it can sound pleading rather than commanding. ね softens it further (忘わすれないでね); よ presses. Said to a stranger or a superior it is far too blunt, and you want ないでください at minimum, or ないでいただけますか if you are genuinely asking a favour.

～ないで · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two neighbouring endings live on the same stem and are worth having ready: ～ないでほしい「I'd rather you didn't」, which reports a wish instead of issuing an order, and ～ないでくれる？／～ないでくれない？「could you not…?」, casual but softer than the bare form because it is a question.

In writing - reports, instructions, signage, anything with a formal voice - the joining ないで is routinely replaced by ずに. That swap is not optional in a stiff register; 確認かくにんしないで送おくりました in a written apology reads as spoken language.

～ないで · ～ないで vs ～ずに

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register. ないで is spoken and neutral; ずに is written and formal. Only ずに has an irregular form: する becomes せずに, never ❌しずに.

ずに is a survival from classical Japanese, and it attaches to the same negative stem: 書かかない → 書かかずに, 食たべない → 食たべずに, 来くる → 来こずに.

彼かれは何なにも**言いわずに**部屋へやを出でて行いった。
He left the room without saying a word.

無理むりをせずにゆっくり休やすんでください。
Please rest properly and don't push yourself.

～ないで · ～ないで vs ～ずに (cont.)

Two limits on ずに. It cannot carry a request: there is no ❌見みずにください - requests are ないで territory. And the instead of reading is weaker with ずに; for that sense ないで is the natural choice in speech.

You will also meet bare ず with no に, joining clauses in written Japanese: 誰だれにも相談そうだんせず、一人ひとりで決きめた. Treat it as the same word wearing its most formal clothes.

～ないで · ～ないで vs ～なくて

Rule of thumb - ないで tells you how the next thing happened; なくて tells you why it happened.

朝あさごはんを**食たべないで**会社かいしゃへ行いった。
I went to work without eating breakfast.

朝あさごはんを**食たべなくて**、おなかがすいた。
I didn't eat breakfast, so I got hungry.

～ないで · ～ないで vs ～なくて (cont.)

The first sentence is about the manner of going; the second gives the cause of the hunger. The practical consequence is that なくて cannot introduce a request or a command - ❌食たべなくてください is not Japanese - and that a ないで clause must be followed by something the same subject deliberately did. This distinction has its own page at this level; it repays the time.

～ないで · ～ないで vs ～ないように

Rule of thumb - ないで is without doing it; ないように is so that it does not happen. One reports, the other aims.

窓まどを**閉しめないで**出でかけた。
I went out without closing the window.

風邪かぜをひかないように、上着うわぎを持もって行いきます。
I'll take a jacket so I don't catch a cold.

～ないで · ～ないで vs ～ないように (cont.)

The difference matters most in requests. 遅おくれないでください is a flat "please don't be late"; 遅おくれないようにしてください is "please make sure you're not late", which frames the same content as advice and lands far more gently. ように also survives being said upward, where the bare negative request does not.

～ないで · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 見みないて → ✅ 見みないで
    The negative て-form is ないで. There is no form ❌ないて anywhere in the language.

  • ❌ 静しずかじゃないで集中しゅうちゅうできない。 → ✅ 静しずかじゃなくて集中しゅうちゅうできない。
    ないで attaches only to verbs. Adjectives and nouns join with なくて / でなくて.

  • ❌ 準備じゅんびをしずに出でかけた。 → ✅ 準備じゅんびをせずに出でかけた。
    する is irregular before ずに: the stem is せ, not し.

  • ❌ 朝あさごはんを食たべなくて出でかけた。 → ✅ 朝あさごはんを食たべないで出でかけた。
    Going out is not caused by not eating. When the negative describes the manner of the main action, it must be ないで.

～ないで · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、そこに座すわらないで。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、そちらには座すわらないでください。
    Bare ないで is intimate and blunt. Upward it needs ください, or better, a question form.

～ないで · Practice 1

1. 傘かさを持もた____出でかけたので、ぬれてしまった。

傘かさを持もたないで出でかけたので、ぬれてしまった。

Not taking an umbrella is the manner in which the going-out happened, so the joining form is ないで. Writing 持もたなくて would claim that going out was caused by the lack of an umbrella.

～ないで · Practice 2

2. 会議かいぎの資料しりょうを確認かくにん（しないで・しなくて）送おくってしまいました。

資料しりょうを確認かくにんしないで送おくってしまいました。

The sending is the main event and the not-checking describes how it was done - carelessly, in one motion. しなくて would need a consequence after it, such as 確認かくにんしなくて、あとで困こまった.

～ないで · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, in a formal written register: "He signed the contract without reading it."

彼かれは契約書けいやくしょを**読よまずに**サインした。

ずに is the written counterpart of ないで, and a sentence about contracts is exactly where it belongs. 読よまないで is not wrong, but it sounds spoken.

～ないで · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昨日きのうは宿題しゅくだいをしずにねてしまった。

✅ 昨日きのうは宿題しゅくだいをせずに寝ねてしまった。

The ず ending takes an irregular stem from する - せ. Every other verb uses its ordinary ない-stem, which is why this one is so easy to get wrong.

～ないで · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 電気でんきを消けさないで。 (b) 電気でんきを消けさないでください。

(a) is bare and intimate. With no ending after it, it reads as an immediate, emotional "don't!" - the kind of thing said to a family member or a close friend, and depending on the tone it can sound closer to pleading than to ordering.

(b) adds the standard polite request ending. It is neutral, usable with a colleague or a stranger, and it sounds like an instruction rather than a reaction. Between the two sit 消けさないでね (softer) and 消けさないでくれる？ (casual but framed as a question).

～ないで vs ～なくて

Meaning · the two negative て-forms - without doing versus because … not

Japanese has one positive て-form and two negative ones. ～ないで joins a non-event to an action and says how that action happened. ～なくて joins a non-event to a consequence and says why it happened. They are not stylistic variants, and swapping them usually produces a sentence that means nothing.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Formation

Both start from the ない-form. The split is in the ending, and in what the word being negated is.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb → without doing ない-form + で 言いう → 言いわないで
Verb → because not ない-form − い + くて 言いう → 言いわなくて
Verb (する) しないで／しなくて 練習れんしゅうする → 練習れんしゅうしないで／しなくて
Verb (来くる) 来こないで／来こなくて 来くる → 来こないで／来こなくて
い-adjective ～くない → ～くなくて (no ないで) 安やすい → 安やすくなくて
な-adjective じゃない → じゃなくて (no ないで) 便利べんりだ → 便利べんりじゃなくて
Noun じゃない → じゃなくて (no ないで) 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいじゃなくて
Written equivalent of ないで ない-form − ない + ずに 言いう → 言いわずに

～ないで vs ～なくて · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - なくて can negate anything; ないで only ever negates a verb. So if the word before the blank is an adjective or a noun, the answer is なくて before you have thought about meaning at all.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Examples

上着うわぎを**着きないで**外そとに出でた。
I went outside without putting a jacket on.

返事へんじが**来こなくて**、心配しんぱいです。
No reply has come, and I'm worried.

席せきを**立たたないで**待まっていてください。
Please wait without leaving your seat.

意味いみがわからなくて、先生せんせいに聞ききました。
I didn't understand what it meant, so I asked the teacher.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Examples (cont.)

息子むすこは野菜やさいを**食たべないで**肉にくばかり食たべる。
My son leaves the vegetables and eats nothing but meat.

部屋へやが**広ひろくなくて**、ベッドが入はいりませんでした。
The room wasn't big enough, so the bed wouldn't fit.

原因げんいんは雨あめじゃなくて、工事こうじでした。
The cause wasn't the rain - it was the roadworks.

電車でんしゃが**動うごかなくて**、面接めんせつに三十分さんじゅっぷんも遅おくれてしまいました。
The trains weren't running, and I ended up half an hour late for my interview.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Examples (cont.)

誰だれにも**相談そうだんしないで**会社かいしゃを辞やめたので、親おやにひどく怒おこられました。
I quit my job without talking to anyone about it, and my parents were furious.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Nuance & register

Neither form is marked for politeness. Both sit in the middle of the sentence, and the ending decides the register.

What なくて is marked for is control. Causal なくて is at its most natural when the thing that failed to happen was outside the speaker's hands - the bus, the weather, an email, your own memory or ability: 見みつからなくて, 間まに合あわなくて, 来こなくて, 思おもい出だせなくて. When the non-action was a choice you made, native speakers reach for なかったので／なかったから instead: 勉強べんきょうしなかったので、試験しけんに落おちた rather than ❌勉強べんきょうしなくて、試験しけんに落おちた.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Nuance & register (cont.)

The other half of that restriction is what comes after なくて. It wants a state, a feeling or an involuntary result - 困こまった, 寂さびしい, 安心あんしんした, よかった, 大変たいへんだった. It does not want a deliberate next action, and it flatly cannot take a request, an invitation or an intention. That single fact resolves most of the exam questions on this pair.

Two frozen expressions are worth memorising whole, because you will use them constantly and both take なくて: 来こられなくてすみません「sorry I couldn't come」 and 大おおきい事故じこにならなくてよかった「thank goodness it wasn't a serious accident」.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, ないで has a written twin, ずに, and なくて does not. In a formal report 確認かくにんせずに replaces 確認かくにんしないで, while a causal なくて in the same document would normally be rewritten as ないため - 届とどかないため、再送さいそういたします.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Manner vs cause

Rule of thumb - ask what the second clause is. An action performed → ないで. A result suffered → なくて.

めがねをかけないで運転うんてんした。
I drove without wearing my glasses.

めがねがなくて運転うんてんできなかった。
I didn't have my glasses, so I couldn't drive.

In the first, the driving happened and the not-wearing describes the state I was in while doing it - one event, described two ways. In the second there are two facts in a chain: no glasses, therefore no driving. English blurs this because both come out as "without" or "didn't … so"; Japanese does not.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Manner vs cause (cont.)

A useful test: if you can insert 「そのまま」 or 「その状態じょうたいで」 before the main verb and the sentence still works, you want ないで.

～ないで vs ～なくて · What can follow each

Rule of thumb - requests, commands, invitations and intentions can only follow ないで. なくて is followed by things that happen to you.

砂糖さとうを**入いれないで**ください。
Please don't put sugar in it.

砂糖さとうが**入はいっていなくて**、安心あんしんしました。
There was no sugar in it, which was a relief.

～ないで vs ～なくて · What can follow each (cont.)

There is no ❌入いれなくてください, no ❌行いかなくて、帰かえろう, no ❌待またなくて、先さきに始はじめます. If the sentence ends in ください, ましょう, つもり, たい or an imperative, the negative in front of it is ないで.

The mirror of that rule: an emotion word after the negative almost forces なくて. 眠ねむれなくて辛つらかった, 会あえなくて残念ざんねんです, 雨あめが降ふらなくてよかった.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Adjectives and nouns: only なくて exists

Rule of thumb - ないで is verb-only. Adjectives and nouns have exactly one negative て-form, and it is なくて / でなくて.

この部屋へやは静しずかじゃなくて、勉強べんきょうできません。
This room isn't quiet, so I can't study.

値段ねだんは高たかくなくて、味あじもよかったです。
It wasn't expensive, and it tasted good too.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Adjectives and nouns: only なくて exists (cont.)

Note the second sentence: with adjectives, なくて is not always causal. It can simply be the and of a list - "not A, and B" - exactly as the positive て-form links two descriptions. The same neutral linking shows up in corrections, where じゃなくて means "not A but B": 明日あしたじゃなくて、あさってです.

For nouns the formal written variant is ではなくて, and in the stiffest registers ではなく with no て at all: 対象たいしょうは学生がくせいではなく、社会人しゃかいじんです.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ ここに荷物にもつを置おかなくてください。 → ✅ ここに荷物にもつを置おかないでください。
    A request can only follow ないで. This is the single most common error with the pair, and it survives well past N4 if it is not fixed early.

  • ❌ 部屋へやが明あかるくないで、本ほんが読よめない。 → ✅ 部屋へやが明あかるくなくて、本ほんが読よめない。
    明あかるい is an adjective, and adjectives have no ないで form at all.

  • ❌ 鍵かぎが見みつからないで、困こまりました。 → ✅ 鍵かぎが見みつからなくて、困こまりました。
    Being stuck is a result, not an action performed while not finding the key. Causal chain → なくて.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 練習れんしゅうしなくて、試合しあいに負まけた。 → ✅ 練習れんしゅうしなかったので、試合しあいに負まけた。
    The non-action was a choice. Causal なくて prefers things you could not help; for deliberate causes use なかったので／なかったから.

  • ❌ 朝あさごはんを食たべなくて会社かいしゃに行いきます。 → ✅ 朝あさごはんを食たべないで会社かいしゃに行いきます。
    Going to work is a deliberate act, and skipping breakfast is the manner of doing it, not its cause.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 1

1. 昨日きのうは暑あつくて、エアコンを消けさ____寝ねてしまった。

エアコンを消けさないで寝ねてしまった。

Sleeping is something the speaker did, and leaving the air conditioner on describes the state they were in while doing it. That is manner, so ないで.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 2

2. バスが来こ（ないで・なくて）、一時間いちじかんも待まちました。

バスが来こなくて、一時間いちじかんも待まちました。

The waiting is a consequence the speaker suffered, and the bus not coming was outside their control - the textbook profile for causal なくて.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Please don't decide anything without asking me."

私わたしに聞きかないで決きめないでください。

Two negatives, both ないで, and for different reasons: the first is without asking (manner), the second is the negative request. Neither slot can take なくて.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この店みせは安やすくないで、あまり人気にんきがない。

✅ この店みせは安やすくなくて、あまり人気にんきがない。

安やすい is an い-adjective. Only verbs form ないで; every other word class negates and joins with なくて, built from the くない form.

～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 連絡れんらくしないで帰かえりました。 (b) 連絡れんらくがなくて心配しんぱいしました。

(a) is about the speaker's own behaviour: they went home, and the going home was done without getting in touch first. The focus is on the manner of one action, and it carries a faint sense of something skipped that should have been done.

(b) is about what happened to the speaker as a result of someone else's silence. There are two linked facts - no contact, therefore worry - and the second half is a feeling rather than a deliberate act, which is exactly the environment causal なくて wants.

～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to

Meaning · you have to do it; there is no option of not doing it

This is the same double-negative machine you met at N5 in ～なくてはいけない: if you don't do it, that won't do. What changes here is the conditional in front. ～なければ is the ば-conditional of ない, and it is the tidier, more formal of the two ways in. なければならない is the wording of rules, contracts and careful written Japanese.

～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Formation

ない is an い-adjective, so it conditionalises exactly like one: 高たかい → 高たかければ, therefore ない → なければ. Build the ない-form, drop the final い, add ければ.

～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ない-form − い + ければ 払はらう → 払はらわなければならない
Verb (る-verb) ない-form − い + ければ 続つづける → 続つづけなければならない
Verb (する) しなければならない 提出ていしゅつする → 提出ていしゅつしなければならない
Verb (来くる) 来こなければならない 来くる → 来こなければならない
い-adjective ～くない → ～くなければ 新あたらしい → 新あたらしくなければならない
な-adjective drop だ + でなければ 安全あんぜんだ → 安全あんぜんでなければならない
Noun noun + でなければ 本人ほんにん → 本人ほんにんでなければならない
Polite いけません／なりません 払はらわなければいけません
Past いけなかった／ならなかった 払はらわなければなりませんでした

～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Casual contraction なければ → なきゃ 払はらわなきゃ

Rule of thumb - the ending ならない is a verb, so its polite form is なりません, never ❌ならないです. Same for いけない → いけません.

Note - なければ on its own is just an ordinary conditional and has nothing to do with obligation: 時間じかんがなければ、また今度こんどでいいですよ 「if you haven't got time, another day is fine」. The obligation only appears when いけない or ならない closes the sentence.

～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Examples

もう**出でなければなりません**。
I have to leave now.

宿題しゅくだいをしなければいけない。
I have to do my homework.

明日あしたも会社かいしゃに**来こなければなりません**か。
Do I have to come in tomorrow as well?

申もうし込こみは本人ほんにんでなければなりません。
The application must be made by the person themselves.

～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Examples (cont.)

健康けんこうのために、もっと野菜やさいを**食たべなければいけない**と思おもっている。
I know I ought to eat more vegetables for my health.

自転車じてんしゃは決きめられた場所ばしょにとめなければならない。
Bicycles must be parked in the designated area.

来月らいげつ引ひっ越こすので、今週中こんしゅうちゅうに新あたらしい部屋へやを**決きめなければいけません**。
I'm moving next month, so I have to settle on a new flat this week.

台風たいふうが近ちかづいているので、明日あしたの試合しあいは中止ちゅうしにしなければならないかもしれません。
A typhoon is on the way, so we may have to call tomorrow's match off.

Nuance & register

There is only one meaning in this family, and five common ways to say it. Put them on a single line and the choice becomes mechanical:

  1. ～なければならない - the most formal. Laws, contracts, manuals, academic writing, official announcements. Impersonal: it states a requirement that holds for anyone, not a chore facing one person. 2. ～なければいけません - formal-polite speech and ordinary business writing. Still stiff, but it can be about you. 3. ～なくてはいけません - the everyday polite form (this is the N5 branch). What you say to a colleague or in a classroom. 4. ～ないといけない - the everyday spoken form, and the most frequent of all in conversation. 5. ～なきゃ／～なくちゃ - casual, contracted, usually with the ending dropped entirely. What people actually say to themselves and to friends.

Nuance & register (cont.)

Two axes are hiding inside that list. なければ versus なくては is a matter of tidiness: なければ is the written-feeling conditional, なくては the more conversational one. ならない versus いけない is a matter of who the requirement belongs to: ならない states a standing rule with no addressee, while いけない points at a situation and often at a person.

Because なければならない is impersonal, it is a safe thing to say upward. You cannot comfortably tell your boss 確認かくにんしなければいけませんよ, but you can report the rule - 規則きそくでは部長ぶちょうのサインが必要ひつようです - and let the rule do the pushing.

Do not use it about a mild preference. なければならない is heavy; if what you mean is "you'd be better off", the pattern is ～たほうがいい, and reaching for the obligation form instead sounds alarming.

Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, the polite negative-past forms come out longer than learners expect: 待またなければなりませんでした「I had to wait」. There is no shortcut, and in speech people simply say 待またなきゃいけなかった.

～なければ vs ～なくては

Rule of thumb - identical meaning. なければ is the neater, more written conditional; なくては is a shade more conversational.

会費かいひは三月さんがつまでに**納おさめなければなりません**。
Membership fees must be paid by March.

会費かいひ、三月さんがつまでに**納おさめなくてはいけませんね**。
We've got to get the membership fee in by March, haven't we.

The requirement is the same; the voice is not. The first could be printed on a form. The second is two people discussing the form.

～なければ vs ～なくては (cont.)

One structural difference worth knowing: なくては contracts to なくちゃ and なければ contracts to なきゃ, and the contractions do not cross. There is no ❌なくきゃ.

いけない vs ならない

Rule of thumb - ならない for a rule that stands on its own; いけない for something a particular person has to do about a particular situation.

受験生じゅけんせいは九時くじまでに入室にゅうしつしなければならない。
Candidates must be seated by nine o'clock.

あ、九時くじまでに着つかなければいけないんだった。
Oh - I have to be there by nine.

ならない is faceless, which is why it dominates in writing and why it sounds odd when someone uses it about their own small errands. いけない is the workhorse of spoken obligation above the casual line. In a ～んです explanation, in a question, in an apology, いけない is almost always the one.

～なければならない vs ～たほうがいい

Rule of thumb - obligation versus advice. なければならない says there is no alternative; たほうがいい says one alternative is better.

薬くすりは食後しょくごに**飲のまなければなりません**。
The medicine must be taken after meals.

薬くすりは食後しょくごに**飲のんだほうがいい**ですよ。
You're better off taking the medicine after meals.

The first is the pharmacist reading the instruction; the second is a friend passing on a tip. Learners routinely over-use the obligation form because English "you have to" is so mild - but Japanese なければならない is not mild, and using it for a suggestion makes a recommendation sound like a regulation.

Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行いくなければなりません。 → ✅ 行いかなければなりません。
    The pattern builds on the ない-form, never on the dictionary form: 行いかない → 行いかなければ.

  • ❌ 学生がくせいだなければいけません。 → ✅ 学生がくせいでなければいけません。
    Nouns and な-adjectives negate through で(は)ない, so the conditional is でなければ.

  • ❌ 急いそがなければならないです。 → ✅ 急いそがなければなりません。
    ならない is the negative of the verb なる. Verbs do not take です to become polite.

  • ❌ 部屋へやが明あかるいなければいけません。 → ✅ 部屋へやが明あかるくなければいけません。
    い-adjectives negate to くない first, and only then take ければ.

Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 来こなければならなくないです。（intending "you don't have to come"）→ ✅ 来こなくてもいいです。
    You cannot negate the obligation form to release someone from it. The opposite of なければならない is a different pattern: なくてもいい.

～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 三月さんがつまでに申もうし込こまなければならない。 (b) 三月さんがつまでに申もうし込こまなければいけない。

(b) attaches the same requirement to a situation and usually to a person: the speaker, or whoever they are talking to, has this to deal with. Add ね or んです and it becomes unmistakably conversational - which you cannot really do to (a).

～ないといけない

Meaning · have to; must - the version people actually say out loud

Third conditional, same machine. ～なくては and ～なければ build obligation out of if you don't … that won't do; ～ないと does the same with the と conditional. What makes it worth a page of its own is frequency: in conversation this form outnumbers the textbook ones, and it has a trick the others do not - it can stop in the middle and still be a complete sentence.

～ないといけない · Formation

Take the ない-form and add と. Then close with いけない, だめだ, or nothing at all.

Rule of thumb - the verb in front of と must be negative. 行いかないといけない is "I have to go"; 行いくといけない is a different sentence altogether - "it would be bad if I went."

～ないといけない · Examples

そろそろ**帰かえらないといけない**。
I have to head home soon.

銀行ぎんこうに**行いかないといけません**。
I have to go to the bank.

あ、ごみを**出ださないと**。
Oh - I need to put the rubbish out.

靴くつが小ちいさくなったので、新あたらしいのを**買かわないといけない**。
My shoes have got too small, so I have to buy new ones.

～ないといけない · Examples (cont.)

このプールは会員かいいんでないといけないんですか。
Do you have to be a member to use this pool?

子こどもが熱ねつを出だしたので、早はやく迎むかえに**行いかないといけなくなりました**。
My child has come down with a fever, so now I have to go and collect them early.

週末しゅうまつまでにこの本ほんを読よみ終おわらないといけないんです。
The thing is, I have to finish this book by the weekend.

来週らいしゅうの出張しゅっちょうの前まえに、ホテルもチケットも自分じぶんで予約よやくしないといけません。
Before next week's business trip I have to book the hotel and the tickets myself.

～ないといけない · Nuance & register

～ないといけない sits in the middle of the obligation ladder and leans casual. In plain form it is what friends, family and colleagues use with each other all day. In いけません it is perfectly usable at work, though it still sounds like speech rather than writing - a report or a notice would say なければならない.

The ending falls off constantly. 急いそがないと, 買かわないとね, そろそろ寝ねないとな are not fragments; the trailing と carries the whole meaning, and adding いけない would sound oddly complete. Learners consistently under-produce this and it is one of the quickest wins in sounding natural. ね invites agreement, な is a masculine-leaning mutter to oneself.

～ないといけない · Nuance & register (cont.)

～ないとだめ swaps a softer ending for a blunter one. だめ is flat and physical - "no good" - so 出ださないとだめだよ presses harder than 出ださないといけないよ. Said to a child or a close friend it is normal; said to a colleague it can sting.

Two things to file away. First, ならない almost never follows と: you will occasionally hear ～ないとならない, but it is uncommon and いけない is the safe choice. Second, in Kansai the whole family is replaced by ～なあかん (行いかなあかん, せなあかん), which you will hear constantly in the west and in comedy, and which no textbook prepares you for.

～ないといけない · Nuance & register (cont.)

As with every obligation form, aiming it at someone above you is strong. Reporting your own obligation is always safe; telling your boss 確認かくにんしないといけませんよ is not.

～ないといけない · ～ないといけない vs ～なければならない

Rule of thumb - same requirement, different room. ないと belongs in the kitchen; なければならない belongs on the noticeboard.

申込書もうしこみしょは窓口まどぐちに**提出ていしゅつしなければならない**。
Application forms must be submitted at the counter.

申込書もうしこみしょ、窓口まどぐちに**出ださないといけないんだって**。
Apparently you have to hand the form in at the counter.

The information is identical. The first is impersonal and could be printed; the second is one person relaying it to another, complete with んだ and hearsay って. Mixing them up is not a grammar error, it is a costume error - and it is just as noticeable.

～ないといけない · ～ないと, and stopping there

Rule of thumb - a bare ～ないと at the end of an utterance is a finished sentence, not a broken one. The unspoken いけない is understood.

やばい、電車でんしゃ来くる。走はしらないと。
Uh-oh, the train's coming. We've got to run.

明日あした試験しけんだから、今日きょうは**寝ねないとね**。
I've got an exam tomorrow, so I'd better get some sleep.

This works because Japanese lets a conditional trail off when the consequence is obvious, and "that would be bad" is always obvious. The same trick is available to なきゃ and なくちゃ, which is why the casual end of this family so often sounds unfinished to a learner's ear. It is not.

Obligation ～ないと vs the plain conditional ～ないと

Rule of thumb - if a consequence follows, と is just "if". Obligation only appears when the consequence is いけない/だめ, or is left unsaid.

早はやく**出でないと**、電車でんしゃに間まに合あわないよ。
If we don't leave soon we won't make the train.

早はやく**出でないと**。
We've got to get going.

Same five words, two different sentences. In the first, と behaves exactly as it does in any conditional - the second half spells out what goes wrong. In the second there is no second half, so the listener supplies the standard one and the whole thing reads as obligation.

Obligation ～ないと vs the plain conditional ～ないと (cont.)

This is also why ないと cannot be used for a rule stated in the abstract. A conditional needs a situation; a regulation with no situation attached wants なければならない.

～ないといけない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日あしたは早はやく**起おきるといけない**。 → ✅ 明日あしたは早はやく**起おきないといけない**。
    The obligation lives in the negative. 起おきるといけない means "it would be a problem if I got up", which is the opposite of the intended sentence.

  • ❌ 三人以上さんにんいじょうだといけません。（intending "there must be three or more people"）→ ✅ 三人以上さんにんいじょうでないといけません。
    Nouns negate with でない before と. だと is the affirmative conditional and flips the meaning.

～ないといけない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 待またないといけないでした。 → ✅ 待またないといけませんでした。／待またないといけなかった。
    いけない is a negative predicate in its own right; make it past by conjugating it (いけなかった) or polite-past (いけませんでした), not by hanging でした on the plain form.

  • ❌ 急いそがないとならないです。 → ✅ 急いそがないといけません。／急いそがなければなりません。
    ならない is at home after なければ/なくては, not after と, and it never takes です.

  • ❌（in a written report）至急しきゅう対応たいおうしないといけない。 → ✅ 至急しきゅう対応たいおうしなければならない。
    Not ungrammatical, just spoken. In documents the と branch of the family reads as if someone forgot they were writing.

～ないといけない · Practice 1

1. 明日あしたは六時ろくじの電車でんしゃに乗のる。だから五時ごじに起おき____いけない。

五時ごじに起おきないといけない。

The obligation form needs the negative in front of と: 起おきない + と + いけない. The literal logic is "if I don't get up at five, that's no good."

～ないといけない · Practice 2

Here と is doing ordinary conditional work, and the two halves have to line up: not running leads to not making it. 走はしると間まに合あわない would claim that running is what makes you late.

The trailing ないと with the ending left off is the natural shape for remembering a task out loud. 書かかないといけない is fine too, but the fuller form sounds like reporting the obligation rather than reacting to it.

Without the negative, the sentence asks whether showing your passport would cause a problem. The obligation reading requires 見みせない before と.

～ないといけない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部屋へやを片付かたづけないといけないよ。 (b) 部屋へやを片付かたづけないとだめだよ。

(a) states the requirement and lets よ add a nudge. It is the ordinary spoken way to point something out, and it can be said sympathetically - the speaker may well be on the listener's side about the chore.

(b) replaces the neutral ending with だめ, which is flat and unarguable. It is what a parent says to a child or one close friend says to another, and it carries disapproval that (a) does not. To a colleague it would sound like being told off.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ

Meaning · gotta - the same obligation, squeezed down for speech

These are not new grammar. なくちゃ is なくては with the syllables worn off, and なきゃ is なければ with the same treatment. What they add is register: they are what obligation sounds like in an actual conversation, and they usually appear with the いけない/ならない tail chopped off entirely. Learners are blindsided by なきゃ because no textbook sentence ever contains it and every real one does.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Formation

Japanese contracts ては to ちゃ everywhere (見みては → 見みちゃ), and ければ to きゃ. Apply that to the two obligation conditionals and you get these.

Verb (する) しなくちゃ／しなきゃ 洗濯せんたくする → 洗濯せんたくしなきゃ

Rule of thumb - the two contractions come from different parents and do not mix. なくては → なくちゃ; なければ → なきゃ. There is no ❌なくきゃ and no ❌なけちゃ.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Examples

もう**寝ねなきゃ**。
I've got to go to bed.

あ、電気でんき消けさなきゃ。
Oh - I need to turn the light off.

明日あした早はやいから、そろそろ**帰かえらなくちゃ**。
I've got an early start, so I should be heading home.

宿題しゅくだい、今日きょう中じゅうに**終おわらせなきゃいけない**んだ。
The thing is, I've got to get the homework finished today.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Examples (cont.)

冷蔵庫れいぞうこが空からっぽだ。買かい物ものに**行いかなきゃ**。
The fridge is empty. I need to go shopping.

傘かさ、持もって**行いかなくちゃだめだよ**。午後ごごから雨あめだって。
You've got to take an umbrella - apparently it's raining from the afternoon.

来週らいしゅう友ともだちの結婚式けっこんしきだから、新あたらしい服ふくを**買かわなきゃ**いけないなあ。
My friend's wedding is next week, so I'm going to have to buy something new to wear.

部屋へやがこんなに散ちらかっているのに、片付かたづけなくちゃと思おもうだけで何なにもしていない。
The room is in this state and all I do is think I ought to tidy it, without ever actually starting.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Nuance & register

Casual. That is the whole point, and it is also the whole restriction. なくちゃ and なきゃ belong in speech among friends, family and close colleagues, and in writing only where writing imitates speech - messages, chat, blogs, dialogue in manga and novels. They do not appear in reports, emails to clients, exam answers or announcements.

They are not sloppy or uneducated, and they are not gender-marked: adults of every age and both sexes use them constantly. What they are is unguarded. Saying 帰かえらなきゃ lets the listener see you making a decision, which is exactly right with a friend and wrong in front of a customer.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Nuance & register (cont.)

なきゃ is the more frequent of the two in everyday Tokyo speech. なくちゃ is a touch lighter and rounder, and it turns up often when someone is talking to a child or being deliberately breezy; a few speakers hear it as slightly cuter than なきゃ, but nobody would call it a different level of politeness. The stretched なくっちゃ adds emphasis - これは見みなくっちゃ「this I've got to see」.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Nuance & register (cont.)

The tail is normally dropped. When it is kept, it stays in plain form: なきゃいけない, なきゃだめ, and in very casual Tokyo speech なきゃなんない (from ならない). Attaching a polite tail is the classic learner error - you cannot half-contract a sentence, and ❌行いかなきゃいけません mixes two registers inside one predicate. If you need politeness, expand the whole thing back out to なければなりません.

One more thing that catches people out in listening: because the ending is missing, these forms often end an utterance on a rising or trailing intonation and sound unfinished. あ、洗濯せんたくしなきゃ… is a complete thought. Do not wait for the rest of it.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · ～なきゃ vs ～なくちゃ

Rule of thumb - the same word twice over. Choose by ear, not by meaning; なきゃ is the more common default.

そろそろ**出でなきゃ**。
I'd better get going.

そろそろ**出でなくちゃ**。
I'd better get going.

There is no difference of meaning, obligation strength or politeness to report, and a page that invented one would be lying. The only observable tendencies are that なきゃ is heard more often overall, なくちゃ sounds slightly softer and is common in speech directed at children, and only なくちゃ has the emphatic stretched form なくっちゃ.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · ～なきゃ vs ～ないと

Rule of thumb - both are casual spoken obligation. ないと is neutral and usable slightly further up the politeness scale; なきゃ is more informal and more self-directed.

会議かいぎの資料しりょう、作つくらないといけませんね。
We'll need to put the meeting materials together, won't we.

うわ、資料しりょう作つくらなきゃ。
Ugh - I've got to make the handouts.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · ～なきゃ vs ～ないと (cont.)

ないと survives being made polite: 行いかないといけません is fine at work. なきゃ does not. And なきゃ leans towards things the speaker is telling themselves, which is why it so often follows あ or うわ and precedes nothing at all.

～なきゃ as obligation vs ～なきゃ as plain "unless"

Rule of thumb - なきゃ is just contracted なければ. If a consequence follows it, it is an ordinary conditional and has nothing to do with obligation.

自分じぶんで**見みなきゃ**わからないよ。
You won't know unless you look for yourself.

自分じぶんで**見みなきゃ**。
I've got to go and look for myself.

The first is "if you don't look, you won't understand" - the いけない is not implied and never was. The second has nothing after it, so the listener fills in the missing "that won't do" and hears an obligation.

～なきゃ as obligation vs ～なきゃ as plain "unless" (cont.)

The same holds for なくちゃ, and it explains a shape you will meet often: 安やすくなきゃ買かわない「I won't buy it unless it's cheap」, 君きみじゃなきゃだめだ「it has to be you」.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行いかなきゃです。 → ✅ 行いかなきゃ。／行いかなければなりません。
    There is no polite version of a contraction. です cannot be bolted onto なきゃ; if you need politeness, expand the form.

  • ❌ 行いくなきゃ → ✅ 行いかなきゃ
    The contraction still starts from the ない-form. Build 行いかない first, then squeeze.

  • ❌ 急いそがなくきゃ → ✅ 急いそがなくちゃ／急いそがなきゃ
    The two contractions have different parents and cannot be blended. なくては→なくちゃ, なければ→なきゃ.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 会員かいいんだなきゃだめです。 → ✅ 会員かいいんじゃなきゃだめだよ。
    Nouns negate to じゃない, so the contracted conditional is じゃなきゃ. Note the ending changed too - だめです after じゃなきゃ is a register mismatch.

  • ❌（in an email to a client）明日あしたまでに送おくらなきゃいけません。 → ✅ 明日あしたまでに送おくらなければなりません。
    Contractions do not belong in written business Japanese, however polite the tail you attach to them.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 1

1. やばい、電車でんしゃ行いっちゃう。走はしら____。

走はしらなきゃ。（also fine: 走はしらなくちゃ。）

The situation is urgent, spoken and self-directed, which is the natural home of the contracted forms with the ending dropped. Anything longer would sound like the speaker had time to think.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 2

2. 部長ぶちょうに（報告ほうこくしなきゃいけません・報告ほうこくしなければなりません）。

報告ほうこくしなければなりません。

なきゃ is a casual contraction and いけません is a polite ending; putting them in one predicate mixes registers inside a single word. Pick one level - fully contracted for speech among friends, fully expanded when you are being polite.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, as something you say to yourself on remembering it: "Oh no, I have to return the library books today."

あ、今日きょう図書館としょかんの本ほん返かえさなきゃ。

Muttered self-obligation drops the いけない and drops the particles too. The interjection at the front is part of the pattern: these forms almost always follow a moment of realisation.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この映画えいが、絶対ぜったい見みるなきゃ。

✅ この映画えいが、絶対ぜったい見みなきゃ。（emphatic: 見みなくっちゃ。）

なきゃ replaces なければ, which is built on the ない-form - 見みない → 見みなければ → 見みなきゃ. The dictionary form never appears in front of it.

～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 早はやく**予約よやくしなきゃ**席せきがなくなるよ。 (b) 早はやく**予約よやくしなきゃ**。

(a) is not an obligation sentence at all. なきゃ here is simply contracted なければ working as an ordinary conditional, and the second half states the consequence: book early, or the seats will be gone.

(b) leaves the consequence out. Because the missing half is understood to be "that won't do", the whole utterance is heard as the speaker realising they have to act. The contraction is identical in both; what decides the reading is whether anything follows it.

～なくてもいい

Meaning · you don't have to; it's fine not to

～なくてもいい is the exact negative counterpart of ～てもいい. Same shape, same も of concession, same verdict いい - only the thing being conceded is now a non-action: "even if you don't do it, that's fine." It does not tell anyone to stop. It removes a requirement and leaves the choice open.

～なくてもいい · Formation

Negate first, swap the final い of ない for くても, then finish with いい.

～なくてもいい · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ない-form − い + くてもいい 待まつ → 待またなくてもいい
Verb (る-verb) ない-form − い + くてもいい 着きる → 着きなくてもいい
Verb (する) しなくてもいい 予約よやくする → 予約よやくしなくてもいい

い-adjective ～くない → ～くなくてもいい 新あたらしい → 新あたらしくなくてもいい

Attaches to Form Example
Softer / politer いい → かまいません, 大丈夫だいじょうぶです 待またなくてもかまいません

Rule of thumb - the particle carries the whole meaning. ～なくて**は**いけない = must; ～なくて**も**いい = needn't. Everything else in the two patterns is shared scaffolding.

～なくてもいい · Examples

靴くつは**脱ぬがなくてもいいです**。
You don't have to take your shoes off.

急いそがなくてもいいよ。
There's no need to rush.

返事へんじは**書かかなくてもいいです**か。
Is it all right if I don't write back?

服装ふくそうはスーツでなくてもいいそうです。
I hear it doesn't have to be a suit.

～なくてもいい · Examples (cont.)

部屋へやは**広ひろくなくてもいい**けど、駅えきに近ちかいほうがいい。
The flat doesn't have to be big, but I'd rather it were near a station.

そんなに**心配しんぱいしなくてもいい**んじゃない？
Don't you think you're worrying more than you need to?

今日きょうは仕事しごとが早はやく終おわったので、残業ざんぎょうはしなくてもいいことになりました。
Work finished early today, so it turns out we don't have to stay late.

レポートは手書てがきでも印刷いんさつでもよくて、表紙ひょうしはつけなくてもかまいません。
The report may be handwritten or printed, and you needn't add a cover page.

～なくてもいい · Nuance & register

The pattern itself is neutral; いいです makes it polite in form. What is not neutral is the social act. Saying "you don't have to" means you are in a position to lift the requirement, and that is a position of authority. Between equals it is generous. Aimed upward it is presumptuous: telling your manager 部長ぶちょうは来こなくてもいいですよ sounds like you are giving them permission to skip work.

Upward, the same content goes through different wording: ご無理むりなさらないでください, お気きになさらないでください, そのままで結構けっこうです. Note that 結構けっこうです is genuinely ambiguous - it can mean "that's fine as it is" or "no thank you" - and tone and context are the only things separating them.

～なくてもいい · Nuance & register (cont.)

In speech the も disappears constantly: 行いかなくていいよ, 待またなくていいから. This is not a different pattern and carries no change in meaning; it is simply what fast Japanese does to the phrase. Learners who have only seen なくてもいい in a textbook often fail to recognise なくていい in conversation. The more emphatic casual variant is なくたっていい (謝あやまらなくたっていいよ).

Three neighbours worth knowing, in rising order of formality: なくても大丈夫だいじょうぶ (warm, spoken), なくてもかまいません「it doesn't matter if you don't」(neutral, business), なくても結構けっこうです「it is not required」(formal, institutional). かまわない is the one to reach for when you want to sound like you truly do not mind.

～なくてもいい · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, the pattern is not only about other people. ～なくてもいい about yourself reports relief or a decision: 明日あしたは早はやく起おきなくてもいいから、映画えいがでも見みよう.

～なくてもいい · ～なくてもいい vs ～てもいい

Rule of thumb - ～てもいい opens a door: you may do it. ～なくてもいい closes a duty: you needn't do it. Neither one pushes you in either direction.

写真しゃしんを**撮とってもいい**ですか。
Is it okay if I take photos?

写真しゃしんを**撮とらなくてもいい**ですか。
Is it okay if I don't take photos?

The first asks whether the action is permitted; the second asks whether the action is required. In a museum you would ask the first; on a job where photographing is part of your task, you would ask the second.

～なくてもいい · ～なくてもいい vs ～てもいい (cont.)

They pair up into a neat square with the obligation forms. てもいい may, てはいけない must not, なくてはいけない must, なくてもいい needn't. Permission and prohibition are built on the て-form; obligation and exemption on the negative stem.

～なくてもいい · ～なくてもいい vs ～ないでください

Rule of thumb - "you don't have to" is not "please don't". なくてもいい leaves the action available; ないでください takes it away.

手伝てつだわなくてもいいですよ。
You don't have to help.

手伝てつだわないでください。
Please don't help.

～なくてもいい · ～なくてもいい vs ～ないでください (cont.)

English blurs these - "you don't have to help" is often meant as a polite refusal - but Japanese does not. The first sentence releases someone who was offering; the second turns them away, and would be startling from a colleague. When you want to decline an offer kindly, the natural wording is 大丈夫だいじょうぶです、ありがとうございます, not either of these.

～なくてもいい vs ～なくてもかまいません・～なくても大丈夫

Rule of thumb - same exemption, three temperatures. 大丈夫だいじょうぶ is warm, いい is neutral, かまいません is businesslike.

無理むりに全部ぜんぶ食たべなくても大丈夫だいじょうぶだよ。
Honestly, you don't have to finish it all.

当日とうじつは印鑑いんかんをお持もちにならなくてもかまいません。
You do not need to bring a personal seal on the day.

～なくてもいい vs ～なくてもかまいません・～なくても大丈夫 (cont.)

大丈夫だいじょうぶ says don't worry about it and belongs in speech between people who know each other. かまいません says it makes no difference to us and belongs in counters, forms and customer service. Plain いい sits between them and is never wrong, which is why it is the form to learn first.

～なくてもいい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 払はらわなくてはいいです。 → ✅ 払はらわなくてもいいです。
    なくては pairs with いけない; なくても pairs with いい. Mixing them produces a pattern that does not exist.

  • ❌ 学生がくせいだなくてもいいです。 → ✅ 学生がくせいでなくてもいいです。
    Nouns and な-adjectives negate through で(は)ない, so the join is でなくても.

  • ❌ 新あたらしいじゃなくてもいい。 → ✅ 新あたらしくなくてもいい。
    新あたらしい is an い-adjective, so its negative is くない and the join is くなくても.

～なくてもいい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌（meaning "you don't have to come tomorrow"）明日あしたは来こないでください。 → ✅ 明日あしたは来こなくてもいいですよ。
    Straight from English. The ないでください version instructs the person to stay away, which is a completely different message.

  • ❌ 課長かちょうは出席しゅっせきしなくてもいいですよ。 → ✅ 課長かちょう、ご都合つごうが悪わるければ、ご欠席けっせきでも問題もんだいございません。
    Releasing someone from a duty implies you had the authority to impose it. Upward, state the situation instead of granting an exemption.

～なくてもいい · Practice 1

1. このレストランは予約よやくし____いいですよ。いつも空すいていますから。

予約よやくしなくてもいいですよ。

The restaurant is always empty, so booking is not required - the exemption reading. Note the particle: なくても with いい, never なくては.

～なくてもいい · Practice 2

2. 会議かいぎのとき、ネクタイを（しなくてもいいです・しないでください）。

Either is grammatical, but they say different things. しなくてもいいです means a tie is optional; しないでください instructs people not to wear one. For a normal dress-code announcement the first is what is meant.

～なくてもいい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, politely: "You don't need to bring anything."

何なにも**持もって来こなくてもいいですよ**。 （more formal: 何なにもお持もちいただかなくてもかまいません。）

The よ matters: without it the sentence can read as a flat instruction, whereas telling a guest not to trouble themselves should sound warm. 来くる takes the irregular negative stem 来こ.

～なくてもいい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 明日あしたのパーティーは正式せいしきな服ふくだなくてもいいです。

✅ 明日あしたのパーティーは正式せいしきな服ふくでなくてもいいです。

だ never survives in front of a negative join. A noun negates as 服ふくではない, and the exemption form built on that is 服ふくでなくてもいい.

～なくてもいい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 全部ぜんぶ読よまなくてもいいです。 (b) 全部ぜんぶ読よまなくてはいけません。

(a) removes a requirement: reading all of it is optional, and the listener may read as much or as little as they like. One particle - も - is doing that work.

(b) imposes one: all of it has to be read, and there is no choice in the matter. The は closes off the alternative that も opens. Because the two sentences differ by a single kana in the middle of a long predicate, they are easy to mishear and worth saying aloud a few times.

～必要がある

Meaning · there is a need to do it; it is necessary to do it

Read 必要ひつよう. 必要ひつよう is a noun meaning necessity, and ある is the ordinary verb of existence - so the pattern literally says a need to do X exists. That is why it feels cooler than the obligation forms: it does not push anybody, it states a fact about the situation.

～必要がある · Formation

The verb in front stays in the dictionary form. Nouns join with の.

Rule of thumb - the negative flips the particle. Affirmative 必要ひつようがある, negative 必要ひつようはない. The は is doing what は always does with a negative: marking the thing being denied. 必要ひつようがない is possible, but は is the everyday choice.

Note - the negation goes on ある, never on the verb in front. "You don't need to go" is 行いく必要ひつようはない, not ❌行いかない必要ひつようがある, which would mean something like "there is a need for you not to go."

～必要がある · Examples

パスポートを見みせる**必要ひつようがあります**。
You need to show your passport.

急いそぐ**必要ひつようはありません**。
There's no need to hurry.

もう一度いちど確認かくにんする**必要ひつようがある**。
We need to check it one more time.

この書類しょるいは翻訳ほんやくの**必要ひつようがありますか**。
Does this document need translating?

～必要がある · Examples (cont.)

心配しんぱいする**必要ひつようはない**よ。全部ぜんぶうまくいっているから。
You've no need to worry - everything's going fine.

海外かいがいで働はたらくには、ビザを申請しんせいする**必要ひつようがあります**。
To work abroad, you need to apply for a visa.

去年きょねん作つくった資料しりょうなので、数字すうじを新あたらしいものに直なおす**必要ひつようがあります**。
These materials are from last year, so the figures need updating.

会員かいいんカードをお持もちの方かたは、受付うけつけで住所じゅうしょを書かく**必要ひつようはございません**。
Members do not need to write their address at reception.

～必要がある · Nuance & register

～必要ひつようがある is the objective member of the family. It describes a requirement as a property of the situation, with nobody's authority behind it, which makes it the default in manuals, instructions, application guidance, business email and academic writing. If you have ever read a Japanese form, you have read this pattern.

That neutrality has a practical use: it lets you tell someone what has to happen without telling them what to do. ご確認かくにんいただく必要ひつようがございます is still direct, but 三月さんがつまでに提出ていしゅつする必要ひつようがあります said about a shared deadline points at the deadline, not at the listener.

～必要がある · Nuance & register (cont.)

The flip side is that it sounds stiff about small personal errands. Nobody says 今日きょうは買かい物ものをする必要ひつようがある to a friend - that is 買かい物もの行いかないと territory. Using the formal pattern for a trip to the supermarket sounds like reading from a report.

The negative is the half you will use most, and it is unusually warm for such a formal pattern. 謝あやまる必要ひつようはないよ「you don't need to apologise」 and 気きにする必要ひつようはありません「there's no need to worry about it」 are everyday reassurances. In casual speech が drops out altogether: そんなに急いそぐ必要ひつようない.

～必要がある · Nuance & register (cont.)

At the formal end, ある is replaced by its humble-polite equivalents: 必要ひつようがございます and 必要ひつようはございません, which is what shop staff and official notices use.

～必要がある · ～必要がある vs ～なければならない

Rule of thumb - 必要ひつようがある reports that a need exists; なければならない says the alternative is unacceptable. The first describes, the second compels.

この薬くすりは毎日まいにち飲のむ**必要ひつようがあります**。
This medicine needs to be taken every day.

この薬くすりは毎日まいにち飲のまなければなりません。
You must take this medicine every day.

～必要がある · ～必要がある vs ～なければならない (cont.)

Both are correct and a doctor might say either. The first sounds like an explanation of how the treatment works; the second sounds like an instruction being issued to you. When the requirement comes from a rule with force behind it - a law, a contract, a school regulation - なければならない is the stronger fit. When it comes from the nature of the task, 必要ひつようがある is.

There is also a grammatical difference worth noticing: なければならない attaches to a negative stem, while 必要ひつようがある attaches to a plain dictionary form. That makes the second much easier to build under time pressure.

～必要がある · ～必要はない vs ～なくてもいい

Rule of thumb - なくてもいい grants an exemption; 必要ひつようはない denies that a requirement ever existed.

明日あしたは**来こなくてもいいですよ**。
You don't have to come in tomorrow.

明日あしたは**来くる必要ひつようはありません**。
There is no need for you to come in tomorrow.

The first is a person releasing another person, which is why it needs よ or a smile to sound kind. The second is a statement about the schedule, and because it does not position the speaker as the one granting permission, it is often the safer thing to say upward or to a stranger.

～必要がある · ～必要はない vs ～なくてもいい (cont.)

In their negative-reassurance use they come very close: 心配しんぱいしなくてもいい and 心配しんぱいする必要ひつようはない are near-synonyms, the second slightly firmer, as if arguing the worry away rather than waving it off.

～必要がある · ～必要がある vs ～が必要だ

Rule of thumb - する必要ひつようがある needs an action; が必要ひつようだ needs a thing.

ここで靴くつを**脱ぬぐ必要ひつようがあります**。
You need to take your shoes off here.

ここでは上うわばきが**必要ひつようです**。
You need indoor shoes here.

～必要がある · ～必要がある vs ～が必要だ (cont.)

The two are often interchangeable in translation and never in grammar. A verb before 必要ひつよう requires the dictionary form and the が…ある frame; a noun before it takes が and the plain な-adjective predicate 必要ひつようだ／です. A noun can also be forced into the ある frame with の - 修理しゅうりの必要ひつようがある - which sounds distinctly more formal than 修理しゅうりが必要ひつようだ.

～必要がある · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行いきます必要ひつようがあります。 → ✅ 行いく必要ひつようがあります。
    The verb in front of 必要ひつよう is plain and non-past. ます cannot appear inside the sentence; politeness goes on あります at the end.

  • ❌ 行いかない必要ひつようがあります。 → ✅ 行いく必要ひつようはありません。
    To say there is no need, negate ある, not the verb. Negating the verb creates a need not to go, which is a different and much rarer idea.

  • ❌ ビザ必要ひつようがあります。 → ✅ ビザの必要ひつようがあります。／ビザが必要ひつようです。
    A noun cannot sit straight against 必要ひつよう. Either link it with の or switch to the が必要ひつようだ pattern.

～必要がある · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部屋へやが静しずかな必要ひつようがあります。 → ✅ 部屋へやは静しずかでなければなりません。
    Adjectives do not take this pattern at all. Requirements about qualities go through the obligation forms.

  • ❌（to a friend）そろそろ帰かえる必要ひつようがある。 → ✅ そろそろ帰かえらないと。
    Not wrong, just wrongly dressed. For your own small obligations in conversation, the ないと / なきゃ end of the family is what people actually use.

The verb stays in the dictionary form, and the question is about whether a requirement exists - exactly what this pattern is for. On a form, it is more natural than asking 押おさなくてもいいですか.

～必要がある · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, as a line of written guidance: "Applicants need to submit two photographs."

～必要がある · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 明日あしたは来こない必要ひつようがありません。

Two things went wrong. The verb must be affirmative and in the dictionary form, and the negation belongs on ある. Note also the particle flip: negatives of this pattern normally take は.

～必要がある · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 返事へんじを書かく**必要ひつようはありません**。 (b) 返事へんじを**書かかなくてもいいです**。

(a) states that the process does not call for a reply. The speaker is describing how things work, which makes it neutral and slightly formal - the wording of a notice or of someone explaining a procedure.

(b) tells the listener they are released from writing one. It implies the speaker is in a position to decide, so it sounds more personal and, depending on who is speaking to whom, either kind or presumptuous.

必要

Meaning · necessary; needed - the word itself, not the pattern

Read 必要ひつよう. It is a noun that behaves as a な-adjective: something が必要ひつようだ「is needed」, and a 必要ひつような thing is a required one. The verb pattern ～必要ひつようがある is built out of this word, but the word does plenty of work on its own, and getting its particles right is what stops learners writing English with Japanese vocabulary in it.

必要 · Formation

The thing that is needed takes が, not を - 必要ひつよう is not a verb and has no object.

Attaches to Form Example

必要 · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - 必要ひつよう is a な-adjective, so it never takes くない or かった. The negative is 必要ひつようじゃない／必要ひつようない, and the past is 必要ひつようだった.

Note - the written antonym is 不要ふよう, which you will meet on forms and labels rather than in speech: 記入きにゅう不要ふよう「no entry required」, 返却へんきゃく不要ふよう「no need to return this」.

必要 · Examples

パスポートが**必要ひつようです**。
You'll need your passport.

必要ひつようなものだけ持もって来きてください。
Please bring only what you need.

その説明せつめいは**必要ひつようない**よ。
That explanation isn't necessary.

申もうし込こみに**必要ひつような**書類しょるいは三みっつです。
There are three documents required for the application.

必要 · Examples (cont.)

急きゅうに現金げんきんが**必要ひつようになった**。
I suddenly found myself needing cash.

この仕事しごとに経験けいけんは**必要ひつようではありません**。
No experience is required for this job.

一人ひとり暮ぐらしを始はじめるとき、思おもっていたより多おおくのものが**必要ひつようだった**。
When I started living on my own, I needed more things than I had expected.

災害さいがいのときに本当ほんとうに**必要ひつような**のは、水みずと情報じょうほうだと言いわれています。
What you really need in a disaster, they say, is water and information.

必要 · Nuance & register

必要ひつよう is a Sino-Japanese word, and like most of them it leans formal and slightly abstract. It is completely ordinary in polite speech, business email, notices and writing. In casual conversation, though, Japanese usually reaches for the plain verb いる instead: a friend asks 袋ふくろいる？, not 袋ふくろが必要ひつようですか. At a convenience store counter you will hear both, because staff speech is formal.

The negatives are worth separating. 必要ひつようない is short, everyday and can sound abrupt - そんなの必要ひつようない is close to "we don't need that rubbish." 必要ひつようありません is the neutral polite version and the one to use with strangers. 必要ひつようではありません is stiffer again and belongs in writing. As a refusal at a shop counter, the standard phrase is not any of these but 大丈夫だいじょうぶです or 結構けっこうです.

必要 · Nuance & register (cont.)

必要ひつようとする is a genuine verb and a register marker: it appears in journalism, official documents and formal description - 支援しえんを必要ひつようとしている地域ちいき「areas that require assistance」. Using it in conversation would sound like reading a press release.

One more thing to notice: 必要ひつよう describes what a purpose demands. It carries no warmth. That is why it is the right word for documents, tools, time and money, and the wrong word for people - see the contrast below.

必要 · 必要 vs いる

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different word class and different register. いる is a verb and casual; 必要ひつよう is a な-adjective and formal.

レシート、いる？
D'you want the receipt?

レシートは**必要ひつようですか**。
Would you like a receipt?

必要 · 必要 vs いる (cont.)

Both take their object with が (or with no particle at all in speech), which is the one thing they share structurally. いる - written 要いる, though usually left in kana - is a う-verb: いります, いらない, いった. Beware that its past and て-forms collide with 入はいる, 行いく and the existence verb いる, which is why it is far more common in the non-past.

いる is also limited to things. For an action you need ～必要ひつようがある or one of the obligation forms: there is no ❌行いくがいる.

必要 · 必要 vs 大切・大事

Rule of thumb - 必要ひつよう = required for a purpose. 大切たいせつ／大事だいじ = important, precious, valued. Do not use 必要ひつよう about people.

家族かぞくはとても**大切たいせつです**。
My family means a great deal to me.

この仕事しごとには集中力しゅうちゅうりょくが**必要ひつようです**。
This job requires concentration.

必要 · 必要 vs 大切・大事 (cont.)

Swap them and both sentences go wrong in interesting ways. 家族かぞくが必要ひつようです sounds like you are shopping for one - the word treats its object as a resource. And 集中力しゅうちゅうりょくが大切たいせつです is fine but says something milder: concentration matters, rather than the job being impossible without it.

大事だいじ is the more conversational of the two importance words; 大切たいせつ is a shade warmer and more careful. Both are な-adjectives, like 必要ひつよう itself.

必要 · 必要だ vs 必要がある

Rule of thumb - a noun is needed → が必要ひつようだ. An action is needed → ～する必要ひつようがある.

予約よやくが**必要ひつようです**。
A reservation is required.

予約よやくする**必要ひつようがあります**。
You need to make a reservation.

The information is the same and the framing is not. The first names a thing the situation demands, and would suit a sign on a door. The second describes something the listener has to do, and suits an explanation.

必要 · 必要だ vs 必要がある (cont.)

A noun can be pushed into the ある frame with の (予約よやくの必要ひつようがあります), which sounds noticeably more bureaucratic. A verb, on the other hand, cannot use the だ frame at all - there is no ❌予約よやくするが必要ひつようです.

必要 · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 必要ひつようの書類しょるい → ✅ 必要ひつような書類しょるい
    必要ひつよう is a な-adjective when it modifies a noun. の looks tempting because the word is also a noun, but it is not how the phrase is built.

  • ❌ パスポートを必要ひつようです。 → ✅ パスポートが必要ひつようです。
    There is no verb here, so there is no object. The needed thing is the subject and takes が.

  • ❌ この靴くつは必要ひつようくない。 → ✅ この靴くつは必要ひつようじゃない。
    Only い-adjectives negate with くない. 必要ひつよう takes じゃない, ではない, or bare ない.

  • ❌ 病院びょういんに行いくが必要ひつようです。 → ✅ 病院びょういんに行いく**必要ひつようがあります**。
    A verb cannot be marked with が. To say an action is needed, switch to the 必要ひつようがある frame.

必要 · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌（to a friend, about a plastic bag）袋ふくろが必要ひつようですか。 → ✅ 袋ふくろいる？
    Grammatical and oddly formal. Among friends the everyday word for needing a thing is いる.

必要 · Practice 1

1. この手続てつづきに____な物ものを全部ぜんぶ持もって来きました。

この手続てつづきに**必要ひつよう**な物ものを全部ぜんぶ持もって来きました。

Modifying a noun, 必要ひつよう behaves as a な-adjective, so the connector is な. The に marks the purpose the things are needed for.

必要 · Practice 2

2. ペン、（いる・必要ひつようでいらっしゃいますか）？ - said to a close friend.

ペン、いる？

Between friends the plain verb is what people use; the honorific version is not merely over-polite, it is comic. 必要ひつよう itself is not rude here, but it still sounds like a form rather than a question about a pen.

必要 · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, as a line on a website: "A credit card is not required."

クレジットカードは**必要ひつようありません**。

必要ひつようありません is the standard polite negative and the usual wording on notices. 必要ひつようない would be too blunt for a public-facing page, and 必要ひつようではございません would be stiffer than most sites go.

必要 · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 明日あしたは早はやく起おきるが必要ひつようです。

✅ 明日あしたは早はやく起おきる**必要ひつようがあります**。

が can only mark a noun. When what is needed is an action, the dictionary form attaches straight to 必要ひつよう and the sentence closes with ある.

必要 · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この会社かいしゃには君きみが**必要ひつようだ**。 (b) この会社かいしゃにとって君きみは**大切たいせつな人ひとだ**。

(a) says the company cannot function without you. 必要ひつよう frames a person as something the organisation requires, so the compliment is real but cool - it is about what you produce. In a film this is the line a boss says to stop someone resigning.

(b) says you are valued. 大切たいせつ is about worth rather than utility, and it is the word you would use about a person if you wanted the sentence to sound warm. Both are natural Japanese; only one of them is affectionate.
