こと - nominaliser

Meaning · turns a verb, an adjective or a whole clause into a noun, so the sentence can talk about it

Japanese has no infinitive and no "-ing" noun. When you need an action to behave like a thing - to be liked, to be important, to be decided at a meeting - you put こと on the end of the clause. What comes out is an ordinary noun and takes が, を, は or です like any other.

こと - nominaliser · Formation

こと attaches to the plain form. Only な-adjectives and nouns change shape first, and they change in the way they always do before a noun.

こと - nominaliser · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - never ❌学生がくせいだこと. A noun needs である in front of こと, and in everyday Japanese most speakers avoid the problem altogether by using 学生がくせいだということ, which is the clause-packaging pattern treated separately at this level.

Note - こと is also an ordinary noun meaning "thing, matter", and it keeps that job here: 日本にほんのことを知しりたい ("I want to know about Japan"), 田中たなかさんのこと ("the Tanaka business", or simply Tanaka as a topic). The nominaliser is the same word doing the same thing to a clause instead of a noun.

こと - nominaliser · Examples

絵えをかくことが好すきです。
I like drawing.

約束やくそくの時間じかんを守まもることは大切たいせつです。
Keeping to the time you agreed is important.

私わたしの趣味しゅみは古ふるい切手きってを集あつめることです。
My hobby is collecting old stamps.

外国がいこくで働はたらくことを考かんがえています。
I'm thinking about working abroad.

こと - nominaliser · Examples (cont.)

兄あにが来月らいげつ結婚けっこんすることは、まだ誰だれにも話はなしていません。
I haven't told anyone yet that my brother is getting married next month.

日本語にほんごで電話でんわをかけることが、まだ少すこし怖こわいです。
Making phone calls in Japanese still frightens me a little.

新あたらしい仕事しごとで一番いちばん大変たいへんなのは、朝あさ五時ごじに起おきることです。
The hardest part of the new job is getting up at five in the morning.

部長ぶちょうが来週らいしゅう出張しゅっちょうすることは、昨日きのうの会議かいぎで決きまりました。
It was settled at yesterday's meeting that the manager is going away on business next week.

こと - nominaliser · Nuance & register

The nominaliser itself carries no politeness. What it carries is a slight lean towards the written and the abstract, which is the one thing that separates it from its rival の. こと names a fact, a rule, a plan, a proposition - something you can think about, decide, promise or announce. の names an event you are watching, hearing or living through. Where both are grammatical, の is the more spoken of the two, and a page of conversation will contain many more of them.

こと never contracts. の shrinks to ん before です, and the packaging form ということ shrinks to ってこと in casual speech, but plain こと stays as it is in every register.

趣味しゅみは…ことです deserves a line of its own because it is the first thing you will need it for. Self-introductions, job interviews and profile forms are full of it, and there の is not an option at all - the reason is in the Contrast section.

こと - nominaliser · Nuance & register (cont.)

One more こと exists and it looks identical on the page. A verb plus a bare こと and a full stop, with nothing after it, is not a noun phrase at all: it is a written instruction, the こと of classroom notices and exam papers, and it is treated as its own point at N3. The test is mechanical: if a particle or です follows the こと, you are looking at the nominaliser on this page. If the sentence stops dead, you are looking at a rule.

こと - nominaliser · こと vs の

Rule of thumb - の for what you see, hear and take part in; こと for facts, plans and propositions. Where either would work, の is the spoken choice - but three situations take the decision out of your hands.

Verbs of direct perception require の. 見みる, 聞きく, 聞きこえる, 見みえる, 感かんじる are watching an event happen, and an event is not a fact.

外そとで雨あめが降ふり出だしたのに気きがつかなかった。
I didn't notice that it had started raining outside.

こと - nominaliser · こと vs の (cont.)

A nominalised clause standing in front of だ／です requires こと. 趣味しゅみは走はしることです is right; ❌走はしるのです is not available, because that string already means something else - the explanatory んです.

A set of fixed frames requires こと. ことができる, たことがある, ことにする, ことになる are frozen expressions and admit no substitution: ❌泳およぐのができる.

Everywhere else both are grammatical. There is one pair worth memorising because the two versions genuinely mean different things:

こと - nominaliser · こと vs の (cont.)

電話でんわするのを忘わすれた。
I forgot to ring.

電話でんわしたことを忘わすれた。
I forgot that I had rung.

The first is an action that never happened; the second is a fact that slipped your mind. That is the difference between the two words in miniature.

こと - nominaliser · ことができる vs the potential form

Rule of thumb - the same meaning at two lengths. The potential form (話はなせる) is the default in speech; ことができる is longer, flatter and more official, and it is what notices and manuals use.

ここで切符きっぷを買かうことができます。
Tickets may be purchased here.

ここで切符きっぷが**買かえます**。
You can buy tickets here.

Both are correct and mean the same thing. The こと in ことができる is frozen in place - it cannot become の, and it cannot be dropped - which is a good reminder that こと is not always a free choice.

こと - nominaliser · Nominalising こと vs the instruction こと

Rule of thumb - look at what comes after the こと. A particle or です means it is a noun. A full stop and nothing else means it is a rule.

使つかった皿さらを洗あらうことが、今週こんしゅうの係かかりの仕事しごとです。
Washing the dishes we've used is this week's duty.

使つかった皿さらは自分じぶんで洗あらうこと。
Wash your own dishes.

The first sentence talks about the washing-up as a thing; the second orders it. The second has no politeness level and no addressee - it is the voice of a notice on a kitchen wall - and that impersonal instruction こと is a separate grammar point you meet at N3. Nothing may follow it, so the moment you see が, を, は or です after the こと, you are back on this page.

こと - nominaliser · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 彼かれが学生がくせいだことを知しらなかった。 → ✅ 彼かれが学生がくせいだということを知しらなかった。
    A plain noun cannot sit in front of こと with だ on it. Everyday Japanese uses だということ; formal writing uses であること.

  • ❌ 部屋へやが静しずかことが一番いちばん大事だいじです。 → ✅ 部屋へやが静しずかなことが一番いちばん大事だいじです。
    な-adjectives keep their な in front of こと, exactly as they do in front of any other noun.

  • ❌ 隣となりの人ひとがピアノを弾ひいていることが聞きこえます。 → ✅ …弾ひいているのが聞きこえます。
    You are hearing an event, not a fact. Verbs of direct perception take の and nothing else.

こと - nominaliser · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 私わたしの仕事しごとは子こどもに英語えいごを教おしえるのです。 → ✅ …教おしえることです。
    In front of です, only こと works. With の the sentence turns into the explanatory んです and stops saying what you meant.

  • ❌ 朝あさ早はやく起おきること苦手にがてです。 → ✅ 朝あさ早はやく起おきることが苦手にがてです。
    Once the clause has become a noun it needs a particle like any other noun. The nominaliser does not replace が or を; it makes room for one.

こと - nominaliser · Practice 1

1. 私わたしの夢ゆめは、いつか自分じぶんの店みせを持もつ____です。

自分じぶんの店みせを持もつことです。

The nominalised clause is standing directly in front of です as the predicate, and that slot only accepts こと. 持もつのです would be read as the explanatory んです instead.

こと - nominaliser · Practice 2

2. Choose: 子こどもたちが公園こうえんで走はしり回まわっている（の・こと）が窓まどから見みえます。

…走はしり回まわっているのが窓まどから見みえます。

見みえる is direct perception: the running is happening in front of your eyes, so the nominaliser has to be の. こと would turn it into a fact you had been told about, which is not something a window can show you.

こと - nominaliser · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I promised my mother that I would come home by nine."

九時くじまでに家いえに帰かえることを母ははと約束やくそくしました。

A promise is made about a proposition, not about an event you are watching, so こと is the natural choice. 帰かえると母ははに約束やくそくした with the quotative と is also perfectly good Japanese.

こと - nominaliser · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 毎朝まいあさ散歩さんぽするのが私わたしの習慣しゅうかんで、健康けんこうにいいのは続つづけるのです。

✅ 毎朝まいあさ散歩さんぽするのが私わたしの習慣しゅうかんで、健康けんこうにいいのは続つづけることです。

The first の is fine - it is followed by が, and either nominaliser would do there. The second one is in front of です, where only こと is possible.

こと - nominaliser · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference: (a) 鍵かぎをかけるのを忘わすれました。 (b) 鍵かぎをかけたことを忘わすれました。

(a) says the locking never happened. の names the act itself, and 忘わすれる reports that you failed to carry it out - the door is unlocked.

(b) says the locking did happen, and the memory of it is what went missing. こと names the fact that you locked up, which is why the past form かけた is possible here and impossible in (a). The door is locked; you are just about to go back and check it.

～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun

Meaning · takes a whole sentence and hands it on as a single noun - "the fact that ～", "that ～"

こと on its own nominalises a verb phrase. ということ nominalises a finished statement: the quotative という wraps the clause up with its own ending intact, だ and all, and こと then turns the parcel into a noun the rest of the sentence can take as its subject or object.

～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun · Formation

という attaches to a plain-form clause exactly as it does after 言いう and 思おもう. Nouns and な-adjectives keep their だ, which is the whole reason the pattern exists.

な-adjective ～だ + ということ 無理むりだ → 無理むりだということ

～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the だ is what tells the two nominalisers apart. Plain こと cannot follow だ (❌中止ちゅうしだこと), so a clause ending in a noun or a な-adjective needs ということ.

Note - the same という sits inside a family of expressions built the same way: という話はなし, という意味いみ, という理由りゆう. こと is simply the most general noun you can put there, which is why this one turns up far more often than the others.

～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun · Examples

山田やまださんが来こないということを知しっていましたか。
Did you know that Yamada isn't coming?

会議かいぎは中止ちゅうしだということを聞ききました。
I heard that the meeting has been called off.

日本にほんの夏なつが暑あついということは、前まえから知しっていました。
I already knew that summers in Japan are hot.

失敗しっぱいしても続つづけるということが大事だいじだと思おもいます。
I think what matters is carrying on even when you fail.

～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun · Examples (cont.)

彼かれが本当ほんとうのことを話はなしているということが、だんだん分わかってきました。
It gradually became clear that he was telling the truth.

どこで待まち合あわせるかということを、まだ決きめていません。
We still haven't settled the question of where to meet.

父ちちが若わかいころ東京とうきょうに住すんでいたということは、この写真しゃしんを見みるまで知しりませんでした。
I had no idea my father lived in Tokyo when he was young until I saw this photograph.

部長ぶちょうに、来週らいしゅうまでにお返事へんじがほしいということを伝つたえておいてください。
Please pass on to the manager that we would like an answer by next week.

～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun · Nuance & register

ということ is register-neutral but noticeably heavy. It is four syllables of scaffolding, and Japanese drops it whenever the sentence can stand without it: 来こないと聞ききました says the same thing as 来こないということを聞ききました in half the space. Reach for the full form when the clause is long, when it is doing something a bare と cannot do (taking が, taking は, sitting in front of です), or when you want the content held up for inspection rather than slipped past.

In casual speech it contracts to ってこと, and this is extremely common: 明日あした休やすみだってこと、聞きいてる? The contraction is conversational only - it would look wrong in a report - but it is what you will actually hear, and learners who only know the long form miss it entirely.

～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because the pattern begins with a quotative, it leans towards content that came from somewhere: something said, written, decided, noticed or worked out. That is not a rule - 暑あついということ above is nobody's utterance - but it explains why the pattern feels natural after 聞きく, 知しる, 伝つたえる, 意味いみする and awkward after a verb of direct perception.

One warning about the shape. Put だ／です on the end of this string and let the sentence stop there, and you have a different pattern altogether: the hearsay ということだ, "I hear that ～", which is a separate point at N3. On this page the ということ is always followed by something - a particle, or a predicate that takes it as an argument. The next section sets the two side by side.

～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun · ということ vs こと

Rule of thumb - こと nominalises an action. ということ nominalises a statement. If the clause ends in a noun or a な-adjective, only ということ is available, because plain こと cannot follow だ.

毎日まいにち走はしることは体からだにいいです。
Running every day is good for you.

彼かれが毎日まいにち走はしっているということは、誰だれも知しりませんでした。
Nobody knew that he ran every day.

～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun · ということ vs こと (cont.)

The first names an activity; the second names a piece of information about somebody. Where the clause is a complete statement with a subject in it, the longer form is the natural one. Note the difference this makes with a noun in front: 事故じこのことを聞ききました is "I heard about the accident" - a topic - while 事故じこだったということを聞ききました is "I heard that it was an accident", a proposition.

ということ vs ということだ

Rule of thumb - look at what comes after it. A particle means it is a noun and the sentence is still going. だ／です closing the sentence means it is a report: "I hear that ～".

工事こうじは来月らいげつ終おわるということを、係かかりの人ひとに確認かくにんしました。
I checked with the person in charge that the work finishes next month.

工事こうじは来月らいげつ終おわるということです。
The work finishes next month, I gather.

ということ vs ということだ (cont.)

Same eleven characters, two different grammars. In the first the ということ is the object of 確認かくにんする - it is a noun, and it could equally take が or は. In the second nothing follows, です closes the sentence, and the whole frame has become a sentence-final marker that puts the information at arm's length; that hearsay use is treated at N3. The one place the test needs care is a cleft frame - 問題もんだいなのは…ということです - where the sentence does end on です but the のは in front makes it plainly the nominaliser again.

ということ vs the bare quotative と

Rule of thumb - と feeds a clause straight into 言いう, 思おもう, 聞きく. ということ makes it a noun first, so it can take a particle or be the subject.

林はやしさんは行いかないと言いっていました。
Hayashi said he wasn't going.

林はやしさんが行いかないということは、みんなに伝つたえました。
I've told everyone that Hayashi isn't going.

ということ vs the bare quotative と (cont.)

With 言いう and 思おもう, と is shorter and better, and a learner who writes ということ every time sounds laboured. But と cannot be marked with は or が, and it cannot be the subject of an adjective, so the moment the clause needs to move around the sentence, the full nominaliser is the only option.

～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun · Common mistakes

  • ❌（meaning "I heard that it was cancelled"）中止ちゅうしのことを聞ききました。 → ✅ 中止ちゅうしだということを聞ききました。
    中止ちゅうしのこと is a topic - "about the cancellation". To report the statement itself you need the clause packaged with という.

  • ❌ この町まちは静しずかということを知しりませんでした。 → ✅ この町まちは静しずかだということを知しりませんでした。
    A な-adjective keeps its だ in front of という. This is the opposite of what happens before plain こと, where the same word takes な.

  • ❌ 高たかいだということが問題もんだいです。 → ✅ 高たかいということが問題もんだいです。
    い-adjectives never take だ. Only nouns and な-adjectives do.

～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 彼かれが来こないということだを知しらなかった。 → ✅ 彼かれが来こないということを知しらなかった。
    The だ belongs to the sentence-final hearsay pattern. Inside a sentence, where a particle follows, the noun ends at こと.

  • ❌ 子こどもが泣ないているということを見みました。 → ✅ 子こどもが泣ないているのを見みました。
    You cannot watch a proposition. Verbs of direct perception take の, never こと and never ということ.

～やすい

Meaning · easy to do - or, with some verbs, liable to happen

～やすい attaches to a verb and produces an い-adjective. What that adjective describes is the thing, not the person: the pen is easy to write with, the white shirt is quick to get dirty. You already met this machinery at N5 with ～すぎる - same ます-stem, same い-adjective coming out the other end.

～やすい · Formation

Cut the verb back to its ます-stem and add やすい. The result conjugates exactly like 高たかい.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + やすい 使つかう → 使つかいやすい
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + やすい 覚おぼえる → 覚おぼえやすい
Verb (する) し + やすい 説明せつめいする → 説明せつめいしやすい
Verb (来くる) 来き + やすい 来くる → 来きやすい

Note - う-verb / る-verb are the labels used throughout this book; you will also meet them as 五段ごだん/Group 1 and 一段いちだん/Group 2 elsewhere.

～やすい · Formation (cont.)

Once やすい is on, everything an い-adjective does is available:

い-adjective form Rule Example
Polite + です 使つかいやすいです
Negative やすい → やすくない 使つかいやすくない
Past やすい → やすかった 使つかいやすかった

Rule of thumb - the thing that is easy takes は or が, never を. Adding やすい turns the verb into an adjective, and adjectives do not take objects: この本ほんは読よみやすい, not ❌この本ほんを読よみやすい.

～やすい · Examples

このペンは**書かきやすい**。
This pen is easy to write with.

ひらがなは**覚おぼえやすい**です。
Hiragana is easy to learn.

白しろいシャツは**汚よごれやすい**。
White shirts get dirty easily.

先生せんせいの説明せつめいは**分わかりやすかった**です。
The teacher's explanation was easy to follow.

～やすい · Examples (cont.)

疲つかれているときは**間違まちがえやすい**から、気きをつけてください。
You slip up easily when you're tired, so please be careful.

この説明書せつめいしょは字じが小ちいさくて、あまり**読よみやすくない**。
The print in this manual is small, so it isn't very easy to read.

この靴くつは軽かるくて**歩あるきやすい**ので、旅行りょこうのときはいつもこれをはいています。
These shoes are light and easy to walk in, so I always wear them when I travel.

春はるは天気てんきが**変かわりやすい**ですから、かさを持もって出でかけたほうがいいですよ。
The weather turns quickly in spring, so you'd do well to take an umbrella out with you.

～やすい · Nuance & register

The two meanings are not two words. Which one a listener hears is decided by the verb underneath.

  • A verb someone chooses to do - 書かく, 使つかう,
    歩あるく, 飲のむ - gives ease. The thing has a property that makes the action comfortable.
  • A verb that just happens - 汚よごれる, 割われる,
    忘わすれる, 太ふとる, 変かわる - gives propensity. Nobody is finding anything easy; the event simply occurs readily.

The propensity reading is often, but not always, a complaint. 壊こわれやすい is a defect; 覚おぼえやすい is a compliment. Do not assume やすい is the positive one and にくい the negative one - each takes its colour from the verb.

～やすい · Nuance & register (cont.)

～やすい is register-neutral. It is equally at home in casual speech （このアプリ、使つかいやすいね）, in polite conversation, and in written product copy, where 持もちやすい・洗あらいやすい・落おちやすい are everyday advertising vocabulary.

Two derived forms are worth having now. やすくなる means "become easier" or "start happening more readily": 漢字かんじを覚おぼえてから、新聞しんぶんが読よみやすくなりました。 And やすさ is its noun - the さ nominaliser applied to the whole compound: この車くるまは運転うんてんのしやすさで人気にんきがあります。

～やすい · ～やすい vs ～にくい

Rule of thumb - they are opposites in meaning but not perfect mirrors in use. Negating a やすい word by swapping in にくい is safe when the verb is a happening; it can shift the sense when the verb is a choice.

この皿さらは**割われやすい**から、気きをつけて。
These plates break easily, so be careful.

この皿さらは丈夫じょうぶで**割われにくい**です。
These plates are sturdy and don't break easily.

～やすい · ～やすい vs ～にくい (cont.)

That pair is a clean mirror. Now compare 来きやすい, an everyday word meaning "easy to get to", with 来きにくい, which a listener will usually take socially - awkwardness about turning up - rather than as a statement about distance. The lesson is to learn the useful compounds as words, not to generate every possible one mechanically.

～やすい · ～やすい vs 簡単だ

Rule of thumb - 簡単かんたんだ judges the task; ～やすい credits a thing with making the task go smoothly.

図ずがあるので、この問題もんだいは**考かんがえやすい**です。
There's a diagram, so this question is easy to think through.

Compare この問題もんだいは簡単かんたんです, which says the question is not difficult. The やすい sentence says nothing about its difficulty - it says the diagram makes the thinking go smoothly. That gap matters when you write a review: このアプリは簡単かんたんです sounds like the app barely does anything, while 使つかいやすい praises how it was built.

～やすい · ～やすい vs the potential form

Rule of thumb - the potential form asks can you at all?; ～やすい asks how smoothly?

この字じは小ちいさいですが、読よみやすいです。
The characters are small, but they're easy to read.

読よめます is a yes-or-no ability. やすい is a comfort rating, and a sentence can carry both at once: 眼鏡めがねをかければ読よめますが、読よみやすくはありません。 "I can read it with glasses on, but it isn't comfortable reading."

～やすい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 使つかうやすい → ✅ 使つかいやすい
    やすい attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form. 使つかいます → 使つかい + やすい.

  • ❌ 読よみやすいでした → ✅ 読よみやすかったです
    The compound is an い-adjective, so the past tense is built on its い, and です simply rides on top without changing.

  • ❌ この本ほんを読よみやすいです。 → ✅ この本ほんは読よみやすいです。
    Once やすい is attached there is no verb left to take an object, so the former object becomes the topic or the subject.

  • ❌ うちは**来こやすいです。 → ✅ うちは来きやすい*です。
    来くる contributes its ます-stem here, the same 来き you see in 来きます - not the ない-form stem 来こ.*

～やすい · Practice 1

1. このスプーンは小ちいさいので、子こどもでも使つか____です。

使つかいやすいです

The stem is 使つかい, taken from 使つかいます, and やすい goes straight onto it. The spoon has a property that makes using it comfortable, which is the ease reading.

～やすい · Practice 2

2. この紙かみはうすいので、（破やぶれやすい・破やぶれにくい）です。

この紙かみはうすいので、破やぶれやすいです。

Thin paper tears readily, so you want the propensity reading. Note that 破やぶれる is intransitive, so nobody is finding anything easy here - the tearing happens by itself.

～やすい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "This town is quiet and easy to live in."

この町まちは静しずかで**住すみやすい**です。

住すむ gives the stem 住すみ, and a な-adjective links onward with で. 住すみやすい is the ordinary way to praise a place as liveable.

～やすい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この辞書じしょを引ひきやすいじゃないです。

✅ この辞書じしょは引ひきやすくないです。

There are two faults. The object particle を cannot survive, because the compound is an adjective and adjectives take no object; and じゃない is the negative for nouns and な-adjectives, while やすい needs やすくない.

～やすい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この皿さらは割わりやすい。 (b) この皿さらは割われやすい。

The difference lives in the verb, not in やすい. (a) uses transitive 割わる, so somebody is deliberately breaking the plate and finding it easy - the sort of remark you would make while smashing old crockery on purpose.

(b) uses intransitive 割われる, so nothing is deliberate: the plate is fragile and breaks of its own accord. This is the sentence you actually want when warning someone to handle it carefully.

～にくい

Meaning · hard to do - the action meets resistance, physical or social

～にくい is built the same way as ～やすい, from the ます-stem, and it comes out as an い-adjective describing the thing. But it is not simply "not easy". にくい says there is something in the way: the meat is tough, the handle is the wrong shape, the request is embarrassing to make.

Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + にくい 食たべる → 食たべにくい
Verb (する) し + にくい 運転うんてんする → 運転うんてんしにくい

～にくい · Examples

この肉にくは固かたくて**食たべにくい**。
This meat is tough and hard to eat.

その漢字かんじは**書かきにくい**です。
That kanji is awkward to write.

部屋へやが暗くらくて、字じが**読よみにくい**。
The room is dark and the writing is hard to read.

すみません、ちょっと**言いいにくい**ことなんですが…
Sorry - this is a little awkward to bring up, but…

～にくい · Examples (cont.)

この問題もんだいは説明せつめいが長ながくて、分わかりにくかったです。
The write-up for this question was long, and it was hard to follow.

この傘かさは風かぜに強つよくて、壊こわれにくいそうです。
Apparently this umbrella stands up to wind and doesn't break easily.

新あたらしいスマホは画面がめんが大おおきすぎて、片手かたてでは**使つかいにくい**です。
The new phone's screen is too big, so it's awkward to use one-handed.

先輩せんぱいには少すこし**頼たのみにくかった**ので、同おなじクラスの友ともだちに手伝てつだってもらいました。
It felt a bit awkward to ask my senior, so I got a classmate to help me instead.

～にくい · Nuance & register

にくい covers three kinds of resistance, and it is worth naming them because learners tend to notice only the first.

  1. Physical - the shape, size or state of a thing fights the action: 切きりにくい, 持もちにくい, 歩あるきにくい. 2. Social or emotional - nothing physical is stopping you; it is simply uncomfortable: 言いいにくい, 聞ききにくい, 断ことわりにくい. This is one of the most useful things にくい does, and it has no neat English equivalent. 3. Resistance to a spontaneous event - with a verb that happens by itself, にくい means the thing holds out against it: 汚よごれにくい, 冷さめにくい, 太ふとりにくい. This sense is a selling point, and packaging copy is full of it.

～にくい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The social sense earns its keep as a softener. Opening a difficult request with 言いいにくいんですが or お願ねがいしにくいのですが tells the listener you know you are imposing, and it is standard in polite adult speech at any level of formality.

にくい itself is register-neutral: no politeness, no gender or age marking. In casual speech people often reach for づらい instead (see below), but にくい is never wrong and is the safer choice in writing.

Two derived forms carry over from やすい. にくくなる - "become harder": 年としを取とると、新あたらしい言葉ことばが覚おぼえにくくなります。 And the noun in さ: この道具どうぐは使つかいにくさが問題もんだいです。

～にくい · ～にくい vs ～づらい

Rule of thumb - づらい is about the speaker's own discomfort while doing something on purpose. にくい is about the situation, and it can also describe a thing resisting an event nobody is doing.

づらい is 辛つらい, "painful", in suffix form, and that origin still shows: it reports how the action feels to the person performing it.

足あしが痛いたくて、階段かいだんが**上のぼりづらい**です。
My leg hurts, so the stairs are hard going.

このドアは重おもくて**開あけにくい**です。
This door is heavy and hard to open.

～にくい · ～にくい vs ～づらい (cont.)

Both would be understood either way round, and for volitional actions （言いいにくい／言いいづらい） the two are close to interchangeable, with づらい a shade more subjective and more colloquial. The clear split is at sense 3 above: for a thing resisting a spontaneous event, にくい is the settled choice. 壊こわれづらい is increasingly heard in casual speech, but many speakers still prefer 壊こわれにくい, and it is what you will see in print.

～にくい · ～にくい vs ～がたい

Rule of thumb - がたい means almost impossible to bring oneself to, it is formal and written, and it attaches to a short fixed list of verbs.

その話はなしは**信しんじがたい**。
That story is hard to credit.

Compare 信しんじにくい, which is the everyday spoken version and sounds milder. がたい lives on a handful of verbs of thought and feeling - 信しんじる, 想像そうぞうする, 理解りかいする, 忘わすれる - and never on a physical action: ❌切きりがたい is not Japanese, while 切きりにくい is ordinary. Treat がたい as vocabulary to recognise rather than a productive pattern.

～にくい · ～にくい vs the negative potential

Rule of thumb - にくい says the action is possible but unpleasant. The negative potential says it cannot be done at all.

この字じは小ちいさくて**読よみにくい**ですが、読よめます。
This print is small and hard on the eyes, but I can read it.

Swap in 読よめません and the sentence contradicts itself. This distinction is worth practising because English uses "can't read this" loosely for both. If a magnifying glass would solve it, you want にくい.

～にくい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 食たべるにくい → ✅ 食たべにくい
    Like やすい and すぎる, にくい attaches to the ます-stem, never the dictionary form.

  • ❌ この本ほんは難むずかしにくいです。 → ✅ この本ほんは**読よみにくい*です。
    にくい attaches to verbs only. There is no way to hang it on an adjective; if you want "hard", the adjective 難むずかしい already says it.*

  • ❌ 使つかいにくいでした → ✅ 使つかいにくかったです
    The whole compound is an い-adjective, so the past tense is formed on its い.

～にくい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この靴くつを歩あるきにくいです。 → ✅ この靴くつは歩あるきにくいです。
    There is no verb left to take an object once にくい is attached, so the thing being described becomes the topic.

～にくい · Practice 1

1. このコップは口くちが小ちいさいので、洗あら____です。

洗あらいにくいです

The stem is 洗あらい, from 洗あらいます. A narrow opening physically gets in the way of washing, which is exactly the resistance にくい reports.

～にくい · Practice 2

2. この服ふくは色いろが濃こいので、汚よごれが（目立めだちやすい・目立めだちにくい）です。

汚よごれが**目立めだちにくい**です。

Dark colours hide marks, so the stains resist standing out. This is the third sense: a thing holding out against something that happens by itself, which is the one advertising copy uses constantly.

～にくい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "That's a hard thing to ask my boss."

それは部長ぶちょうには**聞ききにくい**ことです。

Nothing physical prevents the question; the obstacle is social, and that is precisely what にくい conveys. 聞ききづらい would also work here and sounds slightly more personal.

～にくい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この机つくえは重おもくて運はこぶにくいでした。

✅ この机つくえは重おもくて**運はこびにくかった**です。

Two faults in one short sentence: にくい needs the ます-stem 運はこび, not the dictionary form, and the past of an い-adjective is にくかった, never にくいでした.

～にくい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この質問しつもんは答こたえにくいです。 (b) この質問しつもんには答こたえられません。

(a) says an answer is available but uncomfortable to give - you know the answer and would rather not say it, or the question is delicate. It leaves the door open and often precedes an answer anyway.

(b) shuts the door: you cannot answer, whether from ignorance or from a rule that forbids it. Choosing (b) when you mean (a) sounds far more final than you probably intend, which is why (a) is the diplomatic option.

～さ

Meaning · turns an adjective into a noun - the height, the weight, the difficulty of it

At N5 you learned to nominalise a whole clause with の. さ does something narrower and more mechanical: it takes a single adjective and gives you the noun for the dimension it measures. 高たかい is "high"; 高たかさ is "height" - a scale that short things have too.

～さ · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
い-adjective drop い + さ 長ながい → 長ながさ
な-adjective stem (no な, no だ) + さ 便利べんりだ → 便利べんりさ
いい／よい よ + さ (irregular) いい → よさ
ない な + さ ない → なさ
～やすい／～にくい drop い + さ 読よみやすい → 読よみやすさ
～たい たい → たさ 見みたい → 見みたさ

～さ · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - whatever you would put in front of くない (for い-adjectives) or in front of です (for な-adjectives) is what さ attaches to. If you can still see an い or a な, you have cut too little.

A さ-noun is an ordinary noun from that moment on. It takes が, を, の, に like any other: 重おもさを測はかる、寒さむさに驚おどろく、 広ひろさが足たりない。

Note - さ is productive: given an adjective, you can generally build its さ-noun and be understood. The exceptions are な-adjectives that are already nouns in their own right - 健康けんこう, 自由じゆう, 安心あんしん - where the bare word normally does the job and the さ-form sounds laboured.

～さ · Examples

この山やまの**高たかさ**は三千さんぜんメートルです。
This mountain is three thousand metres high.

テーブルの**長ながさ**を測はかってください。
Please measure the length of the table.

この荷物にもつの**重おもさ**はどのくらいですか。
How much does this parcel weigh?

今年ことしの夏なつの**暑あつさ**は特別とくべつでした。
The heat this summer was something else.

～さ · Examples (cont.)

先生せんせいのやさしさに感謝かんしゃしています。
I'm grateful for my teacher's kindness.

この町まちのよさは、人ひとが親切しんせつなところです。
What's good about this town is that the people are kind.

日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうしてみて、漢字かんじの**難むずかしさ**がやっと分わかりました。
Now that I've tried studying Japanese, I finally understand how hard kanji are.

この部屋へやは**広ひろさ**が十分じゅうぶんですが、窓まどが少すくなくて暗くらいです。
This room is big enough, but it has few windows and it's dark.

～さ · Nuance & register

The single most useful thing to understand about さ is that it is neutral. 高たかさ does not mean "tallness" in the sense of being tall; it names the scale, and a low thing has one too. That is why この机つくえの高たかさは七十ななじゅうセンチです is perfectly ordinary Japanese about an unremarkable desk. The same goes for 長ながさ, 重おもさ, 広ひろさ, 深ふかさ, 速はやさ - these are the words you use to ask for a measurement.

With adjectives that judge rather than measure - 難むずかしさ, 楽たのしさ, 大切たいせつさ, やさしさ - the noun names how much or the quality of being so, and it tends to turn up after verbs of realising and feeling: 大切たいせつさに気きづく、楽たのしさを知しる。

～さ · Nuance & register (cont.)

さ is register-neutral, and it is if anything commoner in writing than in speech: product specifications, recipes, weather reports and essays all lean on it. Nothing about it sounds stiff in conversation either.

Note - do not confuse this suffix with the casual sentence-final particle さ, as in そんなの関係かんけいないさ. That one attaches to a finished sentence, is markedly casual, and leans male in most speech. Two unrelated words that happen to share a syllable.

～さ · ～さ vs ～み

Rule of thumb - さ is productive and measures. み is not productive: it belongs to a fixed set of established words, and it names something felt or concrete rather than a quantity.

This is the pair learners most want to treat as a free choice, and it is not one. You may build a さ-noun from any adjective; you may only use the み-nouns that already exist.

この石いしの**重おもさ**は五ごキロです。
This stone weighs five kilos.

部長ぶちょうの言葉ことばには**重おもみ**があります。
The manager's words carry weight.

～さ · ～さ vs ～み (cont.)

The established pairs are worth learning as vocabulary: 深ふかさ is a depth in metres while 深ふかみ is a deep spot or a richness of character; 甘あまさ is a degree of sweetness while 甘あまみ is the taste itself; 楽たのしさ is how enjoyable something is while 楽たのしみ has become a plain noun meaning something you look forward to. Some adjectives have only the み form in common use - the noun for hurting is 痛いたみ, not 痛いたさ, unless you specifically mean the degree of the pain.

Anything outside that set is not available: ❌新あたらしみ, ❌難むずかしみ, ❌広ひろみ. When in doubt, use さ.

～さ · ～さ vs こと／の

Rule of thumb - さ nominalises one adjective and names its degree. こと and の nominalise a whole clause and name the fact.

川かわの**深ふかさ**に驚おどろきました。
I was taken aback by how deep the river was.

Compare 川かわが深ふかいことに驚おどろきました, "I was surprised at the fact that the river was deep." The さ version takes the depth for granted and points at its degree; the こと version presents the depth as news. The さ one is also the only option once you attach a number: 深ふかさは二にメートルです, never ❌深ふかいことは二にメートルです.

～さ · ～さ vs こと／の (cont.)

Because さ only takes adjectives, a verb has no さ-form at all: for "swimming", you still need 泳およぐこと or 泳およぐの.

～さ · ～さ vs leaving the adjective alone

Rule of thumb - use さ only when the sentence needs a noun in that slot. If the adjective can simply be the predicate, leave it as an adjective.

Learners who have just met さ tend to sprinkle it everywhere, and the result is a sentence that says the same thing twice:

この山やまは**高たかい**です。
This mountain is high.

～さ · ～さ vs leaving the adjective alone (cont.)

❌この山やまの高たかさは高たかいです is not a stronger version of that sentence; it is a broken one, roughly "the height of this mountain is high." Use さ when a noun is genuinely required - as the object of 測はかる, after に, before です with a number, or as the topic of a comparison: この山やまの高たかさは富士山ふじさんと同おなじくらいです。

～さ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 高たかいさ → ✅ 高たかさ
    The final い of an い-adjective is replaced, not kept.

  • ❌ 静しずかなさ／静しずかださ → ✅ 静しずかさ
    な-adjectives contribute their bare stem. Neither な nor だ survives.

  • ❌ この町まちのいさ → ✅ この町まちのよさ
    いい is irregular everywhere, and さ is no exception - it builds on the older stem よ, exactly as よくない and よかった do.

  • ❌ 旅行りょこうの楽たのしみを友ともだちに話はなしました。（meaning "I told them how fun the trip was"）→ ✅ 旅行りょこうの楽たのしさを友ともだちに話はなしました。
    楽たのしみ is a separate word meaning something you are looking forward to, so the sentence with み says you talked about your anticipation.

～さ · Practice 1

1. このプールの深ふか____は二にメートルです。

深ふかさは二にメートルです

A number of metres needs a noun in front of です, and that noun is the measurement word. Drop the い of 深ふかい and add さ.

～さ · Practice 2

2. 来週らいしゅうの旅行りょこうが（楽たのしさ・楽たのしみ）です。

来週らいしゅうの旅行りょこうが**楽たのしみ**です。

This is the fixed lexical み word, meaning something you look forward to, and it is the only natural choice here. 楽たのしさ would name how enjoyable the trip was, which you cannot yet know about next week.

～さ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I was surprised at the size of this city."

この町まちの**大おおきさ**に驚おどろきました。

驚おどろく takes に, and に needs a noun in front of it, so the adjective has to be nominalised. 大おおきい drops its い and takes さ.

～さ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この荷物にもつの重おもいさを教おしえてください。

✅ この荷物にもつの**重おもさ**を教おしえてください。

The い of 重おもい is replaced by さ, not followed by it. A useful check: the finished noun should end in exactly one さ and contain no い from the adjective ending.

～さ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この石いしの重おもさ (b) この石いしの重おもみ

(a) is the measurable weight, the thing a scale reports in kilos. It is neutral and works for a pebble as well as a boulder.

(b) is the felt heaviness - the sense of substance or gravity the stone gives you when you pick it up, and by extension the impressiveness of something. It is one of the established み words, and it is a judgement rather than a measurement. You cannot generate new み nouns this way; where no fixed み word exists, さ is what you use.

～方 - way of doing

Meaning · the way of doing something - how to do it, or the manner in which someone does it

Read かた. Attach it to a verb's ます-stem and you get a noun: 作つくり方かた "how to make it", 話はなし方かた "the way someone speaks". This is the single most productive noun-building suffix at N4, and it turns any verb you already know into something you can ask about, teach, learn and criticise.

～方 - way of doing · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + 方かた 使つかう → 使つかい方かた
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + 方かた 開あける → 開あけ方かた
Verb (する) the noun is 仕方しかた する → 仕方しかた
Noun + する Noun + の + 仕方しかた 予約よやくする → 予約よやくの仕方しかた
Verb (来くる) 来き + 方かた 来くる → 来き方かた（rare）

Rule of thumb - the object particle を becomes の. 漢字かんじを書かく → 漢字かんじの書かき方かた. Nothing in the phrase is a verb any more, so nothing can take an object.

～方 - way of doing · Formation (cont.)

A destination に／へ usually survives, with の added after it: 空港くうこうへ行いく → 空港くうこうへの行いき方かた. Some fixed pairs simply drop the particle instead - バスに乗のる is normally バスの乗のり方かた - so learn the common ones as phrases.

The finished word is an ordinary noun and behaves like one: 使つかい方かたが分わからない、作つくり方かたを習ならう、話はなし方かたで分わかる。

～方 - way of doing · Formation (cont.)

Note - 方 is only one of the things a ます-stem can do. The bare stem is itself a noun-maker, and a good deal of everyday vocabulary is built that way: 動うごく → 動うごき, 始はじまる → 始はじまり, 帰かえる → 帰かえり, 考かんがえる → 考かんがえ, 休やすむ → 休やすみ. Not every verb yields a usable one, so treat these as words to collect rather than forms to generate - but when you meet a noun that looks like a ます-stem, it usually is one.

～方 - way of doing · Examples

この機械きかいの**使つかい方かた**を教おしえてください。
Please show me how to use this machine.

この漢字かんじの**読よみ方かた**が分わかりません。
I don't know how to read this kanji.

カレーの**作つくり方かた**は意外いがいと簡単かんたんです。
Curry is easier to make than you'd think.

彼女かのじょの**話はなし方かた**はとてもていねいです。
The way she speaks is very polite.

～方 - way of doing · Examples (cont.)

空港くうこうへの**行いき方かた**をスマホで調しらべた。
I looked up how to get to the airport on my phone.

おはしの**持もち方かた**は子こどものときに習ならいました。
I learned how to hold chopsticks when I was a child.

パソコンの調子ちょうしが悪わるいので、修理しゅうりの**仕方しかた**をネットで探さがしています。
My computer is playing up, so I'm hunting online for how to repair it.

同おなじことを伝つたえても、**言いい方かた**によって相手あいての気持きもちは変かわります。
Even when you convey the same thing, how you put it changes how the other person feels.

～方 - way of doing · Nuance & register

～方 splits into two closely related senses, and it is worth knowing which one you are producing.

  • Method - 作つくり方かた,
    使つかい方かた, 行いき方かた, 直なおし方かた. These answer "how do I…?" and are what you ask a shop assistant or a recipe.
  • Manner - 話はなし方かた,
    歩あるき方かた, 食たべ方かた, 笑わらい方かた. These describe someone's personal style and almost always come with an evaluation attached: 食たべ方かたがきれいですね。

～方 - way of doing · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because the manner sense evaluates a person, it can carry criticism. 言いい方かたが悪わるかった - "I put that badly" - is a standard apology, and あの人ひとの話はなし方かたはちょっと… is a soft way of saying you dislike how someone talks. Nothing about the suffix itself is rude; the judgement comes from the adjective you pair it with.

The suffix is register-neutral and equally normal in speech and writing. 使つかい方かたを教おしえていただけますか is a polite request; 使つかい方かた分わかる? is what you say to a friend. Only the surrounding grammar changes.

～方 - way of doing · Nuance & register (cont.)

One high-frequency idiom: 仕方しかたがない - "it can't be helped", "there's nothing for it". In casual speech this contracts to 仕方しかたない or, very commonly, しょうがない. Learn it as a set phrase; the 方 inside it is the same word, but the meaning of the whole is fixed.

～方 - way of doing · ～方 vs 方法

Rule of thumb - ～方 is built out of a specific verb and is everyday. 方法ほうほう is a free-standing noun meaning "method", and it is more abstract and more formal.

この問題もんだいの**解とき方かた**を教おしえてください。
Please show me how to solve this problem.

Compare この問題もんだいを解決かいけつする方法ほうほうを考かんがえましょう - "let's think of a way to resolve this issue." The 方 version is about the concrete steps of one action; 方法ほうほう takes a whole clause in front of it and suits reports, meetings and instructions. In a manual you will often see both: 支払しはらい方法ほうほう for the section heading and 払はらい方かた in the shop.

～方 - way of doing · ～方 vs どうやって

Rule of thumb - どうやって asks the question. ～方 packages the answer as a noun you can then do things to.

このお茶ちゃの**いれ方かた**は少すこし難むずかしいです。
The way you brew this tea is a little tricky.

You could ask このお茶ちゃはどうやっていれますか and get the same information, but only the noun can be the subject of 難むずかしい, the object of 習ならう, or the thing you 忘わすれた. That is the whole point of nominalising: どうやって is a question word and cannot sit in a noun slot.

～方 - way of doing · 方 vs 方

Rule of thumb - after a ます-stem, 方 is always かた. The ほう reading belongs to the comparison word ～の方ほうが, which follows a noun or a plain-form clause, not a stem.

バスの**乗のり方かた**が分わからなくて、駅員えきいんさんに聞ききました。
I didn't know how to ride the bus, so I asked a station attendant.

Set that against 電車でんしゃの方ほうが速はやいです, "the train is faster", where 方 means "side" and is read ほう. There is a third word here too: 方 read かた as a free noun is the polite word for a person - あの方かたはどなたですか. Same character, three jobs; the stem test sorts out the suffix instantly.

～方 - way of doing · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 読よむ**方かた** → ✅ 読よみ方かた
    方 attaches to the ます-stem, like やすい and すぎる. The dictionary form before 方 produces the ほう comparison word instead, which means something else entirely.

  • ❌ 漢字かんじを書かき方かた → ✅ 漢字かんじの書かき方かた
    The compound is a noun, so its former object is attached with の.

  • ❌ 運転うんてんし方かた → ✅ 運転うんてんの仕方しかた
    A noun + する verb uses the lexicalised noun 仕方しかた, linked with の.

～方 - way of doing · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この言葉ことばの読よみ**方ほうを教おしえてください。 → ✅ …読よみ方かた**…
    A written mistake becomes a spoken one the moment you read it aloud. On a ます-stem the reading is かた, always.

～方 - way of doing · Practice 2

～方 - way of doing · Practice 4

～方 - way of doing · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれの字じの書かき方かたはきれいです。 (b) 彼かれは字じを書かくのが上手じょうずです。

(a) puts the manner itself under the microscope: his stroke order, his hand, the shape of his writing. 方 names a style, so the compliment is about how the action is carried out.

(b) uses the N5 nominaliser の on the whole action and rates his ability at it. It says he is good at writing, without committing to anything about what the result looks like. Use 方 when the way of doing it is the point, and の when the doing is.

～がる

Meaning · to show signs of feeling something - how you report another person's inner state

Japanese does not let you simply announce what somebody else feels. ～がる solves that: it takes an adjective of emotion or sensation and turns it into a verb meaning "behaves as though X". You are no longer claiming to know what is in their head; you are describing what you can see them doing.

～がる · Formation

がる attaches to the adjective stem and produces an ordinary う-verb.

Attaches to Form Example
い-adjective drop い + がる 寒さむい → 寒さむがる
な-adjective stem + がる 嫌いやだ → 嫌いやがる
ほしい ほし + がる ほしい → ほしがる
～たい たい → たがる 行いきたい → 行いきたがる

Once formed, it conjugates as a う-verb like 作つくる:

Verb form Example

Negative 怖こわがらない
Past 怖こわがった

Rule of thumb - がる makes a transitive verb, so what the feeling is aimed at takes を, not the が the original adjective wanted: おもちゃがほしい → おもちゃをほしがる.

～がる · Formation (cont.)

Note - がる only attaches to adjectives describing how someone feels: 寒さむい, 暑あつい, 痛いたい, 怖こわい, うれしい, 悲かなしい, さびしい, 恥はずかしい, うらやましい, ほしい, 嫌いやだ, 残念ざんねんだ. It cannot attach to an adjective describing a thing from outside: there is no ❌高たかがる or ❌赤あかがる.

～がる · Examples

The children are feeling the cold.

妹いもうとは犬いぬを**怖こわがります**。
My little sister is afraid of dogs.

父ちちは新あたらしい時計とけいをほしがっている。
My father's had his eye on a new watch.

息子むすこは注射ちゅうしゃのあとで腕うでを**痛いたがって**いました。
My son was making a fuss about his arm after the injection.

～がる · Examples (cont.)

猫ねこはおふろを**嫌いやがる**ので、洗あらうのが大変たいへんです。
Cats hate baths, so washing them is hard work.

みんなが**不思議ふしぎがって**いたので、理由りゆうを説明せつめいしました。
Everyone seemed puzzled, so I explained the reason.

山田やまださんは一人ひとりで住すんでいるので、ときどきさびしがります。
Yamada lives on his own, so he gets lonely from time to time.

先輩せんぱいは留学りゅうがくの話はなしを聞きいて、ずいぶんうらやましがっていました。
My senior sounded thoroughly envious when he heard about the exchange programme.

～がる · Nuance & register

The heart of がる is evidence. 痛いたがっている does not mean "is in pain"; it means the person is behaving in a way that shows pain - wincing, holding the spot, saying it hurts. That is why the pattern belongs to third persons: you can see other people's behaviour, but you feel your own sensations directly.

Bare がる versus がっている. The plain form describes a habit or a general truth: 子こどもは注射ちゅうしゃを怖こわがる is "children are afraid of injections", a statement about children at large. For what someone is doing right now, use がっている.

～がる · Nuance & register (cont.)

First person. You would not normally say ❌私わたしは今いま寒さむがっています - for your own present feeling, the plain adjective 寒さむいです is what you want. But がる is fine about yourself when you step outside your own experience: describing a habit （私わたしは昔むかしから暗くらいところを怖こわがります）or looking back on your younger self.

Tone. Because it reports behaviour, がる can slide into "making a fuss about", and with some verbs it tends to sound faintly critical or amused. That tendency, plus the fact that it is a bald description of someone's inner life, makes it a poor fit for talking about a superior. Prefer a hedge: 部長ぶちょうは残念ざんねんそうでした, or 残念ざんねんだとおっしゃっていました.

～がる · Nuance & register (cont.)

The がり nouns. Drop the verb ending and you get a noun for the kind of person: 寒さむがり (someone who feels the cold), 暑あつがり, 怖こわがり, and with 屋 attached, さびしがり屋や and 恥はずかしがり屋や. These are everyday self-descriptions - 私わたし、寒さむがりなんです is a perfectly normal thing to say in January.

がる on たい gives たがる, which is common enough to get its own treatment; everything above about evidence, third persons and politeness applies there too.

～がる · ～がる vs ～そうだ

Rule of thumb - そうだ is a guess from how something looks at a glance. がる reports what someone is actually doing.

彼かれは上着うわぎをぬいで、ずっと**暑あつがって**います。
He's taken his jacket off and has been hot the whole time.

Compare 彼かれは暑あつそうです, "he looks hot" - one look, one inference, no claim about behaviour over time. Two further differences follow from this. そうだ works on things as well as people （このケーキ、おいしそう）, while がる needs someone with feelings; and そうだ is a much softer thing to say about a superior, which is why it is the polite way out.

～がる · ～がる vs the plain adjective

Rule of thumb - in a plain main-clause statement, an emotion adjective about a third person needs がる or a hedge. About yourself, use the bare adjective.

留学生りゅうがくせいのリーさんは国くにの料理りょうりが食たべられなくて、さびしがっています。
Lee, the exchange student, can't get food from home and is feeling homesick.

A flat ❌リーさんはさびしいです claims direct access to Lee's feelings. The escapes are がる, or one of the reporting frames you already have: さびしいそうです, さびしいと言いっています, さびしいようです. Inside a subordinate clause the restriction lifts entirely - さびしい人ひとは連絡れんらくしてください is fine.

～がる · ほしがる vs ほしい

Rule of thumb - ほしい is an い-adjective and takes が; ほしがる is a verb and takes を. The particle change is the giveaway that the word class changed.

娘むすめは自分じぶんのパソコンをほしがっています。
My daughter wants a computer of her own.

Set that against 私わたしは自分じぶんのパソコンがほしいです for your own want. The same asymmetry you met with たい at N5 is at work: the adjective is for the inside view, the がる verb for the outside one. And as with たい, ほしがっている is the normal shape for a want someone has right now, while bare ほしがる suits a habit.

～がる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 寒さむいがる → ✅ 寒さむがる
    The final い is replaced, exactly as it is for さ and すぎる.

  • ❌ 弟おとうとはおもちゃがほしがっています。 → ✅ 弟おとうとはおもちゃをほしがっています。
    がる builds a transitive verb, and transitive verbs take を. Leaving が in place is the single commonest slip with this pattern.

  • ❌ この店みせは**高たかがる**。 → ✅ この店みせは高たかいです。
    がる needs an adjective about someone's feelings. 高たかい describes a price, not an emotion, so there is nothing for がる to report.

  • ❌ 私わたしは今いま寒さむがっています。 → ✅ 私わたしは今いま寒さむいです。
    You do not need evidence for your own present sensation. Reserve がる for other people, or for yourself viewed as a type of person.

Drop the い of ほしい and add がる, then put it in the て-form for a want that is happening now. Notice that the particle on アイスクリーム is を, because ほしがる is a verb.

～がる · Practice 2

2. 弟おとうとは新あたらしい自転車じてんしゃを（ほしいです・ほしがっています）。

Your brother is a third person, so a flat ほしいです would be claiming to feel his desire yourself. The を already in the sentence is another clue that the verb form is the one being asked for.

～がる · Practice 3

This is a standing trait rather than something happening at this moment, so the plain form is right; 怖こわがっています would describe her right now. The thing feared takes を.

～がる · Practice 4

～がる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれは寒さむそうです。 (b) 彼かれは寒さむがっています。

(a) is your inference from appearance in the moment - he is thinly dressed, his shoulders are hunched, so he looks cold. You are guessing, and you are being tentative about it.

(b) says he is behaving in a way that displays being cold: rubbing his hands, saying it is freezing, edging towards the heater. It is a report of observable conduct rather than a guess, and depending on tone it can carry a hint that he is making rather a lot of it.

～たがる

Meaning · someone else wants to do something - as read off their behaviour

～たがる is がる applied to たい. At N5 you learned that plain ～たい is only for your own wishes and for asking about the listener's; ～たがる is the form that lets you talk about everybody else. It is a verb, not an adjective, and it reports a want the way an observer sees it: from the outside, in what the person does.

～たがる · Formation

Take the ます-stem, add たがる. Equivalently: build the たい form and swap たい for たがる.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + たがる 飲のむ → 飲のみたがる
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + たがる 出でかける → 出でかけたがる
Verb (する) し + たがる 運動うんどうする → 運動うんどうしたがる
Verb (来くる) 来き + たがる 来くる → 来きたがる

The result is a plain う-verb and conjugates like one:

Verb form Example
Polite 行いきたがります

Rule of thumb - the object takes を, never the が that たい permits. 水みずが飲のみたい, but 水みずを飲のみたがっている. たい is an adjective; たがる is a verb, and verbs take を.

My son won't eat his vegetables.

Children want to know about everything - that's just how they are.

彼かれは自分じぶんのことをあまり**話はなしたがらない**。
He isn't very forthcoming about himself.

友ともだちがその映画えいがを**見みたがって**いたので、一緒いっしょに見みに行いきました。
A friend of mine had been wanting to see that film, so we went to see it together.

父ちちは病院びょういんに**行いきたがらない**ので、家族かぞくはみんな心配しんぱいしています。
My father won't go to the doctor, so the whole family is worried.

クラスのみんなが京都きょうとへ**行いきたがって**いたので、週末しゅうまつに旅行りょこうを計画けいかくしました。
Everyone in the class had been wanting to go to Kyoto, so I planned a weekend trip.

～たがる · Nuance & register

The negative is the workhorse. ～たがらない is at least as common as the positive, and it does a job English handles with "won't": 薬くすりを飲のみたがらない is not a neutral report of an absent desire - it says the person is visibly resisting. Doctors, parents and teachers use it constantly.

Bare form versus ～たがっている. As with がる, the plain form states a habit or a general truth - 彼かれはいつも新あたらしいことを試ためしたがる - while たがっている reports what someone wants at this moment. If you are describing what is going on in front of you, ている is almost always the right choice, and leaving it off is a common learner slip.

～たがる · Nuance & register (cont.)

Tone. Because it describes someone from outside, たがる can carry a faint edge - "keeps wanting to", "is always trying to". That edge is what powers 目立めだちたがり屋や, "attention seeker", and it is why the pattern is a bad idea about anyone you are being polite to. Do not say ❌部長ぶちょうは早はやく帰かえりたがっています. Say instead 部長ぶちょうは早はやく帰かえりたいとおっしゃっていました, or お帰かえりになりたいようです.

Yourself. たがる about your own present wish is wrong - you do not need to observe yourself. It does become available when you describe your own character from a distance: 私わたしは昔むかしから人前ひとまえで歌うたいたがらない性格せいかくです。

～たがる · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register. The pattern itself is neutral: no politeness marking, no gender or age association, equally at home in speech and writing. What is marked is who you point it at.

～たがる · ～たがる vs ～たい

Rule of thumb - たい for me and for you; たがる for everyone else. Three things change at once: the person, the particle, and the word class.

妹いもうとはピアノを**習ならいたがって**います。
My little sister wants to take piano lessons.

Say 私わたしはピアノが習ならいたいです about yourself and every piece of the sentence differs: が is allowed, and the polite form is です on an adjective rather than ます on a verb. Mixing the two produces ❌習ならいたがりたいです or ❌習ならいたがるです, both of which come from forgetting that たがる is a verb.

～たがる · ～たがる vs ～たい (cont.)

The restriction is on plain main-clause statements only. Inside a modifying clause, plain たい about a third person is fine: 参加さんかしたい人ひとは受付うけつけに来きてください。

～たがる · ～たがっている vs ～たがる

Rule of thumb - たがっている for a want someone has now; bare たがる for a habit or a general truth about a group.

赤あかちゃんがだっこされたがっています。
The baby wants to be picked up.

Compare 赤あかちゃんは夜よるになるとだっこされたがる, "babies want to be held once evening comes" - a pattern of behaviour rather than a report on this baby right now. The generic use is where you will most often meet the bare form, frequently rounded off with ものだ.

～たがる · ～たがる vs ～たいそうです・～たいと言っています

Rule of thumb - たがる says you saw it. The reporting frames say you heard it, and they are the polite route upward.

田中たなかさんは会議かいぎに出でたがっていません。
Tanaka doesn't want to sit in on the meeting.

That sentence claims to read Tanaka from his conduct, which is fine among peers and rude about a superior. The alternatives shift the source of the information: 出でたくないそうです ("I hear he doesn't"), 出でたくないと言いっています ("he says he doesn't"), 出でたくないようです ("he seems not to"). All three are softer, and the last two are what you use in a workplace when the person outranks you.

～たがる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行いくたがる → ✅ 行いきたがる
    たがる builds on the ます-stem, exactly like たい does.

  • ❌ 子こどもがジュースが飲のみたがっています。 → ✅ 子こどもがジュースを飲のみたがっています。
    The が option belongs to たい. Once the word is a verb, the object must be marked with を.

  • ❌ 弟おとうとは行いきたがったです。 → ✅ 弟おとうとは行いきたがっていました。
    Learners carry たい's adjective endings across. たがる is a う-verb: the past polite is 行いきたがりました, and for a past state, 行いきたがっていました.

  • ❌ 先生せんせいは新あたらしい辞書じしょを買かいたがっています。 → ✅ 先生せんせいは新あたらしい辞書じしょを買かいたいとおっしゃっていました。
    Grammatical, but it inspects a teacher's motives out loud. Report what they said instead.

～たがる · Practice 1

1. 妹いもうとは外国がいこくで働はたら____います。

働はたらきたがっています

The stem 働はたらき takes たがる, and the て-form plus います gives the current-desire shape. Your sister is a third person, so plain 働はたらきたいです would be overstepping.

～たがる · Practice 2

2. （私わたし・弟おとうと）は海うみへ行いきたがっています。

**弟おとうと**は海うみへ行いきたがっています。

たがる describes a desire inferred from someone's behaviour, which rules out yourself: you do not need evidence of your own wishes. For the first person the sentence would be 私わたしは海うみへ行いきたいです.

～たがる · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "My grandmother doesn't want to use a smartphone."

祖母そぼはスマホを**使つかいたがりません**。

The negative of a う-verb, and the form that carries the "won't, resists it" flavour English gets from "doesn't want to". 使つかいたがらない is the plain equivalent.

～たがる · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 友ともだちは新あたらしいカメラがほしたがっています。

✅ 友ともだちは新あたらしいカメラをほしがっています。

たがる attaches to a verb stem, and ほしい is an adjective, so there is no ほしたがる - the form built from ほしい is ほしがる. The particle also has to move from が to を.

～たがる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれはもっと話はなしたがっています。 (b) 彼かれはもっと話はなしたいと言いっています。

(a) is your reading of his behaviour: he keeps leaning in, he has not finished, you can tell he wants to keep going. Nobody has necessarily said anything, and the judgement is yours - which is why it would be presumptuous about a superior.

(b) hands the claim back to him. He said it; you are relaying it. This version is safe in any company, and it is what you would write in a message to a manager.

～がする

Meaning · a sensation reaches you - a smell, a sound, a taste, a feeling

する is not always "do". With a small set of sensation nouns it means something closer to "there is" or "it comes to me": 音おとがする, "there's a sound"; においがする, "there's a smell". Nobody is performing anything. The sensation arrives, and the noun naming it is the subject.

～がする · Formation

Sensation noun + が + する. There is no conjugating of the noun; only する moves.

Attaches to Form Example
Sound 音おと・声こえ + がする 変へんな音おとがする
Smell におい・香かおり + がする いいにおいがする
Taste 味あじ + がする 甘あまい味あじがする
Feeling, hunch 気き・感かんじ + がする 気きがする
Bodily sensation 寒気さむけ・頭痛ずつう・めまい + がする めまいがする
Sense of a presence 気配けはい + がする 人ひとの気配けはいがする

する is irregular and carries the whole conjugation:

～がする · Formation (cont.)

Verb form Example
Polite 音おとがします
Negative 音おとがしない
Past 音おとがした
て-form 音おとがして
Ongoing 音おとがしている

Rule of thumb - the sensation is the subject, so the particle is が. Never ❌においをする. Nothing is being done to the smell; the smell is what is there.

～がする · Formation (cont.)

Note - the list of nouns that work this way is short and fixed. Sight is not on it: for something visible you use 見みえる, never ❌山やまがする. Pain is not on it either - it is 頭あたまが痛いたいです or 痛いたみがあります, though the noun 頭痛ずつう does take がする.

～がする · Examples

だれかの**声こえがする**。
I can hear someone's voice.

台所だいどころからいいにおいがします。
There's a lovely smell coming from the kitchen.

このスープは少すこし甘あまい**味あじがします**。
This soup tastes slightly sweet.

外そとで変へんな**音おとがした**ので、窓まどを開あけてみました。
There was an odd noise outside, so I opened the window to look.

～がする · Examples (cont.)

朝あさから**寒気さむけがする**ので、今日きょうは会社かいしゃを休やすみます。
I've had chills since this morning, so I'm taking the day off work.

どこかでお会あいしたような**気きがします**。
I have a feeling we've met somewhere before.

この部屋へやは古ふるい本ほんのにおいがして、なんだか落おち着つきます。
This room smells of old books, and something about that is calming.

さっきから上うえの階かいで人ひとが歩あるいている**音おとがして**、なかなか眠ねむれません。
I've been hearing someone walking about upstairs for a while now, and I can't get to sleep.

～がする · Nuance & register

The defining feature of がする is that the sensation is unbidden and often unidentified. 音おとがする is what you say when something made a noise and you do not yet know what: you noticed, you did not investigate. That is why it pairs so naturally with 変へんな, 大おおきな and だれかの - words that admit you have not pinned the source down.

The noun can be modified freely, and this is where most of the expressive work happens: レモンの味あじがする, 焦こげたにおいがする, 子こどもの声こえがする. A の phrase in front means "the smell/taste/sound of that thing", which is how you say something tastes like something else.

～がする · Nuance & register (cont.)

Tense matters more than usual here. がした reports one moment - a bang, a whiff. がする in the plain present covers a sensation happening now or as a general property （このお菓子かしはバターのにおいがする）. がしている is available for something clearly ongoing, but Japanese reaches for the plain present far more readily than English does with "is smelling".

気がする deserves separate attention. It is one of the most used expressions in the spoken language: どこかで聞きいたことがあるような気きがする, 「忘わすれ物ものをしたような気きがする」. It reports a hunch you cannot justify, and because it commits you to nothing it is very useful for softening - 少すこし高たかいような気きがします is a gentler way to say something is overpriced.

～がする · Nuance & register (cont.)

The pattern is register-neutral: identical in casual and polite speech, and completely standard in writing. The medical uses - 寒気さむけがする, 吐はき気けがする, めまいがする - are the phrases you actually need at a clinic, and they are worth memorising as units.

～がする · ～がする vs 聞こえる・見える

Rule of thumb - 聞きこえる says you heard a particular thing. がする says there was a sound. And there is no visual がする: for sight, only 見みえる.

夜中よなかに猫ねこの**声こえがして**、目めが覚さめました。
A cat's cry in the night woke me up.

～がする · ～がする vs 聞こえる・見える (cont.)

Compare 猫ねこの声こえが聞きこえました, "I could hear a cat." Both are correct, and the difference is where the sentence puts you. 聞こえる is about your ears reaching the sound and implies you made it out; がする is about the sound being there at all, and sits comfortably with not knowing what it was. This is also why 気配けはいがする works while ❌気配けはいが聞きこえる does not - a presence is not a thing you hear.

～がする · 気がする vs と思う

Rule of thumb - 気きがする is a hunch with no grounds behind it. と思おもう is an opinion you would defend.

今日きょうは雨あめが降ふりそうな**気きがします**。
I've got a feeling it's going to rain today.

Say 雨あめが降ふると思おもいます instead and you sound as though you checked the forecast. 気がする explicitly declines to give a reason, which makes it the safer form when you are half-remembering something or disagreeing gently with someone senior.

～がする · ～がする vs ～をしている

Rule of thumb - がする is a sensation coming to you. をしている describes a property a thing has - its colour, its shape, its expression.

この石いしは変へんわった形かたちをしています。
This stone has an unusual shape.

Same verb, opposite direction, opposite particle. 色いろをしている, 形かたちをしている, 顔かおをしている are the three you will actually meet: 青あおい目めをした猫ねこ, 悲かなしそうな顔かおをしていた. Sensations never take を, and appearances never take が in this construction.

～がする · Common mistakes

  • ❌ いいにおいをします。 → ✅ いいにおいがします。
    The smell is the subject of the sentence, not an object someone acts on.

  • ❌ この花はなはいいにおいがあります。 → ✅ この花はなはいいにおいがします。
    English "has a nice smell" pushes learners towards ある. Japanese treats a perceived smell as something that does - it する.

  • ❌ 昨日きのうから頭痛ずつうをしています。 → ✅ 昨日きのうから頭痛ずつうがします。
    Same particle error in a medical setting, where being understood matters most.

～がする · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ あそこに大おおきい建物たてものがします。 → ✅ あそこに大おおきい建物たてものが**見みえます**。
    がする covers hearing, smell, taste and bodily feeling - never sight.

～がする · Practice 1

1. 台所だいどころからカレーのにおい____。

カレーのにおいがします

Smell is one of the sensations that take this pattern, and the の phrase in front means the smell of curry. Polite present is します; in casual speech you would simply say がする.

～がする · Practice 2

2. となりの部屋へやから音おとが（します・あります）。

となりの部屋へやから音おとがします。

A sound that reaches you is reported with する, not ある. Using ある would be treating the noise as an object sitting in the next room rather than something you are perceiving right now.

～がする · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "This tea tastes a little bitter."

このお茶ちゃは少すこし苦にがい**味あじがします**。

The adjective modifies 味あじ, and the whole sensation is the subject of します. You could also say このお茶ちゃは少すこし苦にがいです; the 味あじがする version foregrounds the taste as something you are registering.

～がする · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 朝あさからめまいをしていて、立たてません。

✅ 朝あさからめまいがしていて、立たてません。

めまい belongs to the bodily-sensation group, and every member of that group takes が. The を version would suggest you are producing the dizziness on purpose.

～がする · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 下したの階かいで音おとがします。 (b) 下したの階かいの音おとが聞きこえます。

(a) reports that a noise is occurring downstairs. It leaves the source open - you noticed something, you could not say what, and the sentence is what you would say while looking up from your book.

(b) puts your ears at the centre: the sound from downstairs reaches you and you can make it out. It suits describing how thin the walls are, or answering someone who asked whether you can hear anything. Where (a) is about the event, (b) is about your perception of it.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences

Meaning · singles out one part of a sentence and puts it in the spotlight - "the one who did it was…", "it was last year that…"

Take a sentence, nominalise all of it except the piece you care about, and put that piece after は…だ. 弟おとうとが窓まどを割わった becomes 窓まどを割わったのは弟おとうとです - "the one who broke the window was my brother." Everything before のは is treated as already agreed; everything after it is the answer.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Formation

The clause takes the plain form and then の, exactly as with the N5 nominaliser. The focused element follows は, with だ or です closing the sentence.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (plain) dictionary form + のは…だ 作つくる → 作つくるのは母ははです
Verb (past) た-form + のは…だ 落おとした → 落おとしたのは弟おとうとです
Verb (negative) ない-form + のは…だ 来こなかった → 来こなかったのは田中たなかさんです
い-adjective plain form + のは…だ 安やすい → 安やすいのはこちらです
な-adjective ～な + のは…だ 好すきだ → 好すきなのは秋あきです
Focus = a reason …のは…からです 休やすんだのは熱ねつがあったからです

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the subject inside the の-clause takes が, never は. The sentence already has its は, and it belongs to the のは frame: 私わたしが買かったのはこの本ほんです.

When the focus is a time, a place or a person, the particle that element would have carried is normally dropped: 会あったのは駅えきです. Keeping it - 駅えきででした - is possible in careful writing but sounds heavy, and learners are safer without it. A reason is the exception: it keeps から and adds です, as in the last row above.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Formation (cont.)

Note - the の here is a light noun meaning "the one / the thing / the time". You can often replace it with the specific noun for extra clarity: 割わった**人ひとは弟おとうとです, 日本にほんに来きたとき**は去年きょねんでした. の is the general-purpose version, and it is what you reach for when no particular noun suggests itself.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Examples

この絵えをかいたのは妹いもうとです。
My little sister is the one who drew this picture.

私わたしが**好すきなのは秋あきです**。
The season I like is autumn.

日本にほんに**来きたのは去年きょねんの春はるです**。
It was last spring that I came to Japan.

悪わるいのは私わたしです。本当ほんとうにすみませんでした。
I'm the one at fault. I really am sorry.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Examples (cont.)

最初さいしょに**気きがついたのは田中たなかさんでした**。
Tanaka was the one who noticed first.

昨日きのう休やすんだのは熱ねつがあったからです。
The reason I was off yesterday is that I had a temperature.

私わたしが毎朝まいあさ飲のむのはコーヒーではなく紅茶こうちゃです。
What I drink every morning isn't coffee but tea.

会議かいぎで**反対はんたいしたのは山田やまださんだけ**で、ほかの人ひとはみんな賛成さんせいしました。
The only person who objected at the meeting was Yamada; everyone else was in favour.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Nuance & register

A cleft splits a sentence into two halves that do different jobs. The の-clause is the part you and your listener are already taking for granted - somebody broke the window, I came to Japan at some point. The part after は is the news, and because the frame exists only to deliver it, the emphasis is much stronger than in an ordinary sentence.

That makes clefts the natural answer to a question word. Ask だれが割わったんですか and 割わったのは弟おとうとです answers it exactly; ask いつ来きましたか and 来きたのは先週せんしゅうです does the same. It is equally the form for correcting someone, because the frame lets you accept everything except the one piece you are disputing: 私わたしが言いったのはそういう意味いみではありません。

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Nuance & register (cont.)

The pattern is register-neutral. In polite speech the ending is です／でした; in casual speech it becomes だ, or vanishes altogether - 落おとしたの、弟おとうと? is a perfectly ordinary question between friends. It is if anything even commoner in writing, where 大切たいせつなのは… and 問題もんだいなのは… open paragraphs constantly.

One shape worth learning whole: 大切たいせつなのは…ことです. When the focus itself is an action rather than a thing, it has to be nominalised too, and there the nominaliser must be こと, not の - see the next section.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · ～のは○○だ vs plain が focus

Rule of thumb - が presents its noun as the news. The cleft does that and states out loud what everyone is presupposing, which makes it more emphatic and more exclusive.

昨日きのう遅おくれたのは**私わたしです**。
The one who was late yesterday was me.

Compare 昨日きのう私わたしが遅おくれました, which simply reports the event with the subject highlighted. The cleft takes for granted that somebody was late - it is the sentence you use when that is already on the table - and it carries a clear implication that nobody else was. That exclusivity is why clefts are so useful for owning up, for correcting, and for singling one item out of a list.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · の vs こと as the nominaliser

Rule of thumb - の when the focus is a person, thing, time or place. こと when what you are naming is itself an action or a proposition, and always when the nominalised clause sits before だ／です as the predicate.

For a cleft that identifies an entity, only の works: 落おとしたのは弟おとうとです, never ❌落おとしたことは弟おとうとです. A person is not an event, so こと has nothing to name.

Turn the sentence round, though, and the requirement flips:

私わたしの趣味しゅみは**本ほんを読よむことです**。
My hobby is reading.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · の vs こと as the nominaliser (cont.)

Here the nominalised clause is the predicate, and ❌読よむのです is not available - that string already means something else entirely, the explanatory んです. The same rule governs 大切たいせつなのは毎日まいにち続つづけることです.

Two more places where the choice is fixed, both worth memorising. Before a verb of direct perception - 見みる, 聞きく, 聞きこえる, 見みえる - only の is possible, because you witness an event rather than a fact: 子こどもが泣ないているのを聞ききました. And a set of idioms takes こと and nothing else: ことができる, たことがある.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · ～のは○○だ vs ～んです

Rule of thumb - both contain の, and they do opposite things. んです explains the whole situation. のは…だ isolates one element of it.

来週らいしゅう出張しゅっちょうするのは大阪おおさかです。
The place I'm travelling to next week is Osaka.

Set that beside 来週らいしゅう大阪おおさかに出張しゅっちょうするんです, "the thing is, I'm off to Osaka next week" - an explanation offered as background, with no single element picked out. If you can point at one word and say "that is the answer", you want the cleft; if the whole sentence is the answer, you want んです.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私わたしは一番いちばん好すきなのは夏なつです。 → ✅ 私わたしが一番いちばん好すきなのは夏なつです。
    The subject sits inside the nominalised clause, and a clause like that takes が. Two は in one sentence leaves the first one homeless.

  • ❌ 静しずかだのは朝あさだけです。 → ✅ 静しずかなのは朝あさだけです。
    な-adjectives take な before a nominalising の, the same shape they use before ので and のに.

  • ❌ 大切たいせつなのは練習れんしゅうするのです。 → ✅ 大切たいせつなのは練習れんしゅうすることです。
    A nominalised clause standing before です must use こと. With の the sentence turns into an explanatory んです and stops meaning what you wanted.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 皿さらを割わったことは猫ねこです。 → ✅ 皿さらを割わったのは猫ねこです。
    When the focus is a creature, a thing or a time, the nominaliser is の. こと would be naming an event, which cannot equal a cat.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Practice 1

1. この料理りょうりを作つくった____父ちちです。

作つくったのは父ちちです

The cook is a person, so the light noun の is the one you want, followed by は to mark everything before it as shared background. The past form 作つくった goes straight in front of の.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Practice 2

2. 私わたしの夢ゆめは世界せかいを旅行りょこうする（の・こと）です。

私わたしの夢ゆめは世界せかいを旅行りょこうすることです。

A nominalised clause immediately in front of です has to use こと. Writing するのです would produce the explanatory んです pattern, which says something completely different.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "It was in Osaka that we first met."

初はじめて会あったのは大阪おおさかでした。

The meeting is presupposed and the place is the news, which is exactly what a cleft is for. Note that the で you would use in 大阪おおさかで会あいました is dropped once the place moves into the focus slot.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昨日きのう学校がっこうを休やすんだのは頭あたまが痛いたかったです。

✅ 昨日きのう学校がっこうを休やすんだのは頭あたまが痛いたかったからです。

When the element in focus is a reason rather than a thing, the clause has to be closed with から before です. Without it the sentence claims that the act of staying home was a headache.

～のは○○だ - cleft sentences · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 弟おとうとが皿さらを落おとしました。 (b) 皿さらを落おとしたのは弟おとうとです。

(a) reports the event as a whole, with the subject marked as the new piece of information. It works as an answer to "what happened?" and does not assume the listener knows anything yet.

(b) assumes the broken plate is already common knowledge and supplies the missing name. It answers "who did it?", it excludes everyone else, and depending on the situation it can sound like an accusation or like clearing someone's name. Use it when the event is settled and only the identity is in question.
