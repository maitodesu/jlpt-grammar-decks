～ようだ

Meaning · it seems that; it appears that - a conclusion the speaker has reached themselves

～ようだ reports an inference. The speaker has some evidence - something seen, heard, felt, or worked out - and offers a careful conclusion drawn from it. The judgement belongs to the speaker, which is exactly what separates it from らしい, where the judgement belongs to somebody else.

～ようだ · Formation

よう is a noun meaning "appearance, manner", so everything attaches to it the way things attach to any noun you learned to modify at N5: verbs and い-adjectives in plain form, な-adjectives with な, nouns with の.

～ようだ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + ようだ 降ふる → 降ふるようだ
Verb (past) た-form + ようだ 降ふった → 降ふったようだ
Verb (negative) ない-form + ようだ 来くる → 来こないようだ
Verb (ている) ている + ようだ 寝ねている → 寝ねているようだ
い-adjective plain form + ようだ 忙いそがしい → 忙いそがしいようだ
な-adjective ～な + ようだ 大変たいへんだ → 大変たいへんなようだ
Noun Noun + の + ようだ 事故じこ → 事故じこのようだ
Polite ようだ → ようです 降ふるようです
Past ようだ → ようだった／ようでした 降ふったようでした

～ようだ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Before a verb ようだ → ように 降ふるように見みえる
Before a noun ようだ → ような 夢ゆめのような話はなし

Rule of thumb - ようだ inflects exactly like a な-adjective: ようだ, ような, ように, ようで, ようでした. And nothing in front of it ever keeps its だ: ❌事故じこだようだ.

～ようだ · Examples

外そとは寒さむいようです。
It seems to be cold outside.

だれか**来きたようだ**。
Looks like somebody's arrived.

この辞書じしょは中村なかむらさんのようです。
This dictionary appears to be Nakamura's.

六月ろくがつなのに、今日きょうは夏なつのようだ。
It's only June, but today feels like summer.

～ようだ · Examples (cont.)

熱ねつがあるようなので、今日きょうは早はやく帰かえります。
I seem to have a temperature, so I'm going home early today.

道みちがぬれているから、夜よるのうちに雨あめが**降ふったようだ**。
The road is wet, so it must have rained during the night.

彼かれの話はなし方かたを聞きくと、この町まちに長ながく住すんでいるようですね。
From the way he talks, it sounds like he's lived in this town a long time.

課長かちょうは今いま会議かいぎ中ちゅうのようですので、あとでもう一度いちどおかけします。
The section chief seems to be in a meeting just now, so I'll call again later.

～ようだ · Nuance & register

ようです is the neutral polite form and is safe everywhere - with strangers, with customers, in a report. Plain ようだ is the written form and the one you meet in newspapers and essays; spoken out loud between friends it sounds a little buttoned-up, and casual speech reaches for みたい instead. Nothing about ようだ is male- or female-marked.

The adverb どうやら is its natural partner and makes the hedge audible: どうやら道みちを間違まちがえたようだ。 A speaker who wants to sound less sure adds かもしれない; one who wants to sound sure drops the hedge entirely.

～ようだ · Nuance & register (cont.)

ようだ has a second job that has nothing to do with guessing: it is also the politeness device you use to say an awkward thing without saying it flatly. 私わたしにはできないようです is softer than 私わたしにはできません, because it presents your refusal as an observation rather than a decision. In business Japanese そのようです ("that does appear to be the case") is the standard way to confirm something you were not personally present for.

Finally, ようだ is fine about your own body, because the evidence there is still indirect: 風邪かぜをひいたようだ - you feel bad, you have not seen a doctor. It is not fine about facts you simply know.

～ようだ · Contrast

The four evidentials, on one axis. らしい leans on outside evidence - what you heard, read or were told. ようだ leans on your own evidence - what you saw, felt and reasoned from. みたい is ようだ in casual clothes. Appearance そうだ is narrower than any of them: the immediate look of a thing, before reasoning starts. Hearsay そうだ is off the axis altogether - it is a plain relay of somebody's words.

～ようだ · ～ようだ vs ～らしい

Rule of thumb - ようだ is my conclusion from my evidence. らしい is somebody else's information, which I am passing on and not vouching for.

山本やまもとさんは会社かいしゃをやめたようだ。
It looks like Yamamoto has quit - his desk is cleared and nobody's seen him.

山本やまもとさんは会社かいしゃをやめたらしい。
Apparently Yamamoto has quit - that's what people are saying.

Because ようだ commits you to the reasoning, it is the wrong choice when you name an outside source out loud. A sentence that opens with ニュースによると wants そうだ or らしい to finish it.

～ようだ · ～ようだ vs ～みたい

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register, different attachment. ようだ is written and formal; みたい is spoken and casual. Nouns take の before ようだ and nothing before みたい.

今日きょうの彼かれは子こどものようだ。
He's behaving like a child today.

今日きょうの彼かれ、子こどもみたいだね。
He's acting like such a kid today, isn't he.

The second is what you actually say to a friend. Putting みたい into a written report, or ようだ into a chat about a coworker, is not an error of grammar - it is an error of tone, and it is heard as one.

～ようだ · ～ようだ vs the two ～そうだ

The word そうだ covers two unrelated grammars, and they attach differently. Line all three up on one adjective and the difference is impossible to miss.

Rule of thumb - plain form + そうだ = "I hear that". Adjective stem + そうだ = "looks". Plain form + ようだ = "seems, judging by the evidence".

このケーキは甘あまいそうだ。
I hear this cake is sweet. (someone told me; I haven't tried it)

このケーキは**甘あまそうだ**。
This cake looks sweet. (I'm looking straight at it)

～ようだ · ～ようだ vs the two ～そうだ (cont.)

このケーキは甘あまいようだ。
This cake seems to be sweet. (everyone is leaving half of it, so I conclude…)

Note the shape: 甘あまい keeps its い before hearsay そうだ and before ようだ, and loses it before appearance そうだ. That single い is the whole difference between reporting a rumour and describing a photograph.

～ようだ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 病気びょうきだようです → ✅ 病気びょうきのようです
    A noun connects to よう with の, never with だ. The same rule you learned for noun-modifying clauses at N5 applies here.

  • ❌ 静しずかようだ → ✅ 静しずかなようだ
    A な-adjective keeps its な in front of よう, because よう is a noun.

  • ❌ 降ふるようだです → ✅ 降ふるようです
    ようだ and ようです are one form at two politeness levels. You never stack them.

～ようだ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 私わたしはきのう京都きょうとへ行いったようです。 → ✅ 私わたしはきのう京都きょうとへ行いきました。
    You were there. ようだ needs indirect evidence; using it about your own known actions sounds as though you have lost your memory.

～ようだ · Practice 1

1. 受付うけつけにだれもいない。今日きょうは休やすみ____ようだ。

今日きょうは休やすみのようだ。

休やすみ is a noun, and nouns attach to よう with の. Only な-adjectives take な; verbs and い-adjectives attach in plain form with nothing added.

～ようだ · Practice 2

2. ニュースによると、来週らいしゅうは寒さむく（なるようだ・なるそうだ）。

来週らいしゅうは寒さむくなるそうだ。

The sentence names its source out loud, so it is a relay, not a deduction. Once you have said ニュースによると, the evidence is other people's, and hearsay そうだ (or らしい) is what finishes the sentence.

～ようだ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "It seems the train has stopped."

電車でんしゃが**止とまっているようです**。

Use ている for the resulting state - the train stopped and is still stopped - then attach ようです to that plain form. Nothing goes between the verb and よう.

～ようだ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼かれは元気げんきようですね。

✅ 彼かれは元気げんきなようですね。

元気げんき is a な-adjective, so it needs な to modify the noun よう. Dropping it is the commonest slip learners make with this pattern.

～ようだ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼女かのじょはもう帰かえったようだ。 (b) 彼女かのじょはもう帰かえったらしい。

In (a) the speaker worked it out: her coat is gone, the light is off, so the conclusion is theirs and they would defend it. In (b) the speaker heard it from somebody - a colleague mentioned it - and is repeating the information at a slight distance, without taking responsibility for whether it is true.

～ように・～ような

Meaning · as; like; in the way that - the adverbial and adnominal faces of ようだ

ようだ is a な-adjective, so it has the two forms every な-adjective has. ～ように goes in front of a verb or adjective and says the action happens in that manner. ～ような goes in front of a noun and says the thing is of that kind. This page is about attaching them to a clause; attaching them to a bare noun takes の first, and that is the sibling pattern のように／のような.

～ように・～ような · Formation

Attachment is identical to ようだ - only the ending changes according to what comes next.

～ように・～ような · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + ように／ような 言いった → 言いったように
Verb (negative) ない-form + ように／ような 知しらない → 知しらないような
Verb (ている) ている + ように／ような 動うごいている → 動うごいているように
い-adjective plain form + ように／ような 冷つめたい → 冷つめたいような
な-adjective ～な + ように／ような 好すきだ → 好すきなように
Noun Noun + の + ように／ような 夢ゆめ → 夢ゆめのような
Before a verb / adjective ように ように見みえる
Before a noun ような ような話はなし
End of sentence ようだ／ようです 動うごいているようです

～ように・～ような · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - look at the next word, not the previous one. Noun next → ような. Anything else next → ように. It is the same choice you already make between 静しずかな部屋へや and 静しずかに話はなす.

～ように・～ような · Examples

私わたしが書かいたように読よんでください。
Please read it the way I wrote it.

思おもっていたような店みせではなかった。
It wasn't the sort of shop I'd been picturing.

雲くもが動うごいているように見みえる。
The clouds look as though they're moving.

先生せんせいが言いったように、この漢字かんじには読よみ方かたが二ふたつある。
As the teacher said, this kanji has two readings.

～ように・～ような · Examples (cont.)

子こどもでも分わかるような説明せつめいをしてください。
Please give an explanation that even a child could follow.

写真しゃしんで見みたようなきれいな海うみだった。
It was a beautiful stretch of sea, just like the one in the photo.

山田やまださんがやっているようにやれば、きっとうまくいきますよ。
If you do it the way Yamada does, I'm sure it'll go fine.

思おもったようには進すすまなかったが、なんとか時間じかんまでに終おわらせた。
It didn't go the way I'd expected, but I got it finished in time somehow.

～ように・～ような · Nuance & register

ように and ような are register-neutral and belong equally to speech and writing. Their casual twins are みたいに and みたいな, which say the same thing in a looser voice; in a report or a presentation you want the よう forms.

Several fixed phrases are worth learning whole, because they open sentences in written and semi-formal Japanese:

  • ご存ぞんじのように - "as you know"
  • 次つぎのように - "as follows"
  • 上うえで述のべたように - "as stated above"

～ように・～ような · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - ように has a second, unrelated job: purpose. After a verb that is not under your direct control - a potential form, a negative, or an intransitive - ように means "so that": 忘わすれないようにメモしておく, 字じが見みえるように前まえに座すわる. That ように is not making a comparison at all, and it is the root of the separate patterns ようにする, ようになる and ように言いう. Tell them apart by asking whether the clause describes how something is done (comparison) or why (purpose).

The purpose sense also gives Japanese its wish formula, left hanging with no main clause: 合格ごうかくできますように。 - "May I pass." You see it written on 絵馬えま at shrines and hear it muttered before exams.

～ように・～ような · Contrast

The four evidentials, on one axis. らしい leans on outside evidence - what you heard, read or were told. ようだ leans on your own evidence - what you saw, felt and reasoned from. みたい is ようだ in casual clothes. Appearance そうだ is narrower than any of them: the immediate look of a thing. Hearsay そうだ is off the axis - it is a plain relay of somebody's words. ように and ような inherit their position from ようだ.

～ように・～ような · ～ように vs ～ような

Rule of thumb - ような is an adjective and must be followed by a noun. ように is an adverb and must be followed by a verb or an adjective. Nothing else distinguishes them.

母ははが作つくるようなカレーが食たべたい。
I want to eat curry like the kind my mother makes.

母ははが作つくるようにカレーを作つくってみた。
I tried making curry the way my mother makes it.

The first compares two curries; the second compares two methods. Swap the endings and both sentences break - ような with a verb after it and ように with a noun after it are simply ungrammatical, not just odd.

～ように・～ような · ～ように vs ～みたいに

Rule of thumb - identical meaning, different level. ように is neutral to formal; みたいに is casual and slightly more emphatic. And the attachment to a noun differs: Noun + のように but Noun + みたいに.

説明書せつめいしょに書かいてあるように組くみ立たててください。
Please assemble it as described in the instructions.

さっき言いったみたいにやってみてよ。
Try doing it like I said a minute ago.

The first belongs in a manual; the second belongs in a kitchen. Say the first to a friend and you sound like a manual.

～ように・～ような · comparison ～ように vs purpose ～ように

Rule of thumb - if you can replace it with "in the same way as", it is comparison. If you can replace it with "so that", it is purpose. Purpose ように almost always follows a potential verb, a negative, or an intransitive one.

彼かれが走はしるように走はしってみたが、全然ぜんぜん速はやくならなかった。
I tried running the way he runs, but I didn't get any faster at all.

後うしろの人ひとにも聞きこえるように、大おおきな声こえで話はなした。
I spoke loudly so that the people at the back could hear too.

Same three syllables, two different grammars. In the first, ように answers how; in the second, it answers what for.

～ように・～ような · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 言いったようなやってください → ✅ 言いったようにやってください
    ような may only be followed by a noun. In front of a verb it has to be ように.

  • ❌ 子こどもような字じ → ✅ 子こどものような字じ
    A bare noun needs の before よう. Only clauses attach directly.

  • ❌ 好すきようにやっていいよ → ✅ 好すきなようにやっていいよ
    な-adjectives keep their な in front of よう, because よう is a noun.

  • ❌ 夢ゆめのようだ話はなし → ✅ 夢ゆめのような話はなし
    ようだ ends a sentence. To put it in front of a noun you need the な form, the same way 元気げんきだ becomes 元気げんきな.

～ように・～ような · Practice 1

1. 矢印やじるしが示しめしている____進すすんでください。

矢印やじるしが示しめしているように進すすんでください。

The next word is a verb, so the adverbial form ように is required. "Proceed in the manner the arrow indicates" - this is comparison of manner, not purpose.

～ように・～ような · Practice 2

2. 田中たなかさんが作つくった（ように・ような）レポートを書かきたい。

田中たなかさんが作つくったようなレポートを書かきたい。

レポート is a noun, so the adnominal ような is the only option. The sentence compares two reports; if you wanted to compare two ways of writing, you would say 作つくったように書かきたい.

～ように・～ような · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "It wasn't as difficult as I had thought."

思おもっていたようには難むずかしくなかった。

Adding は to ように is what produces the "not as … as" reading, because the は marks a contrast: difficult in the way I imagined, no - difficult in some other way, possibly. Without は the sentence sounds oddly flat.

～ように・～ような · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 地図ちずで見みたように建物たてものはここにはなかった。

✅ 地図ちずで見みたような建物たてものはここにはなかった。

The word after the pattern is 建物たてもの, a noun, so it must be ような. With ように the sentence would try to say "the building wasn't here in the manner I saw on the map", which means nothing.

～ように・～ような · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 外国人がいこくじんにもわかるように、ゆっくり話はなした。 (b) 外国人がいこくじんが**話はなすように**、ゆっくり話はなした。

(a) is purpose: the slow speech was aimed at making comprehension possible, so ように means "so that". It sits on a potential verb, which is the usual signal. (b) is comparison: it says the speaker imitated the manner of a foreigner speaking, which is a strange thing to claim - and that strangeness is exactly how you feel the two uses pulling apart.

～のように・～のような

Meaning · like; similar to; such as - comparing something to a noun

When the thing you are comparing to is a noun rather than a clause, よう needs の to hold on to it. Noun + のように goes in front of a verb or adjective; Noun + のような goes in front of another noun. The same pair has a second job that looks unrelated but is not: introducing an example - "fruit such as apples".

～のように・～のような · Formation

The の is not optional and it is not the possessive の you learned at N5 - it is the connector that lets a noun modify another noun.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun, before a verb or adjective Noun + のように 雪ゆき → 雪ゆきのように白しろい
Noun, before a noun Noun + のような + Noun 夢ゆめ → 夢ゆめのような話はなし
Noun, at the end of a sentence Noun + のようだ／のようです 夢ゆめのようです
Giving an example Noun や Noun + のような + Noun りんごやみかんのような果物くだもの
この・その・あの drop the の そのような／そのように
Casual equivalent Noun + みたいな／みたいに 夢ゆめみたいな話はなし

～のように・～のような · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - の before よう, nothing before みたい. And with この・その・あの the の is already inside the word, so you write そのような, never ❌そののような.

～のように・～のような · Examples

雪ゆきのように白しろい。
As white as snow.

人形にんぎょうのような顔かおですね。
She has a face like a doll's, doesn't she.

鳥とりのように空そらを飛とんでみたい。
I'd love to fly through the sky like a bird.

机つくえの上うえに仕事しごとが山やまのようにある。
There's a mountain of work on my desk.

～のように・～のような · Examples (cont.)

みかんやバナナのような果物くだものが好すきです。
I like fruit such as mandarins and bananas.

彼かれは毎日まいにちのようにこの店みせに通かよっている。
He turns up at this shop practically every day.

昨日きのうの痛いたみが、うそのようになくなった。
Yesterday's pain vanished as if it had never been there.

初はじめて会あったのに、昔むかしからの友ともだちのような気きがした。
Even though we'd only just met, I felt as if we'd been friends for years.

～のように・～のような · Nuance & register

のように and のような are neutral: fine in an essay, fine in conversation, not marked for age or gender. In relaxed speech みたいに and みたいな are commoner, and they are what you will hear far more often between friends. Going the other direction, formal writing has its own set-piece openers: ご覧らんのように - "as you can see", 以下いかのように - "as below".

Two families of fixed expressions are worth memorising rather than analysing:

～のように・～のような · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • Exaggerated quantity or manner - 山やまのように (heaps of),
    飛とぶように売うれる (selling like hot cakes), うそのように (unbelievably).
  • "Practically every…" - 毎日まいにちのように,
    毎週まいしゅうのように. These do not mean literally every day; they mean often enough that it feels like it.

Note - のように compares; と同おなじ, which you met at N5, equates. 兄あにのように背せが高たかい says you are tall in the way your brother is tall; 兄あにと同おなじくらい背せが高たかい says the two heights match. If a measurement is involved, you probably want 同おなじ.

～のように・～のような · Contrast

The four evidentials, on one axis. らしい leans on outside evidence - what you heard, read or were told. ようだ leans on your own evidence - what you saw, felt and reasoned from. みたい is ようだ in casual clothes. Appearance そうだ is narrower than any of them: the immediate look of a thing. Hearsay そうだ is off the axis - a plain relay of somebody's words. のように and のような are the noun-comparing corner of the ようだ family.

～のように・～のような · ～のように／のような vs ～みたいに／みたいな

Rule of thumb - same meaning; よう needs の and みたい does not. よう is neutral to formal, みたい is casual.

父ちちのような技術者ぎじゅつしゃになりたいと思おもっています。
I hope to become an engineer like my father.

おれもお父とうさんみたいなエンジニアになりたいな。
I wanna be an engineer like my dad too.

Both sentences say the same thing about the same ambition. The first would go in a university application; the second is a fifteen-year-old talking. Notice that the の disappears the moment you switch to みたい - that is the mechanical half of the difference, and it is where learners slip.

～のように・～のような · Noun + のような vs Noun + らしい

Rule of thumb - Xのような means resembling X, and probably not X. Xらしい means a proper X, behaving as an X should. They are close to opposites.

男おとこのような人ひとが立たっていた。
Someone who looked like a man was standing there. (I couldn't tell)

男おとこらしい人ひとだと言いわれている。
People say he's a very manly sort.

The first says the resemblance is all you have; the second says the category fits so well it is worth remarking on. Use のような about someone you are unsure of and you are being cautious; use it about someone whose gender is obvious and you have said something strange.

～のような "resembling" vs ～のような "such as"

Rule of thumb - if what precedes のような is a member of the noun that follows, it is "such as". If it is outside that category, it is a comparison.

バラのような花はなを買かってきてください。
Please buy some flowers - roses, say. (roses are flowers → "such as")

火ひのような花はなが咲さいていた。
Flowers like flames were in bloom. (fire is not a flower → comparison)

Japanese does not mark the difference; the listener works it out from whether the category contains the example. English does mark it, which is why translating in either direction here needs a moment's thought.

～のように・～のような · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 雪ゆきように白しろい → ✅ 雪ゆきのように白しろい
    A bare noun cannot attach to よう directly. Only a clause can.

  • ❌ そののような話はなし → ✅ そのような話はなし
    その already ends in の. Adding another is the commonest slip in this whole pattern.

  • ❌ 子こどものように字じ → ✅ 子こどものような字じ
    A noun follows, so the form must be のような. のように only goes in front of verbs and adjectives.

  • ❌ 彼かれは男おとこのような人ひとだ。(meaning "he's manly") → ✅ 彼かれは男おとこらしい人ひとだ。
    のような distances the noun from the category. To praise someone for fitting the category, you need らしい.

～のように・～のような · Practice 1

1. この部屋へやは冬ふゆ____さむいですね。

この部屋へやは冬ふゆのようにさむいですね。

冬ふゆ is a noun, so it needs の before よう, and the word that follows is an adjective, so the adverbial ように is required rather than ような.

～のように・～のような · Practice 2

2. 彼女かのじょはモデル（のような・みたいな）人ひとです。Which fits a job interview?

彼女かのじょはモデルのような人ひとです。

Both are grammatical and mean the same thing, but みたいな is casual and would sound too loose in an interview. Note also that みたい takes no の - writing ❌モデルのみたいな mixes the two patterns.

～のように・～のような · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I want to live in a quiet town like Kyoto."

京都きょうとのような静しずかな町まちに住すみたいです。

Here Kyoto genuinely is a quiet town, so this is the "such as" reading: Kyoto is offered as an example of the category. The pattern goes in front of the whole noun phrase, adjective included.

～のように・～のような · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: あのような人ひとのように服ふくを着きたい。

✅ あの人ひとのような服ふくを着きたい。

The original stacks the pattern twice. Decide what you are comparing: if it is the clothes, the noun after the pattern is 服ふく, so you want あの人ひとのような服ふく with a single のような.

～のように・～のような · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 先生せんせいのような人ひとになりたい。 (b) 先生せんせいらしい人ひとになりたい。

(a) points at one particular teacher you admire and says you want to resemble that person. (b) says you want to be the sort of person who is recognisably a teacher - to fit the role well. The first is about a model you have in mind; the second is about living up to a category, and it would only make sense if you are a teacher already or about to be one.

～みたい

Meaning · seems like; looks like; is like - the everyday spoken form of ようだ

～みたい says exactly what ようだ says: the speaker has drawn a conclusion from evidence, or is comparing one thing to another. What changes is the register - this is the form you actually hear in conversation - and the attachment, which is simpler: みたい never wants the の or the な that よう insists on.

～みたい · Formation

Everything goes in front of みたい in its bare form. That is the whole rule.

Note - this みたい is always written in kana. 見みたい with the kanji is a different word entirely - the ます-stem of 見みる plus たい, "want to see". 見みたい映画えいが is a film you want to watch; 映画えいがみたい is "it's like a film". The two are commonly explained as related - みたい is usually traced back to a contraction of 見みたよう - but in modern Japanese they are separate words, and writing this one with kanji is simply an error.

～みたい · Examples

外そと、雨あめが降ふってるみたいだよ。
It looks like it's raining out there.

あの人ひと、先生せんせいみたいだね。
That person seems to be a teacher.

田中たなかくんは今日きょう休やすみみたいです。
Tanaka seems to be off today.

家いえのかぎをかけ忘わすれたみたい。
I think I forgot to lock up the house.

～みたい · Examples (cont.)

この辺へんは夜よるになると静しずかみたいだ。
This neighbourhood seems to go quiet once night falls.

弟おとうとはゲームをしていて、こっちの話はなしが聞きこえていないみたいだ。
My brother's playing a game and doesn't seem to hear a word I'm saying.

林はやしさんは新あたらしい仕事しごとが楽たのしいみたいですね。
Hayashi seems to be enjoying the new job.

何度なんど電話でんわしてもつながらないから、まだ会議かいぎが終おわっていないみたいだ。
I can't get through however many times I call, so the meeting apparently isn't over yet.

～みたい · Nuance & register

みたい is casual. Between friends, family and close colleagues it is the default; ようだ in the same conversation sounds like reading from a memo. Adding です - みたいです - raises it to a polite-but-relaxed level that is perfectly normal at work in speech, but it never becomes formal. In a written report, a notice, a news article or a speech, switch to ようです.

Sentence-final みたい with nothing after it is extremely common in speech: 「終おわったみたい」「留守るすみたい」. The だ is optional, and dropping it softens the sentence; keeping the だ makes it flatter and blunter, a tendency associated more with male speech, though it is not a rule and plenty of speakers of any gender use both.

～みたい · Nuance & register (cont.)

みたい also does emotional work that ようだ is too stiff for. 「うそみたい!」 is "I can't believe it!", not a claim about lying. 「夢ゆめみたい」 is what you say when something wonderful happens. These are frozen exclamations; learn them whole.

～みたい · Contrast

The four evidentials, on one axis. らしい leans on outside evidence - what you heard, read or were told. ようだ leans on your own evidence - what you saw, felt and reasoned from. みたい is ようだ in casual clothes. Appearance そうだ is narrower than any of them: the immediate look of a thing. Hearsay そうだ is off the axis - a plain relay of somebody's words.

～みたい · ～みたい vs ～ようだ

Rule of thumb - identical meaning. みたい for speech, ようだ for writing and formal speech. Attachment: Noun + みたい but Noun + のようだ; な-adj + みたい but な-adj + なようだ.

山田やまださん、もう帰かえっちゃったみたい。
Looks like Yamada's already gone home.

山田やまだは既すでに退社たいしゃしたようです。
It appears that Yamada has already left for the day.

Same fact, two worlds. The second is what you would write in a message to a client; say it to a friend and you sound like an answering machine.

～みたい · ～みたい vs ～らしい

Rule of thumb - both are common in casual speech, but they point in opposite directions. みたい says I worked it out. らしい says somebody told me - and quietly declines responsibility for it.

加藤かとうさん、引ひっ越こすみたいだよ。箱はこがたくさんあった。
Kato seems to be moving - there were boxes everywhere.

加藤かとうさん、引ひっ越こすらしいよ。課長かちょうが言いってた。
Kato's apparently moving - the section chief said so.

The second half of each sentence is doing the diagnostic work. みたい invites "what makes you think so?"; らしい invites "who told you?".

～みたい · ～みたい vs appearance ～そう

Rule of thumb - そう reports the surface: what the thing looks like right now, with no reasoning. みたい reports a conclusion, however small the leap. And they attach differently: おいしいみたい keeps the い; おいしそう drops it.

このスープ、おいしそう。
This soup looks delicious. (I'm looking at it; I haven't tasted it)

このスープ、おいしいみたい。
This soup is apparently good. (people are going back for more, so I conclude…)

Say おいしそう in front of a bowl you are about to eat. Say おいしいみたい about a bowl in front of somebody else. Getting this backwards is the single commonest error in this corner of the grammar.

～みたい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 学生がくせいのみたいです → ✅ 学生がくせいみたいです
    The の belongs to よう. みたい attaches to a bare noun.

  • ❌ 元気げんきなみたい → ✅ 元気げんきみたい
    な-adjectives drop the な in front of みたい, unlike in front of よう.

  • ❌ この映画えいが、面白おもしろい見みたい。 → ✅ この映画えいが、面白おもしろいみたい。
    Never write this pattern with kanji. 見みたい means "want to see" and turns the sentence into nonsense.

  • ❌ (in a written notice) 会議かいぎは中止ちゅうしになったみたいです。 → ✅ 会議かいぎは中止ちゅうしになったようです。
    Grammatical but out of register. Written announcements do not use みたい.

～みたい · Practice 1

1. 部屋へやの電気でんきが消きえている。だれもいない____。

だれもいないみたい（だ）。

The verb goes in front of みたい in plain form with nothing added, and the final だ is optional in speech. The speaker is reasoning from the dark room, which is what makes this みたい rather than らしい.

～みたい · Practice 2

2. となりの部屋へやから音おとがする。だれか（いるみたい・いるらしい）。

だれかいるみたい。

You are hearing the noise yourself, so the evidence is first-hand and the conclusion is yours. らしい would suggest somebody had told you there was a person next door, which is not the situation the sentence describes.

～みたい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, casually: "This bag seems to be Suzuki's."

このかばん、鈴木すずきさんみたいだよ。

A bare noun takes みたい with nothing between them. The formal equivalent would be 鈴木すずきさんのようです, where the の is compulsory.

～みたい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼かれは料理りょうりが上手じょうずなみたいですね。

✅ 彼かれは料理りょうりが上手じょうずみたいですね。

上手じょうず is a な-adjective, and な-adjectives shed their な before みたい. The version with な is the one you would use before よう: 上手じょうずなようですね.

～みたい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) あの子こ、さむそう。 (b) あの子こ、さむいみたい。

(a) is a reading of the surface: the child is hunched up and shivering, and you say so on sight, with no reasoning in between. (b) is a small deduction - perhaps the child asked for a blanket, or keeps going back indoors - so you are concluding rather than describing. Both are natural; (a) is what you say the instant you look over.

～みたいに・～みたいな

Meaning · like; similar to - the adverbial and adnominal forms of みたい

みたい inflects like a な-adjective, so it has the same two faces as ようだ. ～みたいに goes in front of a verb or adjective: the action happens in that manner. ～みたいな goes in front of a noun: the thing is of that kind. These are the casual counterparts of のように and のような, and the whole difference in form is that no の appears anywhere.

Rule of thumb - choose by what comes next. Noun next → みたいな. Anything else → みたいに. And never insert の or な before みたい, however strongly the よう patterns have trained you to.

Note - the casual counterparts of このような・そのような・あのような are not ❌これみたいな and friends but the ordinary demonstratives こんな, そんな, あんな, and for the adverbial, こんなに, そんなに, あんなに. そんな話はなしは聞きいたことがない is what a speaker actually says.

～みたいに・～みたいな · Examples

猫ねこみたいに静しずかに歩あるいてね。
Walk quietly, like a cat.

お父とうさんみたいな人ひとと結婚けっこんしたい。
I want to marry someone like my dad.

今日きょうは春はるみたいにあたたかいね。
It's as warm as spring today, isn't it.

水みずみたいにうすいコーヒーだった。
The coffee was as weak as water.

～みたいに・～みたいな · Examples (cont.)

さっき説明せつめいしたみたいに、ボタンを二回にかい押おしてください。
Press the button twice, the way I explained a moment ago.

夢ゆめを見みているみたいな一日いちにちだった。
It was the kind of day that felt like a dream.

彼かれは子こどもみたいに大声おおごえで笑わらっていた。
He was laughing at the top of his voice like a child.

毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうしていれば、兄あにみたいに速はやく泳およげるようになるよ。
If you practise every day, you'll end up swimming as fast as my brother.

～みたいに・～みたいな · Nuance & register

These are casual forms and they carry that everywhere they go. In a school essay, a company email or a formal presentation, use のように and のような. In speech among friends, みたいに and みたいな are so much the default that the よう forms sound self-consciously careful.

みたいな has a further, very modern life as a hedge in young people's speech, tacked on after a quoted thought or a rough description: 「もうやめようかな、 みたいな」 - "sort of, like, 'maybe I should quit'". This use is heard constantly among speakers in their teens and twenties, is disliked by some older listeners, and should stay out of anything you write. It is worth recognising, not worth copying.

みたいに combines freely with a negative to mean "not like…": 人ひとみたいに上手じょうずにはできない - "I can't do it as well as other people can." The は there is the same contrastive は you met at N5.

～みたいに・～みたいな · Contrast

The four evidentials, on one axis. らしい leans on outside evidence - what you heard, read or were told. ようだ leans on your own evidence - what you saw, felt and reasoned from. みたい is ようだ in casual clothes. Appearance そうだ is narrower than any of them: the immediate look of a thing. Hearsay そうだ is off the axis - a plain relay of somebody's words.

～みたいに・～みたいな · ～みたいに vs ～みたいな

Rule of thumb - みたいな must be followed by a noun; みたいに must be followed by a verb or an adjective. That is the only difference, and it is absolute.

山やまみたいな波なみが来きた。
A wave like a mountain came in.

波なみが山やまみたいに高たかくなった。
The waves rose as high as mountains.

The first describes the wave as an object; the second describes how it rose. Put みたいな in front of a verb and the sentence has no predicate to hang on.

～みたいに・～みたいな · ～みたいに／みたいな vs ～のように／のような

Rule of thumb - identical meaning, opposite ends of the register scale. And the form differs by exactly one syllable: Noun + のような versus Noun + みたいな.

氷こおりみたいに冷つめたい手てだね。
Your hands are freezing - like ice.

氷こおりのように冷つめたい風かぜが吹ふいていた。
A wind as cold as ice was blowing.

The first is something you say holding somebody's hand; the second is a line from a novel. Neither is more correct - but a written description in みたいに reads as sloppy, and a remark to a friend in のように reads as arch.

～みたいに・～みたいな · ～みたいに vs ～そうに

Rule of thumb - みたいに compares the action to something else. そうに describes how the doer appears while doing it, with no comparison at all.

妹いもうとはうれしそうに話はなしていた。
My sister was talking happily. (she looked happy)

妹いもうとは子こどもみたいに話はなしていた。
My sister was talking like a child. (she sounded childish)

Learners mix these because both translate with "-ly" or "like". Ask yourself whether a second thing is being brought into the sentence. If yes, みたいに. If you are just reading somebody's face, そうに.

～みたいに・～みたいな · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 子こどものみたいな字じ → ✅ 子こどもみたいな字じ
    The の belongs to よう alone. Attaching both is the classic crossover error.

  • ❌ 猫ねこみたいな歩あるく → ✅ 猫ねこみたいに歩あるく
    A verb follows, so the adverbial form is required.

  • ❌ そんなみたいな話はなし → ✅ そんな話はなし
    こんな・そんな・あんな already are the casual "like this/that" words. They never combine with みたいな.

  • ❌ 元気げんきなみたいに見みえる → ✅ 元気げんきみたいに見みえる
    な-adjectives shed their な in front of みたい. Only よう keeps it.

～みたいに・～みたいな · Practice 1

1. 弟おとうとは大人おとな____話はなし方かたをする。

弟おとうとは大人おとなみたいな話はなし方かたをする。

The word after the blank, 話はなし方かた, is a noun, so the adnominal みたいな is required. Had the sentence been 大人おとなみたいに話はなす, with a verb following, it would take みたいに instead.

～みたいに・～みたいな · Practice 2

2. 報告書ほうこくしょに書かくとき、（みたいに・のように）どちらを使つかいますか。

報告書ほうこくしょではのようにを使つかいます。

みたいに is casual and out of place in a written report. The two mean the same thing, so the choice is purely one of register - and register is exactly what a report is judged on.

～みたいに・～みたいな · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, casually: "Don't eat like a dog."

犬いぬみたいに食たべないで。

The comparison sits in front of the verb, so it takes みたいに, and the noun 犬いぬ attaches with nothing in between. The formal version would be 犬いぬのように, which would sound oddly stately for a scolding.

～みたいに・～みたいな · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼女かのじょはモデルみたいに背せが高たかい人ひとです。

✅ 彼女かのじょはモデルみたいな人ひとです。／✅ 彼女かのじょはモデルみたいに背せが高たかいです。

The original tries to do both jobs at once. Either compare the person - みたいな plus a noun - or compare the height - みたいに plus an adjective. Pick one and the sentence settles.

～みたいに・～みたいな · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 父ちちはさびしそうに窓まどの外そとを見みていた。 (b) 父ちちは**子こどもみたいに**窓まどの外そとを見みていた。

(a) reads his expression: he looked lonely, and that is all the sentence claims. Nothing is being compared to anything. (b) brings a second thing into the picture - a child - and says the manner of his watching resembled it, wide-eyed and absorbed. One is a description of a face, the other is a simile.

～そうだ - hearsay

Meaning · I hear that; they say that - passing on information you got from somewhere else

This ～そうだ is a relay. Somebody told you, you read it, you saw it on the news, and now you are handing the same content on unchanged. You are not judging it and not adding to it.

Warning - there are two grammars spelled そうだ, and they are unrelated. This one attaches to the plain form and means "I hear that". The other attaches to the stem and means "looks like". 高たかいそうだ = "I hear it's expensive". 高たかそうだ = "it looks expensive". One い is the entire difference.

～そうだ - hearsay · Formation

～そうだ - hearsay · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - hearsay そうだ never conjugates. There is no ❌そうだった, no ❌そうな, no ❌そうに, no ❌そうじゃない. Tense and negation go on the clause in front of it: 降ふったそうです, not ❌降ふるそうでした. This is the sharpest formal difference from the other そうだ, which conjugates freely.

～そうだ - hearsay · Examples

山田やまださんは来こないそうです。
I hear Yamada isn't coming.

あの店みせのラーメンは高たかいそうだ。
They say the ramen at that place is expensive.

天気てんき予報よほうによると、明日あしたは雪ゆきだそうです。
According to the forecast, tomorrow will be snowy.

新あたらしい駅えきはとても便利べんりだそうだ。
The new station is supposed to be very convenient.

～そうだ - hearsay · Examples (cont.)

母ははの話はなしでは、おばあさんは若わかいころ東京とうきょうに住すんでいたそうだ。
According to my mother, my grandmother lived in Tokyo when she was young.

先輩せんぱいは大学だいがくのとき毎日まいにちアルバイトをしていたそうです。
Apparently my senior worked a part-time job every single day at university.

新聞しんぶんによると、その事故じこでけがをした人ひとはいなかったそうだ。
According to the paper, nobody was injured in the accident.

山口やまぐちさんによると、来月らいげつから会議かいぎの時間じかんが変かわるそうです。
Yamaguchi says the meeting time changes from next month.

～そうだ - hearsay · Nuance & register

そうです is neutral polite and turns up everywhere: news reports, workplace updates, ordinary conversation. Plain そうだ is the written form; spoken between friends it sounds bookish, and casual Japanese reaches instead for the quotative って - 「来こないって」 - or for らしい.

Two structural facts follow from the fact that this is a pure relay:

  • It cannot be questioned. ❌来くるそうですか is not how you ask.
    If you want to check what somebody heard, you say 来くるそうですね (seeking agreement) or 来くるんですか.
  • It cannot be hedged. You do not write
    ❌たぶん来くるそうです. The hedging belongs to whoever said it first, not to you.

～そうだ - hearsay · Nuance & register (cont.)

Naming the source is optional but very common, and it is what makes the sentence sound responsible rather than gossipy. ～によると is the neutral formula; ～の話はなしでは is slightly softer and more personal; ～の話はなしによると is the fullest and most formal of the three.

～そうだ - hearsay · Contrast

The four evidentials, on one axis. らしい leans on outside evidence - what you heard, read or were told. ようだ leans on your own evidence - what you saw, felt and reasoned from. みたい is ようだ in casual clothes. Appearance そうだ is narrower than any of them: the immediate look of a thing. Hearsay そうだ is off the axis - it does not weigh evidence at all, it forwards a message.

This is the distinction the whole page exists for.

Rule of thumb - plain form + そうだ = "I hear". Stem + そうだ = "looks". Nouns can take the first and never the second.

このかばんは**高たかそうだ**。

  1. Conjugation. Appearance そうだ becomes 高たかそうな, 高たかそうに, 高たかそうだった. Hearsay そうだ does none of that. 2. Nouns. 学生がくせいだそうだ ("I hear he's a student") is fine; ❌学生がくせいそうだ does not exist, because appearance そうだ cannot attach to a noun at all. 3. Source phrases. ～によると can only introduce the hearsay one.

～そうだ - hearsay · ～そうだ vs ～らしい

Rule of thumb - そうだ forwards a message with its source intact. らしい says "this is going around" - the source is vaguer, and a little of the speaker's own judgement leaks in.

～そうだ - hearsay · ～そうだ vs ～らしい (cont.)

The first is a report you would stand behind because you can name who said it. The second is what you say when you are not quite sure where you picked it up. There is also a formal difference: らしい conjugates like an い-adjective and can modify a noun - 中止ちゅうしになったらしい計画けいかく - while そうだ can only end a sentence.

～そうだ - hearsay · ～そうだ vs ～と言っていた

Rule of thumb - と言いっていた quotes a person saying a thing. そうだ reports the content and leaves the speech act behind.

田中たなかさんは、今日きょうは早はやく帰かえると**言いっていました**。
Tanaka said he'd be leaving early today.

田中たなかさんは今日きょう早はやく帰かえるそうです。
Tanaka is apparently leaving early today.

Use the first when the fact that he said it matters - you are reporting a conversation. Use the second when only the information matters. In a workplace, the first is the safer choice if somebody might later ask who told you.

～そうだ - hearsay · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 彼かれは医者いしゃそうです。 → ✅ 彼かれは医者いしゃだそうです。

  • ❌ 元気げんきなそうです → ✅ 元気げんきだそうです

～そうだ - appearance · Nuance & register

そうだ is register-neutral and enormously common in speech. As a one-word reaction to food it is almost obligatory - 「おいしそう!」 said over somebody's plate is a compliment, not a comment, and its absence is noticed.

Two restrictions are worth knowing, because breaking either sounds distinctly odd:

  • Not for properties you can simply see. Colour, shape and
    きれい are already right in front of you, so そう has nothing to add: you say きれいですね, not ❌きれいそうですね. そう is for a property you are inferring from the surface - tenderness, expense, difficulty.
  • Not about yourself. ❌私わたしはねむそうです is strange,
    because you do not need to read your own face. Say 私わたしはねむいです. そう about other people's inner states - うれしそう, 寂さびしそう - is exactly right, because their feelings are only available to you through appearance.

～そうだ - appearance · Contrast

The four evidentials, on one axis. らしい leans on outside evidence - what you heard, read or were told. ようだ leans on your own evidence - what you saw, felt and reasoned from. みたい is ようだ in casual clothes. ～そうに・～そうな · Nuance & register

～らしい · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Adverbial (suffix sense only) らしく 自分じぶんらしく生いきる
Negative (suffix sense only) らしくない 君きみらしくない
Noun form (suffix sense only) らしさ 彼かれらしさ

Rule of thumb - the two senses split on the negative. The suffix sense has らしくない ("not very ～, unlike them"). The evidential sense does not: to say "apparently he isn't coming", negate the verb, not らしい - 来こないらしい, never ❌来くるらしくない.

～らしい · Examples

試験しけんは来週らいしゅうらしいよ。
The exam's apparently next week.

この道みちは夜よるあぶないらしいです。
I gather this road is dangerous at night.

今日きょうは春はるらしい天気てんきですね。
Proper spring weather today, isn't it.

となりの家いえは犬いぬを飼かい始はじめたらしい。
The people next door have apparently got themselves a dog.

～らしい · Examples (cont.)

そんなことを言いうなんて、君きみらしくないね。
Saying a thing like that isn't like you at all.

学生がくせいらしく、まじめに勉強べんきょうしなさい。
Study properly, the way a student should.

部長ぶちょうは来年らいねん大阪おおさかに行いくらしいですが、まだはっきりしません。
Word is the department head is moving to Osaka next year, but nothing's settled yet.

近所きんじょの人ひとの話はなしでは、この辺へんに新あたらしいスーパーができるらしいです。
According to the neighbours, a new supermarket is going up around here.

～らしい · Nuance & register

らしい is at home in ordinary conversation, plain or polite. Its defining flavour is distance: you are relaying something without putting your name to it. That distance is useful when you genuinely do not know, and damaging when you are expected to. A doctor saying 熱ねつがあるらしいです about his own patient sounds as though he has not checked; a shop assistant saying 明日あした届とどくらしいです about your order sounds as though nobody is in charge. In those situations use そうです with a named source, or state the fact plainly.

For the same reason, らしい is not the form for formal writing. A report uses とのことだ or hearsay そうだ.

～らしい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The suffix sense is a compliment machine. 男おとこらしい, 女おんならしい, 子こどもらしい, 自分じぶんらしい, 先生せんせいらしい - each says the person or thing lives up to what the noun implies. Be aware that the gendered pair carries traditional expectations and, in present-day Japanese, has a somewhat conservative ring for many speakers; it is still used, but it is not neutral in the way 自分じぶんらしい is.

Note - 自分じぶんらしく生いきる, "live as yourself", and the noun 自分じぶんらしさ, "one's own character", are set phrases you will meet constantly in advertising and self-help writing. The らしさ form belongs to the suffix sense only.

～らしい · Contrast

The four evidentials, on one axis. らしい leans on outside evidence - what you heard, read or were told. ようだ leans on your own evidence - what you saw, felt and reasoned from. みたい is ようだ in casual clothes. Appearance そうだ is narrower than any of them: the immediate look of a thing. Hearsay そうだ is off the axis - a plain relay of somebody's words.

～らしい · ～らしい vs hearsay ～そうだ

Rule of thumb - both pass information on, but そうだ forwards it intact and sourced, while らしい reports what is in the air. If you can say who told you, そうだ is the honest choice.

先生せんせいの話はなしによると、来月らいげつテストがあるそうです。
According to the teacher, there's a test next month.

来月らいげつテストがあるらしいよ。
There's a test next month, apparently.

～らしい · ～らしい vs hearsay ～そうだ (cont.)

There is a formal difference too, and it is decisive in an exam. らしい conjugates and can modify a noun - 中止ちゅうしになったらしい試合しあい - while hearsay そうだ is frozen at the end of a sentence and can do neither.

～らしい · ～らしい vs ～ようだ／～みたい

Rule of thumb - らしい points outward at a source. ようだ and みたい point inward at the speaker's own reasoning. Ask "what makes you say that?": if the answer is "someone said so", it is らしい.

林はやしさんは来年らいねん結婚けっこんするらしい。
Hayashi is apparently getting married next year. (that's the word going round)

林はやしさんは来年らいねん結婚けっこんするようだ。
It looks as though Hayashi is getting married next year. (the ring, the house-hunting)

The two shade into each other in practice, and a speaker choosing between them is often choosing how much responsibility to take. ようだ puts the speaker's judgement behind the claim; らしい sets it down on the table and steps back.

～らしい · Noun + らしい vs Noun + っぽい

Rule of thumb - らしい means "admirably like a ～". っぽい means "～-ish", and usually with a shrug or a complaint attached. They are not interchangeable, and learners reach for the wrong one constantly.

子こどもらしいまっすぐな質問しつもんだった。
It was a straightforward question, in the good way children ask them.

子こどもっぽい話はなし方かたはやめたほうがいい。
You should stop talking in that childish way.

～らしい · Noun + らしい vs Noun + っぽい (cont.)

っぽい is casual and productive - 安やすっぽい, 水みずっぽい, 忘わすれっぽい - and it very often carries mild disapproval. らしい in the same slot is praise. There is a third option, Noun + のような, which is neither: it merely notes a resemblance without saying whether the thing is really in the category.

～らしい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 彼かれは医者いしゃだらしい → ✅ 彼かれは医者いしゃらしい
    Nouns attach bare. The だ is required before hearsay そうだ and forbidden here

  • a difference worth drilling, because the two patterns sit next to each other.

  • ❌ 元気げんきならしい → ✅ 元気げんきらしい
    な-adjectives also drop everything. な belongs in front of よう.

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんは来くるらしくない。(meaning "apparently isn't coming") → ✅ 田中たなかさんは来こないらしい。
    The evidential sense has no negative. Negate the clause instead. らしくない only exists in the "not like a proper ～" sense.

～らしい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 子こどもっぽい元気げんきな声こえがいいですね。(intended as praise) → ✅ 子こどもらしい元気げんきな声こえがいいですね。
    っぽい would turn the compliment into a complaint about immaturity.

～らしい · Practice 1

1. ニュースで見みたんだけど、あの橋はしは来年らいねんまで使つかえない____よ。

あの橋はしは来年らいねんまで使つかえないらしいよ。

The information came from outside the speaker, which is what らしい marks, and it attaches straight to the plain negative 使つかえない. Note that the negation lives on the verb, never on らしい itself.

～らしい · Practice 2

2. 机つくえの上うえがきれいに片かたづいている。彼かれは帰かえった（らしい・ようだ）。Which fits better if nobody told you?

彼かれは帰かえったようだ。

You are reasoning from a tidy desk that you can see with your own eyes, so the judgement is yours. らしい would imply somebody had mentioned it, which the situation as described rules out.

～らしい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "That was a very Japanese answer."

とても日本人にほんじんらしい答こたえでしたね。

This is the suffix sense: the answer fits the category admirably. らしい attaches to the bare noun and then modifies 答こたえ like an い-adjective.

～らしい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 試合しあいは中止ちゅうしだらしいです。

✅ 試合しあいは中止ちゅうしらしいです。

Nouns take らしい bare. The だ you are remembering belongs to hearsay そうだ: 中止ちゅうしだそうです is the correct sentence with that pattern.

～らしい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼女かのじょは**先生せんせいらしい**。 (b) 彼女かのじょは**先生せんせいのようだ**。

(a) is ambiguous, and the ambiguity is real: it can mean "apparently she's a teacher" (evidential, someone told you) or "she is very much the teacher" (suffix, she has the manner). Context and intonation decide. (b) has only one reading - she resembles a teacher, and the implication is that she probably is not one. That is the general rule of thumb: らしい puts the person inside the category, のようだ keeps them outside it.
