～と思う

Meaning · I think (that) … - 思(おも)う is "think", and と is the string that ties a whole sentence into a parcel for it to carry

と is a quotation marker. It takes an entire sentence, wraps it up, and hands it to a verb of thinking or saying. と思(おも)う is therefore not "think about" - it reports a complete thought, word for word, as your opinion. The parcel is always tied in plain form, no matter how polite the sentence around it is.

～と思う · Formation

と follows the plain form of the quoted clause. Nouns and な-adjectives keep their だ.

～と思う · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + と思(おも)う 来(く)る → 来(く)ると思(おも)う
Verb (past) た-form + と思(おも)う 終(お)わった → 終(お)わったと思(おも)う
Verb (negative) ない-form + と思(おも)う 間(ま)に合(あ)わない → 間(ま)に合(あ)わないと思(おも)う
い-adjective plain form + と思(おも)う 安(やす)い → 安(やす)いと思(おも)う
な-adjective + だ + と思(おも)う 無理(むり) → 無理(むり)だと思(おも)う
Noun + だ + と思(おも)う 冗談(じょうだん) → 冗談(じょうだん)だと思(おも)う
Volitional - "I'm thinking of…" よう-form + と思(おも)う 休(やす)もう → 休(やす)もうと思(おも)う

～と思う · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Somebody else's opinion plain form + と思(おも)っている 正(ただ)しい → 正(ただ)しいと思(おも)っている

Rule of thumb - nothing polite goes in front of と. ❌行(い)きますと思(おも)います → ✅行(い)くと思(おも)います. The politeness of the whole sentence is carried by the final verb and nowhere else.

Note - the だ that disappears before でしょう stays before と. It is 大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)でしょう but 大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)だと思(おも)います.

～と思う · Examples

あの店(みせ)はもう閉(し)まっていると思(おも)う。
I think that shop has already closed.

この傘(かさ)は山田(やまだ)さんのだと思(おも)います。
I think this umbrella is Yamada's.

試験(しけん)はそんなに難(むずか)しくないと思(おも)うよ。
I don't think the test will be all that hard.

今年(ことし)の冬(ふゆ)は去年(きょねん)より暖(あたた)かいと思(おも)いませんか。
Don't you think this winter is milder than last year's?

来月(らいげつ)から自転車(じてんしゃ)で通(かよ)おうと思(おも)っています。
I'm thinking of cycling to work from next month.

～と思う · Examples (cont.)

妹(いもうと)は自分(じぶん)の料理(りょうり)が世界(せかい)で一番(いちばん)おいしいと思(おも)っている。
My sister is convinced her own cooking is the best in the world.

会議(かいぎ)は三時(さんじ)に終(お)わると思(おも)いますが、念(ねん)のため確認(かくにん)しておきます。
I think the meeting finishes at three, but I'll check to be safe.

正直(しょうじき)に言(い)うと、あの計画(けいかく)はうまくいかないと思(おも)います。
To be honest, I don't think that plan is going to work.

～と思う · Nuance & register

思(おも)う is the plain form and 思(おも)います the polite one; everything before と is untouched by that choice. This is the single most useful thing to know about the pattern, and the single most common place learners slip.

Beyond grammar, と思(おも)います is a softener. A bare assertion - その方法(ほうほう)は無理(むり)です - commits you to a fact. Adding と思(おも)います reframes it as one person's reading of the situation, which is why Japanese workplace speech is full of it. Overusing it is a real thing too: answering a question about your own department with 多分(たぶん)そうだと思(おも)います can sound as if you have not bothered to find out.

～と思う · Nuance & register (cont.)

In casual speech you will hear って in place of と - 無理(むり)だって思(おも)った - and the whole clause often ends in んだけど to leave the door open: 明日(あした)でもいいと思(おも)うんだけど。 At the other end, formal writing prefers the passive と思(おも)われます or と考(かんが)えられます, which removes the "I" altogether.

One limit worth stating plainly: the plain-form rule is about indirect quotation. If you put actual quotation marks round someone's words you are reproducing what came out of their mouth, so anything can appear inside: 「明日(あした)は休(やす)みます」と言(い)っていました。 That is a direct quote, and it is not an exception to the rule - it is a different operation.

～と思う · ～と思(おも)う vs ～と思(おも)っている

Rule of thumb - 思(おも)う is a thought formed now, at the moment of speaking. 思(おも)っている is a view somebody holds - and it is the only option for a flat statement about a third person.

その色(いろ)のほうが似合(にあ)うと思(おも)う。
I think that colour suits you better.

父(ちち)は私(わたし)をまだ子(こ)どもだと思(おも)っている。
My father still thinks of me as a child.

You cannot see into someone else's head, so a bare ❌父(ちち)は…と思(おも)う claims access you do not have. The ている turns it into an observation about a state you can infer from outside. About yourself both work: 私(わたし)もそう思(おも)っています just adds that you have thought so for a while.

～と思う · ～と思(おも)う vs ～でしょう

Rule of thumb - と思(おも)う labels the claim as mine. でしょう estimates how likely the claim is. One is about the speaker, the other about the world.

来年(らいねん)は野菜(やさい)の値段(ねだん)が上(あ)がると思(おも)います。
I think vegetable prices will go up next year.

来年(らいねん)は野菜(やさい)の値段(ねだん)が上(あ)がるでしょう。
Vegetable prices will probably go up next year.

The second is what a news presenter says; the first is what you say at dinner. Because と思(おも)います keeps the responsibility on you, it is usually the safer choice when you might be wrong, and the wrong choice when you are supposed to be the authority.

～と思う · ～ないと思(おも)う vs ～と思(おも)わない

Rule of thumb - English negates "think"; Japanese negates the thought. "I don't think it will rain" is 降(ふ)らないと思(おも)う, not 降(ふ)ると思(おも)わない.

彼女(かのじょ)はその話(はなし)を知(し)らないと思(おも)います。
I don't think she knows about it.

と思(おも)わない is not ungrammatical - it is simply much stronger. It rejects the proposition rather than doubting it, so it lands as disagreement:

私(わたし)はそうは思(おも)いません。
I don't see it that way.

Note the は on そう, which is typical: negating 思(おも)う almost always comes with contrastive は, and that is part of why it feels like pushing back.

～と思う · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日(あした)は雨(あめ)が降(ふ)りますと思(おも)います。 → ✅ 明日(あした)は雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ると思(おも)います。
    Nothing in ます-form may sit in front of と. Politeness belongs to the last verb.

  • ❌ この辞書(じしょ)は便利(べんり)と思(おも)う。 → ✅ この辞書(じしょ)は便利(べんり)だと思(おも)う。
    な-adjectives and nouns keep だ before と. Only い-adjectives and verbs attach bare.

  • ❌ 弟(おとうと)はその映画(えいが)がつまらないと思(おも)います。 → ✅ 弟(おとうと)はその映画(えいが)がつまらないと思(おも)っています。
    A plain statement about what someone else thinks needs ている - or a hedge such as 思(おも)っているようです.

  • ❌ 時間(じかん)があると思(おも)いません。 → ✅ 時間(じかん)がないと思(おも)います。
    Put the negative inside the quoted thought. The outer negative is reserved for disagreeing.

～と思う · Practice 1

1. 田中(たなか)さんは来週(らいしゅう)出張(しゅっちょう)____思(おも)います。

田中(たなか)さんは来週(らいしゅう)出張(しゅっちょう)だと思(おも)います。

出張(しゅっちょう) is a noun, and a noun predicate keeps its だ in front of the quotative と. Dropping it is only correct before でしょう.

～と思う · Practice 2

2. Choose: 姉(あね)は自分(じぶん)が正(ただ)しい（と思(おも)います・と思(おも)っています）。

姉(あね)は自分(じぶん)が正(ただ)しいと思(おも)っています。

The subject is a third person, so a bare と思(おも)います would claim direct knowledge of her mind. ている presents the belief as something you observe her holding.

～と思う · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I don't think he will come today."

彼(かれ)は今日(きょう)来(こ)ないと思(おも)います。

The negative goes inside the quoted thought, not on 思(おも)う. 来(く)ると思(おも)いません would read as flatly rejecting the idea rather than doubting it.

～と思う · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: あの人(ひと)は親切(しんせつ)ですと思(おも)います。

✅ あの人(ひと)は親切(しんせつ)だと思(おも)います。

Two things go wrong at once: です cannot appear before と, and the な-adjective still needs its plain copula だ. The polite ending survives on 思(おも)います.

～と思う · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この案(あん)は失敗(しっぱい)すると思(おも)います。 (b) この案(あん)は失敗(しっぱい)するでしょう。

(a) marks the prediction as your own reading and keeps the responsibility with you - appropriate in a meeting where you are offering an opinion someone may argue with.

(b) states it as a likely outcome, the way a forecast or an analysis does. It sounds more detached and more authoritative, which is fine from an expert and presumptuous from a newcomer.

って

Meaning · the spoken shortcut - "they said…", "you know…", "the thing called…"

って is what と (and often という, and sometimes は) becomes in relaxed speech. It quotes, it reports hearsay, it introduces a name, and it puts a topic on the table with a little more energy than は does. One syllable, several jobs - but they all come from the same place: って presents a chunk of language and asks the listener to take it as a unit.

って · Formation

Like と, って follows the plain form. Nouns and な-adjectives keep だ when って is quoting; they drop it when って is marking a topic.

って · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb - quoting plain form + って（+ 言(い)う／聞(き)く／思(おも)う） 辞(や)める → 辞(や)めるって言(い)ってた
Noun / な-adjective - quoting + だ + って 中止(ちゅうし) → 中止(ちゅうし)だって聞(き)いた
Sentence-final - hearsay plain form + って（falling tone） 来(こ)ない → 来(こ)ないって
Direct quote 「…」+ って 「まだだ」 → 「まだだ」って言(い)われた
Naming - replaces という Noun + って + Noun 林(はやし) → 林(はやし)さんって人(ひと)
Topic - replaces は / というのは Noun + って（no だ） 敬語(けいご) → 敬語(けいご)って難(むずか)しい

って · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - って inherits the と rule exactly: the clause in front of it is plain, however polite you are being. ❌晴(は)れますって → ✅晴(は)れるって. The one exception is a direct quote in 「」, where you are copying someone's actual words.

って · Examples

田中(たなか)さん、明日(あした)来(こ)ないって。
Tanaka says he's not coming tomorrow.

会議(かいぎ)、中止(ちゅうし)だって。
Apparently the meeting's off.

漢字(かんじ)って覚(おぼ)えるのが大変(たいへん)だね。
Kanji really are a slog to memorise, aren't they.

さっき先生(せんせい)が今日(きょう)の宿題(しゅくだい)はないって言(い)ってたよ。
The teacher said a moment ago that there's no homework today.

林(はやし)さんって人(ひと)から電話(でんわ)がありましたよ。
There was a phone call from someone called Hayashi.

って · Examples (cont.)

「もう帰(かえ)りたい」って顔(かお)をしているよ。
You've got an "I want to go home already" look on your face.

妹(いもうと)に貸(か)した本(ほん)、まだ読(よ)んでないって言(い)うんだけど、もう一(いっ)か月(げつ)経(た)つよ。
My sister says she still hasn't read the book I lent her - and it's been a month.

来年(らいねん)留学(りゅうがく)するって本当(ほんとう)？いつ決(き)めたの？
Is it true you're studying abroad next year? When did you decide?

って · Nuance & register

って is spoken casual and nothing else. It belongs in conversation with friends, family and people you are on plain-form terms with. It does not belong in an email to a client, a report, an essay, or a sentence you are otherwise ending in ます. The neutral replacements are と for quoting and そうです／ とのことです for hearsay.

Intonation does a lot of work. Falling って at the end of a sentence is hearsay - 雨(あめ)になるって。 Rising って? is a request for confirmation, close to "wait, did you say…?" - 明日(あした)休(やす)みだって？ Both are the same particle.

Two extensions you will hear constantly and will not find in a textbook: っていうか at the head of a sentence, which retracts what you just said and restarts ("or rather…"), and ってば, which repeats something impatiently - だから違(ちが)うってば。 The second is distinctly childish or intimate; it is not a neutral emphasis marker.

って · Nuance & register (cont.)

There is a semi-polite hearsay form, ～んですって, which keeps a です on the end: となりの店(みせ)、閉(し)めるんですって。 It is polite in form but chatty in feel, and in modern Tokyo speech it leans female. ～だってです is not a form at all.

って · って vs と

Rule of thumb - same grammar, different register: って is と in a T-shirt. But って also covers jobs と cannot do, namely naming and topic-marking.

課長(かちょう)は出席(しゅっせき)できないと言(い)っていました。
The section chief said he can't attend.

課長(かちょう)、出席(しゅっせき)できないって。
The boss says he can't make it.

Both report the same fact. The first is what you write in a message to the team; the second is what you say over your shoulder to the colleague next to you. Where the two part company is self-introduction and any fixed formula: 田中(たなか)と申(もう)します is the set phrase, and って there is simply wrong.

って · topic って vs は

Rule of thumb - は sets a topic quietly. って sets a topic and signals that you are about to say something about the category itself, usually with a reaction attached.

日本(にほん)の夏(なつ)は暑(あつ)いです。
Summers in Japan are hot.

日本(にほん)の夏(なつ)って暑(あつ)いね。
Japanese summers are hot, aren't they.

The って version reads as a comment brought up fresh, and it pairs naturally with ね, よ and evaluative adjectives. It is close to 日本(にほん)の夏(なつ)というのは, which is exactly what it is a contraction of - and that is why it can also mean "what is…?": 「バイト」って何(なに)？

って · hearsay って vs ～そうだ

Rule of thumb - identical content, opposite registers. って is what you say; そうです is what you write.

山田(やまだ)さん、来月(らいげつ)引(ひ)っ越(こ)すって。
Yamada's moving house next month, apparently.

山田(やまだ)さんは来月(らいげつ)引(ひ)っ越(こ)すそうです。
I hear that Yamada is moving house next month.

One practical difference: って easily carries a named source in front of it - 母(はは)が寒(さむ)くなるって言(い)ってた - while そうだ usually leaves the source vague or marks it with によると. If you want to say who told you, って is the more natural spoken frame.

って · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私(わたし)は田中(たなか)って申(もう)します。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は田中(たなか)と申(もう)します。
    Self-introduction is a fixed humble formula. って cannot be dropped into a phrase that is formal by definition.

  • ❌ 天気予報(てんきよほう)で明日(あした)は晴(は)れますって言(い)ってた。 → ✅ …晴(は)れるって言(い)ってた。
    An indirect quote takes the plain form, exactly as with と. ます only survives inside 「」.

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、会議(かいぎ)は中止(ちゅうし)だってです。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、会議(かいぎ)は中止(ちゅうし)だそうです。
    You cannot make って polite by bolting です on. Switch to そうです or とのことです.

って · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ (in a written report) 調査(ちょうさ)の結果(けっか)、問題(もんだい)はなかったって。 → ✅ …問題(もんだい)はなかったとのことです。
    って is a spoken form. In writing it reads as though the report were a text message.

って · Practice 1

1. 友達(ともだち)に：「山田(やまだ)さん、今日(きょう)来(こ)られない____。」

山田(やまだ)さん、今日(きょう)来(こ)られないって。

Sentence-final って after a plain form reports what you were told, with the source left implicit. Said with a falling tone it means "apparently", not "did you say?".

って · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural option for an email to a client: 納期(のうき)は来週(らいしゅう)（だって・だそうです）。

納期(のうき)は来週(らいしゅう)だそうです。

って is casual spoken Japanese and never appears in business writing. The hearsay meaning is unchanged; only the register is fixed.

って · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, casually: "Someone called Kimura came looking for you."

木村(きむら)さん**って人(ひと)**が来(き)たよ。

って before a noun is the contraction of という and is the everyday way of naming someone the listener may not know. 木村(きむら)さんという人(ひと) is the same thing in a neutral register.

って · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼(かれ)は新(あたら)しい仕事(しごと)を探(さが)していますって言(い)ってた。

✅ 彼(かれ)は新(あたら)しい仕事(しごと)を探(さが)しているって言(い)ってた。

The quoted clause must be plain. って is only a casual と, so it obeys the same restriction: nothing in ます-form immediately in front of it.

って · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 一人暮(ひとりぐ)らしはお金(かね)がかかります。 (b) 一人暮(ひとりぐ)らしってお金(かね)がかかるよね。

(a) is a neutral, polite statement of fact - the kind of line that could open a paragraph in an article about living costs.

(b) raises "living alone" as a topic the two of you both know something about, and adds a shared reaction with よね. って marks the category as newly put on the table and invites agreement; it is conversation, not exposition.

かどうか

Meaning · whether or not - packs a yes/no question inside a bigger sentence

かどうか takes a question that could be answered yes or no, ties it into a single lump, and hands that lump to a verb like 分(わ)かる, 知(し)らない or 調(しら)べる. It is literally "か（yes?）どう（how?）か" - is it so, or how is it? The English that maps onto it is whether, and mapping it onto if instead is where nearly all the trouble starts.

かどうか · Formation

か attaches to plain forms. Nouns and な-adjectives lose their だ - the opposite of what happens before the quotative と.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + かどうか 届(とど)く → 届(とど)くかどうか
Verb (past) た-form + かどうか 送(おく)った → 送(おく)ったかどうか
Verb (negative) ない-form + かどうか 要(い)らない → 要(い)らないかどうか
い-adjective plain form + かどうか 新(あたら)しい → 新(あたら)しいかどうか
な-adjective drop だ + かどうか 安全(あんぜん)だ → 安全(あんぜん)かどうか
Noun drop だ + かどうか 本物(ほんもの)だ → 本物(ほんもの)かどうか
Both options spelled out ～か～ないか 行(い)く → 行(い)くか行(い)かないか

かどうか · Formation (cont.)

Typical main verbs after it: 分(わ)かる, 知(し)らない, 聞(き)く, 調(しら)べる, 確(たし)かめる, 迷(まよ)う, 覚(おぼ)えていない, 決(き)める.

Rule of thumb - nothing polite and no だ in front of か. ❌行(い)きますかどうか, ❌安全(あんぜん)だかどうか → ✅行(い)くかどうか, ✅安全(あんぜん)かどうか.

Note - the particle that would follow the lump (を, が) is normally left out: 届(とど)いたかどうか確(たし)かめます. は does appear, and adds contrast: 届(とど)いたかどうかは、まだ分(わ)かりません。

かどうか · Examples

これでいいかどうか、見(み)てください。
Please have a look and tell me whether this is all right.

明日(あした)雪(ゆき)が降(ふ)るかどうか分(わ)かりません。
I don't know whether it will snow tomorrow.

この水(みず)が安全(あんぜん)かどうか、先(さき)に調(しら)べたほうがいいよ。
You should check first whether this water is safe.

受(う)かったかどうかは、来週(らいしゅう)の金曜日(きんようび)に分(わ)かります。
Whether I passed or not will be clear next Friday.

パーティーに出(で)るかどうか、まだ迷(まよ)っているんです。
I still can't make up my mind whether to go to the party.

かどうか · Examples (cont.)

彼(かれ)が本当(ほんとう)のことを言(い)っているかどうか、私(わたし)には判断(はんだん)できません。
I'm in no position to judge whether he's telling the truth.

サイズが合(あ)うかどうか、試着(しちゃく)してから決(き)めようと思(おも)います。
I'll try it on before deciding, to see whether the size works.

申(もう)し込(こ)みが締(し)め切(き)りに間(ま)に合(あ)ったかどうか、事務所(じむしょ)に確認(かくにん)してみます。
I'll check with the office whether my application made the deadline.

かどうか · Nuance & register

かどうか is register-neutral. It appears unchanged in a chat with friends, in a lecture and in a contract; as always, the politeness of the sentence lives on the final verb. It is also one of the few N4 patterns with no casual contraction of its own - although in relaxed speech people often drop どうか entirely and leave a bare か: 間(ま)に合(あ)うか分(わ)かんない。 That is shorter, not ruder.

The one thing かどうか cannot do is finish a sentence. It is a lump that some other verb has to pick up, so 来(く)るかどうか。 on its own is a fragment. If you want to end there, you need the wondering forms instead - 来(く)るかな, 来(く)るでしょうか.

かどうか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two fixed expressions are worth having ready. かどうかは別(べつ)として ("setting aside whether…") concedes a point before moving on, and かどうか分(わ)かりませんが is the standard hedge for offering something you are not sure will help: お役(やく)に立(た)つかどうか分(わ)かりませんが、こちらの資料(しりょう)をどうぞ。

かどうか · かどうか vs か

Rule of thumb - if the embedded question contains a question word （何(なに), どこ, だれ, いつ, どう, どちら）, use bare か. かどうか is only for questions whose answer is yes or no.

昨日(きのう)何(なに)を注文(ちゅうもん)したか覚(おぼ)えていません。
I don't remember what I ordered yesterday.

昨日(きのう)注文(ちゅうもん)したかどうか覚(おぼ)えていません。
I don't remember whether I ordered yesterday.

The first asks about the contents of the order, the second about whether the ordering happened at all. Sticking どうか onto the first - ❌何(なに)を注文(ちゅうもん)したかどうか - is ungrammatical, because 何(なに) has already supplied the question and どうか has nothing left to ask.

かどうか · かどうか vs か (cont.)

Bare か is also available for yes/no questions, especially in speech: 空(あ)いているか見(み)てきます。 かどうか simply spells the alternative out and is the clearer choice in writing.

かどうか · かどうか vs ～たら／～ば

Rule of thumb - English "if" is two unrelated words. "If" you could replace with whether → かどうか. "If" meaning on condition that → たら／ば／と.

木村(きむら)さんが参加(さんか)するかどうか、聞(き)いてみます。
I'll ask whether Kimura is taking part.

木村(きむら)さんが参加(さんか)したら、席(せき)を一(ひと)つ増(ふ)やします。
If Kimura takes part, I'll add one more seat.

These are not stylistic variants - they say different things. The first is about the speaker's ignorance; the second is about a consequence. "I don't know if he's coming" is ❌来(き)たら分(わ)かりません and ✅来(く)るかどうか分(わ)かりません. This is the most common single error with the pattern, and it comes straight out of English.

かどうか · かどうか vs ～か～ないか

Rule of thumb - same meaning, more deliberate delivery. And when the two options are not yes and no but two different things, only the spelled-out version works.

出席(しゅっせき)するかどうか、金曜日(きんようび)までにお返事(へんじ)ください。
Please reply by Friday as to whether you'll attend.

電車(でんしゃ)で行(い)くかバスで行(い)くか、まだ決(き)めていません。
I haven't decided yet whether to go by train or by bus.

The second could not be rewritten with かどうか: there is no no to negate, just two alternatives. Keep かどうか for the yes/no case and lay both options out when they are genuinely two options.

かどうか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この部屋(へや)が静(しず)かだかどうか確(たし)かめます。 → ✅ この部屋(へや)が静(しず)かかどうか確(たし)かめます。
    Nouns and な-adjectives drop だ before か. It is だ before と, no だ before か - the two quotative-ish particles behave in opposite ways.

  • ❌ 田中(たなか)さんが来(き)ますかどうか知(し)りません。 → ✅ 田中(たなか)さんが来(く)るかどうか知(し)りません。
    The embedded clause is plain. ます belongs to the end of the sentence, not to the middle of it.

  • ❌ 荷物(にもつ)が届(とど)いたら分(わ)かりません。 → ✅ 荷物(にもつ)が届(とど)いたかどうか分(わ)かりません。
    "I don't know if the parcel arrived" is whether, not a condition. たら here would mean "once it arrives, I won't understand", which is nonsense.

  • ❌ どこで買(か)えるかどうか教(おし)えてください。 → ✅ どこで買(か)えるか教(おし)えてください。
    With a question word present, どうか has nothing to do. Drop it.

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 注文(ちゅうもん)した品物(しなもの)が届(とど)いたかどうか確(たし)かめます。 (b) 注文(ちゅうもん)した品物(しなもの)が届(とど)いたら確(たし)かめます。

かもしれない

Meaning · might; maybe - the possibility is open, and the speaker refuses to say more than that

Take the pieces apart and the word explains itself: か (is it so?) + も (even) + 知(し)れない (cannot be known) - "it cannot even be known whether it is so." かもしれない does not say something is likely. It says the door is open, and leaves the door on the other side open too. That is the whole of its meaning, and it is why it sits at the bottom of the confidence ladder.

This is awkward to raise, but there may be an error somewhere in these figures.

かもしれない · Nuance & register

Plain かもしれない, polite かもしれません. In speech the tail is very often chopped off to かも - そうかも。／行(い)けないかも。 - and かも on its own is a complete reply, roughly "could be". かもね softens it further and かもよ warns. None of these short forms belong in writing; かもしれない itself is perfectly at home in an essay.

Its social use is at least as important as its logical one. Because かもしれません commits to nothing, it is the standard tool for raising something delicate: a risk nobody has mentioned, an error in a superior's work, a disagreement you do not want to turn into a confrontation. 私(わたし)の説明(せつめい)が足(た)りなかったかもしれません takes the blame without quite admitting it, which is exactly why it is said so often.

かもしれない · Nuance & register (cont.)

The flip side is that overusing it sounds evasive. If your manager asks when your own work will be finished, 明日(あした)終(お)わるかもしれません reads as "I haven't really thought about it". About your own actions and your own knowledge, commit: 終(お)わると思(おも)います.

もしかしたら and ひょっとすると at the head of the sentence flag the guess in advance and push it further towards "just possibly". They pair with かもしれない almost automatically, and a sentence that opens with もしかしたら and then ends flatly in です sounds unfinished.

かもしれない · かもしれない vs でしょう・だろう

Rule of thumb - でしょう is an estimate: the speaker thinks it probably is so. かもしれない is an admission: the speaker will only say it could be so, and that the opposite could be too.

午後(ごご)は風(かぜ)が強(つよ)くなるでしょう。
The wind will probably get stronger this afternoon.

午後(ごご)は風(かぜ)が強(つよ)くなるかもしれません。
The wind might get stronger this afternoon.

The forecaster says the first; the person deciding whether to hang the washing out says the second. There is a neat test: you can chain かもしれない with its own negation - 強(つよ)くなるかもしれないし、ならないかもしれない - and it sounds entirely normal, because both branches were open all along. Doing the same with でしょう sounds like someone contradicting themselves.

かもしれない · かもしれない vs はずだ

Rule of thumb - the difference is what justifies the claim. はずだ rests on a reason you could name if challenged. かもしれない rests on nothing at all, and needs nothing.

課長(かちょう)はもう着(つ)いているはずです。七時(しちじ)の電車(でんしゃ)に乗(の)ると言(い)っていましたから。
The section chief should already be there - he said he'd take the seven o'clock train.

課長(かちょう)はもう着(つ)いているかもしれません。
The section chief might already be there.

Ask どうして？ after the first and you owe an answer; the timetable is the grounds. Ask it after the second and "no particular reason, it's just possible" is a complete reply. This is also why はずです can be wrong in a way かもしれません cannot: if he is not there, your reasoning has failed, and Japanese has a form for exactly that disappointment - はずだったのに.

かもしれない · かもしれない vs ～と思(おも)う

Rule of thumb - と思(おも)う puts an opinion on the record. かもしれない declines to have one.

こちらの辞書(じしょ)のほうが使(つか)いやすいと思(おも)います。
I think this dictionary is easier to use.

こちらの辞書(じしょ)のほうが使(つか)いやすいかもしれません。
This dictionary might be easier to use.

Both are polite; only the second leaves the listener free to disagree without contradicting you, which is why it is the gentler way to suggest a change to someone senior. The cost is authority: if you are the person who is supposed to know, かもしれません shifts the decision back onto the listener.

かもしれない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 彼女(かのじょ)は元気(げんき)だかもしれません。 → ✅ 彼女(かのじょ)は元気(げんき)かもしれません。
    Nouns and な-adjectives lose だ in front of か. Compare 元気(げんき)だと思(おも)います, where the だ stays.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)は行(い)きますかもしれません。 → ✅ 明日(あした)は行(い)くかもしれません。
    The clause in front is plain. The politeness is already carried by かもしれません.

  • ❌ さっきの説明(せつめい)は間違(まちが)っているかもしれませんでした。 → ✅ さっきの説明(せつめい)は間違(まちが)っていたかもしれません。
    Past tense goes on the embedded clause, not on かもしれない. There is no ❌かもしれませんでした.

  • ❌ 「レポートはいつ出(だ)せますか。」「来週(らいしゅう)出(だ)せるかもしれません。」 → ✅ 「来週(らいしゅう)出(だ)せると思(おも)います。」
    Not a grammar error, a register one. About your own work, かもしれません sounds like you have not looked at the calendar.

はずだ

Meaning · it should be; it must be; it's supposed to be - an expectation you could justify if asked

はず is a noun meaning something like what follows. Attach a clause to it and you are saying: given what I know, this is how things ought to line up. The key word is given. はずだ always rests on grounds - a timetable, a promise, a rule, a fact you were told - and if somebody asks どうして？ you are expected to produce them.

はずだ · Formation

はず is a noun, so the clause in front takes the forms a noun-modifying clause takes: な-adjectives get な, nouns get の.

It's Thursday, so that shop should be shut.

This road should be the shorter one, though I'm not too sure.

彼(かれ)は運転(うんてん)が上手(じょうず)なはずです。10年(ねん)も毎日(まいにち)運転(うんてん)していますから。
He should be a good driver - he's driven every day for ten years.

That's odd. I'm sure I put it here, and yet I can't find it.

あの二人(ふたり)は同(おな)じ高校(こうこう)だから、知(し)り合(あ)いのはずですよ。
Those two went to the same school, so they should know each other.

My new card was supposed to arrive last week, and I haven't even had word.

はずだ · Nuance & register

はずだ is plain, はずです polite, and the pattern is otherwise register-neutral: it turns up in conversation, in business email and in written explanation without changing shape. In casual speech people say はずだよ, はずでしょ, and - dropping the copula entirely - 来(く)るはず、来(く)るはず。 as a mutter of reassurance.

The past form はずだった is worth learning as a unit. It does not mean "it should have been" in the sense of blame; it means the expectation existed and reality did not cooperate. It nearly always comes with のに or が, and it carries audible disappointment.

There is also a small idiom that catches learners off guard: はずだ after a discovery means "no wonder". You notice something, work out why, and say so.

どうりで寒(さむ)いはずだ。窓(まど)が開(あ)いている。
No wonder it's cold - the window's open.

はずだ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Here the speaker is not predicting anything; they are confirming, after the fact, that the world lines up. It is the same はず seen from the other end.

Finally, a limit: はずだ is poor at describing your own intentions, because you do not need to deduce what you have decided. 私(わたし)は明日(あした)行(い)くはずです sounds as though your movements are arranged by someone else. That said, when they genuinely are - a shift roster, a schedule you have been handed - はず about yourself is fine: 明日(あした)は休(やす)みのはずなんですが、確(たし)かめてみます。

はずだ · はずだ vs べきだ

Rule of thumb - English "should" is two words. "I expect it is so" → はずだ. "It is the right thing to do" → べきだ.

彼(かれ)はその話(はなし)をもう知(し)っているはずです。
He should already know about it. (I expect he does)

彼(かれ)にその話(はなし)を早(はや)く知(し)らせるべきです。
We should tell him about it soon. (it's the right thing to do)

The test is substitution. If "should" can become "is supposed to" or "I expect", it is はずだ, and the sentence is a prediction. If it can become "ought to", it is べきだ, and the sentence is a judgement. Telling a friend ❌早(はや)く寝(ね)るはずです does not advise them to go to bed; it predicts that they will.

はずだ · はずだ vs かもしれない・でしょう

Rule of thumb - a ladder of confidence, かもしれない → でしょう → はずだ - but はずだ is not merely the top rung. It is a deduction from stated grounds, where the other two are estimates.

部長(ぶちょう)は会議(かいぎ)中(ちゅう)のはずです。三時(さんじ)からだと聞(き)きました。
The director should be in a meeting - I was told it started at three.

部長(ぶちょう)は会議(かいぎ)中(ちゅう)でしょう。
The director is probably in a meeting.

Strip the second sentence of the first one and はずです starts to feel unsupported; でしょう never needs support. The practical consequence is that はずだ can be refuted: if the meeting was cancelled, your premise was wrong and you say 会議(かいぎ)中(ちゅう)のはずだったんですが. A でしょう that misses was only ever a guess.

はずだ · はずだ vs つもり・予定(よてい)

Rule of thumb - つもり is what I have decided, 予定(よてい) is what the diary says, はず is what I work out about someone or something else.

来月(らいげつ)引(ひ)っ越(こ)すつもりです。
I'm planning to move next month.

田中(たなか)さんは来月(らいげつ)引(ひ)っ越(こ)すはずです。
Tanaka should be moving next month, as far as I know.

Swap them and the meaning goes badly wrong: つもり about a third person claims to know their intentions, and はず about your own decision hands your life over to somebody else's timetable. 予定(よてい)です sits between them - it reports an arrangement without saying whose idea it was, which is why it is the safest thing to say at work.

はずだ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この辞書(じしょ)は便利(べんり)はずです。 → ✅ この辞書(じしょ)は便利(べんり)なはずです。
    はず is a noun, so a な-adjective in front of it needs its な.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)は祝日(しゅくじつ)はずですよ。 → ✅ 明日(あした)は祝日(しゅくじつ)のはずですよ。
    A noun links to はず with の. Dropping it is the same error one step along.

  • ❌ 体(からだ)に悪(わる)いから、たばこをやめるはずです。 → ✅ 体(からだ)に悪(わる)いから、たばこをやめるべきです。
    This is advice, not prediction. English "should" hides the difference; Japanese does not.

  • ❌ 私(わたし)は来年(らいねん)大学(だいがく)を受(う)けるはずです。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は来年(らいねん)大学(だいがく)を受(う)けるつもりです。
    Your own intention is not something you deduce. はず here sounds as if someone else had signed you up.

会議(かいぎ) and はず are both nouns, so they need の between them. Note that the other repair a learner reaches for - ❌会議(かいぎ)だはず - is equally wrong; だ is what the quotative と wants, not what はず wants.

はずだ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 山田(やまだ)さんは来(こ)ないはずです。 (b) 山田(やまだ)さんは来(こ)ないかもしれません。

(a) says you have a reason to expect his absence - he told you, or he is away, or the rota says so. If someone challenges you, you are supposed to be able to name it, and if he walks in you have been wrong about something.

(b) only concedes that his not coming is possible. No reason is implied and none is owed; it is a warning not to count on him rather than a claim about what will happen.

はずがない

Meaning · there's no way; it cannot possibly be - the negative twin of はずだ, and much stronger than it looks

はず is the noun "what follows from what I know". はずがない says there is no such noun to be had: nothing in what I know leaves room for this. It is not "probably not". It is a flat refusal to entertain the idea, and like はずだ it normally comes with a reason attached.

はずがない · Formation

Identical to はずだ: はず is a noun, so な-adjectives take な and nouns take の. Only the ending changes.

If you drink coffee every night, no wonder you can't sleep properly.

はずがない · Nuance & register

はずがない is emphatic. On the scale that runs 来(こ)ないかもしれません → 来(こ)ないでしょう → 来(こ)ないはずです → 来(く)るはずがありません, it sits at the far end: the last of those does not estimate a low probability, it rules the possibility out.

That force gives it a temperature. はずがない is often said with feeling - indignation, loyalty, disbelief - and あの人(ひと)が犯人(はんにん)のはずがありません is a defence of someone, not a calculation. Used against another person's claim it is combative: telling your manager そんなことがあるはずがありません is close to "that's simply not the case", and in a workplace you would usually reach for ないと思(おも)いますが instead.

はずがない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register: はずがない is the neutral form and works in speech and writing. はずがありません is its polite version. In casual speech the が drops - そんなはずないよ - which is a very common short reply. はずはない swaps が for contrastive は and narrows the denial to this one point: whatever else may be true, that cannot be.

はずがない · はずがない vs ないはずだ

Rule of thumb - はずがない denies the possibility. ないはずだ expects the negative outcome. The first says the idea is out of the question; the second says you would be surprised.

木村(きむら)さんがこの会(かい)に来(く)るはずがない。
There's no way Kimura would come to this.

木村(きむら)さんはこの会(かい)には来(こ)ないはずだ。
I don't think Kimura is coming to this one.

Both end up predicting his absence, but only the first would make you argue with someone who claimed to have seen him. ないはずだ is an ordinary expectation with ordinary grounds - a message he sent, a scheduling clash - and it can be quietly wrong. はずがない cannot be quietly wrong; if he walks in, something you believed was false.

はずがない · はずがない vs わけがない

Rule of thumb - very close in meaning. はずがない reasons from grounds; わけがない tends to be blunter and more emotional, and belongs more to speech.

この値段(ねだん)で本物(ほんもの)のはずがありません。
At this price it can't be genuine.

あんな話(はなし)、信(しん)じるわけがないでしょう。
As if anyone would believe a story like that.

Swapping them rarely produces something wrong, but the flavour shifts: わけがない carries a shrug or a snort with it, and is at home in an argument. はずがない keeps the shape of an inference even when it is said with heat, which is why it is the safer of the two in writing.

はずがない · はずがない vs ～ことができない

Rule of thumb - English "can't" is two things. "It is impossible that…" → はずがない. "is unable to…" → the potential form or ことができない.

弟(おとうと)はまだ泳(およ)ぐことができません。
My little brother can't swim yet.

弟(おとうと)が一人(ひとり)であそこまで泳(およ)いだはずがありません。
There's no way my brother swam out that far on his own.

The first is about his ability, the second about your belief. A sentence like ❌弟(おとうと)は泳(およ)ぐはずがありません, meant as "he can't swim", comes out as "there is no possibility of him swimming", which sounds like you are denying an accusation.

はずがない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ そんな作業(さぎょう)が簡単(かんたん)はずがない。 → ✅ そんな作業(さぎょう)が簡単(かんたん)なはずがない。
    はず is a noun; a な-adjective in front of it keeps its な.

  • ❌ これが彼(かれ)の字(じ)はずがありません。 → ✅ これが彼(かれ)の字(じ)のはずがありません。
    A noun links with の. The の is easy to lose when there is already another の earlier in the sentence.

  • ❌ 祖母(そぼ)はパソコンを使(つか)うはずがありません。（meaning: she isn't able to）→ ✅ 祖母(そぼ)はパソコンを使(つか)えません。
    Inability is the potential form. はずがない is about what you refuse to believe, not about what someone is capable of.

  • ❌ 彼(かれ)が約束(やくそく)を忘(わす)れないはずがない。 → ✅ 彼(かれ)が約束(やくそく)を忘(わす)れるはずがない。
    Two negatives cancel. The first sentence means "there's no way he'd fail to forget", which is the opposite of the intended defence.

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部長(ぶちょう)はその書類(しょるい)を見(み)ていないはずです。 (b) 部長(ぶちょう)がその書類(しょるい)を見(み)たはずがありません。

かな

Meaning · I wonder - a question aimed at yourself, said out loud

かな is the question particle か plus the soft な you tack onto a thought you are having rather than an enquiry you are making. Grammatically the sentence is a question; socially it is not, because nobody has been asked. That gap is what makes かな so useful: it lets you put a question into the room without putting anyone on the spot.

かな · Formation

かな · Nuance & register

かな is casual. It belongs with plain-form speech and with people you are relaxed around; the polite way to wonder is でしょうか, and to a customer or a superior that is what you should reach for. (In easy-going polite speech you will also hear ですかね and ますかね - a blend of the two registers that works among colleagues but is too loose for a formal setting.)

Its usefulness comes from the fact that it is addressed to nobody. Because you have not asked, the listener is free to answer or not, and that makes かな the natural way to float a request - 明日(あした)、車(くるま)貸(か)してもらえないかな - or to dissent gently: それはちょっと違(ちが)うかな is a good deal easier to hear than 違(ちが)います. The same trick works inside polite speech through かなと思(おも)います, which is common even in careful ます-style conversation.

かなあ, with the vowel stretched, is more wistful and more felt. On its own - どうかなあ。 - it can be a complete, non-committal answer meaning something like "hmm, I'm not sure about that."

かな · Nuance & register (cont.)

On who says it: かな is unmarked and used by speakers of every age and gender. Its close relative かしら leans strongly female in modern speech, and かね leans older and somewhat male. If you want one wondering form that never sounds odd coming out of your mouth, かな is it.

かな · かな vs か

Rule of thumb - か asks the listener. かな asks the air. Only one of them obliges anybody to answer.

山田(やまだ)さんは来(き)ますか。
Is Yamada coming?

山田(やまだ)さん、来(く)るかな。
I wonder whether Yamada's coming.

There is a second reason to prefer かな in casual speech: a plain-form question ending in bare か - 来(く)るか。 - sounds abrupt and rather rough, more like a demand than a question. Casual questions are normally marked by rising intonation alone （来(く)る？）or softened with かな. Bare か after a plain form is not a neutral casual question.

かな · かな vs かしら

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different speaker profile. かな is the unmarked choice; かしら leans distinctly female and slightly formal or old-fashioned.

今日(きょう)は傘(かさ)、いるかな。
Will I need an umbrella today?

今日(きょう)は傘(かさ)、いるかしら。
Will I need an umbrella today?

The sentences mean the same thing. What differs is what the listener infers about the speaker - and these are tendencies, not rules: plenty of women use かな as their default, and かしら is far more common in novels and drama than on the street.

かな · ～かな vs ～ないかな

Rule of thumb - put a negative in front of かな and wondering turns into hoping.

雨(あめ)、やむかな。
I wonder if the rain will stop.

雨(あめ)、やまないかなあ。
I do wish the rain would stop.

Literally the second asks "won't it stop?", but what it expresses is a wish. The stretched かなあ and a falling, sighing delivery are the usual signals. Context still matters: 来(こ)ないかな can also be a straight negative wondering - "I wonder if he isn't coming" - so listen for the tone.

かな · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この部屋(へや)、静(しず)かだかな。 → ✅ この部屋(へや)、静(しず)かかな。
    な-adjectives and nouns drop だ in front of か, just as they do before かどうか and かもしれない.

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)、この漢字(かんじ)の読(よ)み方(かた)は何(なに)かな。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)、この漢字(かんじ)の読(よ)み方(かた)は何(なん)でしょうか。
    かな is a casual, self-directed form. Pointed at a teacher it sounds as though you are talking past them.

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、これ、見(み)てもらえないかな。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、これ、見(み)ていただけないでしょうか。
    The indirect-request use of かな works downward and sideways, not upward.

  • ❌ (in an email to a client) ご都合(つごう)はいかがかな。 → ✅ ご都合(つごう)はいかがでしょうか。
    かな has no place in writing that is not a message to a friend.

かしら

Meaning · I wonder - the same wondering particle as かな, in a distinctly softer and more feminine key

かしら is a worn-down か知(し)らぬ - "I don't know whether…". Like かな it turns a question inward, so that it hangs in the air rather than landing on anybody. What separates the two is not meaning but who is heard saying it: in modern standard Japanese かしら leans strongly female, and carries a slightly refined, slightly old-fashioned air with it.

かしら · Nuance & register

Two separate things are going on, and keeping them apart saves a lot of confusion.

Register. かしら is casual. It sits on plain forms and belongs to relaxed speech, so despite its genteel ring it cannot be used where です／ます is required. What makes it sound refined is the company it keeps: it pairs naturally with polite vocabulary - どなた, いらっしゃる, くださる, ご覧(らん)になる - and that combination produces the soft, well-mannered effect people associate with it.

かしら · Nuance & register (cont.)

Speaker profile. In present-day standard Japanese かしら is heard mainly from women, and more from older speakers than younger ones; many younger women use かな as their default. In fiction and dubbing it is a stock signal for an elegant, mature or old-fashioned female character. These are tendencies, not rules - men did use it freely in earlier periods, and it survives in some regional and literary usage - but a learner who adopts かしら as a neutral "I wonder" will be heard as marked. If you want a wondering particle that raises no eyebrows, use かな.

かしら also does the two extra jobs かな does. With a negative it becomes a wish or a hint - 早(はや)く春(はる)が来(こ)ないかしら - and in the form ～てくださらないかしら it is an unusually soft request. Note the softness has a ceiling: it is gentle among equals, and still too informal to aim at a client.

かしら · かしら vs かな

Rule of thumb - identical grammar, identical meaning. The difference is entirely in who is expected to say it: かな is unmarked, かしら leans female and a touch formal.

この荷物(にもつ)、重(おも)すぎるかな。
Is this parcel too heavy, I wonder.

この荷物(にもつ)、重(おも)すぎるかしら。
Is this parcel too heavy, I wonder.

Nothing in the content changes. かな would be unremarkable from anyone; かしら sketches a speaker. Because the marking is one-directional - かな is safe for everybody, かしら is not - the practical advice is to recognise かしら fluently and produce かな, unless you are deliberately matching the speech style of the people around you.

かしら · かしら vs でしょうか

Rule of thumb - かしら is soft; でしょうか is polite. Softness is not politeness, and only one of them is safe upward.

会議(かいぎ)は何時(なんじ)からだったかしら。
What time was the meeting again?

会議(かいぎ)は何時(なんじ)からだったでしょうか。
Might I ask what time the meeting was again?

The first is fine muttered to a colleague you know well and wrong addressed to a director. でしょうか keeps you in ます／です style while still avoiding a blunt question, which is why it is the workhorse of polite enquiry.

かしら · ～かしら vs ～ないかしら

Rule of thumb - as with かな, adding a negative turns the wondering into hoping or into a hint.

田中(たなか)さん、気(き)がつくかしら。
I wonder whether Tanaka will notice.

誰(だれ)か気(き)がついてくれないかしら。
I do wish somebody would notice.

The second is not really a question about anyone's likelihood of noticing; it is a wish, and said in front of the right person it is a request that never had to be made. This indirectness is the main reason かしら survives in polite female speech: it asks for things without asking.

かしら · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この説明(せつめい)で十分(じゅうぶん)だかしら。 → ✅ この説明(せつめい)で十分(じゅうぶん)かしら。
    な-adjectives and nouns lose だ before か, in かしら exactly as in かな and かどうか.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)は来(き)ますかしら。 → ✅ 明日(あした)は来(く)るかしら。／いらっしゃるでしょうか。
    かしら attaches to plain forms. Adding ます in front does not make it polite; it just breaks it.

  • ❌ (in an email to a client) ご確認(かくにん)いただけるかしら。 → ✅ ご確認(かくにん)いただけないでしょうか。
    However genteel it sounds, かしら is casual speech and reads as startlingly informal in business writing.

かしら · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ A learner using かしら as their everyday "I wonder" in mixed company. → ✅ かな.
    Not an error of grammar but of self-presentation. かしら tells the listener something about you that you may not have meant to say.

かしら · Practice 1

1. もう八時(はちじ)ね。あの店(みせ)、まだ開(あ)いている____。

まだ開(あ)いているかしら。

A plain-form verb takes かしら directly. The sentence is a thought said out loud rather than a question put to anyone, which is precisely the slot this particle fills.

かしら · Practice 2

2. Choose the form suitable for asking your department head: 資料(しりょう)は明日(あした)までに（できるかしら・できるでしょうか）。

資料(しりょう)は明日(あした)までにできるでしょうか。

かしら is soft but casual, and softness does not substitute for politeness when you are speaking upward. でしょうか keeps the enquiry indirect while staying in polite style.

かしら · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese using かしら: "I wonder whether this cake is sweet enough."

このケーキ、甘(あま)さは足(た)りているかしら。

An い-adjective or a plain verb attaches with nothing in between. Said while tasting something you have made yourself, this is the classic setting for かしら - a question addressed to nobody in particular.

かしら · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 田中(たなか)さん、この辺(へん)は静(しず)かだかしら。

✅ 田中(たなか)さん、この辺(へん)は静(しず)かかしら。

静(しず)か is a な-adjective, and its だ disappears before か. The habit comes from 静(しず)かだと思(おも)う, where the だ is required - と and か behave in opposite ways.

かしら · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 雪(ゆき)になるかな。 (b) 雪(ゆき)になるかしら。

The two sentences say the same thing: the speaker is wondering, out loud, whether it will turn to snow. Neither is more or less certain than the other.

What differs is the picture the listener forms of the speaker. (a) is unmarked and could come from anyone. (b) reads as female and slightly refined or old-fashioned - a tendency rather than a rule, but a strong enough one that the choice carries information.

かい

Meaning · turns a plain-form sentence into a yes/no question - friendly, familiar, and noticeably old-fashioned

かい is the question particle か with the edges rounded off. A plain-form sentence ending in bare か sounds curt, almost like an interrogation; add the い and the same question comes out warm - the tone of a parent asking a child how school went. That warmth is the whole point of the particle, and it comes with a speaker profile attached.

かい · Formation

Plain forms only. Nouns and な-adjectives drop だ.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + かい 行(い)く → 行(い)くかい
Verb (past) た-form + かい 終(お)わった → 終(お)わったかい
Verb (negative) ない-form + かい 要(い)らない → 要(い)らないかい
い-adjective plain form + かい 痛(いた)い → 痛(いた)いかい
な-adjective drop だ + かい 元気(げんき)だ → 元気(げんき)かい
Noun drop だ + かい 本当(ほんとう)だ → 本当(ほんとう)かい
Explanatory ～のかい／～んかい 来(き)たのかい

かい · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - かい is for yes/no questions only. With a question word in the sentence you need だい instead: ❌どこへ行(い)くかい → ✅どこへ行(い)くんだい. The pair かい／だい splits the work that polite ですか does on its own.

かい · Examples

元気(げんき)かい。
How are you keeping?

もう宿題(しゅくだい)は終(お)わったかい。
Have you finished your homework yet?

お茶(ちゃ)でも飲(の)むかい。
Fancy a cup of tea?

一人(ひとり)でここまで来(き)たのかい。
Did you come all this way on your own?

その荷物(にもつ)、重(おも)くないかい。持(も)とうか。
Isn't that bag heavy? Let me take it.

かい · Examples (cont.)

そうかい。それは大変(たいへん)だったね。
Is that right. That must have been rough.

久(ひさ)しぶりだね。仕事(しごと)のほうは順調(じゅんちょう)かい。
Long time no see. Is work going all right?

まだ諦(あきら)めていないかい。それなら、もう一度(いちど)やってみなさい。
So you haven't given up? In that case, have another go.

かい · Nuance & register

かい is plain-style and cannot be made polite: there is no ❌かいです, and ますかい is not a form of standard Tokyo Japanese. If you need politeness, the question is ですか／ますか.

Its social shape matters more than its grammar. In modern standard Japanese かい leans male and older, and it leans downward or sideways - a parent to a child, a grandparent to a grandchild, a teacher to a pupil, a senior colleague being kind to a junior. Younger speakers use it rarely in earnest; where they do, it is often a deliberate impression of that older voice. Translated fiction and dubbing are full of it for exactly this reason: it is the standard way to make a character sound avuncular. As always these are tendencies rather than rules, but they are strong enough that a learner using かい with a superior will sound not merely informal but oddly patronising.

かい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The default casual yes/no question in ordinary modern speech is not かい at all - it is the plain form said with a rise: 行(い)く？／食(た)べた？ かい is the marked, warmer, older-sounding alternative to that.

One expression worth knowing on its own: そうかい. Said with interest it means "oh really?"; said flat it means "oh, is that so" and sounds dismissive, even sulky. The words are identical, so listen to the delivery.

かい · かい vs だい

Rule of thumb - かい answers yes or no. だい answers a question word. They are a matched pair and cannot be swapped.

明日(あした)も来(く)るかい。
Are you coming tomorrow as well?

明日(あした)は何時(なんじ)に来(く)るんだい。
What time are you coming tomorrow?

だい normally follows the explanatory の／ん, which is why you see んだい far more often than bare だい after a verb; after a noun it stands alone - 名前(なまえ)は何(なん)だい。 Both particles carry the same familiar, male-leaning, somewhat dated flavour, so they turn up together in the mouth of the same character.

かい · かい vs か

Rule of thumb - bare か on a plain form is blunt; かい is the same question made kind.

分(わ)かったか。
Got that?

分(わ)かったかい。
Did that make sense?

The first is the tone of an instructor or a superior checking compliance, and it can land as a challenge. The second checks the same fact with none of the pressure. This is worth remembering in reverse too: bare plain-form か is not a neutral casual question, and a learner reaching for informality often produces something much sharper than intended.

かい · かい vs rising intonation

Rule of thumb - the everyday casual question is the plain form with a rise. かい adds warmth and a generation.

これ、食(た)べる？
Are you going to eat this?

これ、食(た)べるかい。
Would you like this?

Both are casual and both are friendly. The difference is that the first is unmarked in any age group, while the second sketches an older speaker addressing someone younger. If you are a learner in your twenties talking to friends, the rise is what you want; keep かい for recognising, and for the moments when you are consciously playing the older role.

かい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ どこで買(か)ったかい。 → ✅ どこで買(か)ったんだい。
    A question word rules かい out. かい is strictly yes/no; だい handles the rest.

  • ❌ 試験(しけん)は心配(しんぱい)だかい。 → ✅ 試験(しけん)は心配(しんぱい)かい。
    な-adjectives and nouns lose だ in front of か, as they do before every other か-based ending.

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、もうお帰(かえ)りかい。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、もうお帰(かえ)りですか。
    かい is friendly, but it is friendly downward. Aimed upward it manages to be informal and condescending at the same time.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)も来(き)ますかい。 → ✅ 明日(あした)も来(く)るかい。／来(き)ますか。
    かい attaches to plain forms. There is no polite version of it - you switch particles instead.

かい · Practice 1

1. （おじいさんが孫(まご)に）そんなに走(はし)って、疲(つか)れていない____。

疲(つか)れていないかい。

A yes/no question in plain style, asked kindly and downward - the exact situation かい exists for. Bare 疲(つか)れていないか would sound like a sergeant checking on a recruit.

かい · Practice 2

2. Choose: 昨日(きのう)は（どこへ行(い)ったかい・どこへ行(い)ったんだい）。

昨日(きのう)はどこへ行(い)ったんだい。

The sentence contains どこ, so it is not a yes/no question and かい cannot be used. だい is the question-word member of the pair, and it normally follows the explanatory ん.

かい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese in the かい register: "Is that lunch enough for you?"

その昼(ひる)ごはんで足(た)りるかい。

A plain-form verb takes かい directly. The English "for you" needs no pronoun - the particle itself makes clear you are asking the person in front of you.

かい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 先生(せんせい)、この問題(もんだい)の答(こた)えは三番(さんばん)かい。

✅ 先生(せんせい)、この問題(もんだい)の答(こた)えは三番(さんばん)ですか。

The form is well built, but the direction is wrong. かい is used by the senior party to the junior one; a student asking a teacher needs plain ですか.

かい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) もう帰(かえ)るのか。 (b) もう帰(かえ)るのかい。

(a) is blunt. Depending on the delivery it can be a neutral checking of a fact, but it easily reads as displeasure - "you're leaving already?" - and it leans distinctly masculine and rough.

(b) asks the same thing gently. The い takes the sharpness out and adds a note of concern, as though the speaker is older than the listener and mildly sorry to see them go.

じゃないか

Meaning · isn't it? - an assertion wearing the clothes of a negative question

じゃないか is not really a question. It states something and dares the listener to disagree: look, it's raining / I did tell you / hey, that's rather good. The negative-question shape is what makes it feel shared rather than imposed - you are inviting the other person to notice what you have noticed. Which of its several flavours you get depends almost entirely on tone and situation.

じゃないか · Formation

Attaches to plain forms. Nouns and な-adjectives drop だ.

Written / rhetorical ではないか 言(い)ったではないか

じゃないか · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Floating a suspicion ～んじゃないか 間違(まちが)っているんじゃないか

Rule of thumb - no だ in front. ❌嘘(うそ)だじゃないか. The じゃ is already the contracted では of the copula, so the copula cannot appear twice.

じゃないか · Examples

ほら、やっぱり雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ってきたじゃないか。
See - it's started raining after all.

いいじゃないか。一杯(いっぱい)くらい。
Oh, come on. One drink won't hurt.

あれ、財布(さいふ)、ここにあったじゃない。
Oh - the wallet was here all along.

昨日(きのう)、遅(おく)れるなって言(い)ったじゃないか。
I told you yesterday not to be late, didn't I.

なかなか上手(じょうず)じゃないか。いつ習(なら)ったんだ？
Hey, that's not bad at all. When did you learn?

じゃないか · Examples (cont.)

駅(えき)の前(まえ)に大(おお)きな本屋(ほんや)があったじゃないですか。あそこが閉(し)まるそうです。
You know that big bookshop in front of the station? Apparently it's closing.

そんなに急(いそ)がなくてもいいじゃないですか。まだ時間(じかん)はありますよ。
There's really no need to rush, is there. We've still got time.

この説明(せつめい)だと、読(よ)む人(ひと)が混乱(こんらん)するんじゃないでしょうか。
Wouldn't this explanation leave the reader confused?

じゃないか · Nuance & register

The forms make a ladder, and the rungs are far apart.

じゃないか with the か said out loud is blunt and, in casual speech, leans male. じゃない with a rise - 安(やす)いじゃない？ - is the wider-used everyday version and sounds softer. じゃん is very casual and youthful. Going the other way, じゃないですか is the ordinary polite form, ではありませんか is formal and a little theatrical, and ではないか belongs to written argument, where it works as a rhetorical "is it not the case that…?" rather than as a question anybody answers.

Three things it does, all recognisable in context:

じゃないか · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • Pointing out - you have spotted something the listener has not.
    ここに書(か)いてあるじゃないですか。
  • Reproach or reminder - you said this before and were ignored.
    だから気(き)をつけろと言(い)ったじゃないか。
  • Discovery - pleased surprise. 意外(いがい)とおいしいじゃないか。

A warning about the polite form. じゃないですか is often used to open a topic by presenting something as shared knowledge - 去年(きょねん)、地震(じしん)があったじゃないですか. That is fine when the listener genuinely does know. Used about things they could not possibly know - 私(わたし)、甘(あま)い物(もの)が苦手(にがて)じゃないですか - it is widely criticised in Japan as pushy, because it assigns the listener knowledge they never had. Say んですが instead.

じゃないか · じゃないか vs plain じゃない

Rule of thumb - same syllables, two different sentences. Falling じゃない is a plain negative. Rising じゃない, and anything with か attached, is an assertion seeking agreement.

これは私(わたし)のかばんじゃない。
This isn't my bag.

これ、私(わたし)のかばんじゃない？
Hang on - this is my bag, isn't it?

Adding か removes the ambiguity entirely: 私(わたし)のかばんじゃないか can only be the second reading. In writing, where intonation is unavailable, the question mark or the か is doing all the work, so leave it in.

じゃないか · じゃないか vs でしょう

Rule of thumb - both fish for agreement. でしょう assumes the listener already knows and asks them to confirm. じゃないか asserts and presses, often implying they had overlooked it.

昨日(きのう)話(はな)したでしょう？
I mentioned it yesterday, remember?

昨日(きのう)話(はな)したじゃないか。
I did tell you yesterday!

The first is a reminder; the second is close to an accusation. That extra push is why じゃないか is risky upward: telling a manager 間違(まちが)っているじゃないですか is not "isn't this wrong?" but "this is wrong and you missed it."

じゃないか · じゃないか vs ～んじゃないか

Rule of thumb - bare じゃないか asserts something you are sure of. んじゃないか floats a suspicion you are not sure of at all.

この数字(すうじ)、間違(まちが)っているじゃないか。
This figure is wrong - look.

この数字(すうじ)、間違(まちが)っているんじゃないか。
I've a feeling this figure might be wrong.

The ん turns the whole thing tentative, and the polite んじゃないでしょうか is one of the most useful phrases in a Japanese workplace: it raises a doubt without accusing anyone of anything. When you want to question a superior's work, this is the form to reach for.

じゃないか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この辺(へん)は静(しず)かだじゃないですか。 → ✅ この辺(へん)は静(しず)かじゃないですか。
    じゃ is already the copula. な-adjectives and nouns attach bare.

  • ❌ もう終(お)わりましたじゃないですか。 → ✅ もう終(お)わったじゃないですか。
    The clause in front is plain. です on the end carries all the politeness the sentence needs.

  • ❌ 課長(かちょう)、この計算(けいさん)、違(ちが)うじゃないですか。 → ✅ 課長(かちょう)、この計算(けいさん)、違(ちが)うんじゃないでしょうか。
    Polite in form, confrontational in force. Adding ん and でしょう converts an accusation into a query.

  • ❌ 私(わたし)、北海道(ほっかいどう)の出身(しゅっしん)じゃないですか。 → ✅ 私(わたし)、北海道(ほっかいどう)の出身(しゅっしん)なんですが。
    You cannot present your own biography as something the listener already knows. This misuse is a well-known irritant.

じゃないか · Practice 1

1. ほら、傘(かさ)を持(も)っていけと言(い)った____。びしょぬれだよ。

傘(かさ)を持(も)っていけと言(い)ったじゃないか。

The speaker warned the listener earlier and was ignored, which is the reproach use. でしょう would soften it into a mere reminder, and the drenched listener makes clear this is not a mere reminder.

じゃないか · Practice 2

2. Choose the safer form for pointing out a possible error in your boss's document: この日付(ひづけ)、（違(ちが)うじゃないですか・違(ちが)うんじゃないでしょうか）。

この日付(ひづけ)、違(ちが)うんじゃないでしょうか。

じゃないですか asserts the error and implies it was overlooked. The ん plus でしょうか presents the same observation as a doubt of your own, which leaves the other person room to check it themselves.

じゃないか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, casually: "Look, there's a light on - someone's still in the office."

ほら、電気(でんき)がついているじゃないか。まだ誰(だれ)か残(のこ)っているよ。

The first clause is a discovery you are drawing the listener's attention to, which is exactly what じゃないか marks. In a softer register you could say ついているじゃない？ with a rise.

じゃないか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: あの店(みせ)のラーメン、けっこう有名(ゆうめい)だじゃないですか。

✅ あの店(みせ)のラーメン、けっこう有名(ゆうめい)じゃないですか。

有名(ゆうめい) is a な-adjective, and じゃ is the contracted では of the copula, so putting だ in front doubles it up. Compare 有名(ゆうめい)だと思(おも)います, where the だ is required.

じゃないか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼(かれ)は知(し)らないじゃないですか。 (b) 彼(かれ)は知(し)らないんじゃないでしょうか。

(a) asserts that he does not know and treats it as obvious - the sort of thing you say to someone who has just proposed asking him. It carries a note of "you should have realised that", so it can sting.

(b) offers his not knowing as a possibility you have thought of. It commits to nothing and leaves the decision with the listener, which makes it the form to use when you are speaking upward or when you might turn out to be wrong.
