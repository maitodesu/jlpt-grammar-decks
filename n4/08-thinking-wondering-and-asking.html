～と思う

Meaning · I think (that) … - 思おもう is "think", and と is the string that ties a whole sentence into a parcel for it to carry

と is a quotation marker. It takes an entire sentence, wraps it up, and hands it to a verb of thinking or saying. と思おもう is therefore not "think about" - it reports a complete thought, word for word, as your opinion. The parcel is always tied in plain form, no matter how polite the sentence around it is.

～と思う · Formation

と follows the plain form of the quoted clause. Nouns and な-adjectives keep their だ.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + と思おもう 来くる → 来くると思おもう
Verb (past) た-form + と思おもう 終おわった → 終おわったと思おもう
Verb (negative) ない-form + と思おもう 間まに合あわない → 間まに合あわないと思おもう
い-adjective plain form + と思おもう 安やすい → 安やすいと思おもう
な-adjective + だ + と思おもう 無理むり → 無理むりだと思おもう
Noun + だ + と思おもう 冗談じょうだん → 冗談じょうだんだと思おもう
Volitional - "I'm thinking of…" よう-form + と思おもう 休やすもう → 休やすもうと思おもう
Somebody else's opinion plain form + と思おもっている 正ただしい → 正ただしいと思おもっている

～と思う · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - nothing polite goes in front of と. ❌行いきますと思おもいます → ✅行いくと思おもいます. The politeness of the whole sentence is carried by the final verb and nowhere else.

Note - the だ that disappears before でしょう stays before と. It is 大丈夫だいじょうぶでしょう but 大丈夫だいじょうぶだと思おもいます.

～と思う · Examples

あの店みせはもう閉しまっていると思おもう。
I think that shop has already closed.

この傘かさは山田やまださんのだと思おもいます。
I think this umbrella is Yamada's.

試験しけんはそんなに難むずかしくないと思おもうよ。
I don't think the test will be all that hard.

今年ことしの冬ふゆは去年きょねんより暖あたたかいと思おもいませんか。
Don't you think this winter is milder than last year's?

～と思う · Examples (cont.)

来月らいげつから自転車じてんしゃで通かよおうと思おもっています。
I'm thinking of cycling to work from next month.

妹いもうとは自分じぶんの料理りょうりが世界せかいで一番いちばんおいしいと思おもっている。
My sister is convinced her own cooking is the best in the world.

会議かいぎは三時さんじに終おわると思おもいますが、念ねんのため確認かくにんしておきます。
I think the meeting finishes at three, but I'll check to be safe.

正直しょうじきに言いうと、あの計画けいかくはうまくいかないと思おもいます。
To be honest, I don't think that plan is going to work.

～と思う · Nuance & register

思おもう is the plain form and 思おもいます the polite one; everything before と is untouched by that choice. This is the single most useful thing to know about the pattern, and the single most common place learners slip.

Beyond grammar, と思おもいます is a softener. A bare assertion - その方法ほうほうは無理むりです - commits you to a fact. Adding と思おもいます reframes it as one person's reading of the situation, which is why Japanese workplace speech is full of it. Overusing it is a real thing too: answering a question about your own department with 多分たぶんそうだと思おもいます can sound as if you have not bothered to find out.

～と思う · Nuance & register (cont.)

In casual speech you will hear って in place of と - 無理むりだって思おもった - and the whole clause often ends in んだけど to leave the door open: 明日あしたでもいいと思おもうんだけど。 At the other end, formal writing prefers the passive と思おもわれます or と考かんがえられます, which removes the "I" altogether.

One limit worth stating plainly: the plain-form rule is about indirect quotation. If you put actual quotation marks round someone's words you are reproducing what came out of their mouth, so anything can appear inside: 「明日あしたは休やすみます」と言いっていました。 That is a direct quote, and it is not an exception to the rule - it is a different operation.

～と思う · ～と思う vs ～と思っている

Rule of thumb - 思おもう is a thought formed now, at the moment of speaking. 思おもっている is a view somebody holds - and it is the only option for a flat statement about a third person.

その色いろのほうが似合にあうと思おもう。
I think that colour suits you better.

父ちちは私わたしをまだ子こどもだと思おもっている。
My father still thinks of me as a child.

～と思う · ～と思う vs ～と思っている (cont.)

You cannot see into someone else's head, so a bare ❌父ちちは…と思おもう claims access you do not have. The ている turns it into an observation about a state you can infer from outside. About yourself both work: 私わたしもそう思おもっています just adds that you have thought so for a while.

～と思う · ～と思う vs ～でしょう

Rule of thumb - と思おもう labels the claim as mine. でしょう estimates how likely the claim is. One is about the speaker, the other about the world.

来年らいねんは野菜やさいの値段ねだんが上あがると思おもいます。
I think vegetable prices will go up next year.

来年らいねんは野菜やさいの値段ねだんが上あがるでしょう。
Vegetable prices will probably go up next year.

～と思う · ～と思う vs ～でしょう (cont.)

The second is what a news presenter says; the first is what you say at dinner. Because と思おもいます keeps the responsibility on you, it is usually the safer choice when you might be wrong, and the wrong choice when you are supposed to be the authority.

～と思う · ～ないと思う vs ～と思わない

Rule of thumb - English negates "think"; Japanese negates the thought. "I don't think it will rain" is 降ふらないと思おもう, not 降ふると思おもわない.

彼女かのじょはその話はなしを知しらないと思おもいます。
I don't think she knows about it.

と思おもわない is not ungrammatical - it is simply much stronger. It rejects the proposition rather than doubting it, so it lands as disagreement:

私わたしはそうは**思おもいません**。
I don't see it that way.

～と思う · ～ないと思う vs ～と思わない (cont.)

Note the は on そう, which is typical: negating 思おもう almost always comes with contrastive は, and that is part of why it feels like pushing back.

～と思う · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日あしたは雨あめが**降ふりますと思おもいます**。 → ✅ 明日あしたは雨あめが**降ふると思おもいます**。
    Nothing in ます-form may sit in front of と. Politeness belongs to the last verb.

  • ❌ この辞書じしょは**便利べんりと思おもう**。 → ✅ この辞書じしょは**便利べんりだと思おもう**。
    な-adjectives and nouns keep だ before と. Only い-adjectives and verbs attach bare.

  • ❌ 弟おとうとはその映画えいががつまらないと**思おもいます**。 → ✅ 弟おとうとはその映画えいががつまらないと**思おもっています**。
    A plain statement about what someone else thinks needs ている - or a hedge such as 思おもっているようです.

  • ❌ 時間じかんがあると思おもいません。 → ✅ 時間じかんがないと思おもいます。
    Put the negative inside the quoted thought. The outer negative is reserved for disagreeing.

～と思う · Practice 1

1. 田中たなかさんは来週らいしゅう出張しゅっちょう____思おもいます。

田中たなかさんは来週らいしゅう出張しゅっちょうだと思おもいます。

出張しゅっちょう is a noun, and a noun predicate keeps its だ in front of the quotative と. Dropping it is only correct before でしょう.

～と思う · Practice 2

2. Choose: 姉あねは自分じぶんが正ただしい（と思おもいます・と思おもっています）。

姉あねは自分じぶんが正ただしいと思おもっています。

The subject is a third person, so a bare と思おもいます would claim direct knowledge of her mind. ている presents the belief as something you observe her holding.

～と思う · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I don't think he will come today."

彼かれは今日きょう来こないと思おもいます。

The negative goes inside the quoted thought, not on 思おもう. 来くると思おもいません would read as flatly rejecting the idea rather than doubting it.

～と思う · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: あの人ひとは親切しんせつですと思おもいます。

✅ あの人ひとは**親切しんせつだと思おもいます**。

Two things go wrong at once: です cannot appear before と, and the な-adjective still needs its plain copula だ. The polite ending survives on 思おもいます.

～と思う · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この案あんは失敗しっぱいすると思おもいます。 (b) この案あんは失敗しっぱいするでしょう。

(a) marks the prediction as your own reading and keeps the responsibility with you - appropriate in a meeting where you are offering an opinion someone may argue with.

(b) states it as a likely outcome, the way a forecast or an analysis does. It sounds more detached and more authoritative, which is fine from an expert and presumptuous from a newcomer.

って

Meaning · the spoken shortcut - "they said…", "you know…", "the thing called…"

って is what と (and often という, and sometimes は) becomes in relaxed speech. It quotes, it reports hearsay, it introduces a name, and it puts a topic on the table with a little more energy than は does. One syllable, several jobs - but they all come from the same place: って presents a chunk of language and asks the listener to take it as a unit.

って · Formation

Like と, って follows the plain form. Nouns and な-adjectives keep だ when って is quoting; they drop it when って is marking a topic.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb - quoting plain form + って（+ 言いう／聞きく／思おもう） 辞やめる → 辞やめるって言いってた
Noun / な-adjective - quoting + だ + って 中止ちゅうし → 中止ちゅうしだって聞きいた
Sentence-final - hearsay plain form + って（falling tone） 来こない → 来こないって
Direct quote 「…」+ って 「まだだ」 → 「まだだ」って言いわれた
Naming - replaces という Noun + って + Noun 林はやし → 林はやしさんって人ひと
Topic - replaces は / というのは Noun + って（no だ） 敬語けいご → 敬語けいごって難むずかしい

って · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - って inherits the と rule exactly: the clause in front of it is plain, however polite you are being. ❌晴はれますって → ✅晴はれるって. The one exception is a direct quote in 「」, where you are copying someone's actual words.

って · Examples

田中たなかさん、明日あした来こないって。
Tanaka says he's not coming tomorrow.

会議かいぎ、中止ちゅうしだって。
Apparently the meeting's off.

漢字かんじって覚おぼえるのが大変たいへんだね。
Kanji really are a slog to memorise, aren't they.

さっき先生せんせいが今日きょうの宿題しゅくだいはないって言いってたよ。
The teacher said a moment ago that there's no homework today.

って · Examples (cont.)

林はやしさんって人ひとから電話でんわがありましたよ。
There was a phone call from someone called Hayashi.

「もう帰かえりたい」って顔かおをしているよ。
You've got an "I want to go home already" look on your face.

妹いもうとに貸かした本ほん、まだ読よんでないって言いうんだけど、もう一いっか月げつ経たつよ。
My sister says she still hasn't read the book I lent her - and it's been a month.

来年らいねん留学りゅうがくするって本当ほんとう？いつ決きめたの？
Is it true you're studying abroad next year? When did you decide?

って · Nuance & register

って is spoken casual and nothing else. It belongs in conversation with friends, family and people you are on plain-form terms with. It does not belong in an email to a client, a report, an essay, or a sentence you are otherwise ending in ます. The neutral replacements are と for quoting and そうです／ とのことです for hearsay.

Intonation does a lot of work. Falling って at the end of a sentence is hearsay - 雨あめになるって。 Rising って? is a request for confirmation, close to "wait, did you say…?" - 明日あした休やすみだって？ Both are the same particle.

って · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two extensions you will hear constantly and will not find in a textbook: っていうか at the head of a sentence, which retracts what you just said and restarts ("or rather…"), and ってば, which repeats something impatiently - だから違ちがうってば。 The second is distinctly childish or intimate; it is not a neutral emphasis marker.

There is a semi-polite hearsay form, ～んですって, which keeps a です on the end: となりの店みせ、閉しめるんですって。 It is polite in form but chatty in feel, and in modern Tokyo speech it leans female. ～だってです is not a form at all.

って · って vs と

Rule of thumb - same grammar, different register: って is と in a T-shirt. But って also covers jobs と cannot do, namely naming and topic-marking.

課長かちょうは出席しゅっせきできないと言いっていました。
The section chief said he can't attend.

課長かちょう、出席しゅっせきできないって。
The boss says he can't make it.

って · って vs と (cont.)

Both report the same fact. The first is what you write in a message to the team; the second is what you say over your shoulder to the colleague next to you. Where the two part company is self-introduction and any fixed formula: 田中たなかと申もうします is the set phrase, and って there is simply wrong.

って · topic って vs は

Rule of thumb - は sets a topic quietly. って sets a topic and signals that you are about to say something about the category itself, usually with a reaction attached.

日本にほんの夏なつは暑あついです。
Summers in Japan are hot.

日本にほんの夏なつって暑あついね。
Japanese summers are hot, aren't they.

って · topic って vs は (cont.)

The って version reads as a comment brought up fresh, and it pairs naturally with ね, よ and evaluative adjectives. It is close to 日本にほんの夏なつというのは, which is exactly what it is a contraction of - and that is why it can also mean "what is…?": 「バイト」って何なに？

って · hearsay って vs ～そうだ

Rule of thumb - identical content, opposite registers. って is what you say; そうです is what you write.

山田やまださん、来月らいげつ引ひっ越こすって。
Yamada's moving house next month, apparently.

山田やまださんは来月らいげつ引ひっ越こすそうです。
I hear that Yamada is moving house next month.

って · hearsay って vs ～そうだ (cont.)

One practical difference: って easily carries a named source in front of it - 母ははが寒さむくなるって言いってた - while そうだ usually leaves the source vague or marks it with によると. If you want to say who told you, って is the more natural spoken frame.

って · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私わたしは田中たなかって申もうします。 → ✅ 私わたしは田中たなかと申もうします。
    Self-introduction is a fixed humble formula. って cannot be dropped into a phrase that is formal by definition.

  • ❌ 天気予報てんきよほうで明日あしたは**晴はれますって**言いってた。 → ✅ …晴はれるって言いってた。
    An indirect quote takes the plain form, exactly as with と. ます only survives inside 「」.

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、会議かいぎは中止ちゅうしだってです。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、会議かいぎは中止ちゅうしだそうです。
    You cannot make って polite by bolting です on. Switch to そうです or とのことです.

  • ❌ (in a written report) 調査ちょうさの結果けっか、問題もんだいはなかったって。 → ✅ …問題もんだいはなかったとのことです。
    って is a spoken form. In writing it reads as though the report were a text message.

って · Practice 1

1. 友達ともだちに：「山田やまださん、今日きょう来こられない____。」

山田やまださん、今日きょう来こられないって。

Sentence-final って after a plain form reports what you were told, with the source left implicit. Said with a falling tone it means "apparently", not "did you say?".

って · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural option for an email to a client: 納期のうきは来週らいしゅう（だって・だそうです）。

納期のうきは来週らいしゅうだそうです。

って is casual spoken Japanese and never appears in business writing. The hearsay meaning is unchanged; only the register is fixed.

って · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, casually: "Someone called Kimura came looking for you."

木村きむらさん**って人ひと**が来きたよ。

って before a noun is the contraction of という and is the everyday way of naming someone the listener may not know. 木村きむらさんという人ひと is the same thing in a neutral register.

って · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼かれは新あたらしい仕事しごとを探さがしていますって言いってた。

✅ 彼かれは新あたらしい仕事しごとを**探さがしているって**言いってた。

The quoted clause must be plain. って is only a casual と, so it obeys the same restriction: nothing in ます-form immediately in front of it.

って · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 一人暮ひとりぐらしはお金かねがかかります。 (b) 一人暮ひとりぐらしってお金かねがかかるよね。

(a) is a neutral, polite statement of fact - the kind of line that could open a paragraph in an article about living costs.

(b) raises "living alone" as a topic the two of you both know something about, and adds a shared reaction with よね. って marks the category as newly put on the table and invites agreement; it is conversation, not exposition.

かどうか

Meaning · whether or not - packs a yes/no question inside a bigger sentence

かどうか takes a question that could be answered yes or no, ties it into a single lump, and hands that lump to a verb like 分わかる, 知しらない or 調しらべる. It is literally "か（yes?）どう（how?）か" - is it so, or how is it? The English that maps onto it is whether, and mapping it onto if instead is where nearly all the trouble starts.

かどうか · Formation

か attaches to plain forms. Nouns and な-adjectives lose their だ - the opposite of what happens before the quotative と.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + かどうか 届とどく → 届とどくかどうか
Verb (past) た-form + かどうか 送おくった → 送おくったかどうか
Verb (negative) ない-form + かどうか 要いらない → 要いらないかどうか
い-adjective plain form + かどうか 新あたらしい → 新あたらしいかどうか
な-adjective drop だ + かどうか 安全あんぜんだ → 安全あんぜんかどうか
Noun drop だ + かどうか 本物ほんものだ → 本物ほんものかどうか
Both options spelled out ～か～ないか 行いく → 行いくか行いかないか

かどうか · Formation (cont.)

Typical main verbs after it: 分わかる, 知しらない, 聞きく, 調しらべる, 確たしかめる, 迷まよう, 覚おぼえていない, 決きめる.

Rule of thumb - nothing polite and no だ in front of か. ❌行いきますかどうか, ❌安全あんぜんだかどうか → ✅行いくかどうか, ✅安全あんぜんかどうか.

Note - the particle that would follow the lump (を, が) is normally left out: 届とどいたかどうか確たしかめます. は does appear, and adds contrast: 届とどいたかどうかは、まだ分わかりません。

かどうか · Examples

これでいいかどうか、見みてください。
Please have a look and tell me whether this is all right.

明日あした雪ゆきが降ふるかどうか分わかりません。
I don't know whether it will snow tomorrow.

この水みずが安全あんぜんかどうか、先さきに調しらべたほうがいいよ。
You should check first whether this water is safe.

受うかったかどうかは、来週らいしゅうの金曜日きんようびに分わかります。
Whether I passed or not will be clear next Friday.

かどうか · Examples (cont.)

パーティーに出でるかどうか、まだ迷まよっているんです。
I still can't make up my mind whether to go to the party.

彼かれが本当ほんとうのことを言いっているかどうか、私わたしには判断はんだんできません。
I'm in no position to judge whether he's telling the truth.

サイズが合あうかどうか、試着しちゃくしてから決きめようと思おもいます。
I'll try it on before deciding, to see whether the size works.

申もうし込こみが締しめ切きりに間まに合あったかどうか、事務所じむしょに確認かくにんしてみます。
I'll check with the office whether my application made the deadline.

かどうか · Nuance & register

かどうか is register-neutral. It appears unchanged in a chat with friends, in a lecture and in a contract; as always, the politeness of the sentence lives on the final verb. It is also one of the few N4 patterns with no casual contraction of its own - although in relaxed speech people often drop どうか entirely and leave a bare か: 間まに合あうか分わかんない。 That is shorter, not ruder.

The one thing かどうか cannot do is finish a sentence. It is a lump that some other verb has to pick up, so 来くるかどうか。 on its own is a fragment. If you want to end there, you need the wondering forms instead - 来くるかな, 来くるでしょうか.

かどうか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two fixed expressions are worth having ready. かどうかは別べつとして ("setting aside whether…") concedes a point before moving on, and かどうか分わかりませんが is the standard hedge for offering something you are not sure will help: お役やくに立たつかどうか分わかりませんが、こちらの資料しりょうをどうぞ。

かどうか · かどうか vs か

Rule of thumb - if the embedded question contains a question word （何なに, どこ, だれ, いつ, どう, どちら）, use bare か. かどうか is only for questions whose answer is yes or no.

昨日きのう何なにを注文ちゅうもんしたか覚おぼえていません。
I don't remember what I ordered yesterday.

昨日きのう注文ちゅうもんしたかどうか覚おぼえていません。
I don't remember whether I ordered yesterday.

かどうか · かどうか vs か (cont.)

The first asks about the contents of the order, the second about whether the ordering happened at all. Sticking どうか onto the first - ❌何なにを注文ちゅうもんしたかどうか - is ungrammatical, because 何なに has already supplied the question and どうか has nothing left to ask.

Bare か is also available for yes/no questions, especially in speech: 空あいているか見みてきます。 かどうか simply spells the alternative out and is the clearer choice in writing.

かどうか · かどうか vs ～たら／～ば

Rule of thumb - English "if" is two unrelated words. "If" you could replace with whether → かどうか. "If" meaning on condition that → たら／ば／と.

木村きむらさんが参加さんかするかどうか、聞きいてみます。
I'll ask whether Kimura is taking part.

木村きむらさんが参加さんかしたら、席せきを一ひとつ増ふやします。
If Kimura takes part, I'll add one more seat.

かどうか · かどうか vs ～たら／～ば (cont.)

These are not stylistic variants - they say different things. The first is about the speaker's ignorance; the second is about a consequence. "I don't know if he's coming" is ❌来きたら分わかりません and ✅来くるかどうか分わかりません. This is the most common single error with the pattern, and it comes straight out of English.

かどうか · かどうか vs ～か～ないか

Rule of thumb - same meaning, more deliberate delivery. And when the two options are not yes and no but two different things, only the spelled-out version works.

出席しゅっせきするかどうか、金曜日きんようびまでにお返事へんじください。
Please reply by Friday as to whether you'll attend.

電車でんしゃで行いくかバスで行いくか、まだ決きめていません。
I haven't decided yet whether to go by train or by bus.

The second could not be rewritten with かどうか: there is no no to negate, just two alternatives. Keep かどうか for the yes/no case and lay both options out when they are genuinely two options.

かどうか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この部屋へやが**静しずかだかどうか**確たしかめます。 → ✅ この部屋へやが**静しずかかどうか**確たしかめます。
    Nouns and な-adjectives drop だ before か. It is だ before と, no だ before か - the two quotative-ish particles behave in opposite ways.

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんが**来きますかどうか**知しりません。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんが**来くるかどうか**知しりません。
    The embedded clause is plain. ます belongs to the end of the sentence, not to the middle of it.

  • ❌ 荷物にもつが**届とどいたら**分わかりません。 → ✅ 荷物にもつが**届とどいたかどうか**分わかりません。
    "I don't know if the parcel arrived" is whether, not a condition. たら here would mean "once it arrives, I won't understand", which is nonsense.

かどうか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ どこで買かえるかどうか教おしえてください。 → ✅ どこで買かえるか教おしえてください。
    With a question word present, どうか has nothing to do. Drop it.

かどうか · Practice 1

1. この情報じょうほうが正確せいかく____確たしかめる必要ひつようがあります。

この情報じょうほうが**正確せいかくかどうか**確たしかめる必要ひつようがあります。

正確せいかく is a な-adjective, so its だ disappears before か. Writing ❌正確せいかくだかどうか is the single most frequent form error here.

かどうか · Practice 2

2. Choose: 誰だれが担当たんとうする（か・かどうか）聞きいておきます。

誰だれが担当たんとうするか聞きいておきます。

The clause already contains the question word 誰だれ, so the question has been asked and どうか would be asking a second, different one. かどうか is reserved for yes/no.

かどうか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I don't know whether this key fits that door."

この鍵かぎがあのドアに**合あうかどうか**分わかりません。

"Whether" is the giveaway for かどうか. Note that a conditional （❌合あったら分わかりません）would say something quite different and quite strange.

かどうか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 息子むすこが朝あさごはんを食たべましたかどうか覚おぼえていません。

✅ 息子むすこが朝あさごはんを**食たべたかどうか**覚おぼえていません。

An embedded question takes the plain form, so 食たべました becomes 食たべた. The sentence stays polite thanks to 覚おぼえていません at the end.

かどうか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 注文ちゅうもんした品物しなものが届とどいたかどうか確たしかめます。 (b) 注文ちゅうもんした品物しなものが届とどいたら確たしかめます。

(a) makes the arrival itself the unknown: you do not yet know whether the goods came, and checking is how you will find out.

(b) treats the arrival as a condition that has not been met yet: once the goods do arrive, you will check them (presumably for damage). English "if" covers both, which is exactly why the two get swapped.

かもしれない

Meaning · might; maybe - the possibility is open, and the speaker refuses to say more than that

Take the pieces apart and the word explains itself: か (is it so?) + も (even) + 知しれない (cannot be known) - "it cannot even be known whether it is so." かもしれない does not say something is likely. It says the door is open, and leaves the door on the other side open too. That is the whole of its meaning, and it is why it sits at the bottom of the confidence ladder.

かもしれない · Formation

Attaches to plain forms. Nouns and な-adjectives drop だ.

Rule of thumb - tense lives inside, politeness lives outside. "It might have been wrong" is 間違まちがっていたかもしれません, never ❌間違まちがっているかもしれませんでした.

かもしれない · Examples

電車でんしゃが遅おくれるかもしれない。
The train might be late.

あの人ひとは田中たなかさんの弟おとうとさんかもしれません。
That might be Tanaka's younger brother.

かぎを家いえに忘わすれてきたかもしれない。
I may have left my keys at home.

山やまの上うえは思おもったより寒さむいかもしれないから、上着うわぎを持もっていこう。
It might be colder than we expect up the mountain, so let's take jackets.

かもしれない · Examples (cont.)

もしかしたら、私わたしの勘違かんちがいだったかもしれません。
It's possible I got the wrong end of the stick.

この方法ほうほうは少すこし面倒めんどうかもしれないけど、確実かくじつだよ。
This way might be a bit of a faff, but it's reliable.

返事へんじがないので、メールが届とどいていないかもしれませんね。
There's been no reply, so perhaps the email never arrived.

言いいにくいのですが、この数字すうじはどこかで間違まちがっているかもしれません。
This is awkward to raise, but there may be an error somewhere in these figures.

かもしれない · Nuance & register

Plain かもしれない, polite かもしれません. In speech the tail is very often chopped off to かも - そうかも。／行いけないかも。 - and かも on its own is a complete reply, roughly "could be". かもね softens it further and かもよ warns. None of these short forms belong in writing; かもしれない itself is perfectly at home in an essay.

かもしれない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Its social use is at least as important as its logical one. Because かもしれません commits to nothing, it is the standard tool for raising something delicate: a risk nobody has mentioned, an error in a superior's work, a disagreement you do not want to turn into a confrontation. 私わたしの説明せつめいが足たりなかったかもしれません takes the blame without quite admitting it, which is exactly why it is said so often.

The flip side is that overusing it sounds evasive. If your manager asks when your own work will be finished, 明日あした終おわるかもしれません reads as "I haven't really thought about it". About your own actions and your own knowledge, commit: 終おわると思おもいます.

かもしれない · Nuance & register (cont.)

もしかしたら and ひょっとすると at the head of the sentence flag the guess in advance and push it further towards "just possibly". They pair with かもしれない almost automatically, and a sentence that opens with もしかしたら and then ends flatly in です sounds unfinished.

かもしれない · かもしれない vs でしょう・だろう

Rule of thumb - でしょう is an estimate: the speaker thinks it probably is so. かもしれない is an admission: the speaker will only say it could be so, and that the opposite could be too.

午後ごごは風かぜが強つよくなるでしょう。
The wind will probably get stronger this afternoon.

午後ごごは風かぜが強つよくなるかもしれません。
The wind might get stronger this afternoon.

かもしれない · かもしれない vs でしょう・だろう (cont.)

The forecaster says the first; the person deciding whether to hang the washing out says the second. There is a neat test: you can chain かもしれない with its own negation - 強つよくなるかもしれないし、ならないかもしれない - and it sounds entirely normal, because both branches were open all along. Doing the same with でしょう sounds like someone contradicting themselves.

かもしれない · かもしれない vs はずだ

Rule of thumb - the difference is what justifies the claim. はずだ rests on a reason you could name if challenged. かもしれない rests on nothing at all, and needs nothing.

課長かちょうはもう着ついているはずです。七時しちじの電車でんしゃに乗のると言いっていましたから。
The section chief should already be there - he said he'd take the seven o'clock train.

課長かちょうはもう着ついているかもしれません。
The section chief might already be there.

Ask どうして？ after the first and you owe an answer; the timetable is the grounds. Ask it after the second and "no particular reason, it's just possible" is a complete reply. This is also why はずです can be wrong in a way かもしれません cannot: if he is not there, your reasoning has failed, and Japanese has a form for exactly that disappointment - はずだったのに.

かもしれない · かもしれない vs ～と思う

Rule of thumb - と思おもう puts an opinion on the record. かもしれない declines to have one.

こちらの辞書じしょのほうが使つかいやすいと思おもいます。
I think this dictionary is easier to use.

こちらの辞書じしょのほうが使つかいやすいかもしれません。
This dictionary might be easier to use.

Both are polite; only the second leaves the listener free to disagree without contradicting you, which is why it is the gentler way to suggest a change to someone senior. The cost is authority: if you are the person who is supposed to know, かもしれません shifts the decision back onto the listener.

かもしれない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 彼女かのじょは**元気げんきだかもしれません**。 → ✅ 彼女かのじょは**元気げんきかもしれません**。
    Nouns and な-adjectives lose だ in front of か. Compare 元気げんきだと思おもいます, where the だ stays.

  • ❌ 明日あしたは**行いきますかもしれません**。 → ✅ 明日あしたは**行いくかもしれません**。
    The clause in front is plain. The politeness is already carried by かもしれません.

  • ❌ さっきの説明せつめいは**間違まちがっているかもしれませんでした**。 → ✅ さっきの説明せつめいは**間違まちがっていたかもしれません**。
    Past tense goes on the embedded clause, not on かもしれない. There is no ❌かもしれませんでした.

かもしれない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 「レポートはいつ出だせますか。」「来週らいしゅう出だせるかもしれません。」 → ✅ 「来週らいしゅう出だせると思おもいます。」
    Not a grammar error, a register one. About your own work, かもしれません sounds like you have not looked at the calendar.

かもしれない · Practice 1

1. 空そらが暗くらくなってきましたね。夕方ゆうがたから雨あめ____。

夕方ゆうがたから雨あめかもしれません。

雨あめ is a noun, so it attaches bare - no だ. You could equally say 降ふるかもしれません with the verb; either way the か needs a plain form or a bare noun in front of it.

かもしれない · Practice 2

2. Choose: 新幹線しんかんせんは時間じかんに正確せいかくだから、もう着ついている（かもしれません・はずです）。

もう着ついているはずです。

The sentence supplies a reason - the bullet train runs on time - and a stated reason is precisely what はずです requires. かもしれません would throw that reasoning away and say only that it is possible.

かもしれない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I might have taken the wrong umbrella."

傘かさを**間違まちがえたかもしれません**。

The past tense goes inside the clause, on 間違まちがえる. かもしれません itself never conjugates for tense.

かもしれない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この問題もんだいは簡単かんたんだかもしれませんが、時間じかんがかかります。

✅ この問題もんだいは**簡単かんたんかもしれません**が、時間じかんがかかります。

簡単かんたん is a な-adjective and must drop だ before か. Learners who have just learned 簡単かんたんだと思おもう carry the だ across by habit.

かもしれない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれは来こないでしょう。 (b) 彼かれは来こないかもしれません。

(a) is a prediction the speaker is fairly confident in - "he probably won't come." If you are organising the room, you would set out one chair fewer.

(b) only opens the possibility, and leaves his coming equally open. It is what you say to warn people not to count on him, and it is also the more tactful of the two, since it does not tell anyone what to expect.

はずだ

Meaning · it should be; it must be; it's supposed to be - an expectation you could justify if asked

はず is a noun meaning something like what follows. Attach a clause to it and you are saying: given what I know, this is how things ought to line up. The key word is given. はずだ always rests on grounds - a timetable, a promise, a rule, a fact you were told - and if somebody asks どうして？ you are expected to produce them.

はずだ · Formation

はず is a noun, so the clause in front takes the forms a noun-modifying clause takes: な-adjectives get な, nouns get の.

はずだ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + はずだ 届とどく → 届とどくはずだ
Verb (past) た-form + はずだ 出でた → 出でたはずだ
Verb (negative) ない-form + はずだ 来こない → 来こないはずだ
い-adjective plain form + はずだ 安やすい → 安やすいはずだ
な-adjective + な + はずだ 便利べんりだ → 便利べんりなはずだ
Noun + の + はずだ 休やすみ → 休やすみのはずだ

Expectation that failed はずだった（のに） 届とどくはずだった

はずだ · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - な for な-adjectives, の for nouns. ❌便利べんりはずだ, ❌休やすみはずだ. Compare かもしれない, which takes both bare - the two patterns pull in opposite directions and learners routinely swap them.

はずだ · Examples

銀行ぎんこうは三時さんじまで開あいているはずです。
The bank should be open until three.

今日きょうは木曜日もくようびだから、あの店みせは休やすみのはずだ。
It's Thursday, so that shop should be shut.

昨日きのう送おくりましたから、もう届とどいているはずです。
I posted it yesterday, so it should have arrived by now.

この道みちのほうが近ちかいはずなんだけど、あまり自信じしんがない。
This road should be the shorter one, though I'm not too sure.

はずだ · Examples (cont.)

彼かれは運転うんてんが上手じょうずなはずです。10年ねんも毎日まいにち運転うんてんしていますから。
He should be a good driver - he's driven every day for ten years.

おかしいな。ここに置おいたはずなのに、見みつからない。
That's odd. I'm sure I put it here, and yet I can't find it.

あの二人ふたりは同おなじ高校こうこうだから、知しり合あいのはずですよ。
Those two went to the same school, so they should know each other.

新あたらしいカードは先週せんしゅう届とどくはずだったのに、まだ連絡れんらくもありません。
My new card was supposed to arrive last week, and I haven't even had word.

はずだ · Nuance & register

はずだ is plain, はずです polite, and the pattern is otherwise register-neutral: it turns up in conversation, in business email and in written explanation without changing shape. In casual speech people say はずだよ, はずでしょ, and - dropping the copula entirely - 来くるはず、来くるはず。 as a mutter of reassurance.

The past form はずだった is worth learning as a unit. It does not mean "it should have been" in the sense of blame; it means the expectation existed and reality did not cooperate. It nearly always comes with のに or が, and it carries audible disappointment.

There is also a small idiom that catches learners off guard: はずだ after a discovery means "no wonder". You notice something, work out why, and say so.

はずだ · Nuance & register (cont.)

どうりで寒さむいはずだ。窓まどが開あいている。
No wonder it's cold - the window's open.

Here the speaker is not predicting anything; they are confirming, after the fact, that the world lines up. It is the same はず seen from the other end.

Finally, a limit: はずだ is poor at describing your own intentions, because you do not need to deduce what you have decided. 私わたしは明日あした行いくはずです sounds as though your movements are arranged by someone else. That said, when they genuinely are - a shift roster, a schedule you have been handed - はず about yourself is fine: 明日あしたは休やすみのはずなんですが、確たしかめてみます。

はずだ · はずだ vs べきだ

Rule of thumb - English "should" is two words. "I expect it is so" → はずだ. "It is the right thing to do" → べきだ.

彼かれはその話はなしをもう知しっているはずです。
He should already know about it. (I expect he does)

彼かれにその話はなしを早はやく知しらせるべきです。
We should tell him about it soon. (it's the right thing to do)

はずだ · はずだ vs べきだ (cont.)

The test is substitution. If "should" can become "is supposed to" or "I expect", it is はずだ, and the sentence is a prediction. If it can become "ought to", it is べきだ, and the sentence is a judgement. Telling a friend ❌早はやく寝ねるはずです does not advise them to go to bed; it predicts that they will.

はずだ · はずだ vs かもしれない・でしょう

Rule of thumb - a ladder of confidence, かもしれない → でしょう → はずだ - but はずだ is not merely the top rung. It is a deduction from stated grounds, where the other two are estimates.

部長ぶちょうは会議かいぎ中ちゅうのはずです。三時さんじからだと聞ききました。
The director should be in a meeting - I was told it started at three.

部長ぶちょうは会議かいぎ中ちゅうでしょう。
The director is probably in a meeting.

はずだ · はずだ vs かもしれない・でしょう (cont.)

Strip the second sentence of the first one and はずです starts to feel unsupported; でしょう never needs support. The practical consequence is that はずだ can be refuted: if the meeting was cancelled, your premise was wrong and you say 会議かいぎ中ちゅうのはずだったんですが. A でしょう that misses was only ever a guess.

はずだ · はずだ vs つもり・予定

Rule of thumb - つもり is what I have decided, 予定よてい is what the diary says, はず is what I work out about someone or something else.

来月らいげつ引ひっ越こすつもりです。
I'm planning to move next month.

田中たなかさんは来月らいげつ引ひっ越こすはずです。
Tanaka should be moving next month, as far as I know.

はずだ · はずだ vs つもり・予定 (cont.)

Swap them and the meaning goes badly wrong: つもり about a third person claims to know their intentions, and はず about your own decision hands your life over to somebody else's timetable. 予定よていです sits between them - it reports an arrangement without saying whose idea it was, which is why it is the safest thing to say at work.

はずだ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この辞書じしょは**便利べんりはずです。 → ✅ この辞書じしょは便利べんりなはず*です。
    はず is a noun, so a な-adjective in front of it needs its な.*

  • ❌ 明日あしたは**祝日しゅくじつはずですよ。 → ✅ 明日あしたは祝日しゅくじつのはず**ですよ。
    A noun links to はず with の. Dropping it is the same error one step along.

  • ❌ 体からだに悪わるいから、たばこをやめるはずです。 → ✅ 体からだに悪わるいから、たばこをやめるべきです。
    This is advice, not prediction. English "should" hides the difference; Japanese does not.

  • ❌ 私わたしは来年らいねん大学だいがくを**受うけるはずです。 → ✅ 私わたしは来年らいねん大学だいがくを受うけるつもり**です。
    Your own intention is not something you deduce. はず here sounds as if someone else had signed you up.

はずだ · Practice 1

1. 昨日きのうメールで送おくりましたから、もう読よんでいる____です。

もう読よんでいるはずです。

The first half supplies the grounds - you sent it yesterday - and grounds are exactly what はず is built on. かもしれません would waste the reason you just gave.

はずだ · Practice 2

2. Choose: 子こどもはもっと外そとで遊あそぶ（はず・べき）だと思おもいます。

子こどもはもっと外そとで遊あそぶべきだと思おもいます。

This is a value judgement about what is right, so it takes べき. はず would turn it into a prediction that children are going to play outside more, which is not an opinion anyone holds.

はずだ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "That restaurant should be quiet on a weekday."

あのレストランは平日へいじつなら**静しずかなはず**です。

静しずか is a な-adjective, so it needs な before はず. Note that a learner who has just met かもしれない will be tempted to write ❌静しずかはず by analogy - the two patterns take opposite forms.

はずだ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 田中たなかさんは今日きょう、午後ごごから会議かいぎはずですよ。

✅ 田中たなかさんは今日きょう、午後ごごから**会議かいぎのはず**ですよ。

会議かいぎ and はず are both nouns, so they need の between them. Note that the other repair a learner reaches for - ❌会議かいぎだはず - is equally wrong; だ is what the quotative と wants, not what はず wants.

はずだ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 山田やまださんは来こないはずです。 (b) 山田やまださんは来こないかもしれません。

(a) says you have a reason to expect his absence - he told you, or he is away, or the rota says so. If someone challenges you, you are supposed to be able to name it, and if he walks in you have been wrong about something.

(b) only concedes that his not coming is possible. No reason is implied and none is owed; it is a warning not to count on him rather than a claim about what will happen.

はずがない

Meaning · there's no way; it cannot possibly be - the negative twin of はずだ, and much stronger than it looks

はず is the noun "what follows from what I know". はずがない says there is no such noun to be had: nothing in what I know leaves room for this. It is not "probably not". It is a flat refusal to entertain the idea, and like はずだ it normally comes with a reason attached.

はずがない · Formation

Identical to はずだ: はず is a noun, so な-adjectives take な and nouns take の. Only the ending changes.

はずがない · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + はずがない 勝かつ → 勝かつはずがない
Verb (past) た-form + はずがない 知しっていた → 知しっていたはずがない

はずがない · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the negative goes on ない, not on the verb. 来くるはずがない = "there's no way he's coming." 来こないはずがない = "there's no way he isn't coming" - a double negative, and almost never what a learner means.

はずがない · Examples

こんな時間じかんに田中たなかさんが来くるはずがない。
There's no way Tanaka is coming at this hour.

その話はなしが本当ほんとうのはずがありません。
That story can't possibly be true.

まだ習ならっていないのだから、分わかるはずがないよ。
They haven't been taught it yet, so of course they can't be expected to understand.

一週間いっしゅうかんで全部ぜんぶ覚おぼえられるはずがないでしょう。
There's no way anyone could memorise the lot in a week.

はずがない · Examples (cont.)

あんなに練習れんしゅうしたのだから、失敗しっぱいするはずがありません。
She's practised that hard - there's no chance of her slipping up.

鍵かぎをかけて出でたのだから、誰だれかが入はいれたはずはない。
I locked up when I left, so nobody can have got in.

先月せんげつ入はいったばかりの人ひとがそんな事情じじょうを知しっていたはずがありません。
Someone who only joined last month couldn't possibly have known about that.

毎晩まいばんコーヒーを飲のんでいたら、よく眠ねむれるはずがないよ。
If you drink coffee every night, no wonder you can't sleep properly.

はずがない · Nuance & register

はずがない is emphatic. On the scale that runs 来こないかもしれません → 来こないでしょう → 来こないはずです → 来くるはずがありません, it sits at the far end: the last of those does not estimate a low probability, it rules the possibility out.

That force gives it a temperature. はずがない is often said with feeling - indignation, loyalty, disbelief - and あの人ひとが犯人はんにんのはずがありません is a defence of someone, not a calculation. Used against another person's claim it is combative: telling your manager そんなことがあるはずがありません is close to "that's simply not the case", and in a workplace you would usually reach for ないと思おもいますが instead.

はずがない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register: はずがない is the neutral form and works in speech and writing. はずがありません is its polite version. In casual speech the が drops - そんなはずないよ - which is a very common short reply. はずはない swaps が for contrastive は and narrows the denial to this one point: whatever else may be true, that cannot be.

はずがない · はずがない vs ないはずだ

Rule of thumb - はずがない denies the possibility. ないはずだ expects the negative outcome. The first says the idea is out of the question; the second says you would be surprised.

木村きむらさんがこの会かいに来くるはずがない。
There's no way Kimura would come to this.

木村きむらさんはこの会かいには来こないはずだ。
I don't think Kimura is coming to this one.

Both end up predicting his absence, but only the first would make you argue with someone who claimed to have seen him. ないはずだ is an ordinary expectation with ordinary grounds - a message he sent, a scheduling clash - and it can be quietly wrong. はずがない cannot be quietly wrong; if he walks in, something you believed was false.

はずがない · はずがない vs わけがない

Rule of thumb - very close in meaning. はずがない reasons from grounds; わけがない tends to be blunter and more emotional, and belongs more to speech.

この値段ねだんで本物ほんもののはずがありません。
At this price it can't be genuine.

あんな話はなし、信しんじるわけがないでしょう。
As if anyone would believe a story like that.

Swapping them rarely produces something wrong, but the flavour shifts: わけがない carries a shrug or a snort with it, and is at home in an argument. はずがない keeps the shape of an inference even when it is said with heat, which is why it is the safer of the two in writing.

はずがない · はずがない vs ～ことができない

Rule of thumb - English "can't" is two things. "It is impossible that…" → はずがない. "is unable to…" → the potential form or ことができない.

弟おとうとはまだ泳およぐことができません。
My little brother can't swim yet.

弟おとうとが一人ひとりであそこまで泳およいだはずがありません。
There's no way my brother swam out that far on his own.

はずがない · はずがない vs ～ことができない (cont.)

The first is about his ability, the second about your belief. A sentence like ❌弟おとうとは泳およぐはずがありません, meant as "he can't swim", comes out as "there is no possibility of him swimming", which sounds like you are denying an accusation.

はずがない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ そんな作業さぎょうが**簡単かんたんはずがない**。 → ✅ そんな作業さぎょうが**簡単かんたんなはずがない**。
    はず is a noun; a な-adjective in front of it keeps its な.

  • ❌ これが彼かれの**字じはずがありません**。 → ✅ これが彼かれの**字じのはずがありません**。
    A noun links with の. The の is easy to lose when there is already another の earlier in the sentence.

  • ❌ 祖母そぼはパソコンを**使つかうはずがありません**。（meaning: she isn't able to）→ ✅ 祖母そぼはパソコンを**使つかえません**。
    Inability is the potential form. はずがない is about what you refuse to believe, not about what someone is capable of.

はずがない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 彼かれが約束やくそくを**忘わすれないはずがない**。 → ✅ 彼かれが約束やくそくを**忘わすれるはずがない**。
    Two negatives cancel. The first sentence means "there's no way he'd fail to forget", which is the opposite of the intended defence.

はずがない · Practice 1

1. 昨日きのう自分じぶんで確認かくにんしたのだから、間違まちがっている____。

間違まちがっているはずがありません。

The first clause supplies the grounds, and the conclusion is a flat denial rather than a low estimate. 間違まちがっていないでしょう would water down a reason you have just stated outright.

はずがない · Practice 2

2. Choose: 妹いもうとはまだ三さんさいなので、漢字かんじが（読よめません・読よめるはずがありません）。

妹いもうとはまだ三さんさいなので、漢字かんじが**読よめません**。

This is a plain statement about ability, so the potential negative is what you want. 読よめるはずがありません would be arguing with someone who had suggested she could - grammatical, but a different conversation.

はずがない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "A room this small can't cost eighty thousand yen."

こんなに狭せまい部屋へやが八万円はちまんえんもするはずがありません。

"Can't" here means "it is impossible that", so はずがない is right rather than a potential form. 八万円はちまんえんのはずがありません works too, with the noun linked by の.

はずがない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: あの人ひとが嘘うそはずがない。

✅ あの人ひとが**嘘うそをつくはずがない**。／あの人ひとの話はなしが**嘘うそのはずがない**。

嘘うそ is a noun, so it cannot sit bare in front of はず - either link it with の, or use the verb 嘘うそをつく and attach はずがない to that instead.

はずがない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部長ぶちょうはその書類しょるいを見みていないはずです。 (b) 部長ぶちょうがその書類しょるいを見みたはずがありません。

(a) is an ordinary expectation: as far as you know the document never reached him. If it turns out he did see it, you were simply working from incomplete information.

(b) rules it out. You are asserting that his having seen it is impossible - because the file was locked, or because it was only printed this morning. Said in a meeting, (b) contradicts whoever suggested otherwise, so it carries a good deal more weight and a good deal more risk.

かな

Meaning · I wonder - a question aimed at yourself, said out loud

かな is the question particle か plus the soft な you tack onto a thought you are having rather than an enquiry you are making. Grammatically the sentence is a question; socially it is not, because nobody has been asked. That gap is what makes かな so useful: it lets you put a question into the room without putting anyone on the spot.

かな · Formation

Will we make it in time, I wonder.

この服ふく、似合にあうかな。
Does this outfit suit me?

Hmm - where did I put the keys?

明日あしたの試合しあい、雨あめで中止ちゅうしになるかな。
I wonder if tomorrow's match will be rained off.

かな · Examples (cont.)

ちょっと手伝てつだってもらえないかな。
Any chance you could give me a hand?

早はやくバスが来こないかなあ。足あしが痛いたくなってきた。
I wish the bus would hurry up. My feet are starting to ache.

この色いろ、派手はですぎるかな。地味じみなほうがいいかもしれない。
Is this colour too loud? A plainer one might be better.

上司じょうしに相談そうだんしたほうがいいかなと思おもったんですが、どう思おもいますか。
I was wondering whether I ought to raise it with my boss - what do you reckon?

かな · Nuance & register

かな is casual. It belongs with plain-form speech and with people you are relaxed around; the polite way to wonder is でしょうか, and to a customer or a superior that is what you should reach for. (In easy-going polite speech you will also hear ですかね and ますかね - a blend of the two registers that works among colleagues but is too loose for a formal setting.)

Its usefulness comes from the fact that it is addressed to nobody. Because you have not asked, the listener is free to answer or not, and that makes かな the natural way to float a request - 明日あした、車くるま貸かしてもらえないかな - or to dissent gently: それはちょっと違ちがうかな is a good deal easier to hear than 違ちがいます. The same trick works inside polite speech through かなと思おもいます, which is common even in careful ます-style conversation.

かな · Nuance & register (cont.)

かなあ, with the vowel stretched, is more wistful and more felt. On its own - どうかなあ。 - it can be a complete, non-committal answer meaning something like "hmm, I'm not sure about that."

On who says it: かな is unmarked and used by speakers of every age and gender. Its close relative かしら leans strongly female in modern speech, and かね leans older and somewhat male. If you want one wondering form that never sounds odd coming out of your mouth, かな is it.

かな · かな vs か

Rule of thumb - か asks the listener. かな asks the air. Only one of them obliges anybody to answer.

山田やまださんは来きますか。
Is Yamada coming?

山田やまださん、来くるかな。
I wonder whether Yamada's coming.

かな · かな vs か (cont.)

There is a second reason to prefer かな in casual speech: a plain-form question ending in bare か - 来くるか。 - sounds abrupt and rather rough, more like a demand than a question. Casual questions are normally marked by rising intonation alone （来くる？）or softened with かな. Bare か after a plain form is not a neutral casual question.

かな · かな vs かしら

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different speaker profile. かな is the unmarked choice; かしら leans distinctly female and slightly formal or old-fashioned.

今日きょうは傘かさ、いるかな。
Will I need an umbrella today?

今日きょうは傘かさ、いるかしら。
Will I need an umbrella today?

The sentences mean the same thing. What differs is what the listener infers about the speaker - and these are tendencies, not rules: plenty of women use かな as their default, and かしら is far more common in novels and drama than on the street.

かな · ～かな vs ～ないかな

Rule of thumb - put a negative in front of かな and wondering turns into hoping.

雨あめ、やむかな。
I wonder if the rain will stop.

雨あめ、やまないかなあ。
I do wish the rain would stop.

Literally the second asks "won't it stop?", but what it expresses is a wish. The stretched かなあ and a falling, sighing delivery are the usual signals. Context still matters: 来こないかな can also be a straight negative wondering - "I wonder if he isn't coming" - so listen for the tone.

かな · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この部屋へや、静しずかだかな。 → ✅ この部屋へや、静しずかかな。
    な-adjectives and nouns drop だ in front of か, just as they do before かどうか and かもしれない.

  • ❌ 先生せんせい、この漢字かんじの読よみ方かたは**何なにかな**。 → ✅ 先生せんせい、この漢字かんじの読よみ方かたは**何なんでしょうか**。
    かな is a casual, self-directed form. Pointed at a teacher it sounds as though you are talking past them.

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、これ、見みてもらえないかな。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、これ、見みていただけないでしょうか。
    The indirect-request use of かな works downward and sideways, not upward.

  • ❌ (in an email to a client) ご都合つごうはいかがかな。 → ✅ ご都合つごうはいかがでしょうか。
    かな has no place in writing that is not a message to a friend.

かな · Practice 1

1. 田中たなかさん、今日きょうは休やすみ____。机つくえに荷物にもつがないね。

今日きょうは休やすみかな。

休やすみ is a noun, so かな attaches straight on with no だ. The second sentence supplies the observation that prompted the thought, which is exactly the context かな lives in.

かな · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural form for speaking to a client: 来週らいしゅうの水曜日すいようびはご都合つごうが（いいかな・よろしいでしょうか）。

来週らいしゅうの水曜日すいようびはご都合つごうがよろしいでしょうか。

かな is plain-form casual and cannot be made polite. でしょうか is its polite counterpart, and it does the same softening job while staying in ます／です style.

かな · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, casually: "I wonder if this shop is still open."

この店みせ、まだ**開あいてるかな**。

Plain form in front of かな, and in speech 開あいている usually contracts to 開あいてる. Nothing polite may appear before the particle.

かな · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 課長かちょう、この書類しょるい、明日あしたまでに直なおしてくれないかな。

✅ 課長かちょう、この書類しょるい、明日あしたまでに**直なおしていただけないでしょうか**。

The grammar is fine; the direction is not. くれないかな is a casual nudge aimed at an equal or a junior, and pointing it at a section chief adds presumption to informality.

かな · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 電話でんわ、かかってくるかな。 (b) 電話でんわ、かかってこないかなあ。

(a) is genuine uncertainty: you do not know whether the call will come, and you are turning the question over. It would fit equally well if you were dreading the call.

(b) is a wish. The negative plus the drawn-out かなあ converts the wondering into longing - you are waiting for that call and it is taking too long. The literal reading "I wonder if it won't come" is available too, and only the tone tells them apart.

かしら

Meaning · I wonder - the same wondering particle as かな, in a distinctly softer and more feminine key

かしら is a worn-down か知しらぬ - "I don't know whether…". Like かな it turns a question inward, so that it hangs in the air rather than landing on anybody. What separates the two is not meaning but who is heard saying it: in modern standard Japanese かしら leans strongly female, and carries a slightly refined, slightly old-fashioned air with it.

かしら · Formation

Plain forms only, exactly as with かな. Nouns and な-adjectives drop だ.

Rule of thumb - かしら sounds genteel but is not polite. It attaches to plain forms, never to です／ます, and it does not belong in business speech or writing. The polite way to wonder aloud is still でしょうか.

かしら · Examples

あの人ひと、どなたかしら。
Who is that, I wonder.

この花はな、また咲さくかしら。
Will these flowers bloom again, do you think?

窓まどを閉しめてきたかしら。心配しんぱいになってきたわ。
Did I shut the window? I'm starting to worry.

息子むすこはちゃんと食たべているかしら。
I do wonder whether my son is eating properly.

かしら · Examples (cont.)

すみません、この席せき、空あいているかしら。
Excuse me - is this seat free?

どちらの色いろがいいかしら。迷まよってしまうわ。
Which colour would be better? I can't make up my mind.

ちょっと手てを貸かしてくださらないかしら。
Would you be so kind as to give me a hand?

来週らいしゅうの集あつまりには、あの方かたもいらっしゃるかしらね。
I wonder whether she'll be at next week's gathering too.

かしら · Nuance & register

Two separate things are going on, and keeping them apart saves a lot of confusion.

Register. かしら is casual. It sits on plain forms and belongs to relaxed speech, so despite its genteel ring it cannot be used where です／ます is required. What makes it sound refined is the company it keeps: it pairs naturally with polite vocabulary - どなた, いらっしゃる, くださる, ご覧らんになる - and that combination produces the soft, well-mannered effect people associate with it.

かしら · Nuance & register (cont.)

Speaker profile. In present-day standard Japanese かしら is heard mainly from women, and more from older speakers than younger ones; many younger women use かな as their default. In fiction and dubbing it is a stock signal for an elegant, mature or old-fashioned female character. These are tendencies, not rules - men did use it freely in earlier periods, and it survives in some regional and literary usage - but a learner who adopts かしら as a neutral "I wonder" will be heard as marked. If you want a wondering particle that raises no eyebrows, use かな.

かしら also does the two extra jobs かな does. With a negative it becomes a wish or a hint - 早はやく春はるが来こないかしら - and in the form ～てくださらないかしら it is an unusually soft request. Note the softness has a ceiling: it is gentle among equals, and still too informal to aim at a client.

かしら · かしら vs かな

Rule of thumb - identical grammar, identical meaning. The difference is entirely in who is expected to say it: かな is unmarked, かしら leans female and a touch formal.

この荷物にもつ、重おもすぎるかな。
Is this parcel too heavy, I wonder.

この荷物にもつ、重おもすぎるかしら。
Is this parcel too heavy, I wonder.

Nothing in the content changes. かな would be unremarkable from anyone; かしら sketches a speaker. Because the marking is one-directional - かな is safe for everybody, かしら is not - the practical advice is to recognise かしら fluently and produce かな, unless you are deliberately matching the speech style of the people around you.

かしら · かしら vs でしょうか

Rule of thumb - かしら is soft; でしょうか is polite. Softness is not politeness, and only one of them is safe upward.

会議かいぎは何時なんじからだったかしら。
What time was the meeting again?

会議かいぎは何時なんじからだったでしょうか。
Might I ask what time the meeting was again?

The first is fine muttered to a colleague you know well and wrong addressed to a director. でしょうか keeps you in ます／です style while still avoiding a blunt question, which is why it is the workhorse of polite enquiry.

かしら · ～かしら vs ～ないかしら

Rule of thumb - as with かな, adding a negative turns the wondering into hoping or into a hint.

田中たなかさん、気きがつくかしら。
I wonder whether Tanaka will notice.

誰だれか気きがついてくれないかしら。
I do wish somebody would notice.

The second is not really a question about anyone's likelihood of noticing; it is a wish, and said in front of the right person it is a request that never had to be made. This indirectness is the main reason かしら survives in polite female speech: it asks for things without asking.

かしら · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この説明せつめいで**十分じゅうぶんだかしら**。 → ✅ この説明せつめいで**十分じゅうぶんかしら**。
    な-adjectives and nouns lose だ before か, in かしら exactly as in かな and かどうか.

  • ❌ 明日あしたは**来きますかしら**。 → ✅ 明日あしたは**来くるかしら**。／いらっしゃるでしょうか。
    かしら attaches to plain forms. Adding ます in front does not make it polite; it just breaks it.

  • ❌ (in an email to a client) ご確認かくにんいただけるかしら。 → ✅ ご確認かくにんいただけないでしょうか。
    However genteel it sounds, かしら is casual speech and reads as startlingly informal in business writing.

かしら · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ A learner using かしら as their everyday "I wonder" in mixed company. → ✅ かな.
    Not an error of grammar but of self-presentation. かしら tells the listener something about you that you may not have meant to say.

かしら · Practice 1

1. もう八時はちじね。あの店みせ、まだ開あいている____。

A plain-form verb takes かしら directly. The sentence is a thought said out loud rather than a question put to anyone, which is precisely the slot this particle fills.

かしら · Practice 2

2. Choose the form suitable for asking your department head: 資料しりょうは明日あしたまでに（できるかしら・できるでしょうか）。

かしら is soft but casual, and softness does not substitute for politeness when you are speaking upward. でしょうか keeps the enquiry indirect while staying in polite style.

かしら · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese using かしら: "I wonder whether this cake is sweet enough."

An い-adjective or a plain verb attaches with nothing in between. Said while tasting something you have made yourself, this is the classic setting for かしら - a question addressed to nobody in particular.

かしら · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 田中たなかさん、この辺へんは静しずかだかしら。

静しずか is a な-adjective, and its だ disappears before か. The habit comes from 静しずかだと思おもう, where the だ is required - と and か behave in opposite ways.

かしら · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 雪ゆきになるかな。 (b) 雪ゆきになるかしら。

The two sentences say the same thing: the speaker is wondering, out loud, whether it will turn to snow. Neither is more or less certain than the other.

What differs is the picture the listener forms of the speaker. (a) is unmarked and could come from anyone. (b) reads as female and slightly refined or old-fashioned - a tendency rather than a rule, but a strong enough one that the choice carries information.

かい

Meaning · turns a plain-form sentence into a yes/no question - friendly, familiar, and noticeably old-fashioned

A plain-form sentence ending in bare か sounds curt, almost like an interrogation; add the い and the same question comes out warm - the tone of a parent asking a child how school went. That warmth is the whole point of the particle, and it comes with a speaker profile attached.

その荷物にもつ、重おもくないかい。持もとうか。
Isn't that bag heavy? Let me take it.

そうかい。それは大変たいへんだったね。
Is that right. That must have been rough.

久ひさしぶりだね。仕事しごとのほうは順調じゅんちょうかい。
Long time no see. Is work going all right?

まだ諦あきらめていないかい。それなら、もう一度いちどやってみなさい。
So you haven't given up? In that case, have another go.

かい · Nuance & register

かい is plain-style and cannot be made polite: there is no ❌かいです, and ますかい is not a form of standard Tokyo Japanese. If you need politeness, the question is ですか／ますか.

Its social shape matters more than its grammar. In modern standard Japanese かい leans male and older, and it leans downward or sideways - a parent to a child, a grandparent to a grandchild, a teacher to a pupil, a senior colleague being kind to a junior. Younger speakers use it rarely in earnest; where they do, it is often a deliberate impression of that older voice. Translated fiction and dubbing are full of it for exactly this reason: it is the standard way to make a character sound avuncular. As always these are tendencies rather than rules, but they are strong enough that a learner using かい with a superior will sound not merely informal but oddly patronising.

かい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The default casual yes/no question in ordinary modern speech is not かい at all - it is the plain form said with a rise: 行いく？／食たべた？ かい is the marked, warmer, older-sounding alternative to that.

One expression worth knowing on its own: そうかい. Said with interest it means "oh really?"; said flat it means "oh, is that so" and sounds dismissive, even sulky. The words are identical, so listen to the delivery.

かい · かい vs だい

Rule of thumb - かい answers yes or no. だい answers a question word. They are a matched pair and cannot be swapped.

明日あしたも来くるかい。
Are you coming tomorrow as well?

明日あしたは何時なんじに来くるんだい。
What time are you coming tomorrow?

かい · かい vs だい (cont.)

だい normally follows the explanatory の／ん, which is why you see んだい far more often than bare だい after a verb; after a noun it stands alone - 名前なまえは何なんだい。 Both particles carry the same familiar, male-leaning, somewhat dated flavour, so they turn up together in the mouth of the same character.

かい · かい vs か

Rule of thumb - bare か on a plain form is blunt; かい is the same question made kind.

分わかったか。
Got that?

分わかったかい。
Did that make sense?

The first is the tone of an instructor or a superior checking compliance, and it can land as a challenge. The second checks the same fact with none of the pressure. This is worth remembering in reverse too: bare plain-form か is not a neutral casual question, and a learner reaching for informality often produces something much sharper than intended.

かい · かい vs rising intonation

Rule of thumb - the everyday casual question is the plain form with a rise. かい adds warmth and a generation.

これ、食たべる？
Are you going to eat this?

これ、食たべるかい。
Would you like this?

Both are casual and both are friendly. The difference is that the first is unmarked in any age group, while the second sketches an older speaker addressing someone younger. If you are a learner in your twenties talking to friends, the rise is what you want; keep かい for recognising, and for the moments when you are consciously playing the older role.

かい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ どこで買かったかい。 → ✅ どこで買かったんだい。
    A question word rules かい out. かい is strictly yes/no; だい handles the rest.

  • ❌ 試験しけんは**心配しんぱいだかい**。 → ✅ 試験しけんは**心配しんぱいかい**。
    な-adjectives and nouns lose だ in front of か, as they do before every other か-based ending.

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、もうお帰かえりかい。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、もうお帰かえりですか。
    かい is friendly, but it is friendly downward. Aimed upward it manages to be informal and condescending at the same time.

  • ❌ 明日あしたも**来きますかい**。 → ✅ 明日あしたも**来くるかい**。／来きますか。
    かい attaches to plain forms. There is no polite version of it - you switch particles instead.

かい · Practice 1

1. （おじいさんが孫まごに）そんなに走はしって、疲つかれていない____。

疲つかれていないかい。

A yes/no question in plain style, asked kindly and downward - the exact situation かい exists for. Bare 疲つかれていないか would sound like a sergeant checking on a recruit.

かい · Practice 2

2. Choose: 昨日きのうは（どこへ行いったかい・どこへ行いったんだい）。

昨日きのうはどこへ**行いったんだい**。

The sentence contains どこ, so it is not a yes/no question and かい cannot be used. だい is the question-word member of the pair, and it normally follows the explanatory ん.

かい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese in the かい register: "Is that lunch enough for you?"

その昼ひるごはんで足たりるかい。

A plain-form verb takes かい directly. The English "for you" needs no pronoun - the particle itself makes clear you are asking the person in front of you.

かい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 先生せんせい、この問題もんだいの答こたえは三番さんばんかい。

✅ 先生せんせい、この問題もんだいの答こたえは三番さんばんですか。

The form is well built, but the direction is wrong. かい is used by the senior party to the junior one; a student asking a teacher needs plain ですか.

かい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) もう帰かえるのか。 (b) もう帰かえるのかい。

(a) is blunt. Depending on the delivery it can be a neutral checking of a fact, but it easily reads as displeasure - "you're leaving already?" - and it leans distinctly masculine and rough.

(b) asks the same thing gently. The い takes the sharpness out and adds a note of concern, as though the speaker is older than the listener and mildly sorry to see them go.

じゃないか

Meaning · isn't it? - an assertion wearing the clothes of a negative question

じゃないか is not really a question. It states something and dares the listener to disagree: look, it's raining / I did tell you / hey, that's rather good. The negative-question shape is what makes it feel shared rather than imposed - you are inviting the other person to notice what you have noticed. Which of its several flavours you get depends almost entirely on tone and situation.

じゃないか · Formation

Attaches to plain forms. Nouns and な-adjectives drop だ.

Rule of thumb - no だ in front. ❌嘘うそだじゃないか. The じゃ is already the contracted では of the copula, so the copula cannot appear twice.

じゃないか · Examples

ほら、やっぱり雨あめが降ふってきたじゃないか。
See - it's started raining after all.

いいじゃないか。一杯いっぱいくらい。
Oh, come on. One drink won't hurt.

あれ、財布さいふ、ここにあったじゃない。
Oh - the wallet was here all along.

昨日きのう、遅おくれるなって言いったじゃないか。
I told you yesterday not to be late, didn't I.

じゃないか · Examples (cont.)

なかなか上手じょうずじゃないか。いつ習ならったんだ？
Hey, that's not bad at all. When did you learn?

駅えきの前まえに大おおきな本屋ほんやがあったじゃないですか。あそこが閉しまるそうです。
You know that big bookshop in front of the station? Apparently it's closing.

そんなに急いそがなくてもいいじゃないですか。まだ時間じかんはありますよ。
There's really no need to rush, is there. We've still got time.

この説明せつめいだと、読よむ人ひとが混乱こんらんするんじゃないでしょうか。
Wouldn't this explanation leave the reader confused?

じゃないか · Nuance & register

The forms make a ladder, and the rungs are far apart.

じゃないか with the か said out loud is blunt and, in casual speech, leans male. じゃない with a rise - 安やすいじゃない？ - is the wider-used everyday version and sounds softer. じゃん is very casual and youthful. Going the other way, じゃないですか is the ordinary polite form, ではありませんか is formal and a little theatrical, and ではないか belongs to written argument, where it works as a rhetorical "is it not the case that…?" rather than as a question anybody answers.

Three things it does, all recognisable in context:

じゃないか · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • Pointing out - you have spotted something the listener has not.
    ここに書かいてあるじゃないですか。
  • Reproach or reminder - you said this before and were ignored.
    だから気きをつけろと言いったじゃないか。
  • Discovery - pleased surprise. 意外いがいとおいしいじゃないか。

じゃないか · Nuance & register (cont.)

A warning about the polite form. じゃないですか is often used to open a topic by presenting something as shared knowledge - 去年きょねん、地震じしんがあったじゃないですか. That is fine when the listener genuinely does know. Used about things they could not possibly know - 私わたし、甘あまい物ものが苦手にがてじゃないですか - it is widely criticised in Japan as pushy, because it assigns the listener knowledge they never had. Say んですが instead.

じゃないか · じゃないか vs plain じゃない

Rule of thumb - same syllables, two different sentences. Falling じゃない is a plain negative. Rising じゃない, and anything with か attached, is an assertion seeking agreement.

これは私わたしのかばんじゃない。
This isn't my bag.

これ、私わたしのかばんじゃない？
Hang on - this is my bag, isn't it?

じゃないか · じゃないか vs plain じゃない (cont.)

Adding か removes the ambiguity entirely: 私わたしのかばんじゃないか can only be the second reading. In writing, where intonation is unavailable, the question mark or the か is doing all the work, so leave it in.

じゃないか · じゃないか vs でしょう

Rule of thumb - both fish for agreement. でしょう assumes the listener already knows and asks them to confirm. じゃないか asserts and presses, often implying they had overlooked it.

昨日きのう話はなしたでしょう？
I mentioned it yesterday, remember?

昨日きのう話はなしたじゃないか。
I did tell you yesterday!

じゃないか · じゃないか vs でしょう (cont.)

The first is a reminder; the second is close to an accusation. That extra push is why じゃないか is risky upward: telling a manager 間違まちがっているじゃないですか is not "isn't this wrong?" but "this is wrong and you missed it."

じゃないか · じゃないか vs ～んじゃないか

Rule of thumb - bare じゃないか asserts something you are sure of. んじゃないか floats a suspicion you are not sure of at all.

この数字すうじ、間違まちがっているじゃないか。
This figure is wrong - look.

この数字すうじ、間違まちがっているんじゃないか。
I've a feeling this figure might be wrong.

The ん turns the whole thing tentative, and the polite んじゃないでしょうか is one of the most useful phrases in a Japanese workplace: it raises a doubt without accusing anyone of anything. When you want to question a superior's work, this is the form to reach for.

じゃないか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この辺へんは**静しずかだじゃないですか**。 → ✅ この辺へんは**静しずかじゃないですか**。
    じゃ is already the copula. な-adjectives and nouns attach bare.

  • ❌ もう**終おわりましたじゃないですか**。 → ✅ もう**終おわったじゃないですか**。
    The clause in front is plain. です on the end carries all the politeness the sentence needs.

  • ❌ 課長かちょう、この計算けいさん、違ちがうじゃないですか。 → ✅ 課長かちょう、この計算けいさん、違ちがうんじゃないでしょうか。
    Polite in form, confrontational in force. Adding ん and でしょう converts an accusation into a query.

じゃないか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 私わたし、北海道ほっかいどうの出身しゅっしんじゃないですか。 → ✅ 私わたし、北海道ほっかいどうの出身しゅっしんなんですが。
    You cannot present your own biography as something the listener already knows. This misuse is a well-known irritant.

じゃないか · Practice 1

1. ほら、傘かさを持もっていけと言いった____。びしょぬれだよ。

傘かさを持もっていけと言いったじゃないか。

The speaker warned the listener earlier and was ignored, which is the reproach use. でしょう would soften it into a mere reminder, and the drenched listener makes clear this is not a mere reminder.

じゃないか · Practice 2

2. Choose the safer form for pointing out a possible error in your boss's document: この日付ひづけ、（違ちがうじゃないですか・違ちがうんじゃないでしょうか）。

この日付ひづけ、違ちがうんじゃないでしょうか。

じゃないですか asserts the error and implies it was overlooked. The ん plus でしょうか presents the same observation as a doubt of your own, which leaves the other person room to check it themselves.

じゃないか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, casually: "Look, there's a light on - someone's still in the office."

ほら、電気でんきがついているじゃないか。まだ誰だれか残のこっているよ。

The first clause is a discovery you are drawing the listener's attention to, which is exactly what じゃないか marks. In a softer register you could say ついているじゃない？ with a rise.

じゃないか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: あの店みせのラーメン、けっこう有名ゆうめいだじゃないですか。

✅ あの店みせのラーメン、けっこう**有名ゆうめいじゃないですか**。

有名ゆうめい is a な-adjective, and じゃ is the contracted では of the copula, so putting だ in front doubles it up. Compare 有名ゆうめいだと思おもいます, where the だ is required.

じゃないか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれは知しらないじゃないですか。 (b) 彼かれは知しらないんじゃないでしょうか。

(a) asserts that he does not know and treats it as obvious - the sort of thing you say to someone who has just proposed asking him. It carries a note of "you should have realised that", so it can sting.

(b) offers his not knowing as a possibility you have thought of. It commits to nothing and leaves the decision with the listener, which makes it the form to use when you are speaking upward or when you might turn out to be wrong.
