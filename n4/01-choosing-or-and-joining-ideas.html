は vs が

Meaning · which of the two to reach for once the "new information" test has done its work

At N5 the rule was: the news comes after は and before が. That is still the first thing to check, and it settles most sentences. This page is about what the rule does not cover - は used to set one thing against another, が used to say "this one and no other", and what happens to both particles once a clause is buried inside a larger sentence.

は vs が · Formation

Neither particle changes shape, and neither changes the noun. What you are choosing between is a job, so the table sorts by job rather than by word class.

Note - a sentence can carry only one topic, but it can carry several は. The first is normally the topic; every later one is contrastive. 私わたしは刺身さしみは食たべません。 - "Me, I don't eat sashimi (whatever else I might eat)."

は vs が · Examples

漢字かんじは読よめますが、書かけません。
I can read kanji, but I can't write it.

荷物にもつは私わたしが持もちます。
I'll take the bags.

さっき変へんな音おとがしたけど、今いまは静しずかだね。
There was an odd noise a minute ago, but it's quiet now.

司会しかいは森もりさんが、受付うけつけは林はやしさんが担当たんとうします。
Mori will run the meeting and Hayashi will look after reception.

は vs が · Examples (cont.)

父ちちが昔むかし使つかっていたカメラは、今いまでもちゃんと動うごきます。
The camera my father used years ago still works perfectly.

野菜やさいの値段ねだんが上あがったので、今年ことしは旅行りょこうをやめました。
Vegetable prices went up, so we've given up on a trip this year.

会議かいぎの時間じかんが変かわったこと、部長ぶちょうには伝つたえましたが、山田やまださんにはまだ伝つたえていません。
I've told the manager the meeting time changed, but I haven't told Yamada yet.

説明せつめいは私わたしがしますので、資料しりょうのコピーは田中たなかさんにお願ねがいできますか。
I'll do the explaining, so could I leave copying the handouts to you, Tanaka?

は vs が · Nuance & register

Both particles are register-neutral: nothing about choosing one makes you sound formal or casual. What they do carry is attitude, and that is where politeness gets involved.

は drags a comparison in behind it whether you want one or not, so an unnecessary は can sound pointed. Volunteering with 私わたしはやります in a room where a job is being handed out reads as "I, at any rate, will do it - never mind the rest of you"; the cooperative version is 私わたしがやります, which simply fills the empty slot of "who". The same edge is useful on purpose: 「今日きょうはちょっと…」 turns an invitation down gently precisely because は implies "another day, gladly."

は vs が · Nuance & register (cont.)

が in its selecting sense is the particle of taking responsibility, and business Japanese leans on it: 私わたしが確認かくにんいたします, こちらが新あたらしい資料しりょうでございます. Learners who default to は in those slots sound oddly detached from what they are saying.

In casual speech both particles are routinely dropped - 「あれ、鍵かぎ、どこ?」 - and the contrast は would have carried is done by stress instead. Note that a contrastive は survives that dropping far more often than a topic は: 「肉にくは食たべない」 keeps its particle because the particle is the whole point of the sentence.

は vs が · Nuance & register (cont.)

Writing gives は one job that speech uses less: definitions. 敬語けいごとは、相手あいてを高たかめる言葉ことばである - "keigo is language that elevates the other party." That とは frame opens dictionary entries and academic articles, and it is always は, never が.

は vs が · Topic は vs contrastive は

Rule of thumb - if you can tack "…whatever about the others" onto the end and the sentence still means what you meant, the は is contrastive. A topic は sits at the head of the sentence; a contrastive は can sit anywhere, including on top of another particle, and it takes the stress in speech.

The topic use simply announces what you are talking about:

弟おとうとは大学だいがくで建築けんちくを勉強べんきょうしています。
My younger brother is studying architecture at university.

The contrastive use is a different move. It lifts its noun out of a field of alternatives and pointedly says nothing about the rest:

は vs が · Topic は vs contrastive は (cont.)

夜よるはコーヒーを飲のみません。
I don't drink coffee at night. (daytime is another matter)

駅えきまでは走はしりましたが、間まに合あいませんでした。
I ran as far as the station, at least - but I didn't make it.

The most useful case is a quantity word plus a negative. は there produces partial negation, and leaving it out flips the meaning:

全部ぜんぶは覚おぼえられませんでした。
I couldn't memorise all of it. (I did learn some)

は vs が · Topic は vs contrastive は (cont.)

Without the は, 全部ぜんぶ覚おぼえられませんでした is normally taken as "I couldn't learn any of it." The same trick works on 全員ぜんいんは, いつもは and 毎日まいにちは.

は vs が · Neutral-description が vs exhaustive が

Rule of thumb - が either reports a whole event as news, or selects one member out of a set. The selecting kind answers a "which one?" and cannot be swapped for は.

Reporting: nothing is being singled out. You are presenting a situation you have just noticed, or announcing one the listener has not heard.

駅えきの前まえに新あたらしいパン屋やができました。
A new bakery has opened in front of the station.

Selecting: everyone already knows the predicate applies to somebody, and が names who. This is why identifying yourself takes が:

は vs が · Neutral-description が vs exhaustive が (cont.)

本日ほんじつ案内あんないいたします、木村きむらが担当たんとうでございます。
I'm Kimura, and I'll be looking after you today.

The selecting が is what makes exhaustive listing possible - handing out roles one by one, with the understanding that between them the list is complete:

土曜日どようびは私わたしが、日曜日にちようびは妻つまが店みせに出でます。
I'll be at the shop on Saturday and my wife on Sunday.

One practical consequence: with an adjective and an already-known subject, が forces the selecting reading and sounds strange as a plain description. この部屋へやが明あかるいです means "this is the bright one"; for "this room is bright" you want この部屋へやは明あかるいです.

は vs が · Where は cannot go, and what it means when it can

Rule of thumb - は scopes over a whole sentence, so it cannot sit inside a clause that is only a part of one. Inside noun-modifying clauses, and inside と・ば・たら・なら・とき clauses, the subject takes が - or の in a noun-modifying clause.

母ははが送おくってくれた野菜やさいを、近所きんじょの人ひとに分わけました。
I shared the vegetables my mother sent me out among the neighbours.

電車でんしゃが止とまったら、タクシーで帰かえります。
If the trains stop, I'll go home by taxi.

❌電車でんしゃは止とまったら is not a stylistic slip - it breaks, because the topic would be trying to scope over a sentence whose main clause is about you, not about trains.

は vs が · Where は cannot go, and what it means when it can (cont.)

The exception is worth knowing, because it looks like a counterexample. Clauses joined with けど・が・から・ので are not embedded; they are full clauses stuck together. は can appear inside those - and when it does, it is always the contrastive は, never a second topic:

時間じかんはかかりますが、必かならず直なおします。
It will take time, granted - but I will definitely fix it.

That is exactly the "…whatever about the rest" reading: time, yes; difficulty, cost and outcome, not commented on. Test any は you meet inside a subordinate clause against that, and it will almost always turn out to be contrast.

は vs が · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 外そとは暗くらくなったら、電気でんきをつけてください。 → ✅ 外そとが暗くらくなったら、電気でんきをつけてください。
    A たら clause is embedded inside the sentence, and a topic cannot live there. Its subject takes が.

  • ❌ 「どちらが田中たなかさんですか。」「私わたしは田中たなかです。」 → ✅ 「私わたしが田中たなかです。」
    私わたしは田中たなかです is a fine self-introduction, but as an answer to "which of you is Tanaka?" it dodges the question. You were asked to select, and selecting is が.

  • ❌ この部屋へやが静しずかです。（meaning "this room is quiet"） → ✅ この部屋へやは静しずかです。
    With a known subject and an adjective, が is heard as "this is the quiet one". For a plain description, use は.

は vs が · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 昨日きのうは雪ゆきは降ふりました。 → ✅ 昨日きのうは雪ゆきが降ふりました。
    The second は forces a contrast - "snow, at least, fell" - which is not what you meant. A reported event takes が.

は vs が · Practice 1

1. 「どうしたんですか。」「エアコン____動うごかないんです。」

エアコンが動うごかないんです。

You are reporting a problem the listener does not know about yet, so the air conditioner is brand-new information - the reporting が. は would imply the two of you were already discussing that particular machine.

は vs が · Practice 2

2. Choose: 時間じかん（は・が）ないので、今日きょう（は・が）ここまでにしましょう。

時間じかんがないので、今日きょうはここまでにしましょう。

The first slot sits inside a ので clause and simply states a fact about time, so it takes が. The second sets today against other days - we stop here today, and next time we will go further - which is contrastive は.

は vs が · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I've read the first half, but I haven't read the second half yet."

前半ぜんはんは読よみましたが、後半こうはんはまだ読よんでいません。

Both halves are grammatical objects and would normally take を, but the sentence exists in order to set them against each other, so を is replaced by contrastive は on both sides.

は vs が · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 雨あめは降ふったら、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしです。

✅ 雨あめが降ふったら、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしです。

The first は is inside a たら clause and has to become が. The second one is in the main clause and is correct as it stands - the match is the topic of the sentence.

は vs が · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 山口やまぐちさんは部長ぶちょうです。 (b) 山口やまぐちさんが部長ぶちょうです。

(a) takes 山口やまぐちさん as already under discussion and tells you something about him: his job is manager. It is the answer to "what does he do?"

(b) starts from the other end. Everyone knows there is a manager; the sentence identifies which person it is - "山口やまぐちさん is the one" - picking him out of the group. It answers "which of them is the manager?", and it is what you say while nodding across the room.

～し

Meaning · and what's more - piling up reasons or qualities, with the hint that there are others

～し joins clauses the way a person builds a case: one point, another point, and an unstated "so, you see." It differs from a plain "and" in two ways. The items are offered as grounds for something, and the list is open - you are naming a couple of reasons out of several, not all of them.

～し · Formation

し attaches to the plain form of whatever ends the clause, and it can equally follow a polite form when the sentence around it is polite.

Rule of thumb - し needs a finished clause in front of it. That is why nouns and な-adjectives must keep their だ（❌静しずかし）while い-adjectives must not add one（❌安やすいだし）.

A second habit worth building in from the start: the clause after し usually marks one of its nouns with も. 雨あめだし、風かぜも強つよい, 安やすいし、味あじもいい. That も is what makes the sentence feel like an accumulation of points rather than a sequence of events.

～し · Examples

この店みせは安やすいし、おいしい。
This place is cheap, and the food's good.

疲つかれたし、そろそろ帰かえろう。
I'm tired - let's head home soon.

明日あしたは休やすみだし、映画えいがでも見みに行いかない?
Tomorrow's a day off - how about going to see a film or something?

この部屋へやは広ひろいし、駅えきにも近ちかいし、ここに決きめました。
The room's big and it's near the station too, so I've settled on this one.

～し · Examples (cont.)

彼かれは約束やくそくを守まもらないし、連絡れんらくもくれないし、もう信しんじられない。
He doesn't keep his promises, he never gets in touch - I just can't trust him any more.

まだ時間じかんもありますし、もう一軒いっけん寄よっていきませんか。
We've still got time, so shall we stop off at one more place?

給料きゅうりょうは悪わるくないし、人間関係にんげんかんけいもいいし、今いまの会社かいしゃを辞やめる理由りゆうがありません。
The pay isn't bad and I get on with everyone, so I've no reason to leave my current company.

「どうして引ひっ越こすんですか。」「家賃やちんが高たかいですし、会社かいしゃからも遠とおいので。」
"Why are you moving?" "The rent's high, and it's a long way from the office."

～し · Nuance & register

し itself is neutral, but it belongs to spoken Japanese. It is at home in conversation, in a chatty email, in a blog post; a formal report would reach for ～上うえに or simply separate sentences instead.

Politeness is decided by the clause, not by し. In polite speech you attach it to the polite form - 安やすいですし, あります**し** - and mixing the two inside one sentence sounds careless. Plain-form し inside an otherwise polite sentence is heard sometimes in relaxed workplace speech, but it is not something to imitate early.

The habit worth copying is trailing し: a sentence that ends on し and simply stops.

～し · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 「今日きょうはやめておきます。明日あした早はやいし。」 - "I'll pass
    today. Early start tomorrow and all."
  • 「べつにいいよ。もう終おわったし。」 - "It's fine. It's over now anyway."

The unfinished shape is doing real work: it hands the conclusion to the listener instead of stating it, which softens a refusal and lets you avoid saying the awkward part out loud. The flip side is that a string of trailing し about someone else can sound like grumbling or excuse-making - 「だって忙いそがしかったし」 is exactly the tone of a child explaining why the homework is not done.

One more thing し does that から cannot: it can list reasons that point in different directions, letting the speaker weigh them. 給料きゅうりょうはいいし、でも残業ざんぎょうも多おおいし… is a person thinking out loud.

～し · ～し vs ～から

Rule of thumb - から gives the reason and pushes the conclusion at the listener. し gives a reason among others and leaves room for more.

熱ねつがあるから、今日きょうは休やすみます。
I've got a fever, so I'm taking today off.

熱ねつもあるし、今日きょうは休やすみます。
I've got a bit of a temperature and so on, so I'm taking today off.

The から version presents one clean cause. The し version implies a small pile of reasons - tiredness, the weather, the fever - of which you have named one. That vagueness is why し sounds softer and less accusatory, and why refusals reach for it.

～し · ～し vs ～から (cont.)

The two combine, and the combination is extremely common: 安やすいし、近ちかいし、いい店みせですから、ぜひ行いってみてください - the し clauses stack the evidence and the final から delivers the verdict.

～し · ～し vs the て-form

Rule of thumb - the て-form strings things together in order; し adds them up as evidence. If the second clause simply happened next, you want て.

朝あさ起おきて、顔かおを洗あらって、家いえを出でました。
I got up, washed my face and left the house.

Putting し in there - ❌朝あさ起おきたし、顔かおを洗あらいました - makes it sound as though getting up were a reason for washing.

With adjectives the two overlap more, and both are correct:

この辞書じしょは軽かるくて、使つかいやすいです。
This dictionary is light and easy to use.

～し · ～し vs the て-form (cont.)

この辞書じしょは軽かるいし、使つかいやすいです。
This dictionary is light, and easy to use into the bargain.

The て version is a flat description. The し version sounds like a recommendation - you can hear "so you should buy it" behind it.

～し · ～し vs ～たり～たり

Rule of thumb - both give an incomplete list, but of different things. し lists whole clauses as reasons; たり lists activities as samples of what went on.

昨日きのうは洗濯せんたくをしたり、部屋へやを片かたづけたりしました。
Yesterday I did the washing, tidied my room and so on.

昨日きのうは洗濯せんたくもしたし、部屋へやも片かたづけた。
Yesterday I did the washing, and I tidied my room as well.

The たり sentence answers "what did you get up to?" - it paints a picture of the day. The し sentence answers "have you done anything useful?" - it is a defence, item by item. Note also that たり must end in する, while し needs nothing after it.

～し · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この町まちは静しずかし、空気くうきもきれいです。 → ✅ この町まちは静しずかだし、空気くうきもきれいです。
    A な-adjective needs its だ before し. The same goes for nouns: 学生がくせいだし.

  • ❌ この本ほんは安やすいだし、面白おもしろいです。 → ✅ この本ほんは安やすいし、面白おもしろいです。
    An い-adjective is already a complete predicate, so adding だ is one word too many.

  • ❌ シャワーを浴あびたし、寝ねました。 → ✅ シャワーを浴あびて、寝ねました。
    し does not narrate a sequence. Two events that simply happened one after the other take the て-form.

  • ❌ 近ちかいし、安やすいですし、いい店みせです。 → ✅ 近ちかいですし、安やすいですし、いい店みせです。
    Keep one politeness level across the whole sentence. Plain し followed by polite し sounds like a slip.

～し · Practice 1

1. この町まちは便利べんり____、家賃やちんも安やすいです。

この町まちは便利べんりだし、家賃やちんも安やすいです。

便利べんり is a な-adjective, so it needs だ to finish the clause before し can attach. The polite alternative is 便利べんりですし.

～し · Practice 2

2. Choose: 昨日きのうは映画えいがを見み（し・て）、ごはんを食たべました。

昨日きのうは映画えいがを見みて、ごはんを食たべました。

Nothing here is a reason for anything - the two events simply happened in order, and that is the て-form's job. し would suggest you were listing evidence for some conclusion you have not stated.

～し · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "This café is close to the station and it isn't expensive, so let's meet here."

この店みせは駅えきから近ちかいし、値段ねだんも高たかくないし、ここで待まち合あわせましょう。

Two し clauses stack the reasons, and the suggestion follows with no connective at all - once し has done the work, だから would be redundant.

～し · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼女かのじょは親切しんせつし、頭あたまもいいです。

✅ 彼女かのじょは親切しんせつだし、頭あたまもいいです。

親切しんせつ is a な-adjective. Without だ the clause never finishes, and し has nothing to attach to. Polite version: 親切しんせつですし.

～し · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 雨あめだから、今日きょうは出でかけません。 (b) 雨あめだし、今日きょうは出でかけません。

(a) names the rain as the reason, full stop. It is a clear, slightly firm statement of cause and effect, and it invites the listener to accept it.

(b) offers the rain as one of the things making you stay in - you are tired, there is nothing on, and besides, it's raining. It sounds more relaxed and less like an argument, which is exactly why it is the friendlier way to decline an invitation.

～たり～たり

Meaning · doing things like A and B - naming a couple of examples out of a larger set

～たり～たり lists samples. Whatever you name, the listener understands that other things happened too, and that the order they are given in means nothing. Its second job is alternation: two states that keep swapping places, one day this, the next day that.

～たり～たり · Formation

Take the plain past（た-form）of each item, add り, and close the whole list with する. The する is not decoration - it is the verb of the sentence, and it carries the tense and the politeness for everything in front of it.

～たり～たり · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb た-form + り 読よむ → 読よんだり
Verb (する) した + り 掃除そうじする → 掃除そうじしたり
Verb (negative) なかった + り 行いく → 行いかなかったり
い-adjective かった + り 暑あつい → 暑あつかったり
な-adjective だった + り 静しずか → 静しずかだったり
Noun だった + り 雨あめ → 雨あめだったり
Closing the list (verbs) …たり…たり + する 歩あるいたり走はしったりする
Closing the list (past, polite) …たり…たり + しました 歌うたったり踊おどったりしました
Closing the list (adjectives / nouns) …だったり…だったり + だ／です 暑あつかったり寒さむかったりです

～たり～たり · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - if you can make the た-form, you can make the たり-form: just add り. The trap is the closing する, which learners drop. It is what conjugates: したり…したりしています, …したりしたい, …したりしないでください.

～たり～たり · Examples

夜よるは音楽おんがくを聞きいたり、ギターを弾ひいたりします。
In the evenings I listen to music, play the guitar, that sort of thing.

教室きょうしつで飲のんだり食たべたりしないでください。
Please don't eat or drink in the classroom.

最近さいきんは暑あつかったり寒さむかったりで、体調たいちょうを崩くずしやすい。
Lately it's hot one day and cold the next, so it's easy to get run down.

父ちちはさっきから部屋へやの中なかを行いったり来きたりしている。
Dad's been pacing up and down the room for a while now.

～たり～たり · Examples (cont.)

会議かいぎの場所ばしょは本社ほんしゃだったり支店してんだったりで、よく間違まちがえます。
The meeting is sometimes at head office, sometimes at the branch, so I often get it wrong.

息子むすこは野菜やさいを食たべたり食たべなかったりで、心配しんぱいです。
My son eats his vegetables some days and not others, which worries me.

日曜日にちようびは、近ちかくの公園こうえんを歩あるいたり、カフェで新聞しんぶんを読よんだりして、ゆっくり過すごしています。
On Sundays I walk in the park nearby, read the paper in a café, and generally take it slow.

一人暮ひとりぐらしを始はじめてから、自分じぶんで料理りょうりを作つくったり部屋へやを片かたづけたりするのが当あたり前まえになりました。
Since I started living alone, cooking for myself and tidying up have just become normal.

～たり～たり · Nuance & register

～たり is register-neutral. Everything about the level of the sentence is decided by the closing verb: 見みたりします is polite, 見みたりする is plain, 見みたりいたします is humble. The たり parts themselves never change.

Two conversational habits to recognise. First, a single たり is normal - it still means "and things like that", and it often softens an accusation: 泣ないたりしないで, 無理むりをしたりしないでね. Second, たりして at the end of a sentence is a hedge, close to English "…or something": 「明日あした、急きゅうに雪ゆきが降ふったりして」 - "knowing our luck it'll snow tomorrow." That use is casual and slightly playful; keep it out of a report.

～たり～たり · Nuance & register (cont.)

In casual speech you will also hear とか doing the same job - 洗あらったりとかして - which is chattier still, and the closing する is sometimes swallowed altogether: 「掃除そうじしたり洗濯せんたくしたり、いろいろ」. Understand it, but write the する.

The alternation use has one shape worth memorising because it appears everywhere: A + たり + A's negative + たり - 降ふったり降ふらなかったり, 来きたり来こなかったり - meaning "sometimes yes, sometimes no". It carries a faint note of unreliability, so it is not a compliment.

～たり～たり · ～たり～たり vs the て-form

Rule of thumb - the て-form reports what happened, in order and in full. たり gives an unordered sample and implies there was more.

朝あさ、コーヒーを飲のんで、メールをチェックして、家いえを出でました。
This morning I had a coffee, checked my email and left the house.

朝あさは、コーヒーを飲のんだりメールをチェックしたりしています。
In the mornings I have a coffee, check my email, and so on.

～たり～たり · ～たり～たり vs the て-form (cont.)

The first is a report of one particular morning, in sequence. The second describes what mornings are generally like, and rearranging the two items would not change it at all. Because of that, たり is wrong when the order matters: ❌歯はをみがいたり寝ねたりしました for "I brushed my teeth and went to bed."

～たり～たり · ～たり～たり vs ～や・～とか

Rule of thumb - same "partial list" idea, different word class. や and とか list nouns; たり lists actions and states.

机つくえの上うえにペンやノートが置おいてあります。
There are pens, notebooks and so on lying on the desk.

机つくえの上うえで書かいたり調しらべたりしています。
I write and look things up at the desk.

～たり～たり · ～たり～たり vs ～や・～とか (cont.)

とか straddles the line - it lists nouns like や, but in casual speech it also lists dictionary-form verbs: 洗あらうとか干ほすとか. That verb use is markedly more casual than たり, and it does not need a closing する.

～たり～たり · ～たり～たり vs ～ながら

Rule of thumb - ながら is two actions happening at the same moment; たり is two actions taking turns, or sampled from a stretch of time.

音楽おんがくを聞ききながら走はしっています。
I run while listening to music. (both at once)

音楽おんがくを聞きいたり走はしったりしています。
I listen to music, go running, that kind of thing. (at different times)

Learners sometimes reach for たり when describing multitasking, and it quietly says the opposite of what they mean. If the two things overlap in time, you want ながら.

～たり～たり · Common mistakes

  • ❌ テレビを見みるたり、本ほんを読よむたりします。 → ✅ テレビを見みたり、本ほんを読よんだりします。
    たり is built on the た-form, never the dictionary form. Make the past tense first, then swap た for たり - including the voiced だり.

  • ❌ 週末しゅうまつは映画えいがを見みたり、友ともだちに会あったり。 → ✅ …会あったりします。
    The closing する is what carries the tense. Leaving it off is fine in casual conversation and wrong in writing or polite speech.

  • ❌ 部屋へやが静しずかたり、うるさかったりします。 → ✅ 部屋へやが静しずかだったり、うるさかったりします。
    な-adjectives and nouns need だった before り; only verbs and い-adjectives have a た-form of their own.

～たり～たり · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 起おきたり、顔かおを洗あらったりして、会社かいしゃに行いきます。 → ✅ 起おきて、顔かおを洗あらって、会社かいしゃに行いきます。
    A fixed morning routine happens in a fixed order, and たり explicitly throws the order away. Use the て-form.

～たり～たり · Practice 1

1. 週末しゅうまつは部屋へやを掃除そうじし____、買かい物ものに行いっ____します。

掃除そうじしたり、買かい物ものに行いったりします。

Both verbs go into the た-form first（した, 行いった）and then take り. The final します is the verb of the whole sentence and supplies the polite present tense.

～たり～たり · Practice 2

2. Choose: 手てを洗あらっ（たり・て）から、ごはんを食たべましょう。

手てを洗あらってから、ごはんを食たべましょう。

The whole point of the sentence is the order - hands first, then food - and てから is what expresses that. たり would say the two happen in no particular order, which is not the advice anyone gives about washing your hands.

～たり～たり · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Lately the weather has been rainy some days and clear on others."

最近さいきんは雨あめが降ふったり晴はれたりしています。

This is the alternation use: two states swapping back and forth rather than a list of activities. しています presents it as an ongoing situation; します would sound like a general truth about the climate.

～たり～たり · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昨日きのうは友ともだちと話はなするたり、ゲームをしたりしました。

✅ 昨日きのうは友ともだちと話はなしたり、ゲームをしたりしました。

話はなす is a う-verb, so its た-form is 話はなした and the たり-form is 話はなしたり. The dictionary form never appears before たり.

～たり～たり · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 昨日きのうは洗濯せんたくをして、買かい物ものに行いきました。 (b) 昨日きのうは洗濯せんたくをしたり、買かい物ものに行いったりしました。

(a) is a report of the day in sequence: the washing first, then the shopping, and by implication that is what you did.

(b) picks two things out of a fuller day and offers them as examples. The order is not being claimed, and the listener understands there were other errands you have not bothered to mention. It is the natural answer to a vague "what did you get up to yesterday?"

～ながら

Meaning · while doing something else - two actions by one person at the same time

～ながら puts two activities on top of each other. The clause with ながら is the background; the main clause at the end is what the sentence is really about. Both actions must belong to the same person, and both must be things that last long enough to overlap.

～ながら · Formation

ながら attaches to the ます-stem and never changes shape itself. All the tense and politeness live in the final verb.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + ながら 歩あるきます → 歩あるきながら
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + ながら 食たべます → 食たべながら
Verb (する) し + ながら 運転うんてんする → 運転うんてんしながら
Verb (ている, concessive) てい + ながら 知しっている → 知しっていながら
い-adjective (concessive) plain form + ながら（も） 狭せまい → 狭せまいながらも
な-adjective (concessive) stem + ながら（も） 残念ざんねん → 残念ざんねんながら
Noun (concessive) Noun + ながら 子こども → 子こどもながら

～ながら · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ながら takes the same stem as ます, so 食たべます → 食たべながら, never ❌食たべるながら. The verb before it also has to be one that takes time: you can walk while talking, but not ❌着つきながら ("while arriving").

Note - the last four rows are a different ながら, meaning "although". It attaches to ている forms, い-adjectives, な-adjective stems and bare nouns - shapes the "at the same time" ながら never takes - so the form itself usually tells you which one you are looking at. See the third Contrast section.

～ながら · Examples

コーヒーを飲のみながら話はなしましょう。
Let's talk over a coffee.

歩あるきながらスマホを見みないでください。
Please don't look at your phone while you're walking.

母ははは鼻歌はなうたを歌うたいながら洗あらい物ものをしている。
My mother is doing the washing-up, humming to herself.

アルバイトをしながら大学だいがくに通かよっています。
I'm working part-time while going to university.

～ながら · Examples (cont.)

残念ざんねんながら、今回こんかいの計画けいかくは中止ちゅうしになりました。
Regrettably, this time the plan has been called off.

悪わるいと知しっていながら、嘘うそをついてしまいました。
I lied, even though I knew it was wrong.

地図ちずを見みながら歩あるいていたのに、同おなじところに戻もどってきてしまった。
I was walking along looking at the map, and still ended up back in the same place.

先輩せんぱいの説明せつめいをメモしながら聞きいていたので、あとでゆっくり見直みなおすことができました。
I took notes while listening to my senior colleague's explanation, so I could go back over it properly later.

～ながら · Nuance & register

ながら is neutral and belongs equally to speech and writing. What learners get wrong is not the register but the weight: whichever verb ends the sentence is the one being reported. テレビを見みながらごはんを食たべる is a sentence about eating; ごはんを食たべながらテレビを見みる is a sentence about watching television. Both are correct, and they answer different questions.

The pattern also stretches over long spans of time, not just a single moment. 働はたらきながら勉強べんきょうする describes years of your life, and this use is completely standard in a self-introduction or a job interview.

～ながら · Nuance & register (cont.)

Modern Japanese has turned ながら into a noun prefix for doing something while distracted: ながら運転うんてん (using your phone at the wheel), ながら食たべ, ながらスマホ. These carry disapproval - they are the words on the warning poster.

The "although" ながら is a different animal in register: it is written and formal, and in conversation it survives mainly in fixed expressions - 残念ざんねんながら (regrettably), しかしながら (however), いつもながら (as always), 失礼しつれいながら (if I may be so bold). Adding も - 狭せまいながらも - makes the concession explicit and sounds more literary still.

～ながら · ～ながら vs ～ている間に

Rule of thumb - ながら needs one person doing both things. If the two clauses have different subjects, you need 間あいだに.

子こどもが昼寝ひるねをしている**間あいだに**、仕事しごとを片かたづけます。
I'll get my work done while the children are having their nap.

子こどもの話はなしを聞ききながら、仕事しごとをしていました。
I was working while listening to my child talk.

～ながら · ～ながら vs ～ている間に (cont.)

In the first, the sleeper and the worker are different people, so ながら is impossible. In the second, one person is doing both. There is a second difference worth noticing: 間あいだに means "at some point during", so the main action fits inside the other one, while ながら means the two run side by side for the whole time.

～ながら · ～ながら vs the て-form

Rule of thumb - て puts one action after another, or gives the means; ながら puts them on top of each other.

駅えきまで歩あるいて行いきます。
I'll walk to the station. (walking is how I go)

歩あるきながら考かんがえましょう。
Let's think it over as we walk.

～ながら · ～ながら vs the て-form (cont.)

The て version answers "how did you get there?"; the ながら version answers "what were you doing at the same time?" Swapping them changes the sentence rather than just its style: 歩あるいて考かんがえる would suggest you walked and then thought.

～ながら · Concessive ～ながら（も）vs ～のに

Rule of thumb - both mean "although". のに is spoken and carries surprise or complaint; ながらも is written, restrained, and often faintly admiring.

小ちいさい店みせながらも、品しなぞろえは立派りっぱだ。
Small though the shop is, the range it stocks is impressive.

小ちいさい店みせなのに、品しなぞろえがすごいね。
The shop's tiny, but look at everything they've got!

～ながら · Concessive ～ながら（も）vs ～のに (cont.)

Two practical limits on the concessive ながら. It attaches only to a short list of things - a ます-stem, a ている form, an い-adjective, a な-adjective stem or a plain noun - so there is no ❌行いったながら. And with verbs it is almost always a ている form, meaning "knowing full well that…": 知しっていながら, 分わかっていながら, 見みていながら.

～ながら · Common mistakes

  • ❌ ごはんを食たべるながらテレビを見みます。 → ✅ ごはんを食たべながらテレビを見みます。
    ながら attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 妹いもうとがピアノを弾ひきながら、私わたしは勉強べんきょうしました。 → ✅ 妹いもうとがピアノを弾ひいている**間あいだに**、私わたしは勉強べんきょうしました。
    Two different people, so ながら cannot join them. The sentence as written claims your sister was studying.

  • ❌ 電気でんきをつけながら出でかけました。 → ✅ 電気でんきをつけたまま出でかけました。
    つける is instantaneous - the light goes on and that's that. For "leaving something in a state", the word is まま.

～ながら · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 歩あるきながらでした、話はなしました。 → ✅ 歩あるきながら話はなしました。
    ながら never takes tense or politeness. Both live on the final verb.

～ながら · Practice 1

1. 音楽おんがくを聞きき____、宿題しゅくだいをしています。

音楽おんがくを聞ききながら、宿題しゅくだいをしています。

One person, two overlapping activities, and the homework is the main event - exactly what ながら is for. The stem is 聞きき, from 聞ききます.

～ながら · Practice 2

2. Choose: 赤あかちゃんが寝ねている（ながら・間あいだに）、買かい物ものに行いってきます。

赤あかちゃんが寝ねている**間あいだに**、買かい物ものに行いってきます。

The baby sleeps and you shop - two different subjects, so ながら is out. 間あいだに also gives the right meaning: the shopping happens somewhere inside the nap, not for the whole of it.

～ながら · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I studied Japanese while working at a hotel."

ホテルで働はたらきながら日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうしました。

ながら happily covers a long stretch of life, not just a single moment. The past tense goes on 勉強べんきょうしました only - 働はたらきながら stays as it is.

～ながら · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 窓まどを開あけながら寝ねてしまいました。（intended: "I fell asleep with the window open."）

✅ 窓まどを開あけたまま寝ねてしまいました。

開あける takes a moment and is then over, so it cannot run alongside sleeping. What you mean is that the window stayed open, and that is まま. As written, the sentence describes you opening the window and sleeping simultaneously.

～ながら · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) テレビを見みながらごはんを食たべます。 (b) ごはんを食たべながらテレビを見みます。

Both describe the same scene, but they report different things. (a) is a sentence about a meal - television is the background noise, and it would answer "how do you usually eat dinner?"

(b) is a sentence about watching television, with the meal as the accompaniment. You would say it to explain how you spend your evenings, or to admit that dinner is not getting your full attention.

～ても

Meaning · even if; even though - the second half happens anyway

～ても sets up a condition and then knocks it down. Whatever is in the ても clause would normally be expected to change the outcome, and the sentence says it does not. Depending on context it covers "even if it happens" (a hypothesis) and "even when it happens" (a repeated fact).

～ても · Formation

The mechanics are simple: build the て-form, add も. The only thing to watch is that adjectives and nouns have their own て-forms.

～ても · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb て-form + も 行いく → 行いっても
Verb (negative) なくて + も 行いかない → 行いかなくても
い-adjective くて + も 高たかい → 高たかくても
い-adjective (negative) くなくて + も 高たかくない → 高たかくなくても
な-adjective で + も 不便ふべん → 不便ふべんでも
Noun で + も 雨あめ → 雨あめでも
Noun / な-adjective (negative) じゃなくて + も 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいじゃなくても
Question word question word + ても どんなに → どんなに待まっても

～ても · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - no tense ever appears before ても. Past, future and habit are all decided by the final verb: ❌降ふったても, ✅降ふっても for "even if it rains" and for "even though it rained".

Note - you already know this pattern from N5 without knowing it. ～てもいい ("you may") is literally "even if you do, it is fine", and ～なくてもいい ("you don't have to") is "even if you don't, it is fine". Same construction, with an adjective supplied for the second half.

～ても · Examples

寒さむくても、毎朝まいあさ走はしっています。
Even when it's cold, I run every morning.

明日あした雨あめが降ふっても、山やまに登のぼります。
Even if it rains tomorrow, we're climbing the mountain.

急いそがなくても間まに合あいますよ。
You'll make it even without rushing.

日曜日にちようびでも開あいている店みせを探さがしています。
I'm looking for a shop that's open even on Sundays.

～ても · Examples (cont.)

いくら説明せつめいしても、彼かれは分わかってくれません。
No matter how much I explain, he just won't understand.

何なにを食たべてもおいしく感かんじないので、少すこし休やすむことにしました。
Nothing tastes good to me at the moment, so I've decided to take a short break.

どんなに練習れんしゅうしても、兄あにには一度いちども勝かてませんでした。
However much I practised, I never once beat my older brother.

留学りゅうがくの費用ひようが高たかくても、一度いちどは海外かいがいで生活せいかつしてみたいと思おもっています。
Even if studying abroad is expensive, I'd like to live overseas at least once.

～ても · Nuance & register

ても is register-neutral; the politeness of the sentence is set entirely by its final verb. It is as much at home in a contract as in a conversation.

The casual spoken contraction is ～たって, and it is everywhere: 行いったって, 見みたって, 高たかくたって, 子こどもだって. Verbs build it on the plain past - take 行いった and add って; い-adjectives use くたって (高たかくたって), and nouns and な-adjectives use だって. It is friendly, slightly emphatic, and out of place in writing or with a superior. 「そんなこと、今いま言いったって遅おそいよ」 - "It's too late to say that now."

ても pairs up with a small family of adverbs that make the concession explicit, and using one makes you sound noticeably more fluent:

～ても · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • たとえ～ても - "even supposing", for hypotheticals: たとえ失敗しっぱいしても。
  • どんなに～ても／いくら～ても - "no matter how much".
  • 何なにを／だれが／どこへ～ても - "no matter what/who/where".

One caution about the noun form. 雨あめでも can be this pattern ("even in the rain"), but でも is also an independent word meaning "…or something" （お茶ちゃでも飲のみませんか）and the sentence-initial "but" （でも、高たかいですよ）. Three unrelated things that look identical; the context always sorts them out, but expect the double-take.

～ても · ～ても vs ～たら・～ば

Rule of thumb - たら and ば say the result depends on the condition; ても says the result comes anyway. Same slot in the sentence, opposite claims.

安やすくなったら、買かいます。
If it gets cheaper, I'll buy it.

安やすくなっても、買かいません。
Even if it gets cheaper, I won't buy it.

This is the pair to burn into memory, because English "if" hides the difference. A useful test: if you could insert "in that case" before the main clause, you want たら; if you could insert "all the same", you want ても.

～ても · ～ても vs ～のに

Rule of thumb - のに reports something that actually happened and adds frustration or surprise. ても is neutral, and it covers hypotheses and repeated situations that のに cannot.

雨あめが降ふっても、試合しあいは行おこなわれます。
The match will go ahead even if it rains.

Swapping in のに there is impossible - nothing has rained yet, and のに cannot talk about a hypothesis. Where both are available, the difference is attitude:

三回さんかいメールを送おくっても、返事へんじが来きません。
I've sent three emails and I still get no reply.

～ても · ～ても vs ～のに (cont.)

三回さんかいメールを送おくったのに、返事へんじが来きません。
I sent three emails and there's still no reply.

The ても version states the situation. The のに version complains about it - you can hear the speaker's irritation, and it would be undiplomatic in front of the person responsible.

～ても · ～ても vs ～けど・～が

Rule of thumb - けど joins two facts; ても names a condition that fails to have its usual effect. Only ても can be hypothetical.

この部屋へやは狭せまいですが、日ひあたりがいいです。
This room is small, but it gets good light.

部屋へやが狭せまくても、日ひあたりがよければ満足まんぞくです。
As long as it gets good light, I don't mind a small room.

The が sentence describes one particular room you have both seen. The ても sentence describes a policy that would hold for any room at all. Note also that ても attaches to a bare stem while けど・が follow a complete clause with its own tense.

～ても · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 値段ねだんが高たかいても、買かいます。 → ✅ 値段ねだんが高たかくても、買かいます。
    An い-adjective has to become くて first. The い never survives in front of も.

  • ❌ 雨あめても出でかけます。 → ✅ 雨あめでも出でかけます。
    Nouns and な-adjectives take で, not て - the same shape they use for the て-form everywhere else.

  • ❌ 明日あした行いくてもいいですか。 → ✅ 明日あした行いってもいいですか。
    ても is built on the て-form, never the dictionary form.

～ても · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 何なにも言いっても、彼かれは怒おこりません。 → ✅ 何なにを言いっても、彼かれは怒おこりません。
    The "no matter what" meaning comes from ても itself, so the question word keeps its ordinary particle. 何なにも plus a negative is a different pattern entirely.

～ても · Practice 1

1. 安やすく____、いらないものは買かいません。

安やすくても、いらないものは買かいません。

Cheapness would normally be a reason to buy, and the sentence overrules it - that is exactly the ても move. 安やすい becomes 安やすくて before も.

～ても · Practice 2

The trip to the sea depends on the weather, so this is a plain condition. 晴はれても would mean you are going regardless - and then mentioning the sunshine would make no sense.

～ても · Practice 3

～ても · Practice 4

～ても · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 薬くすりを飲のんでも、熱ねつが下さがりません。 (b) 薬くすりを飲のんだのに、熱ねつが下さがりませんでした。

(a) describes an ongoing situation flatly: taking the medicine does not bring the fever down, and it may have happened several times. It is what you would say to a doctor when reporting symptoms.

(b) points at one occasion that is over and adds an emotional charge - you took the medicine, so the fever should have gone down, and you are frustrated or puzzled that it did not. のに always carries that "but it didn't work out" feeling; ても does not.

～のに - although

Meaning · although; even though - and the speaker is not happy about it

～のに joins two facts that ought not to fit together, and it always carries the speaker's reaction to the mismatch: surprise, disappointment, or a flat complaint. That feeling is not optional. It is the difference between のに and the neutral "but" you already know.

～のに - although · Formation

"You weren't feeling well? You really should have said something sooner."

～のに - although · Nuance & register

のに is neither polite nor casual in itself - it attaches to plain forms, and the politeness of the sentence is decided at the end. What it is is emotionally loaded, and that is where it gets you into trouble.

Because のに always says "this should not have turned out this way", pointing it at someone's behaviour is a reproach. お願ねがいしたのに、まだ 終おわっていないんですか is an accusation, however politely the sentence ends. In front of a customer or a superior, use が or けど and let the facts speak.

The sentence-final のに is very common in speech and drops the second clause entirely, leaving the complaint hanging:

～のに - although · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 「来きてくれればよかったのに。」 - "You should have come."
  • 「もっと早はやく言いえばいいのに。」 - "I wish he'd say so sooner."
  • 「傘かさ、持もってきたのに…」 - "But I brought an umbrella…"

This is the tone of mild reproach or wistfulness - warm between friends, sharp from a stranger. It combines especially often with せっかく ("after going to all that trouble"), which supplies the disappointment in advance.

のに attaches to plain forms. You will occasionally meet ～ますのに in older or very formal writing, but it sounds dated, and it is not a form to produce.

～のに - although · ～のに vs ～が・～けど

Rule of thumb - が and けど just say "but". のに says "but" and adds "which I find surprising, unfair or annoying".

この辞書じしょは古ふるいですが、まだ使つかえます。
This dictionary is old, but it's still usable.

この辞書じしょは古ふるいのに、まだ使つかえます。
This dictionary is ancient and it still works - remarkable.

There is also a hard grammatical limit. のに reports what happened, so its main clause cannot be a request, an invitation, a command or a statement of intention. All of these need が or けど:

～のに - although · ～のに vs ～が・～けど (cont.)

  • ❌疲つかれたのに、もう少すこし頑張がんばろう。 → ✅疲つかれたけど、もう少すこし頑張がんばろう。
  • ❌雨あめが降ふっているのに、傘かさを貸かしてください。 → ✅雨あめが降ふっているので、傘かさを貸かしてください。

If the end of your sentence is something you are asking for or deciding, のに is the wrong connective.

～のに - although · ～のに vs ～ても

Rule of thumb - のに needs a real event; ても can be a hypothesis. And のに complains, while ても merely states.

雨あめが降ふっても、明日あしたは出でかけます。
I'm going out tomorrow even if it rains.

雨あめが降ふっていたのに、傘かさを持もたずに出でかけました。
It was raining and I went out without an umbrella anyway.

～のに - although · ～のに vs ～ても (cont.)

There is no ❌降ふるのに、出でかけます for "even if it rains" - nothing has happened yet, so there is nothing for のに to object to. The same reason explains why ても and not のに follows question words: いくら待まっても、 どんなに安やすくても.

～のに - although · ～のに "although" vs ～のに "in order to"

Rule of thumb - check the form in front. "Although" follows any complete plain clause - past, negative, な. "In order to" follows a dictionary-form verb only, and the sentence ends in a judgement about difficulty, cost or usefulness.

この道具どうぐは便利べんりなのに、あまり売うれていない。
This tool is handy, and yet it doesn't sell well. (although)

この道具どうぐは野菜やさいを切きるのに便利べんりです。
This tool is handy for cutting vegetables. (in order to)

Two more tests, if the form does not settle it. The purpose のに can be replaced by ために with almost no change; the concessive one cannot. And the purpose のに never takes a comma before it, while the concessive one usually does.

～のに - although · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 元気げんきだのに、学校がっこうを休やすみました。 → ✅ 元気げんきなのに、学校がっこうを休やすみました。
    な-adjectives link with な in the present tense. The same applies to nouns: ❌先生せんせいのに → ✅先生せんせいなのに.

  • ❌ 道みちが混こんでいたのに、遅おくれてしまいました。 → ✅ 道みちが混こんでいたので、遅おくれてしまいました。
    Heavy traffic and arriving late fit together perfectly, so there is no mismatch for のに to react to. It needs two halves that clash.

  • ❌ 明日あした雨あめが降ふるのに、試合しあいをします。 → ✅ 明日あした雨あめが降ふっても、試合しあいをします。
    のに cannot describe a hypothetical. For "even if", use ても.

～のに - although · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 彼かれは新入社員しんにゅうしゃいんなのに、よく働はたらきますね。（said to his manager as praise） → ✅ 彼かれは新入社員しんにゅうしゃいんですが、よく働はたらきますね。
    Grammatical, but のに frames the situation as odd or unsatisfactory, which is not how you compliment somebody's new recruit.

～のに - in order to

Meaning · for doing something - what a thing is good for, and what it costs you

This のに is not the "although" one at all. It is the nominaliser の plus the particle に: the verb is turned into a noun ("the doing of it") and に points at it as a target. The sentence then judges something against that target - this tool is handy for it, that job took two hours to do.

～のに - in order to · Formation

Only the dictionary form goes in front of it, and the sentence has to end in an evaluation: a cost, a length of time, or a judgement of usefulness.

Checking all these documents took a full two hours, so I ended up working late.

～のに - in order to · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and unusually even-handed about where it appears: it is the ordinary way to say what a thing is for, so it turns up in conversation, in instruction manuals, in product descriptions and in recipes alike.

Two shapes to have ready. The のに phrase can be fronted, with a comma, when the purpose is what the sentence is about: 大人数おおにんずうで集あつまるのに、 あの店みせはちょうどいいですよ。 And because の is a genuine nominaliser, ordinary particles can stack on it - のには for contrast, のにも for "for that as well": 作つくるのには時間じかんが かかりますが、食たべるのは一瞬いっしゅんです。

Watch what the sentence is actually judging, because that decides between のに and のが. If the thing being described is a tool, an amount of time or a cost, you want のに. If it is the action itself that is difficult, tiring or fun, you want のが:

～のに - in order to · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • この机つくえは作業さぎょうするのにちょうどいい。 - the desk is being judged.
  • 作業さぎょうするのが大変たいへんだった。 - the work is being judged.

Learners produce ❌覚おぼえるのに難むずかしい constantly. Say 覚おぼえるのが難むずかしい, or keep のに and change the ending to 時間じかんがかかる.

～のに - in order to · ～のに vs ～ために

Rule of thumb - ために is followed by what you do; のに is followed by an assessment. If the second half is an action you deliberately take, it is ために.

健康けんこうのために、毎朝まいあさ野菜やさいジュースを飲のんでいます。
I drink vegetable juice every morning for my health.

健康けんこうを保たもつのに、野菜やさいジュースはとてもいいそうです。
Apparently vegetable juice is very good for keeping healthy.

～のに - in order to · ～のに vs ～ために (cont.)

The first sentence reports a decision you made and carried out. The second rates the juice against a purpose. That is why ❌日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうするのに日本にほんへ行いきます fails: going to Japan is a deliberate act, not a verdict - it needs ために.

There is also a subject rule that catches people out. ために normally requires the same person to have the purpose and do the action; のに has no such restriction, because nobody has to be doing anything at all - この道具どうぐは開あけるのに使つかわれます is fine.

～のに - in order to · ～のに vs ～ように

Rule of thumb - ように follows a verb you cannot simply decide to do: a potential form, a negative, or a change of state. のに follows a plain action.

後うしろの人ひとにも見みえるように、字じを大おおきく書かきました。
I wrote the letters big so that the people at the back could see too.

大おおきい字じは遠とおくから読よむのにいいですね。
Big letters are good for reading from a distance.

～のに - in order to · ～のに vs ～ように (cont.)

ように states a desired outcome and is followed by the action taken to bring it about; のに states a purpose and is followed by a judgement. A quick test: 忘わすれないように ("so as not to forget") could never become ❌忘わすれないのに in that sense, because のに takes no negatives.

～のに - in order to · ～のに "in order to" vs ～のに "although"

Rule of thumb - look at the verb form. Dictionary form and an evaluation at the end means "in order to". Anything else - past, negative, な, an い-adjective - means "although".

この袋ふくろは本ほんを運はこぶのに丈夫じょうぶです。
This bag is sturdy enough for carrying books. (in order to)

この袋ふくろは丈夫じょうぶなのに、軽かるいです。
This bag is sturdy and yet light. (although)

The swap test settles the rest. Try ために in the gap: if the sentence survives, it is the purpose one. Try けど instead: if that survives, it is the concessive one. No sentence takes both, because the two のに want different verb forms in front of them in the first place.

～のに - in order to · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この机つくえは勉強べんきょうしたのに便利べんりです。 → ✅ この机つくえは勉強べんきょうするのに便利べんりです。
    Only the dictionary form goes in front of the purpose のに. The past tense turns it into the "although" one, and the sentence stops making sense.

  • ❌ 大学だいがくに入はいるのに、毎日まいにち勉強べんきょうしています。 → ✅ 大学だいがくに入はいるために、毎日まいにち勉強べんきょうしています。
    Studying every day is something you have decided to do, not an assessment of how hard university entrance is. Deliberate action takes ために.

～のに - in order to · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この漢字かんじは覚おぼえるのに難むずかしいです。 → ✅ この漢字かんじは覚おぼえるのが難むずかしいです。
    難むずかしい describes the act of memorising, so it needs the nominaliser の as its subject. Keep のに only when the sentence is rating a tool, or counting time and money.

  • ❌ 東京とうきょうまで行いくに三時間さんじかんかかります。 → ✅ 東京とうきょうまで行いくのに三時間さんじかんかかります。
    A verb cannot take に directly. The の is what turns it into something a particle can attach to.

それでも

Meaning · but still; and yet - everything just said pointed the other way, and it happened anyway

それでも opens a sentence by picking up the whole situation you have just described and refusing to let it decide the outcome. It concedes the point first, then overrules it. That two-step is why it sounds firmer, and often more thoughtful, than a bare "but".

それでも · Usage

それでも is a conjunction: it stands at the head of a sentence, usually followed by a comma, and it needs a previous sentence to point back at. The それ is doing real work - it means "that whole state of affairs" - so it cannot open a conversation.

寒さむかった。それでも、歩あるいて帰かえった。

It also lives inside a clause when the two halves are joined: 失敗しっぱいするかもしれない。それでも、やってみる価値かちはある。 The second sentence is where the speaker's real position is; それでも marks everything before it as acknowledged and set aside.

それでも · Usage (cont.)

There is one fixed phrase built on it that behaves differently: それでもいい / それでもかまわない - "even so, that's fine". Here それ + でも is the ordinary "even if" construction, and the phrase is the standard way to accept a condition you have just been warned about. 「時間じかんがかかりますよ。」「それでもいいです。」

Two neighbours to keep separate, because they differ by one syllable and mean the opposite thing: それで and そこで both introduce a consequence ("and so"), while それでも introduces the failure of a consequence.

それでも · Examples

この薬くすりはとても苦にがいです。それでも、毎日まいにち飲のんでいます。
This medicine is really bitter. Even so, I take it every day.

彼かれは何度なんども失敗しっぱいした。それでも、あきらめなかった。
He failed over and over. Still, he never gave up.

外そとは雪ゆきが降ふっていた。それでも、子こどもたちは公園こうえんへ走はしっていった。
It was snowing outside. The children ran off to the park regardless.

給料きゅうりょうは安やすいし、残業ざんぎょうも多おおい。それでも、今いまの仕事しごとは続つづけたいと思おもっています。
The pay is poor and there's a lot of overtime. All the same, I want to stay in this job.

それでも · Nuance & register

それでも is register-neutral and equally at home in an essay and in conversation, though everyday speech often just says でも. Choosing the longer word is not merely emphasis: でも brushes the previous statement aside, while それでも picks it up, weighs it, and then decides anyway. That makes it the polite way to disagree with someone who has a point.

「おっしゃることはよく分わかります。それでも、私わたしは反対はんたいです。」

Because of that acknowledging feel, それでも frequently follows a し clause or a list of drawbacks - the speaker sets out the case against, and then states what they are going to do. It also sits naturally in front of a resolution or a value judgement: それでもやってみます, それでも信しんじたい.

Three near neighbours, worth keeping straight:

それでも · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • でも - the everyday "but". Lighter, more casual, and does not necessarily
    concede anything.
  • それなのに - "and yet", with a complaint attached. It says the outcome was
    unfair, where それでも merely says it was unexpected.
  • それにもかかわらず - the same idea in formal written Japanese. Stiff in
    conversation; normal in a report.

And one that is not a neighbour at all: それに means "what's more", adding a second point in the same direction. Learners reach for それでも when they want それに surprisingly often.

それでも · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この店みせは安やすいです。それでも、おいしいです。 → ✅ この店みせは安やすいです。それに、おいしいです。
    Cheap and tasty are both good points, so nothing is being overruled. それでも needs the second sentence to go against the first.

  • ❌ 電車でんしゃが止とまりました。それでも、会社かいしゃに遅おくれました。 → ✅ 電車でんしゃが止とまりました。それで、会社かいしゃに遅おくれました。
    Being late is exactly what you would expect after the trains stop. That is a consequence, so it takes それで.

それでも · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ それでも、今日きょうは会議かいぎがあります。（as the opening line of an email） → ✅ 実じつは、今日きょうは会議かいぎがあります。
    それ has to refer to something already said. With nothing in front of it, the sentence leaves the reader looking for a missing paragraph.

  • ❌ 高たかかったそれでも、買かいました。 → ✅ 高たかかったです。それでも、買かいました。／高たかかったけど、買かいました。
    それでも is not a clause connective. It starts a new sentence; joining two clauses inside one sentence is けど or が.

それでも · Practice 1

1. 何度なんども注意ちゅういしました。____、彼かれは同おなじ失敗しっぱいをくり返かえしています。

それでも、彼かれは同おなじ失敗しっぱいをくり返かえしています。

Being warned repeatedly ought to stop the mistakes, and it has not. それでも picks up that whole first sentence and reports that it made no difference.

それでも · Practice 2

2. Choose: 朝あさから熱ねつがありました。（それでも・それで）、会社かいしゃを休やすみました。

朝あさから熱ねつがありました。それで、会社かいしゃを休やすみました。

Taking the day off is the natural result of having a fever, so this is a consequence - それで. それでも would only work if the second sentence went against expectation, as in それでも、会社かいしゃに行いきました.

それでも · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 雨あめが降ふっていた。それでも、彼女かのじょは待まっていてくれた。 (b) 雨あめが降ふっていた。それなのに、彼女かのじょは傘かさを持もっていなかった。

(a) acknowledges the rain and then reports something admirable that happened in spite of it. それでも is neutral about blame; here it carries a note of gratitude.

(b) uses それなのに, which adds the speaker's dissatisfaction - she should have had an umbrella, and the fact that she did not is being criticised or worried over. Swap the two connectives and each sentence loses its point.
