～とき

Meaning · when; at the time that

とき is the ordinary noun for "time", pressed into service as a joint between two clauses: at the time when X, Y. What lifts it above vocabulary is the tense inside the clause. Japanese sets it by looking at the main verb, not at the present moment - so 行いくとき and 行いったとき can both sit in a sentence about last year and mean quite different things.

～とき · Formation

とき is a noun, so anything that can modify a noun can modify it - and it takes that modifier in the plain form.

～とき · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary) dictionary form + とき 出でかける → 出でかけるとき
Verb (た-form) た-form + とき 出でかけた → 出でかけたとき
Verb (ない-form) ない-form + とき わからない → わからないとき
Verb (ている) ている + とき 運転うんてんしている → 運転うんてんしているとき
い-adjective plain い + とき 忙いそがしい → 忙いそがしいとき
な-adjective な + とき 暇ひまだ → 暇ひまなとき
Noun Noun + の + とき 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいのとき
Noun (past state) Noun + だった + とき 子こどもだったとき
Particles after it とき + は／に／も 寒さむいときは、寒さむいときに

～とき · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
In kanji とき = 時とき 子こどもの時とき
Politeness on the final verb only 来くるとき買かいます

Rule of thumb - the verb in front of とき is plain. ❌来きますとき is not how ordinary Japanese sounds; politeness lives on the last verb of the sentence and nowhere else.

～とき · Formation (cont.)

Note - the に is optional. Adding it pins the clause to a definite point (大学だいがくに入はいったときに買かいました); leaving it off lets the clause read as a general circumstance (寒さむいとき、温あたたかいものが食たべたくなる). With は on the end - ときは - the clause becomes the topic, which is how you state a rule or a piece of advice.

～とき · Examples

暇ひまなとき、何なにをしますか。
What do you do when you have free time?

子こどものとき、ピアノを習ならっていました。
I used to take piano lessons when I was little.

眠ねむいときは、冷つめたい水みずで顔かおを洗あらいます。
When I'm sleepy I wash my face with cold water.

道みちがわからないときは、駅員えきいんさんに聞きいてください。
When you can't work out the way, ask a member of station staff.

～とき · Examples (cont.)

家いえを出でるとき、かぎをかけるのを忘わすれてしまいました。
I forgot to lock up as I was leaving the house.

大阪おおさかに着ついたとき、もう夜よるの十時じゅうじを過すぎていました。
By the time I got to Osaka it was already gone ten at night.

運転うんてんしているときは、携帯けいたいを見みないでください。危あぶないですから。
Please don't look at your phone while you're driving - it's dangerous.

会議かいぎが長ながくなりそうなときは、先さきに昼ひるごはんを食たべておいたほうがいいですよ。
When it looks like the meeting is going to run long, you're better off eating lunch first.

～とき · Nuance & register

とき is register-neutral and appears in every kind of Japanese, from a text message to a contract. The politeness of the sentence is decided entirely by the final verb, so 子こどものとき、よく泣ないた and 子こどものとき、よく泣なきました differ only at the end.

One thing that is worth copying from careful writing: when とき is doing this grammatical job, Japanese usually writes it in kana. The kanji 時とき is kept for cases where it means time as a real thing - 時ときが経たつ, 時ときには. Both spellings are read the same and neither is wrong; kana simply looks more natural on the page.

～とき · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two step-ups you will meet as the register rises. 際さいに is the formal twin of とき and is everywhere in announcements and manuals: ご利用りようの際さいは、係員かかりいんにお申もうし出でください. And 場合ばあい ("in the case that") takes over when the sentence is about a contingency rather than a time: 地震じしんの場合ばあいは、エレベーターを使つかわないでください. Using とき there is not wrong, but 場合 is what the sign says.

Going the other way, casual speech shortens nothing about とき itself - it just drops the に and often the comma, and adds さ or ね: 高校こうこうのときさ、よくここで遊あそんだよね.

～とき · 行くとき vs 行ったとき

Rule of thumb - the tense before とき is measured against the main clause, not against now. Dictionary form: at the moment of the main action, the とき-event has not finished. た-form: it has.

This is the single most tested thing about とき, and English gives you no help, because English says "when" for both.

日本にほんへ行いくとき、スーツケースを買かいました。
I bought a suitcase for my trip to Japan. (bought before I got there)

日本にほんへ行いったとき、スーツケースを買かいました。
I bought a suitcase when I was in Japan. (bought after I arrived)

～とき · 行くとき vs 行ったとき (cont.)

Both sentences are about a finished trip, and both end in ました. Only the form in front of とき tells you which side of the arrival the shopping happened on.

The same logic makes some pairs obviously right and obviously wrong:

お風呂ふろに入はいるとき、服ふくを脱ぬぎます。
You take your clothes off when you get into the bath.

You are not yet in the bath when you undress, so it has to be 入はいるとき. ❌入はいったとき服ふくを脱ぬぐ describes someone undressing in the water.

～とき · ～とき vs ～たら

Rule of thumb - とき names a time; たら sets a condition. If you could replace the English with "at the time when", use とき. If it is really "once" or "if", use たら.

駅えきに着ついたら、電話でんわしてください。
Give me a ring once you get to the station.

駅えきに着ついたとき、あの店みせはもう閉しまっていました。
When I got to the station, that shop had already closed.

たら looks forward and triggers something; とき looks at a point on the timeline and reports what was true there. That is why an instruction about the future almost always takes たら, and why a narrated memory almost always takes とき.

～とき · ～とき vs ～たら (cont.)

とき also reaches places たら cannot go without extra machinery: it attaches straight to a noun with の - 学生がくせいのとき - where たら needs 学生がくせいだったら, and that already sounds hypothetical ("if you were a student").

～とき · ～ているとき vs ～ながら

Rule of thumb - ながら needs one person doing two things at once, and the main action is the one at the end. ているとき has no such restriction: the two clauses can have different subjects entirely.

テレビを見みながらごはんを食たべます。
I eat while watching TV.

私わたしがごはんを食たべているときに、姉あねが帰かえってきました。
My older sister got home while I was eating.

～とき · ～ているとき vs ～ながら (cont.)

Swap them and the second sentence breaks: ❌食たべながら姉あねが帰かえってきた would claim your sister was eating and coming home simultaneously. When the two events belong to different people, ているとき is the only one of the two that works.

～とき · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 子こどもとき、よく熱ねつを出だしました。 → ✅ 子こどものとき、よく熱ねつを出だしました。
    とき is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs の, exactly as in 日本語にほんごの先生せんせい.

  • ❌ 静しずかとき → ✅ 静しずかなとき
    な-adjectives keep their な before any noun, and とき is a noun.

  • ❌ 国くにへ帰かえりますとき、連絡れんらくします。 → ✅ 国くにへ帰かえるとき、連絡れんらくします。
    The clause before とき is plain. Politeness goes on the final verb only.

～とき · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ ガイドブックは京都きょうとへ行いったとき、東京とうきょうの本屋ほんやで買かいました。 → ✅ 京都きょうとへ行いくとき、東京とうきょうの本屋ほんやで買かいました。
    You bought it before you set off, so the trip had not happened yet: dictionary form. The た-form would put the Tokyo bookshop after your arrival in Kyoto.

～とき · Practice 1

1. 大学生だいがくせい____とき、北海道ほっかいどうを旅行りょこうしました。

大学生だいがくせいのとき、北海道ほっかいどうを旅行りょこうしました。

とき is a noun, and a noun modifying another noun needs の between them. Only verbs and adjectives attach to とき directly.

～とき · Practice 2

2. ドアを（開あける・開あけた）とき、このかぎを使つかってください。

ドアを**開あける**とき、このかぎを使つかってください。

The key is what makes the opening possible, so at the moment you use it the door is still shut - the とき-event has not finished. That is the dictionary form. 開あけたとき would have you fiddling with the key after the door was already open.

～とき · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "When I was a high school student, I worked part-time at a convenience store."

高校生こうこうせいのとき、コンビニでアルバイトをしていました。

A noun takes の before とき. The main verb carries both the tense and the politeness, and ていました is the natural choice because the job lasted a stretch of time rather than happening once.

～とき · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 忙いそがしいですとき、電話でんわしないでください。

✅ 忙いそがしいとき、電話でんわしないでください。

An い-adjective goes in front of とき in its plain form. です is a sentence ending and cannot sit in the middle of a clause that modifies a noun.

～とき · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 家いえを出でるとき、電気でんきを消けしました。 (b) 家いえを出でたとき、となりの人ひとに会あいました。

(a) uses the dictionary form because the leaving was not finished yet: you were still inside, on your way out, when you hit the light switch. (b) uses the た-form because the leaving was finished: you were already through the door when you ran into the neighbour.

Swapping them produces nonsense in both directions - turning off the lights from the street, and bumping into the neighbour in your own hallway.

～後で

Meaning · after something; afterwards, later on

N5 taught this word in kana, as ～たあとで. Here it is in kanji, plus the two jobs the kana page left alone: 後で standing on its own as the adverb later, and the four different readings the character takes once it appears in writing.

～後で · Formation

The clause in front of 後で is in the た-form, whatever the tense of the sentence as a whole. A noun joins with の.

～後で · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb た-form + 後あとで 食たべる → 食たべた後あとで
Noun Noun + の + 後あとで 授業じゅぎょう → 授業じゅぎょうの後あとで
On its own (adverb) 後あとで + clause 後あとで連絡れんらくします
Demonstrative その後あとで／この後あと その後あとで決きめましょう
Without で た-form + 後あと 見みた後あと、考かんがえます
With に instead た-form + 後あとに 私わたしの後あとに発表はっぴょうします
Amount of time Quantity + 後ご（に） 三日みっか後ごに、一時間いちじかん後ごに
Sino-Japanese compound Noun + 後ご 卒業そつぎょう後ご、使用しよう後ご
Formal register 後のちほど 後のちほどご連絡れんらくいたします

～後で · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Politeness on the final verb only 着ついた後あとで電話でんわします
❌ ❌ dictionary form + 後あとで ❌食たべる後あとで

Rule of thumb - one character, four readings, and they are not interchangeable. 後あと is after in time, 後うしろ is behind in space, 後ご is the suffix on Sino-Japanese words and on amounts of time, and 後のち is the formal, written one. Reading 三日みっか後ご as あと marks you out instantly.

～後で · Formation (cont.)

Note - で is optional and drops freely, and the whole phrase can take は or も: 食たべた後あとは眠ねむくなる. What you cannot do is put a verb in the dictionary form in front of it - that slot belongs to 前まえに.

～後で · Examples

後あとでメールしますね。
I'll email you later.

薬くすりは食事しょくじの**後あとで**飲のんでください。
Please take the medicine after meals.

仕事しごとの**後あとで**、ジムで泳およぐつもりです。
I'm planning to swim at the gym after work.

宿題しゅくだいを終おわらせた**後あとで**なら、ゲームをしてもいいよ。
You can play games once you've finished your homework.

～後で · Examples (cont.)

試合しあいの**後あとで**、チームのみんなでラーメンを食たべに行いった。
After the match the whole team went out for ramen.

引ひっ越こした**後あとで**、住所じゅうしょが変かわったことを会社かいしゃに伝つたえました。
After I moved, I let the company know my address had changed.

説明せつめいを聞きいた**後あとで**質問しつもんがある方かたは、手てを挙あげてください。
If you have a question after hearing the explanation, please raise your hand.

三十分さんじゅっぷん**後ご**に出発しゅっぱつしますので、それまでに準備じゅんびをお願ねがいします。
We leave in thirty minutes, so please be ready by then.

～後で · Nuance & register

As a clause connector 後で is plain and neutral. As a standalone adverb it carries something extra, and that is worth knowing: 後で is deliberately vague. It means "at some point after now, probably today", and it names no time at all.

That vagueness is friendly among equals - じゃあ、また後あとでね is how you end a conversation you expect to resume - and unhelpful upward. Told 後あとでやります, a manager has learned nothing; 三時さんじまでに出だします is what they wanted. If you need the vagueness and the politeness, the business word is 後のちほど: 後のちほど担当たんとうよりご連絡れんらくいたします. It is one register step above 後で and never sounds casual.

～後で · Nuance & register (cont.)

Spelling is a genuine choice here. In the grammatical use, careful writing often prefers kana - 食たべたあとで - because the kanji pulls the reader towards the "behind" meaning. Inside a compound the kanji is compulsory and the reading switches: 卒業そつぎょう後ご, 開店かいてん後ご, 二年にねん後ご.

One last spoken habit: 後あとにする means "leave it for later" - その話はなしは後あとにしよう. It is not the same construction as 後で, but it lives in the same corner of the language.

～後で · ～た後で vs ～てから

Rule of thumb - てから says the second thing does not happen until the first is done. 後で only says it happens later. When the order is a requirement, use てから; when it is just the order things came in, 後で is enough.

お金かねを入いれてから、ボタンを押おしてください。
Put the money in first, then press the button.

Here てから is doing real work: pressing the button before paying achieves nothing. Swap in 入いれた後あとで and the instruction goes soft - it now merely reports a sequence.

映画えいがの**後あとで**、喫茶店きっさてんで少すこし話はなした。
After the film we chatted for a bit in a coffee shop.

～後で · ～た後で vs ～てから (cont.)

Nothing about the film enabled the chat. This is plain sequence, so 後で fits and てから would sound oddly purposeful.

てから has one job 後で cannot take on at all: measuring elapsed time from a starting point. 日本にほんに来きてから三年さんねんになります - "it's been three years since I came to Japan." ❌来きた後あとで三年さんねんになります is not Japanese. Going the other way, 後で attaches straight to a noun with の, which てから cannot do: 会議かいぎの後あとで has no てから equivalent short of adding a verb.

～後で · 後で vs 後に vs ～後に

Rule of thumb - で is the everyday "afterwards". に locates a point: use it when you are placing something in an order, and you must use the 後ご reading with a measured quantity of time.

田中たなかさんの**後あとに**発表はっぴょうします。
I'm presenting after Tanaka.

五分ごふん**後ご**にまたかけ直なおします。
I'll call back in five minutes.

～後で · 後で vs 後に vs ～後に (cont.)

A quantity of time never takes 後で: ❌五分ごふん後あとで is simply wrong, both in the particle and in the reading. In the ordinary "X happened, then Y" use, 後で and 後に overlap heavily and 後で is the safer spoken default; 後に leans written, and it is what you want when the sentence is about a slot in a sequence rather than about a time.

There is also 後のちに, read のち, which means "later in life" or "subsequently" and belongs to biography and history: 彼かれは後のちに市長しちょうになった. 後で cannot carry that sense.

～後で · 後で vs 今度・そのうち

Rule of thumb - 後で is later today. 今度こんど is next time, on some other occasion. そのうち is eventually, with no occasion in mind at all.

English collapses all three into "later", and picking the wrong one changes what you have promised.

その書類しょるいは**後あとで**見みておきます。
I'll look over that document later on. (today, before I go home)

**今度こんど**いっしょに飲のみに行いきましょう。
Let's go for a drink sometime.

～後で · 後で vs 今度・そのうち (cont.)

That second sentence is an invitation, not an appointment, and everyone hears it that way. If you say 今度こんど電話でんわします to someone waiting for a call in the next hour, they will assume you mean next week. そのうち is vaguer still - そのうち片付かたづけます is close to admitting it may never happen.

～後で · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 食たべる**後あとで**歯はをみがきます。 → ✅ 食たべた後あとで歯はをみがきます。
    後で takes the た-form. The dictionary form in that slot belongs to 前まえに.

  • ❌ 授業じゅぎょう後あとで質問しつもんしました。 → ✅ 授業じゅぎょうの後あとで質問しつもんしました。
    後 is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs の. (The compound 授業じゅぎょう後ご exists and needs no の - but then it is read ご and belongs to written style.)

  • ❌ 二時間にじかん後あとで戻もどります。 → ✅ 二時間にじかん後ごに戻もどります。
    With a measured amount of time the reading is ご and the particle is に.

～後で · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ すぐ終おわるので、今度こんど電話でんわします。 → ✅ すぐ終おわるので、後あとで電話でんわします。
    今度こんど pushes the call to another occasion. For "later today", it has to be 後で.

～後で · Practice 1

1. シャワーを浴あび____後あとで、ビールを飲のみます。

シャワーを浴あびた後あとで、ビールを飲のみます。

The verb in front of 後で is always in the た-form, even though the sentence describes a habit and ends in the present ます. The た marks the shower as complete relative to the beer, not relative to now.

～後で · Practice 2

2. 十分じゅっぷん（後あとで・後ごに）会議かいぎが始はじまります。

十分じゅっぷん後ごに会議かいぎが始はじまります。

A measured quantity of time takes the ご reading and the particle に. ❌十分じゅっぷん後あとで is wrong on both counts, and native listeners notice the reading before they notice the particle.

～後で · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'll explain the details later - let's eat first."

詳くわしいことは**後あとで**説明せつめいします。まず食たべましょう。

後で on its own is the everyday adverb "later", and it is exactly right for pushing something to the end of the same afternoon. 今度こんど would mean the explanation comes on a different day.

～後で · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昼ひるごはんを食たべる後あとで、公園こうえんを散歩さんぽしませんか。

✅ 昼ひるごはんを食たべた後あとで、公園こうえんを散歩さんぽしませんか。

Only the た-form goes in front of 後で. The invitation is still about the future, but the た is measuring the lunch against the walk, not against the present moment.

～後で · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 後あとでご連絡れんらくします。 (b) 後のちほどご連絡れんらくいたします。

Both promise a message with no time attached. (a) is ordinary polite Japanese and fits a colleague, a friend, or a shop you deal with often. (b) is the same promise in business register: 後ほど is the formal word for "later", and いたします lowers the speaker. You would hear (b) from a call centre or a receptionist, and it would sound stiff between friends.

～までに

Meaning · by - at any point up to that limit, but not after it

N5 gave you まで, "until", which measures a stretch of time. Adding に turns the stretch into an edge. までに names the last moment at which something may happen, and says nothing about when inside the window it actually does. It is the particle of deadlines.

～までに · Formation

までに follows a time expression, or a clause in the dictionary form that names the moment the window closes.

～までに · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Time noun Noun + までに 五時ごじ → 五時ごじまでに
Day or date Noun + までに 金曜日きんようび → 金曜日きんようびまでに
Event noun Noun + までに 試験しけん → 試験しけんまでに
Verb dictionary form + までに 出でかける → 出でかけるまでに
Demonstrative それ／いつ + までに それまでに、いつまでに
Emphasis までに + は 八時はちじまでには着つきます
Very often with までに + ～ておく 買かっておく、片付かたづけておく
❌ ❌ た-form + までに ❌着ついたまでに
❌ ❌ ない-form + までに ❌来こないまでに

～までに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the main verb of a までに sentence has to be something that finishes in one go: 出だす, 着つく, 決きめる, 終おわらせる, 帰かえる. If the verb describes something that goes on - 待まつ, 寝ねる, 働はたらく - you want plain まで, not までに.

Note - までには adds "at the latest, and I mean it": 七時しちじまでには戻もどります promises seven as the outer bound, whereas 七時しちじまでに戻もどります merely states the deadline. The は is what turns a fact into a commitment.

～までに · Examples

五時ごじまでに帰かえってきてね。
Be back by five, all right?

レポートは金曜日きんようびまでに出だしてください。
Please hand the report in by Friday.

電車でんしゃが来くるまでに、きっぷを買かっておきます。
I'll buy the ticket before the train gets here.

夏なつが終おわるまでに、一度いちどは海うみに行いきたいなあ。
I'd like to get to the sea at least once before the summer's over.

～までに · Examples (cont.)

来週らいしゅうの月曜日げつようびまでにお返事へんじをいただけますか。
Could I have your reply by Monday next week?

お客きゃくさんが来くるまでに、会議室かいぎしつを片付かたづけておきましょう。
Let's get the meeting room tidied up before the client arrives.

三十歳さんじゅっさいになるまでに、自分じぶんの店みせを持もちたいと思おもっています。
I'm hoping to have my own shop by the time I turn thirty.

申もうし込こみは今月こんげつの終おわりまで受うけ付つけていますが、できれば今週こんしゅうまでに決きめてほしいそうです。
Applications are open to the end of the month, but apparently they'd like a decision by this week if possible.

～までに · Nuance & register

までに is register-neutral and lives in the parts of life that have deadlines: homework, forms, trains, work. Nothing about it is polite or blunt in itself - the verb at the end does that. 金曜きんようまでに出だせ is an order, 金曜きんようまでにご提出ていしゅつください is a notice, and the までに is identical in both.

What it does carry is pressure, because it draws a line. Softening usually happens around it rather than to it. できれば ("if possible"), 念ねんのため ("just in case") and turning the request into a question - 明日あしたまでにいただけますか - all take the edge off without moving the deadline. Dropping に does not soften anything; it changes the meaning.

～までに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two things you will hear rather than read. First, in speech the whole phrase is often reduced to the bare time: じゃ、六時ろくじね. Second, the normal way to ask about a deadline keeps the に in place and adds a verb - いつまでに出だせばいいですか, いつまでにお返事へんじすればよろしいでしょうか. Asking いつまでですか without the に is a different question: it asks how long something lasts, not when it is due.

～までに · までに vs まで

Rule of thumb - まで fills the whole span; までに picks one moment inside it. Ask whether the verb can go on for hours. If it can, まで. If it happens and is over, までに.

This is the whole of the point, and the two are not interchangeable in either direction.

三時さんじまでここで待まっています。
I'll be waiting here until three.

Waiting occupies every minute up to three o'clock, so まで. There is no single instant at which the waiting happens.

三時さんじまでにここへ来きてください。
Please get here by three.

～までに · までに vs まで (cont.)

Arriving takes an instant, and any instant before three will do. Say ❌三時さんじまで来きてください and you have asked someone to keep arriving continuously until three.

The mirror error is just as common. ❌三時さんじまでに待まっていてください asks the listener to complete the act of waiting before three - which is not a thing waiting can do.

～までに · までに vs ～前に

Rule of thumb - 前まえに reports order; までに imposes a limit. Both often fit the same sentence, but only までに implies that being late is a failure.

会議かいぎの**前まえに**資料しりょうを読よみました。
I read the material before the meeting.

会議かいぎまでに資料しりょうを読よんでおいてください。
Please have read the material before the meeting.

The first simply tells you which came first. The second sets a deadline and implies a consequence for missing it, which is why notices, homework instructions and workplace requests reach for までに.

～までに · までに vs ～前に (cont.)

Two mechanical differences. 前まえに attaches to a noun with の - 食事しょくじの前まえに - while までに attaches to the bare noun: 食事しょくじまでに. And 前まえに can follow a duration to mean "ago" - 三日みっか前まえに - which までに can never do.

～までに · までに vs ～中に

Rule of thumb - までに draws a line and lets you act any time before it. ～中じゅうに names a period and asks you to act inside it.

今日きょう中じゅうに返事へんじをします。
I'll get back to you before the day is out.

明日あしたまでに返事へんじをします。
I'll get back to you by tomorrow.

～までに · までに vs ～中に (cont.)

今日きょう中じゅうに is said on the day in question and treats today as a container. 明日あしたまでに can be said days in advance and treats tomorrow as a wall. When the two overlap - 今日きょうまでに versus 今日きょう中じゅうに - the first is what a form says to you in advance, the second is what a colleague says to you at nine in the morning.

Note - watch the reading of 中. After a span you can live through it is じゅう: 今日きょう中じゅうに, 今週こんしゅう中じゅうに, 一日いちにち中じゅう. After a word naming an activity or a portion of the day it is ちゅう: 午前ごぜん中ちゅうに, 会議かいぎ中ちゅう, 仕事しごと中ちゅう. Same character, and no rule will save you at the moment you have to say it aloud.

～までに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 五時ごじまで宿題しゅくだいを出だしてください。 → ✅ 五時ごじまでに宿題しゅくだいを出だしてください。
    Handing something in is momentary, so it needs the deadline particle. Without に you have asked for continuous handing-in until five.

  • ❌ 雨あめがやむまでに、ここで休やすんでいましょう。 → ✅ 雨あめがやむまで、ここで休やすんでいましょう。
    Resting goes on throughout, so the span particle is the right one.

  • ❌ 家いえに着ついたまでに → ✅ 家いえに着つくまでに
    A clause before までに is in the dictionary form. It names the moment the window shuts, and that moment has not arrived yet.

～までに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 夏休なつやすみまでに毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうします。 → ✅ 夏休なつやすみまで毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうします。
    毎日まいにち spreads the action over the whole period, which is exactly what までに forbids.

～までに · Practice 1

1. 九時くじ____空港くうこうに着つかなければなりません。

九時くじまでに空港くうこうに着つかなければなりません。

Arriving is a one-off event, and nine o'clock is the limit it must fall inside. 九時くじまで着つく would ask you to go on arriving all morning.

～までに · Practice 2

2. 雨あめがやむ（まで・までに）、この喫茶店きっさてんにいましょう。

雨あめがやむまで、この喫茶店きっさてんにいましょう。

Staying somewhere occupies the whole stretch of time, so it takes the span particle. までに would need a verb that finishes in a moment - for instance 雨あめがやむまでに注文ちゅうもんしましょう.

～までに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Could you finish this by Wednesday?"

これを水曜日すいようびまでに終おわらせてもらえますか。

までに marks Wednesday as the deadline, and any time before it counts as on time. Turning the request into a question with もらえますか is what keeps it polite; the deadline itself is not softened.

～までに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 家いえに帰かえったまでに、スーパーで買かい物ものをします。

✅ 家いえに帰かえるまでに、スーパーで買かい物ものをします。

The clause naming the deadline goes in the dictionary form. From the point of view of the shopping, getting home has not happened yet, so た is impossible here.

～までに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) レポートは金曜日きんようびまでに出だします。 (b) レポートは金曜日きんようびまで書かきます。

(a) is about a deadline: the report leaves your hands at some single moment no later than Friday, and it might well be Wednesday. (b) is about a span: you will be writing continuously up to Friday, and Friday is when you stop - with no promise that it is finished then.

The particle and the verb move together. Momentary 出だす pulls までに; ongoing 書かく pulls まで.

～ごろ

Meaning · around, about - attached to a point in time and nothing else

ごろ blurs a moment on the clock or the calendar: 三時さんじごろ, 来週らいしゅうごろ. It is not a general-purpose "approximately". The moment you are approximating an amount - three hours, five people, a thousand yen - ごろ is the wrong word and ぐらい is the right one.

～ごろ · Formation

ごろ is a suffix. It goes straight onto the time word, with nothing in between.

～ごろ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Clock time Time + ごろ 三時さんじ → 三時さんじごろ
Date, month, year Noun + ごろ 五月ごがつ → 五月ごがつごろ
Part of the day Noun + ごろ 夕方ゆうがた → 夕方ゆうがたごろ
Question word 何時なんじ／いつ + ごろ 何時なんじごろ、いつごろ
With に ごろ（に） - the に is optional 七時しちじごろ（に）起おきます
With other particles ごろから、ごろまで、ごろには 九時くじごろから
As an independent noun ころ 子こどものころ、あのころ
In kanji 頃ころ／頃ごろ 若わかい頃ころ
❌ Length of time ❌三時間さんじかんごろ ✅三時間さんじかんぐらい

～ごろ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
❌ Number or amount ❌五人ごにんごろ ✅五人ごにんぐらい

Rule of thumb - ask which question the phrase answers. If it answers いつ ("when?"), use ごろ. If it answers どのくらい ("how much? how long?"), use ぐらい. 三時さんじ is a when; 三時間さんじかん is a how-long, and one character is the whole difference.

～ごろ · Formation (cont.)

Note - the に sits after ごろ, never before it: 六時ろくじごろに, ❌六時ろくじにごろ. And once ごろ is there, に is optional - it is dropped more often than not, because an approximate time is already loose.

～ごろ · Examples

毎朝まいあさ七時しちじごろ起おきます。
I get up around seven every morning.

昼ひるごろから雨あめが降ふるそうですよ。
Apparently it's going to rain from around midday.

何時なんじごろそちらに着つきますか。
Roughly what time will you get there?

桜さくらは四月しがつの初はじめごろに咲さきます。
The cherry blossom comes out around the beginning of April.

～ごろ · Examples (cont.)

子こどものころ、この川かわでよく泳およいだものだ。
When I was small I used to swim in this river all the time.

去年きょねんの今いまごろは、まだ大阪おおさかに住すんでいました。
This time last year I was still living in Osaka.

夕方ゆうがたごろから風かぜが強つよくなるので、洗濯物せんたくものは早はやめに取とり込こんでください。
The wind picks up from around evening, so bring the washing in early.

来月らいげつの中なかごろに引ひっ越こす予定よていなので、それまでに荷物にもつをまとめないといけません。
I'm due to move around the middle of next month, so I have to get my things packed before then.

～ごろ · Nuance & register

ごろ is register-neutral and extremely common in speech. It does, however, do something socially useful: it declines to pin anyone down. Saying 三時さんじごろ伺うかがいます promises an afternoon visit without promising the minute, which is why it turns up in arrangements between friends and in service Japanese alike. The flip side is that a business appointment that genuinely starts at three is announced as 三時さんじから - putting ごろ on a fixed start time reads as vagueness where precision was expected.

～ごろ · Nuance & register (cont.)

The ご/こ alternation is not two words. It is one word that voices when it is stuck straight onto a time expression: 今いまごろ, 近ちかごろ, 日ひごろ, 六時ろくじごろ. Left standing on its own - after の, after an adjective, after この／その／あの - it stays unvoiced: 学生がくせいのころ, 若わかいころ, あのころ. Both are written 頃ころ when written in kanji, though modern text usually leaves them in kana.

今いまごろ deserves a line of its own, because it has an idiomatic second life. Literally "around now", it is also the word for too late: 今いまごろ来きても、もう終おわったよ - "turning up now, are you? It's over." Tone carries the difference, and the sting is real.

～ごろ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, ごろ has quietly built a small family of vocabulary meaning "the right time for": 見みごろ (best time to see it), 食たべごろ (perfectly ripe), 手てごろ (a reasonable price or size). 桜さくらは今いまが見みごろです is what the sign in the park says.

～ごろ · ごろ vs ぐらい・くらい

Rule of thumb - ごろ approximates a point; ぐらい approximates an amount. Clock times, dates and eras take ごろ. Durations, counts, prices, distances and sizes take ぐらい.

三時さんじごろ会社かいしゃを出でます。
I'll leave the office at about three.

三時間さんじかんぐらいかかりました。
It took about three hours.

～ごろ · ごろ vs ぐらい・くらい (cont.)

The pair differ by one character, and that character decides everything: 三時 is a place on the clock, 三時間 is a length. Everything countable behaves like the second one - 五人ごにんぐらい, 千円せんえんぐらい, 十じゅうキロぐらい - and none of them will take ごろ.

Note - you will hear 三時さんじぐらいに in casual speech, and speakers do not treat it as an error any more. ごろ is still the standard choice for a clock time and the one an exam expects. What is unambiguously clumsy is stacking them: 三時さんじごろぐらい says the same thing twice.

～ごろ · ごろ vs 約・だいたい

Rule of thumb - ごろ is a suffix and goes after the time. 約やく is a prefix on a number and belongs to written and formal Japanese. だいたい is an ordinary adverb and floats freely.

電車でんしゃは**約やく**十分じゅっぷん遅おくれています。
The train is running about ten minutes late.

会議かいぎはだいたい五時ごじごろ終おわります。
The meeting will finish around five-ish.

～ごろ · ごろ vs 約・だいたい (cont.)

約やく attaches to a quantity, so it patterns with ぐらい, not with ごろ; you see it in timetables, reports and announcements, and it sounds stiff in conversation. だいたい is happy in front of almost anything and can sit in the same sentence as ごろ, as the second example shows - the two are not in competition. In written notices you will also meet 前後ぜんご: 三時さんじ前後ぜんごに到着とうちゃくの予定よてい, "scheduled to arrive around three", which is ごろ in a suit.

～ごろ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 二時間にじかんごろ待まちました。 → ✅ 二時間にじかんぐらい待まちました。
    二時間にじかん is a length of time, not a point on the clock. Lengths take ぐらい.

  • ❌ 六時ろくじにごろ帰かえります。 → ✅ 六時ろくじごろに帰かえります。
    ごろ bonds to the time word first; any particle comes after it. The に is optional, but it can only stand in that order.

  • ❌ 子こどものごろは野球やきゅうが好すきでした。 → ✅ 子こどものころは野球やきゅうが好すきでした。
    After の the word is standing on its own as a noun, so it does not voice.

  • ❌ 十時じゅうじごろぐらいに寝ねます。 → ✅ 十時じゅうじごろ寝ねます。
    Both words mean "about". Using them together is not wrong so much as sloppy - pick one.

～ごろ · Practice 1

1. 映画えいがは八時はちじ____終おわると思おもいます。

映画えいがは八時はちじごろ終おわると思おもいます。

Eight o'clock is a point on the clock, and the sentence is guessing at when, so ごろ is the suffix that fits. Nothing here is being measured, so ぐらい has no job to do.

～ごろ · Practice 2

2. 駅えきまで歩あるいて十五分じゅうごふん（ごろ・ぐらい）かかります。

駅えきまで歩あるいて十五分じゅうごふんぐらいかかります。

十五分じゅうごふん here is a duration - how long the walk takes - and durations take ぐらい. It would only be ごろ if it named a time on the clock, as in 十時じゅうじ十五分じゅうごふんごろ.

～ごろ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I think I'll get home around eleven tonight."

今夜こんやは十一時じゅういちじごろ家いえに着つくと思おもいます。

Eleven is a clock time, so ごろ blurs it. The に after ごろ would not be wrong, but with an approximate time it is usually left out.

～ごろ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 夏休なつやすみは一いっか月げつごろあります。

✅ 夏休なつやすみは一いっか月げつぐらいあります。

一いっか月げつ measures how long the holiday lasts, so it needs ぐらい. Compare 八月はちがつごろ - "around August" - where the same kind of word names a position in the year instead.

～ごろ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 三時さんじに伺うかがいます。 (b) 三時さんじごろ伺うかがいます。

(a) commits to three o'clock. It is what you say when the appointment is fixed and someone is expecting you at that minute - and it means being late is a broken promise.

(b) commits to the middle of the afternoon and leaves yourself ten or fifteen minutes of room. It is friendlier and lower-risk, and it is the normal choice for a casual visit. Said about a formal appointment, though, it can sound as if you have not really committed.

～おきに

Meaning · at intervals of - leaving a gap of X between one occurrence and the next

おきに describes something that repeats with a fixed gap in between. The word to hold on to is gap, not every, because that single idea explains why 十分じゅっぷんおきに means "every ten minutes" while 一日いちにちおきに means "every other day". English translates them differently; Japanese is saying the same thing both times.

～おきに · Formation

おきに is a suffix and needs a number in front of it. It attaches straight to a quantity - of time, of distance, or of countable things.

～おきに · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Minutes, hours Quantity + おきに 十分じゅっぷん → 十分じゅっぷんおきに
Days, weeks, months, years Quantity + おきに 一日いちにち → 一日いちにちおきに
Distance Quantity + おきに 五ごメートル → 五ごメートルおきに
Countable things Counter + おきに 一ひとつ → 一ひとつおきに
People, seats, rows Counter + おきに 一人ひとりおきに、一列いちれつおきに
Before a noun Quantity + おき + の 一日いちにちおきの練習れんしゅう
Vague quantity 数すう～ + おきに 数分すうふんおきに、数すうメートルおきに
❌ Bare unit ❌日ひおきに ✅一日いちにちおきに
❌ Before a noun with な ❌一日いちにちおきな ✅一日いちにちおきの

～おきに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - おきに measures the space between two occurrences, never the length of the cycle. 一日いちにちおきに leaves one whole day empty: Monday, Wednesday, Friday.

Note - why do the English translations diverge? Because of what the unit is. With days, weeks and years, and with seats and people, the unit is a container the event sits inside, so a gap of one means one whole container left empty: 一ひとつおきに座すわる is one seat used, one seat free. With minutes, hours and metres, the event takes no measurable time or space of its own, so a gap of ten minutes simply puts the events ten minutes apart. One definition, two arithmetics.

～おきに · Examples

バスは十分じゅっぷんおきに来きます。
The buses come every ten minutes.

この薬くすりは一日いちにちおきに飲のんでください。
Take this medicine every other day.

一ひとつおきに席せきに座すわってください。
Please sit in every other seat.

祖母そぼには一週間いっしゅうかんおきに電話でんわしています。
I phone my grandmother every other week.

～おきに · Examples (cont.)

道みちの両側りょうがわに、五ごメートルおきに木きが植うえてあります。
Trees are planted every five metres on both sides of the road.

この会かいは二年にねんおきに東京とうきょうで開ひらかれます。
This conference is held in Tokyo every third year.

係かかりの人ひとが三十分さんじゅっぷんおきに見回みまわりに来きますので、何なにかあったら声こえをかけてください。
A member of staff comes round every thirty minutes, so give them a shout if anything's wrong.

去年きょねんの春はるから一日いちにちおきにジムに通かよっていて、おかげで体重たいじゅうが五ごキロ減へりました。
I've been going to the gym every other day since last spring, and I've lost five kilos as a result.

～おきに · Nuance & register

おきに is neutral and faintly precise-sounding - it belongs to timetables, instructions on a packet, and descriptions of routines. Nothing about it is formal or casual, and it does not change shape in polite speech.

It does have one real-world problem, and you should know about it rather than be caught by it: with days, weeks and years, speakers themselves disagree about what おきに means. By the rule above, 三日みっかおきに leaves three days empty and so happens once every four days - but a large minority of native speakers use it to mean once every three days. Everyone agrees about 一日いちにちおき ("every other day"); past that, confidence drops.

～おきに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The practical consequence is that when it genuinely matters, Japanese avoids the word. A pharmacist writes 二日ふつかに一回いっかい, a timetable writes 十分じゅっぷん間隔かんかく, and a coach says 中なか一日いちにちあけて. If you are giving instructions someone has to follow, say how many times per period and skip おきに entirely. If you are merely describing a rhythm, おきに is exactly right.

Before a noun, use の: 一日いちにちおきの練習れんしゅう, 十分じゅっぷんおきのアナウンス. It is a noun-like word, not a な-adjective.

～おきに · おきに vs ごとに

Rule of thumb - ごとに names the cycle; おきに names the gap. So 三日みっかごとに is day 1, day 4, day 7 - and 三日みっかおきに is day 1, day 5, day 9.

二日ふつかごとに水みずをやります。
I water them every two days.

一日いちにちおきに水みずをやります。
I water them every other day.

Those two sentences describe the same schedule, which is the neatest way to feel the difference: a cycle of two days is a gap of one. Whenever you are unsure, convert to ごとに in your head and count.

～おきに · おきに vs ごとに (cont.)

With small units of time the two land in the same place - 十分じゅっぷんごとに and 十分じゅっぷんおきに both describe buses ten minutes apart - which is why the distinction seems to vanish and then reappear the moment you switch to days.

ごとに also does something おきに cannot: it attaches to ordinary nouns and to verbs, meaning "each". 人ひとごとに考かんがえ方かたが違ちがう ("everyone thinks differently"), 会あうごとに背せが高たかくなる ("he's taller every time I see him"). おきに needs a number and always will.

～おきに · おきに vs 毎～

Rule of thumb - 毎まい means every single one, no gap. おきに exists precisely to describe the gaps that 毎まい denies.

毎週まいしゅう日曜日にちようびにテニスをします。
I play tennis every Sunday.

一週間いっしゅうかんおきにテニスをします。
I play tennis every other week.

～おきに · おきに vs 毎～ (cont.)

The mechanical difference is just as important: 毎まい is a prefix on a bare unit - 毎日まいにち, 毎週まいしゅう, 毎月まいつき, 毎年まいとし, 毎回まいかい - and it will not take a number. ❌毎まい三日みっか does not exist; the Japanese for "every three days" has to be 三日みっかごとに or 二日ふつかおきに.

～おきに · おきに vs ～に一回

Rule of thumb - ～に一回いっかい counts occurrences inside a period and is never ambiguous. When you need to be understood exactly, use it instead of おきに.

一日いちにちおきに走はしっています。
I go running every other day.

二日ふつかに一回いっかい走はしっています。
I go running once every two days.

Again the same schedule, said two ways. The second phrasing has no gap-versus-cycle trap in it at all, which is why hospitals, schools and instruction manuals prefer it. おきに is more compact and sounds more like a rhythm you live by; に一回 is what you write when someone will be counting.

～おきに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 毎まい三日みっか掃除そうじします。 → ✅ 三日みっかごとに掃除そうじします。
    毎まい cannot take a number. Use ごとに for a cycle, or 二日ふつかおきに for the same schedule expressed as a gap.

  • ❌ 一日いちにちおきに学校がっこうへ行いっています。（meaning "every day"） → ✅ 毎日まいにち学校がっこうへ行いっています。
    おきに always inserts a gap. If nothing is skipped, the word you want is 毎日まいにち.

  • ❌ おきに十分じゅっぷん電車でんしゃが来きます。 → ✅ 十分じゅっぷんおきに電車でんしゃが来きます。
    おきに is a suffix. The quantity always comes first.

  • ❌ 一日いちにちおきな練習れんしゅうはつらいです。 → ✅ 一日いちにちおきの練習れんしゅうはつらいです。
    It behaves like a noun, so it links to the next noun with の, not な.

～おきに · Practice 1

1. 花はなに水みずをやるのは____おきに、つまり二日ふつかに一回いっかいで十分じゅうぶんです。

花はなに水みずをやるのは**一日いちにち**おきに…

Once every two days means one whole day is skipped between waterings, and a gap of one day is 一日いちにちおきに. The second half of the sentence is the conversion rule in miniature: a gap of one equals a cycle of two.

～おきに · Practice 2

2. バスは（十分じゅっぷんおきに・毎まい十分じゅっぷん）出でています。

バスは**十分じゅっぷんおきに**出でています。

毎まい attaches only to a bare unit - 毎日まいにち, 毎朝まいあさ - and never to a number, so ❌毎まい十分じゅっぷん is not a possible word.

～おきに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Benches are placed every hundred metres along the river."

川かわに沿そって、百ひゃくメートルおきにベンチが置おいてあります。

Distance is a measured quantity, so a gap of a hundred metres simply means the benches are a hundred metres apart - no skipping involved. おきに attaches straight to the quantity.

～おきに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 毎まい二週間にしゅうかん、部長ぶちょうと面談めんだんがあります。

✅ 一週間いっしゅうかんおきに、部長ぶちょうと面談めんだんがあります。 （or 二週間にしゅうかんごとに）

毎まい refuses numbers. Expressed as a gap it is 一週間いっしゅうかんおきに; expressed as a cycle it is 二週間にしゅうかんごとに. Both describe the same fortnightly meeting.

～おきに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 三日みっかおきに通かよっています。 (b) 三日みっかごとに通かよっています。

By the rule, (a) leaves three days empty between visits, so you go once every four days; (b) names a three-day cycle, so you go once every three days. They are not the same schedule, and (a) is the sparser one.

In practice, be careful with (a). Speakers vary on how they count おきに with days, and plenty of people would hear (a) as meaning (b). If the number has to be right, say 四日よっかに一回いっかい and remove the doubt.

～始める

Meaning · start doing something; begin to

始はじめる welded onto the stem of another verb marks the moment an action gets under way. It is the plain, unremarkable "start": no surprise, no drama, and equally usable for something you planned, something you decided on the spot, and a slow change in the weather.

～始める · Formation

Attach 始はじめる to the ます-stem. The result is a single compound verb, and it conjugates as an ordinary る-verb.

～始める · Examples

It's six, so I'm going to start making dinner.

Now that it's May, it's started being hot from the morning on.

～始める · Examples (cont.)

子こどもたちが一人ひとりずつ教室きょうしつに入はいり**始はじめ**た。
The children started filing into the classroom one by one.

Once he starts talking he doesn't stop, so keep an eye on the clock.

My grandfather is out of hospital and started walking a little from last week.

～始める · Nuance & register

Compound verbs of this shape are register-neutral. 書かき始はじめる is as ordinary in a text message as in a report, and the politeness of the sentence is decided at the very end - 書かき始はじめた versus 書かき始はじめました.

What makes 始はじめる the workhorse is that it has no attitude. It does not say the start was sudden, welcome, overdue or surprising; it just puts a mark on the timeline. That neutrality is also why it can appear in forms its rival ～出だす cannot: you can request one (書かき始はじめてください), propose one (食たべ始はじめましょう), or plan one (来月らいげつから通かよい始はじめます).

～始める · Nuance & register (cont.)

With する-nouns there are two routes and they are not quite the same. 勉強べんきょうし始はじめる treats the studying as an activity that has now begun; 勉強べんきょうを始はじめる treats it as a thing you have taken up. For events with a fixed shape - a meeting, a lesson, a ceremony - only the second works: you say 会議かいぎを始はじめます, never ❌会議かいぎし始はじめます.

In speech you will also hear the whole idea carried by 始はじめた on its own - もう始はじめた? - where context supplies the verb. That is ellipsis, not a different pattern.

～始める · ～始める vs ～出す

Rule of thumb - 始はじめる is a neutral start; 出だす is a sudden, unplanned one. If you chose to do it, or you are asking someone else to, it has to be 始はじめる.

来月らいげつから水泳すいえいを習ならい**始はじめ**ます。
I'm starting swimming lessons next month.

後うしろの犬いぬが急きゅうに走はしり**出だし**た。
The dog behind us suddenly took off running.

The first is a decision announced in advance, and ❌習ならい出だします would be strange - you cannot schedule a surprise. The second is an event nobody controlled, which is exactly 出だす's territory.

～始める · ～始める vs ～出す (cont.)

The clearest test is the form of the verb. Requests, invitations and volitionals are closed to 出だす: ❌書かき出だしてください, ❌歌うたい出だそう. Their 始はじめる versions are unremarkable.

～始める · ～始める vs 始まる

Rule of thumb - 始はじめる needs somebody doing the starting; 始はじまる is the thing starting by itself. The pair splits exactly as 開あける／開あく does.

九時くじにミーティングを**始はじめ**ます。
We'll start the meeting at nine. (someone is starting it)

ミーティングは九時くじに**始はじまり**ます。
The meeting starts at nine. (that's the schedule)

～始める · ～始める vs 始まる (cont.)

Note the particles: を with 始はじめる, が／は with 始はじまる. The compound suffix built on the first of these is the only one that exists - there is no ❌読よみ始はじまる. If you want to say a process got under way on its own, either use 始はじめる anyway (雨あめが降ふり始はじめた) or reach for 出だす.

～始める · ～始める vs ～てくる

Rule of thumb - 始はじめる points at the starting line; ～てくる traces a change moving towards now. Both fit gradual change, and they emphasise different halves of it.

葉はが赤あかくなり**始はじめ**ました。
The leaves have started turning red.

葉はが赤あかくなってきました。
The leaves have been turning red.

～始める · ～始める vs ～てくる (cont.)

The first says the process has a beginning and we have just passed it. The second says the change has been accumulating and here is where it has got to. In everyday speech about weather and seasons, ～てくる is the more common of the two, and the two happily combine: 寒さむくなり始はじめてきた is heard, though it is doing the job twice over.

～始める · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 本ほんを読よむ**始はじめる** → ✅ 本ほんを読よみ**始はじめる**
    The suffix attaches to the ます-stem, never to the dictionary form: 読よみます → 読よみ + 始はじめる.

  • ❌ 手紙てがみを書かき**始はじまりました。 → ✅ 手紙てがみを書かき始はじめ*ました。
    始はじまる is intransitive and cannot take the を object. The compound suffix is always 始はじめる.*

  • ❌ 来週らいしゅうから運動うんどう始はじめます。 → ✅ 来週らいしゅうから運動うんどうを始はじめます。／運動うんどうし始はじめます。
    A する-noun needs either を in front of the independent verb, or し to make the compound. It cannot sit bare against 始はじめる.

～始める · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 寒さむい**始はじめました。 → ✅ 寒さむくなり**始はじめました。
    Adjectives have no ます-stem. Turn the adjective into the verb of change first - 寒さむくなる - and attach the suffix to that.

～始める · Practice 1

咲さきます gives the stem 咲さき, and 始はじめる bolts straight onto it. Blossom opening over several days is a gradual process, which is exactly what this suffix wants.

～始める · Practice 3

～始める · Practice 4

The dictionary form cannot carry the suffix. Take the ます-stem 食たべ and attach 始はじめる to it; the invitation ending ましょう then goes on the compound as a whole.

～始める · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) みんなが歩あるき始はじめた。 (b) みんなが歩あるき出だした。

(a) simply records that the walking started. It fits a group that had been waiting for a signal, or a hike that begins at the planned hour - nothing about it is remarkable.

(b) adds abruptness. Something set them off, or they moved before you expected it, and the sentence carries a flicker of surprise. That extra colour is why 出だす cannot be used to announce your own plans or to ask someone to get going.

～出す

Meaning · suddenly start doing; burst out doing

出だす on the stem of another verb says the action broke out. Where ～始はじめる merely marks a beginning, ～出だす adds abruptness: nobody saw it coming, nobody arranged it, and often nobody could have stopped it.

～出す · Formation

Attach 出だす to the ます-stem. The compound is a う-verb, so it conjugates like 話はなす.

～出す · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ます-stem + 出だす 降ふります → 降ふり出だす
Verb (る-verb) ます-stem + 出だす 逃にげます → 逃にげ出だす

❌ Invitation / volitional ❌歌うたい出だそう ✅歌うたい始はじめよう
❌ Instantaneous verb ❌消きえ出だす ✅急きゅうに消きえる

Attaches to Form Example
❌ ❌ dictionary form + 出だす ❌読よむ出だす

Rule of thumb - 出だす removes the plan. Because of that, it cannot appear in any form that assumes control: no ください, no ましょう, no volitional, and no announcement of what you intend to do next week.

～出す · Formation (cont.)

Note - like 始はじめる, this suffix needs a verb with some duration. Something that is over in an instant has no onset to mark: ❌電気でんきが消きえ出だした. Say 急きゅうに消きえた instead, and keep 出だす for things that go on once they have started - rain, crying, running, arguing.

～出す · Examples

赤あかちゃんが急きゅうに**泣なき出だし**た。
The baby suddenly burst into tears.

外そとを見みたら、雪ゆきが**降ふり出だし**ていた。
I looked outside and it had started snowing.

先生せんせいの話はなしがおもしろくて、クラスのみんなが**笑わらい出だし**た。
The teacher's story was so funny the whole class burst out laughing.

バスが**動うごき出だし**てから、かばんを忘わすれたことに気きがついた。
It was only after the bus pulled away that I realised I'd left my bag behind.

～出す · Examples (cont.)

彼女かのじょが何なにも言いわずに**走はしり出だし**たので、みんな驚おどろきました。
She broke into a run without a word, and everyone was taken aback.

静しずかだった教室きょうしつが、ベルの音おとで一度いちどに**騒さわぎ出だし**た。
The classroom had been silent, and at the bell it erupted all at once.

夜中よなかに隣となりの犬いぬが**鳴なき出だし**て、朝あさまで眠ねむれませんでした。
The dog next door started barking in the night and I couldn't sleep till morning.

会議かいぎが終おわりそうになったころ、田中たなかさんが急きゅうに反対はんたいだと**言いい出だし**て、また一時間いちじかんかかりました。
Just as the meeting was winding down, Tanaka suddenly came out against it, and it took another hour.

～出す · Nuance & register

The suffix is register-neutral - it is as at home in a novel as in a complaint to a friend - but it is never emotionally neutral. Something about the event was unexpected, and the speaker is registering that. This is why 出だす collects the same adverbs over and over: 急きゅうに, いきなり, 突然とつぜん.

The loss of control has one consequence learners rarely anticipate: used about your own deliberate action, 出だす sounds as if you surprised yourself. 気きがついたら書かき出だしていた ("I looked up and I was already writing") is a good sentence; ❌ 明日あしたから書かき出だします is not, because you cannot schedule your own outburst.

～出す · Nuance & register (cont.)

言いい出だす deserves separate mention. It means "to come out with", "to be the one who raises it", and it carries a whiff of blame: 言いい出だしたのは君きみだよ - "you're the one who brought it up." That is the suffix doing exactly its usual job, applied to speech.

Finally, be aware that a handful of very common verbs ending in 出だす are fully lexicalised and have nothing to do with suddenness: 思おもい出だす (remember), 取とり出だす (take out), 引ひき出だす (draw out), 貸かし出だす (lend out), 見みつけ出だす (track down). You learn these as words, not as this pattern.

～出す · ～出す vs ～始める

Rule of thumb - same slot, different attitude. 始はじめる is available in every form of the verb; 出だす is available only where nobody is in charge.

来週らいしゅうから朝あさのジョギングを**始はじめ**ます。
I'm taking up jogging in the mornings from next week.

信号しんごうが青あおになって、車くるまが一斉いっせいに**走はしり出だし**た。
The lights went green and the cars all pulled away together.

The first announces a plan; 出だす cannot go there. The second describes something that simply happened, and 走はしり始はじめた would drain the moment of its snap.

～出す · ～出す vs ～始める (cont.)

When the same verb takes either suffix, the difference is a threshold. 泣なき始はじめた reports that crying commenced; 泣なき出だした shows you the face crumpling.

～出す · ～出す vs 急に・いきなり

Rule of thumb - 急きゅうに is an adverb that can modify any event. 出だす is a suffix that can only mark a beginning. They stack, and neither replaces the other.

急きゅうに電気でんきが消きえた。
The lights suddenly went out.

急きゅうに雨あめが**降ふり出だし**た。
It suddenly started to rain.

～出す · ～出す vs 急に・いきなり (cont.)

Going out is instantaneous, so there is no onset for 出だす to mark and only the adverb is possible. Raining lasts, so both work - and the second sentence uses both at once, which is completely normal and not redundant: the adverb says it was sudden, the suffix says this was the start of something that then went on.

～出す · 出す "suddenly begin" vs 出す "outwards"

Rule of thumb - if the first verb is about handling or moving something, 出だす is probably the literal "out". If it is about an activity that can start, it is the aspect marker.

ポケットからかぎを**取とり出だし**た。
I took the key out of my pocket.

犬いぬが庭にわから**逃にげ出だし**た。
The dog got out of the garden and ran off.

Nothing in the first sentence is sudden - the key came out, and that is all the suffix is contributing. The second sits on the border and carries a little of both: escaping is outward motion, and it is also something that happened without warning.

～出す · 出す "suddenly begin" vs 出す "outwards" (cont.)

The one to watch is 思おもい出だす. It means remember, not "suddenly begin to think" - the memory comes out, it does not start. Treat it as a single word.

～出す · Common mistakes

  • ❌ みなさん、そろそろ歌うたい**出だしてください。 → ✅ みなさん、そろそろ歌うたい始はじめ**てください。
    A request assumes the listener is in control, which is precisely what 出だす denies.

  • ❌ 来年らいねんからピアノを習ならい**出だします。 → ✅ 来年らいねんからピアノを習ならい始はじめ**ます。
    You cannot schedule a surprise. Plans announced in advance take 始はじめる.

  • ❌ 本ほんを読よむ**出だす** → ✅ 本ほんを読よみ**出だす**
    The suffix goes on the ます-stem, exactly as 始はじめる does.

  • ❌ 急きゅうに電気でんきが消きえ**出だし*た。 → ✅ 急きゅうに電気でんきが消きえた。
    消きえる happens in an instant, so it has no beginning for the suffix to mark. The adverb alone carries the suddenness.*

This is a plan, stated in advance, about your own action. 出だす is blocked in exactly that situation, because it means the action broke out of its own accord.

～出す · Practice 5

急に

Meaning · suddenly, all at once - and, of an arrangement, at short notice

急きゅうに marks a change that arrived with no warning. Its second job is quieter and just as common: applied to plans and appointments it means unexpectedly, at the last minute, which is why it opens so many apologies.

急に · Usage

急きゅうに is the adverbial form of the な-adjective 急きゅうだ. That matters in practice, because in front of a noun it has to change shape: 急きゅうな用事ようじ ("something urgent that has come up"), 急きゅうな話はなし ("short notice"), 急きゅうな坂さか ("a steep hill" - the same word, applied to a slope). Only the に form modifies a verb.

Its position in the sentence is free; most often it sits immediately before the verb, or at the head of the clause when it colours the whole event. It clusters around three kinds of predicate:

  • change - 急きゅうに暗くらくなる,
    急きゅうに静しずかになる, 急きゅうに変かわる
  • starting and stopping - 急きゅうに降ふり出だす,
    急きゅうに止とまる, 急きゅうに消きえる
  • arrangements - 急きゅうに決きまる,
    急きゅうに休やすむ, 急きゅうに呼よばれる

急に · Usage (cont.)

It does not take degree adverbs. ❌とても急きゅうに is not said - suddenness is not a quantity you can have more of.

The word to keep separate is 急いそいで, the て-form of the verb 急いそぐ. Same kanji, different reading, opposite kind of meaning: 急きゅうに is about the event surprising you, 急いそいで is about you hurrying.

急に · Examples

空そらが**急きゅうに**暗くらくなった。
The sky went dark all of a sudden.

電車でんしゃが**急きゅうに**止とまって、びっくりしました。
The train stopped suddenly and gave me a fright.

急きゅうに用事ようじができたので、今日きょうの約束やくそくはキャンセルさせてください。
Something's come up out of the blue, so please let me cancel today's plans.

先月せんげつまで元気げんきだった祖父そふが**急きゅうに**入院にゅういんすることになって、家族かぞくみんなで病院びょういんに通かよっています。
My grandfather was fine until last month and has suddenly ended up in hospital, so the whole family has been going in to see him.

急に · Nuance & register

急きゅうに is register-neutral and extremely frequent. It is equally at home in a weather report and in a text to a friend, and it carries no politeness of its own.

Its most useful social function is as the opening of an apology. When you have to change something at the last minute, Japanese says so first and explains afterwards: 急きゅうにすみません among friends, 急きゅうで申もうし訳わけありませんが at work, 急きゅうなお願ねがいで恐縮きょうしゅくですが in a formal email. In all three the word is doing the same thing: acknowledging that the other person has had no time to prepare. Leaving it out makes the same request sound inconsiderate.

急に · Nuance & register (cont.)

Watch the shape when a noun follows. 急きゅうだ is a な-adjective, so it is 急きゅうな仕事しごと, 急きゅうな変更へんこう, 急きゅうな出張しゅっちょう. The related noun 急いそぎ exists too and behaves differently: 急いそぎの用事ようじ joins with の, and it means "a job that must be done fast" rather than "a job that came up unexpectedly".

急に · 急に vs 突然・いきなり

Rule of thumb - all three mean "suddenly". 急きゅうに is the everyday default; 突然とつぜん is stronger and more written; いきなり complains that there was no lead-up at all.

急きゅうに会議かいぎが入はいりました。
A meeting's been put in my diary at short notice.

突然とつぜん、部屋へやの電気でんきが消きえた。
All at once the lights in the room went out.

いきなり大声おおごえで話はなしかけられて、こわかった。
Someone started shouting at me out of nowhere, and it was frightening.

急に · 急に vs 突然・いきなり (cont.)

突然とつぜん is dramatic. It suits accidents, disappearances and news, and it reads as slightly literary in ordinary chat. いきなり is the most colloquial and focuses on the missing preliminaries - no greeting, no warning, no run-up - which is why it so often carries irritation.

急きゅうに is the only one of the three that comfortably covers scheduling. 急きゅうに休やすみます is the normal way to say you are taking unplanned leave; ❌突然とつぜん休やすみます would sound as though you had vanished.

急に · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 急きゅうに来きてください。（meaning "come quickly"） → ✅ 急いそいで来きてください。／すぐに来きてください。
    急きゅうに describes an event that surprises people. Telling someone to hurry needs 急いそいで or すぐに.

  • ❌ 急きゅうに用事ようじがあるので、失礼しつれいします。 → ✅ 急きゅうな用事ようじがあるので、失礼しつれいします。
    In front of a noun the な-adjective needs its な. The に form can only modify a verb.

  • ❌ バスがとても急きゅうに止とまった。 → ✅ バスが急きゅうに止とまった。
    Suddenness has no degrees, so とても has nothing to intensify. If you want the event to sound more violent, change the verb, not the adverb.

変かわる is a verb of change, which is where this adverb lives, and the point of the sentence is that the change gives you no warning. The に form is required because a verb follows.

急に · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 急きゅうに出でかけました。 (b) 急いそいで出でかけました。

Same kanji, two different words. (a) is 急きゅうに - the leaving was unplanned. Something came up and you went out when nobody expected you to, and the sentence says nothing about how fast you moved.

～続ける

Meaning · keep doing; go on doing without stopping

続つづける welded onto a verb stem is the mirror image of ～始はじめる. Where that one marks the moment something starts, this one claims the stretch after it: the action ran on, and it did not break off. Very often the sentence is also saying that this took some doing.

～続ける · Formation

Note - the suffix covers two shapes of continuation, and both are normal. One is an unbroken action: 二時間にじかん話はなし続つづけた. The other is a habit kept up over a long period: 十年じゅうねん通かよい続つづけている. What it cannot do is attach to something over in an instant - ❌着つき続つづける of one arrival has nothing to stretch.

～続ける · Examples

彼かれは一時間いちじかんも**話はなし続つづけ**た。
He talked for a solid hour.

駅えきの前まえで三十分さんじゅっぷんも**待まち続つづけ**ました。
I stood waiting outside the station for a full half hour.

毎日まいにち少すこしずつ**練習れんしゅうし続つづけ**れば、きっと上手じょうずになりますよ。
If you keep practising a little every day, you're bound to get good at it.

祖母そぼは六十年ろくじゅうねん同おなじ店みせで**働はたらき続つづけ**ています。
My grandmother has been working in the same shop for sixty years.

～続ける · Examples (cont.)

足あしが痛いたくても、ゴールまで**走はしり続つづけ**た。
My legs hurt, but I kept running all the way to the finish.

外そとが暗くらくなっても、兄あには机つくえに向むかって**書かき続つづけ**ていた。
Even after it got dark outside, my brother stayed at his desk writing.

野菜やさいの値段ねだんが**上あがり続つづけ**ているので、買かい物ものが大変たいへんです。
Vegetable prices just keep going up, so shopping is hard work.

返事へんじが来こなくても、彼女かのじょは毎週まいしゅう友ともだちに手紙てがみを**送おくり続つづけ**ました。
Even though no reply ever came, she went on sending her friend a letter every week.

～続ける · Nuance & register

The suffix is register-neutral, but it is rarely emotionally flat. Because it insists that something did not stop, it tends to pick up either admiration or exasperation from the surrounding sentence. 夢ゆめを追おい続つづける is a compliment; 文句もんくを言いい続つづける is not. That is context, not grammar - but it is why the word is so common in speeches and so common in complaints.

The form you will use most is ～続つづけている, which is how Japanese says "have been doing X for N years": 二十年にじゅうねん住すみ続つづけています. The plain past ～続つづけた closes the stretch off, so it belongs to stories about finished periods.

～続ける · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two neighbours worth knowing. 続つづけて works as a bare adverb meaning "in a row" or "carry on": 三日みっか続つづけて雨あめが降ふった, どうぞ続つづけてください. And with a する-noun, spoken Japanese often prefers the independent verb - 練習れんしゅうを続つづける rather than 練習れんしゅうし続つづける - especially when the activity has a name and a shape.

～続ける · ～続ける vs ～続く

Rule of thumb - if a person is keeping it going, 続つづける. If it simply goes on, 続つづく. The particles follow: を with the first, が with the second.

雨あめが三日みっかも**降ふり続つづい**ている。
It's been raining for three days straight.

息子むすこは夜中よなかまでゲームをし続つづけていた。
My son carried on playing games until the middle of the night.

With weather and other things nobody controls, both suffixes are heard, and 降ふり続つづく is the more usual - it is what a forecast says. With a human agent only 続つづける is possible; there is no ❌話はなし続つづく.

～続ける · ～続ける vs ～続く (cont.)

The independent verbs split the same way, and this is the pair to memorise:

会議かいぎを**続つづけ**ましょう。
Let's carry on with the meeting.

会議かいぎは六時ろくじまで**続つづき**ました。
The meeting went on until six.

～続ける · ～続ける vs ～ている

Rule of thumb - ている says the action is under way; 続つづける says it has not stopped. The first is a snapshot, the second is a claim about the whole stretch.

今いま、レポートを書かいています。
I'm writing the report right now.

朝あさから同おなじレポートを**書かき続つづけ**ています。
I've been writing the same report since this morning.

～続ける · ～続ける vs ～ている (cont.)

Both are true of the same moment, and only the second complains. Note also what ている can do that this suffix cannot: it covers habits (毎朝まいあさ走はしっています) and resulting states (結婚けっこんしています). ❌結婚けっこんし続つづけています is not a way to say you are married - it would mean you keep getting married.

～続ける · ～続ける vs ずっと

Rule of thumb - ずっと is an adverb you drop into any sentence; 続つづける is a suffix that changes the verb. They stack happily, and ずっと reaches places the suffix cannot.

子こどものころからずっとサッカーが好すきです。
I've loved football ever since I was a child.

好すき is not a verb, so there is no stem for 続つづける to attach to; ずっと has no such problem and works with adjectives, nouns and states alike. It also does duty as "a long way back" - ずっと前まえに会あいました - which has nothing to do with continuing at all.

～続ける · ～続ける vs ずっと (cont.)

Where both are available, ずっと reports the span while 続つづける insists on the unbrokenness, and putting them together is completely normal: ずっと待まち続つづけていました.

～続ける · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 本ほんを読よむ**続つづける** → ✅ 本ほんを読よみ**続つづける**
    The suffix takes the ます-stem, never the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 会議かいぎを続つづきます。 → ✅ 会議かいぎを**続つづけます。／会議かいぎが続つづき*ます。
    続つづく is intransitive and cannot take a を object. Pick the verb that matches the particle.*

  • ❌ 夜中よなかまで勉強べんきょう続つづけました。 → ✅ 夜中よなかまで勉強べんきょうし続つづけました。
    A する-noun needs the し to make the compound - or use the independent verb with を: 勉強べんきょうを続つづけました.

～続ける · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この町まちに十年じゅうねん住すみ**続つづけます。（meaning "I've lived here ten years"） → ✅ この町まちに十年じゅうねん住すみ続つづけ**ています。
    A stretch that reaches the present needs ている. The plain 続つづけます is a promise about the future.

You are asking a person to keep going, and the を marks what they are keeping going - both point to the transitive verb. 続つづく would need 話はなしが続つづく, "the conversation carries on by itself", which nobody can be asked to do.

～終わる

Meaning · finish doing something; get to the end of it

終おわる on a verb stem closes the bracket that ～始はじめる opened. It says the action ran its course and reached its natural end - the book was read to the last page, the meal was eaten, the letter was written and signed.

～終わる · Formation

Hand your composition in as soon as you've finished writing it.

洗濯せんたくをし終おわったら、ベランダに干ほしに行いきます。
Once I've finished the washing I'll go and hang it out on the balcony.

～終わる · Examples (cont.)

話はなしを**聞きき終おわっ**てから、ゆっくり考かんがえましょう。
Let's hear the whole story out and then think it over properly.

映画えいがを最後さいごまで**見み終おわっ**たときには、もう夜中よなかの一時いちじだった。
By the time I'd watched the film to the end it was already one in the morning.

試験しけんの問題もんだいを全部ぜんぶ解とき終おわったのに、名前なまえを書かくのを忘わすれてしまいました。
I got through every question on the exam and then forgot to write my name.

荷物にもつを**片付かたづけ終おわっ**たら、近ちかくの喫茶店きっさてんでコーヒーでも飲のみませんか。
When we've finished putting everything away, shall we get a coffee somewhere nearby?

～終わる · Nuance & register

The suffix is register-neutral and enormously frequent, above all in the form ～終おわったら, which is how Japanese sequences the tasks of an ordinary day.

Its close relative ～終おえる means the same thing with a different feel. 終おえる is genuinely transitive and sounds more deliberate and slightly more written: 読よみ終おえる suggests you set out to get through the thing and did. 読よみ終おわる just notes that the reading came to an end. In conversation 終おわる is far more common; in a report or an essay 終おえる often reads better.

～終わる · Nuance & register (cont.)

Around the independent verbs the picture is worth keeping straight. 終おわる is basically intransitive - 会議かいぎが終おわる - and its everyday transitive partners are 終おわらせる ("get it finished") and 終おえる ("bring it to a close"). There is one place where 終おわる does take を, and you will hear it constantly: the fixed announcement これで授業じゅぎょうを終おわります, 本日ほんじつはこれで終おわります. Careful writers prefer 終おわりにします or 終了しゅうりょうします, but the を version is standard spoken Japanese in that formula.

～終わる · ～終わる vs ～終える

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different temperature. 終おわる is the neutral everyday form; 終おえる is transitive, deliberate, and at home in writing.

レポートを書かき**終おわり**ました。
I've finished writing the report.

レポートを書かき**終おえ**ました。
I have completed the report.

The second sounds like a person reporting an achievement, and it would be the better choice in an email to a client. Between friends it is faintly formal.

～終わる · ～終わる vs ～終える (cont.)

The one thing 終おえる cannot do is describe something ending by itself: ❌授業じゅぎょうが終おえる is impossible, because 終おえる always needs someone bringing the thing to an end.

～終わる · ～終わる vs ～てしまう

Rule of thumb - 終おわる is about reaching the end of a process. てしまう is about completion with an attitude - either "the whole lot" or "and I wish I hadn't".

ケーキを**食たべ終おわっ**てから、コーヒーを入いれた。
I made coffee after I'd finished the cake.

一人ひとりでケーキを食たべてしまった。
I went and ate the whole cake on my own.

～終わる · ～終わる vs ～てしまう (cont.)

The first is neutral sequencing. The second reports a completed act with a feeling attached - pride, or more often regret. てしまう also attaches happily to verbs with no duration at all (忘わすれてしまった, 落おとしてしまった), which is exactly where 終おわる is impossible.

～終わる · ～終わる vs 終わる・終わらせる

Rule of thumb - as a suffix it rides on another verb's stem. As an independent verb it needs a noun and が; to say that you finished something, the verb is 終おわらせる or 終おえる.

授業じゅぎょうは三時さんじに**終おわり**ます。
Class finishes at three.

今日きょう中じゅうにこの仕事しごとを**終おわらせ**ます。
I'll get this job finished before the day is out.

～終わる · ～終わる vs 終わる・終わらせる (cont.)

The first has no agent - three o'clock arrives and the class is over. The second has you in it, pushing. That is why a manager says 終おわらせてください and never ❌終おわってください when they mean "get it done"; 終おわってください would be asking the task to end of its own accord.

～終わる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 本ほんを読よむ**終おわる** → ✅ 本ほんを読よみ**終おわる**
    The suffix goes on the ます-stem, like 始はじめる and 続つづける.

  • ❌ 雨あめが降ふり**終おわっ**た。 → ✅ 雨あめがやんだ。
    Rain does not complete anything; it stops. Uncontrolled processes with no finish line take やむ, 止とまる or 終おわる as an independent verb.

  • ❌ 掃除そうじ終おわりました。 → ✅ 掃除そうじし終おわりました。／掃除そうじが終おわりました。
    A する-noun needs either し to build the compound, or が in front of the independent verb.

  • ❌ 駅えきに着つき**終おわっ**た。 → ✅ 駅えきに着ついた。
    Arriving takes no time, so there is no process to finish. The plain past says everything.

Writing a report is a process with a clear end, so the suffix fits, and ～終おわったら is the standard way to sequence "once you've finished X, do Y". 書かいたら alone would not insist that the report is complete.

3. Translate into Japanese: "Once I've finished reading this book, I'll lend it to you."

この本ほんを**読よみ終おわっ**たら、貸かしてあげます。

The を belongs to 読よむ and stays where it is when the suffix is added. 読よみ終おえる would also be correct and would sound a shade more formal.

～終わる · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 雨あめが降ふり終おわってから、散歩さんぽに行いきました。

✅ 雨あめがやんでから、散歩さんぽに行いきました。

Rain has no completion point, only a moment when it stops, so 終おわる cannot describe it. やむ is the verb for weather and noise coming to an end.

～終わる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) お菓子かしを食たべ終おわった。 (b) お菓子かしを食たべてしまった。

(a) says the eating ran to its end. It is neutral and factual, and it usually sets up whatever comes next - you have finished, so now you can do something else.

(b) adds an attitude. Depending on the context it means "I ate the lot" or "oh no, I've eaten them" - the sweets were somebody else's, or you were supposed to be saving them. The grammar is not reporting a finish line so much as a completed act you have feelings about.

～ているところ

Meaning · right in the middle of doing it - at this exact stage

ところ is the ordinary noun for "place". Put after ている, the place it names is on a timeline rather than a map: the point you have reached in an action that is under way. N5 gave you ている for "is doing"; ているところ narrows that to this stage, right now, neither before nor after.

～ているところ · Nuance & register

ているところです is the standard polite way to report status, and this is the single most useful thing to take from the page. On the phone, in an email, at a counter, 今いま確認かくにんしているところです ("I'm checking on that now") says far more than まだです: it tells the other person the work is actually under way, and it buys time without sounding evasive. Business Japanese leans on it heavily.

In casual speech the whole thing contracts. ている becomes てる and ところ becomes とこ: 今いま出でてるとこ, 向むかってるとこだよ. The だ usually disappears too. This is extremely common between friends and completely out of place in writing.

～ているところ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two constructions grow out of the same phrase. With に or へ, ところ sets up an interruption - something arrives at the stage you were at: 出でかけようとしているところに電話でんわが鳴なった. With を, it sets up being caught, usually by a verb of seeing or stopping: サボっているところを見みつかった. Both are worth recognising even before you produce them.

～ているところ · ～ているところ vs ～ている

Rule of thumb - ている is broad; ているところ is narrow. Adding ところ throws away the habit reading and the resulting-state reading and keeps only "in progress at this moment".

毎朝まいあさ六時ろくじに走はしっています。
I run at six every morning.

今いま走はしっているところなので、あとでかけ直なおします。
I'm out running at the moment, so I'll call you back.

～ているところ · ～ているところ vs ～ている (cont.)

The first is a habit, and ❌毎朝まいあさ走はしっているところです is impossible - a habit is not a stage you are standing in. The same goes for states: 窓まどが開あいています ("the window is open") describes a result, and ❌開あいているところです would be describing a window in the middle of opening itself.

Where both are possible, ところ is the emphatic one. It answers "have you done it yet?" with "I am doing it this second", and it is much harder to hear as an excuse.

～ているところ · 食べるところ vs 食べているところ vs 食べたところ

Rule of thumb - one noun, three stages. Dictionary form = about to. ている = in the middle of. た-form = just finished.

これから昼ひるごはんを食たべるところです。
I'm just about to have lunch.

今いま昼ひるごはんを食たべているところです。
I'm having lunch right now.

今いま昼ひるごはんを食たべたところです。
I've just finished lunch.

～ているところ · 食べるところ vs 食べているところ vs 食べたところ (cont.)

Nothing changes but the form of the verb, and the three sentences point at three different minutes. Learn them as a set: the same ところ is doing the same job in all three, and only the tense of the verb moves you along the timeline.

～ているところ · ～ているところ vs ～ている間に

Rule of thumb - ているところ describes the subject's own stage, and usually ends the sentence. 間あいだ opens a window during which something else happens - and the に decides whether the other event fills the window or falls inside it.

The に is the entire difference, and it is worth slowing down for:

母ははが寝ねている**間あいだ**、ずっとそばにいました。
I stayed beside my mother the whole time she was asleep.

母ははが寝ねている**間あいだに**、買かい物ものに行いってきました。
While my mother was asleep, I nipped out and did the shopping.

～ているところ · ～ているところ vs ～ている間に (cont.)

Without に, the second clause must last as long as the window - hence ずっと, and hence verbs like 待まっていた, 読よんでいた. With に, the second clause is a one-off event that fits somewhere inside the window, and it is typically something finishable: going out, making a call, tidying up. Swap them and both sentences go wrong - ❌寝ねている間あいだ、買かい物ものに行いった claims you shopped for the entire nap.

ているところ is doing something different again. It is not a time clause at all; it is a statement about where the subject has got to:

母ははは今いま寝ねているところです。
My mother is asleep just at the moment.

～ているところ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 毎日まいにち運動うんどうしているところです。 → ✅ 毎日まいにち運動うんどうしています。
    A habit has no "stage". ところ only works for something happening at the moment of speaking.

  • ❌ 窓まどが開あいているところです。 → ✅ 窓まどが開あいています。
    This ている is a resulting state, not an action in progress, and ところ cannot attach to it.

  • ❌ 今いま食たべるているところです。 → ✅ 今いま食たべているところです。
    The ている is built on the て-form, not the dictionary form. The dictionary form goes straight onto ところ and means "about to".

  • ❌ 父ちちは十年じゅうねん前まえから同おなじ会社かいしゃで働はたらいているところです。 → ✅ …働はたらいています。
    Ten years is not a stage you are standing in. Long-running situations take plain ている.

～ているところ · Practice 1

The writing is under way at this moment, which is exactly the stage ところ picks out. 書かくところです would mean you have not started yet, and 書かいたところです would mean it is already done - a very different answer to give your boss.

～ているところ · Practice 2

毎朝まいあさ makes this a habit, and ところ cannot express a habit - it names a single stage in a single action. Drop 毎朝まいあさ and put 今いま there instead, and the other option becomes the right one.

～ているところ · Practice 3

This is the standard status report. ているところです tells the other person the work is actually in motion, which is why it is the polite alternative to a bare まだです.

～ているところ · Practice 4

～ているところ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 姉あねが料理りょうりをしている間あいだ、私わたしは皿さらを洗あらっていました。 (b) 姉あねが料理りょうりをしている間あいだに、私わたしは買かい物ものに行いきました。

～ているところ · Practice 5 — answer

(a) has no に, so the two activities are the same length: for the whole time your sister was cooking, you were at the sink. Both clauses describe stretches, and neither has a single point attached to it.

(b) has に, which turns the cooking into a window and puts a one-off event inside it. The shopping trip started and finished somewhere in the middle of the cooking; it did not last the whole time and nobody claims it did.

That one syllable is the whole distinction. A verb that goes on (洗あらっていた, 待まっていた) wants 間あいだ; a verb that finishes (行いった, 電話でんわした) wants 間あいだに.

～ところ

Meaning · about to do; on the point of doing

With a verb in the dictionary form, ところ names the stage just before the action: you have not started, and you are about to. The same construction in the past - ～ところだった - becomes one of the most useful sentences in the language: "I very nearly did."

I was about to go home without hearing the explanation, but a colleague filled me in - a lucky escape.

～ところ · Nuance & register

In its present-tense use, ところです is a status report about the very near future, and it is the natural answer to 今いま何なにしてる? or もう出でた?. In casual speech it contracts to とこ and drops the copula: 今いま出でるとこ. Both registers are extremely common; what you will not hear is this pattern in a formal written notice, where a schedule would be stated with times instead.

The past form is where the real value is. ～ところだった means it nearly happened and it didn't, and it almost always carries relief. It collects a fixed set of adverbs - もう少すこしで, 危あやうく, あぶなく - and it is how Japanese says all of these:

～ところ · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 忘わすれるところでした - "I almost forgot."
  • 遅刻ちこくするところだった - "I nearly ended up late."
  • 買かってしまうところだった - "I almost bought it."

Note that the verb stays in the dictionary form even though the sentence is about the past. The past lives in だった／でした at the end; the verb has to stay un-happened, because the whole point is that it never happened.

One thing this pattern does not do is announce plans. It has a horizon of a few minutes. For anything further out, the words are つもり and 予定よてい.

～ところ · 出るところ vs 出たところ

Rule of thumb - the form of the verb walks you across the event. Dictionary form: not started. た-form: just finished. The ところ is identical in both.

今いま、家いえを出でるところです。
I'm just about to leave the house.

今いま、家いえを出でたところです。
I've just left the house.

～ところ · 出るところ vs 出たところ (cont.)

Said into a phone, these two put you in completely different places: still indoors, or already on the street. The middle member of the set - 出でているところ - is built on ている and covers the stretch in between.

This is why the form in front of ところ deserves more care than any other choice on this page. The word never changes; the verb carries all the information.

～ところ · ～ところだ vs ～つもり・～予定

Rule of thumb - ところ reaches a few minutes into the future. つもり is an intention and 予定よてい is a schedule, and both stretch as far as you like.

来月らいげつ、京都きょうとへ行いくつもりです。
I'm planning to go to Kyoto next month.

今いま駅えきへ行いくところです。
I'm on my way to the station this minute.

❌来月らいげつ京都きょうとへ行いくところです is wrong, and audibly so: ところ claims you are on the very brink of the action, and you cannot be on the brink of something a month away.

～ところ · ～ところだ vs ～つもり・～予定 (cont.)

The reverse substitution is not an error so much as a loss. 今いま行いくつもりです reports your intention and leaves open whether you have moved; 今いま行いくところです says you are already on your feet.

～ところ · ～ところだった vs ～そうになった

Rule of thumb - both mean "almost". ところだった is a whole situation you narrowly avoided; そうになった is a physical event that nearly happened to you.

もう少すこしで遅刻ちこくするところでした。
I very nearly ended up late.

階段かいだんで転ころびそうになりました。
I almost fell on the stairs.

～ところ · ～ところだった vs ～そうになった (cont.)

The mechanics differ too: そうになる attaches to the ます-stem (転ころび, 落おち, 泣なき), while ところ takes the dictionary form. And their coverage differs. そうになる wants something on the verge of physically occurring - falling, dropping, crying, crashing. ところだった is happy with outcomes you avoided by deciding differently: 危あやうく高たかいほうを買かうところだった, "I almost bought the expensive one." そうになる cannot say that.

～ところ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 今いま出でかけますところです。 → ✅ 今いま出でかけるところです。
    ところ is a noun, and a noun takes the plain form in front of it. The politeness is already there in です.

  • ❌ 来年らいねん、アメリカに留学りゅうがくするところです。 → ✅ 来年らいねん、アメリカに留学りゅうがくする**予定よてい**です。
    A year is far outside the range of this pattern. Distant plans take 予定よてい or つもり.

  • ❌ もう少すこしで財布さいふを忘わすれたところでした。 → ✅ もう少すこしで財布さいふを忘わすれるところでした。
    The verb stays in the dictionary form: the forgetting never happened. Only でした moves into the past.

～ところ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 今いま食たべているところだったので、後あとで行いきます。（meaning "I was about to eat"） → ✅ 今いま食たべるところだったので…
    ている puts you in the middle of the meal. If the food is untouched, the dictionary form is the one you want.

～たところ

With the verb in the た-form, ところ names the stage right after the action finished. The emphasis is on right: this is a claim about the clock, not about how fresh the event feels. Minutes ago, not last month.

I got back from the bank a while ago, and I've only just now handed in all the paperwork.

～たところ · Nuance & register

たところです is the polite way to report that something is done this instant, and it is a workplace staple. Asked whether you have sent the file, 今いま送おくったところです is better than 送おくりました because it tells the other person to look now. In casual speech it shortens to たとこ: ごめん、今いま起おきたとこ.

The construction is objective. It is measured against the clock, and it will not stretch. That is its whole personality, and it is exactly what separates it from たばかり, which is measured against how the speaker feels. Because of that, たところ has no emotional colour of its own: it neither excuses nor boasts, it just reports where on the timeline you are standing.

～たところ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two structural limits follow from the same fact. たところ cannot modify a noun - there is no ❌作つくったところの料理りょうり - because it describes a moment rather than a property of the thing. And it has no negative form: "I haven't eaten yet" is まだ食たべていません, never ❌食たべなかったところです.

For narration in the past, use たところだった: 部屋へやに入はいったとき、彼かれは電話でんわを切きったところだった.

～たところ · ～たところ vs ～たばかり

Rule of thumb - たところ measures with a clock; たばかり measures with a feeling. Both translate as "just", and only one of them will stretch to last year.

今いま駅えきに**着ついたところ**です。
I've just arrived at the station.

去年きょねん日本にほんに**来きたばかり**です。
I only came to Japan last year.

～たところ · ～たところ vs ～たばかり (cont.)

The second sentence is impossible with ところ: ❌去年きょねん来きたところです. A year is not a moment. ばかり survives it because the speaker is not reporting the clock - they are saying the arrival is still recent as far as they are concerned, which is why it works as an excuse for not knowing their way around.

The second difference is structural and easy to test. ばかり modifies nouns freely; ところ does not:

買かったばかりのパソコンが壊こわれてしまった。
The computer I'd only just bought has broken.

～たところ · ～たところ vs ～たばかり (cont.)

❌買かったところのパソコン is not Japanese. If a noun follows, ばかり is your only option.

～たところ · ～たところ vs ～たあとで

Rule of thumb - たところ is a statement about now and ends the sentence. ～たあとで is a connector and always has another clause after it.

仕事しごとが**終おわったところ**です。
I've just finished work.

仕事しごとが終おわったあとで、飲のみに行いきます。
After work I'm going out for a drink.

The first sentence is complete; the second would be unfinished without its second half. And they carry different information: あとで says nothing at all about how recent the event is, while たところ says nothing about what happens next.

～たところ · ～たところ vs ～たあとで (cont.)

When you do want to hang a following clause on たところ, it needs a particle - に, へ or で - and the meaning becomes "just as I had done X, Y happened": 席せきに座すわったところに先生せんせいが入はいってきた.

～たところ · ～たところだ vs ～たところ、…

Rule of thumb - with だ／です it means "just finished". Standing at a comma with no copula, it is a formal connective meaning "when I did X, it turned out that Y".

課長かちょうに**聞きいたところ**です。
I've just this minute asked the section chief.

課長かちょうに**聞きいたところ**、会議かいぎは中止ちゅうしになったそうです。
I asked the section chief, and apparently the meeting has been cancelled.

～たところ · ～たところだ vs ～たところ、… (cont.)

The connective version has three tells: no だ, a comma, and a second clause that reports something discovered as a result. It belongs to written and formal Japanese - reports, business email, news - and in speech people say 聞きいてみたら instead. You need to recognise it so that a sentence like the second one does not read to you as "I have just asked."

～たところ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 去年きょねんこの会社かいしゃに入はいったところです。 → ✅ 去年きょねんこの会社かいしゃに入はいったばかりです。
    A year ago is not "just now" on any clock. Subjective recency is ばかり's job.

  • ❌ これは先週せんしゅう買かったところの自転車じてんしゃです。 → ✅ これは先週せんしゅう買かったばかりの自転車じてんしゃです。
    ところ cannot modify a noun. When a noun follows, only ばかり works.

  • ❌ 今いま昼ひるごはんを食たべるところです。（meaning "I've just eaten"） → ✅ 今いま昼ひるごはんを食たべたところです。
    The dictionary form means you have not started. One character decides whether the food is gone.

～たところ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ まだ返事へんじが来こなかったところです。 → ✅ まだ返事へんじが来きていません。
    There is no negative version of this pattern. "Not yet" is まだ～ていません.

～たところ · Practice 1

1. 「もう昼ひるごはんを食たべましたか。」「ええ、今いま食たべ____ところです。」

ええ、今いま食たべたところです。

The た-form places you immediately after the meal, which is what 今いま and the question both call for. 食たべるところです would mean the food is still untouched, and would answer the question with a flat no.

～たところ · Practice 2

2. 三年さんねん前まえに大学だいがくを卒業そつぎょうした（ところ・ばかり）です。

三年さんねん前まえに大学だいがくを卒業そつぎょうしたばかりです。

Three years cannot be squeezed into ところ, which is measured on the clock. ばかり reports how recent it feels to the speaker - here, that they still count as a new graduate.

～たところ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The train has just left, so the next one is in ten minutes."

電車でんしゃは今いま出でたところなので、次つぎは十分じゅっぷん後ごです。

The departure happened seconds ago and the sentence is about the clock, so たところ is exactly right. 出でたばかり would also be understood but would sound like a complaint about your luck rather than a timetable fact.

～たところ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: これは先月せんげつ直なおしたところのテレビです。

✅ これは先月せんげつ直なおしたばかりのテレビです。

Two problems, one fix. ところ cannot sit in front of a noun, and last month is too far back for it anyway. ばかり handles both: it takes の before a noun and it stretches to cover anything the speaker still thinks of as recent.

～たところ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 日本にほんに来きたところです。 (b) 日本にほんに来きたばかりです。

(a) means you have this moment arrived - you are at the airport, or you have just walked out of it, and the suitcase is still in your hand. It is a statement about the clock and it will not stretch past a few minutes.

(b) means you are new here. It might be a week, it might be six months; what it reports is that the arrival is still recent from where you stand. This is the one you use to explain why your Japanese is shaky or why you got lost, and (a) would be strange in that role.

～たばかり

Meaning · only just did it - recent as far as the speaker is concerned

たばかり also translates as "just", but it is measuring something quite different from たところ. ところ reads the clock. ばかり reads the speaker: it says the event is still recent to them, and that judgement stretches. Five minutes, five weeks, sometimes five years - as long as the speaker still feels new to it.

～たばかり · Formation

～たばかり · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
❌ Dictionary form ❌来くるばかりです ✅来きたばかりです

Rule of thumb - recency here is relative to the thing. A bus that left five minutes ago, a job started last month and a building put up five years ago can all take ばかり, because "new" means something different for each. That flexibility is exactly what たところ refuses.

Note - the noun-modifying form is the practical reason to learn this pattern. 生うまれたばかりの赤あかちゃん, 習ならったばかりの漢字かんじ, 買かったばかりの靴くつ - all completely ordinary, and none of them can be built with ところ. Do not drop the の.

～たばかり · Examples

さっき昼ひるごはんを食たべたばかりです。
I only had lunch a little while ago.

この会社かいしゃに入はいったばかりで、まだ何なにもわかりません。
I've only just joined this company, so I don't know anything yet.

焼やいたばかりのパンはやっぱりおいしいね。
Bread straight out of the oven really is good, isn't it.

生うまれたばかりの赤あかちゃんは、一日いちにち中じゅう寝ねています。
A newborn baby sleeps all day long.

～たばかり · Examples (cont.)

習ならったばかりの漢字かんじを、もう忘わすれてしまいました。
I've already forgotten the kanji I only just learned.

去年きょねん買かったばかりのパソコンなのに、もう動うごかなくなった。
I only bought this computer last year and it's already stopped working.

この町まちに引ひっ越こしてきたばかりなので、この辺へんのことはまだよく知しらないんです。
I've only just moved to this town, so I don't really know the area yet.

日本にほんに来きたばかりのころは、コンビニで買かい物ものをするだけでも緊張きんちょうしました。
When I'd only just arrived in Japan, even shopping in a convenience store made me nervous.

～たばかり · Nuance & register

たばかり is register-neutral, and its most characteristic job is explaining yourself. ～たばかりなので is how Japanese excuses inexperience, disorder or ignorance without apologising outright: 始はじめたばかりなので、まだ下手へたです, 引ひっ越こしたばかりで、部屋へやが片付かたづいていません. It buys the speaker some room, and because the recency is subjective, nobody argues with it.

In casual speech it lengthens rather than shortens: ばっかり and ばっか are both extremely common - 今いま起おきたばっかりだよ - and both are firmly spoken-only.

～たばかり · Nuance & register (cont.)

Be aware that the same word ばかり has a much more frequent meaning, "only, nothing but": 肉にくばかり食たべる, 文句もんくばかり言いう, 寝ねてばかりいる. The two are told apart by what comes in front. A た-form gives you "just did"; a noun or a て-form gives you "nothing but". Getting this wrong produces a sentence that means the opposite of what you intended, so it is worth a moment's care.

～たばかり · ～たばかり vs ～たところ

Rule of thumb - ところ is measured on the clock; ばかり is measured by the speaker. In the few minutes after an event both are available; outside that window only ばかり survives.

入学にゅうがくしたばかりなので、友ともだちがまだ少すくないです。
I've only just started at the school, so I don't have many friends yet.

会議かいぎは今いま終おわったところです。
The meeting finished just now.

～たばかり · ～たばかり vs ～たところ (cont.)

Try swapping them. ❌入学にゅうがくしたところなので would claim you enrolled minutes ago, which makes nonsense of the excuse. And 終おわったばかりです, while perfectly grammatical, shifts the emphasis from the timetable to how it feels - closer to "it's barely over" than to "it finished at 3.02".

The second difference is the one that decides most sentences in practice: only ばかり can sit in front of a noun. 届とどいたばかりの荷物にもつ is normal; ❌届とどいたところの荷物にもつ does not exist.

～たばかり · ～たばかり vs ばかり ("only, nothing but")

Rule of thumb - look left. た-form + ばかり = "just did". Noun + ばかり, or て-form + ばかり + いる = "nothing but".

弟おとうとは新あたらしいゲームを買かったばかりだ。
My little brother has only just bought the new game.

弟おとうとはゲームばかりしている。
My little brother does nothing but play games.

～たばかり · ～たばかり vs ばかり ("only, nothing but") (cont.)

Same four syllables, and the two sentences do not even share a topic: the first is about when, the second about proportion. The ている version is the one to watch, because it looks so close to the pattern on this page: 泣ないてばかりいる means "does nothing but cry", while 泣ないたばかりだ would mean "has only just cried".

～たばかり · ～たばかり vs 最近

Rule of thumb - 最近さいきん is an adverb about a period; たばかり attaches to a single completed event. They combine happily, but they are not substitutes.

最近さいきん、よく映画えいがを見みに行いきます。
I've been going to the cinema a lot lately.

この店みせは先月せんげつ開ひらいたばかりです。
This shop only opened last month.

～たばかり · ～たばかり vs 最近 (cont.)

最近さいきん can cover a habit spread over weeks, which ばかり cannot do at all - there is no single event for it to attach to. Conversely ばかり pins the recency to one thing that happened, which is why it, and not 最近さいきん, can go in front of a noun.

Putting them together is completely normal and adds emphasis: 最近さいきん始はじめたばかりの趣味しゅみです, "it's a hobby I've only recently taken up."

～たばかり · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 日本にほんに来くるばかりです。 → ✅ 日本にほんに来きたばかりです。
    This pattern is built on the た-form. The dictionary form gives you an entirely different, much rarer construction.

  • ❌ 買かったばかり車くるまを見みせてください。 → ✅ 買かったばかりの車くるまを見みせてください。
    In front of a noun, ばかり needs の. Leaving it out is the single most common slip with this pattern.

  • ❌ 兄あには寝ねたばかりいます。 → ✅ 兄あには寝ねてばかりいます。
    "Does nothing but sleep" is built on the て-form plus いる. With the た-form the sentence would mean he had only just fallen asleep.

～たばかり · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 日本にほんに来きてばかりです。（meaning "I've only just come to Japan"） → ✅ 日本にほんに来きたばかりです。
    The て-form pushes you into the "nothing but" meaning, which here would be something like "all I do is come to Japan".

～たばかり · Practice 1

1. この町まちに引ひっ越こして来き____ばかりなので、まだ道みちがよくわかりません。

引ひっ越こして来きたばかりなので…

The pattern needs the た-form. Note what the sentence is doing: the move might have been a month ago, and ばかり is being used to excuse not knowing the streets yet - a job the clock-bound たところ could not do.

～たばかり · Practice 2

2. これは五年ごねん前まえに建たった（ところ・ばかり）のビルです。

これは五年ごねん前まえに建たったばかりのビルです。

Two reasons, and either one settles it. Five years is far outside ところ's range, and ところ cannot modify a noun in the first place. For a building, five years old genuinely counts as new - which is exactly the kind of judgement ばかり is built to express.

～たばかり · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "These are cakes I've just baked - please help yourself."

焼やいたばかりのケーキです。どうぞ召めし上あがってください。

A noun follows, so the form must be たばかりの with the の in place. This is the everyday way to say "freshly made", and it is one of the commonest uses of the pattern.

～たばかり · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 息子むすこは勉強べんきょうしないで、遊あそんだばかりいます。

✅ 息子むすこは勉強べんきょうしないで、遊あそんでばかりいます。

"Does nothing but play" is the other ばかり, and it is built on the て-form plus いる. With the た-form the sentence would say he had only just started playing, which is not a complaint at all.

～たばかり · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 新あたらしい仕事しごとを始はじめたばかりです。 (b) 新あたらしい仕事しごとを始はじめたところです。

(a) says you are new to the job. It could be your third day or your third month; what the sentence claims is that you have not settled in yet, and it usually leads into an explanation - you are still learning, you cannot answer that question without asking someone.

(b) reads the clock. It says the starting happened moments ago, which for a job is odd unless you are describing this morning: "I've just this minute started." Native speakers would understand it as the literal instant, and would probably have expected (a).
