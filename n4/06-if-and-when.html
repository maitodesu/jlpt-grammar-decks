と - conditional

Meaning · whenever A happens, B follows - automatically, every time

This is not the と you already know from N5, which joins nouns ("A and B") or marks a companion ("with"). This one joins two clauses and says the second follows from the first as a matter of course. Not a plan, not a choice: this is how the world works.

と - conditional · Formation

The clause in front of と is always plain non-past, no matter when the sentence as a whole happens. There is no past-tense form here at all.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb dictionary form + と 押(お)す → 押(お)すと
Verb (negative) ない-form + と 押(お)さない → 押(お)さないと
い-adjective plain form + と 安(やす)い → 安(やす)いと
い-adjective (negative) くない + と 安(やす)くない → 安(やす)くないと
な-adjective stem + だ + と 静(しず)か → 静(しず)かだと
Noun Noun + だ + と 冬(ふゆ) → 冬(ふゆ)だと
Polite (announcements) ます-form + と 押(お)します → 押(お)しますと

と - conditional · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - nothing that expresses the speaker's will may stand in the result clause. No ～ましょう, no ～てください, no ～たい, no ～たほうがいい, no command, no invitation. と reports what happens, never what anyone decides to do.

Note - the だ in front of と is not optional for nouns and な-adjectives. 学生(がくせい)だと is "if you're a student"; bare 学生(がくせい)と is "with a student" - the N5 particle, and a completely different sentence.

と - conditional · Examples

ここを押(お)すと、音(おと)が出(で)ます。
Press here and it makes a sound.

春(はる)になると、この山(やま)は緑(みどり)になります。
When spring comes, this mountain turns green.

3に5を足(た)すと、8になります。
Three plus five is eight.

早(はや)く出(で)ないと、電車(でんしゃ)に間(ま)に合(あ)いません。
If we don't leave soon, we won't make the train.

父(ちち)は酒(さけ)を飲(の)むと、いつも同(おな)じ話(はなし)を始(はじ)めます。
Whenever my father drinks, he starts telling the same story.

と - conditional · Examples (cont.)

この角(かど)を右(みぎ)に曲(ま)がると、左側(ひだりがわ)に小(ちい)さな花屋(はなや)があります。
Turn right at this corner and there's a little flower shop on your left.

部屋(へや)に入(はい)ると、誰(だれ)もいませんでした。
When I went into the room, there was nobody there.

この店(みせ)は週末(しゅうまつ)になると急(きゅう)に込(こ)んで、一時間(いちじかん)ぐらい待(ま)つことになります。
This place suddenly fills up at weekends, and you end up waiting about an hour.

窓(まど)を開(あ)けると、遠(とお)くで祭(まつ)りの太鼓(たいこ)の音(おと)が聞(き)こえた。
When I opened the window, I could hear festival drums somewhere far off.

と - conditional · Nuance & register

と is register-neutral. It sounds the same in speech and in writing, and the clause before it stays plain even in the politest sentence: 押(お)すと開(ひら)きます - the politeness lives on the final verb only. The one polite variant worth recognising is ますと, which turns up in announcements, instruction manuals and careful business speech: こちらのボタンを押(お)しますと、扉(とびら)が開(ひら)きます。

Where と really lives is anywhere a fixed procedure is being described: recipes, machine instructions, road directions, arithmetic, seasons, and habits. If you can put "every time" in front of the English and it still makes sense, と is probably right.

Two things learners are blindsided by:

と - conditional · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • もし and と do not go together. もし announces a hypothesis; と
    announces a regularity. もし雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ると… is odd. Use たら or ば after もし.
  • ～ないと on its own means "I have to". The consequence is simply left
    unsaid, and everybody fills it in: もう行(い)かないと。 - "I've got to go." これ、今日(きょう)中(じゅう)に出(だ)さないと。 - "I have to hand this in today." Extremely common in casual speech.

Finally, と with a past-tense main clause is not a conditional at all. It is the narrative "upon doing X, …", used for something the speaker then discovered or witnessed. Novels and travel diaries are full of it, and it is the one place a と sentence describes a single occasion rather than a rule.

と - conditional · と vs たら

Rule of thumb - と says every time, automatically. たら says on the occasion that this happens. Only たら can be followed by a request, an invitation, or a plan.

This is the difference that gets sentences rejected, because the ban on will-expressing main clauses after と is absolute. Compare:

駅(えき)に着(つ)いたら、電話(でんわ)してください。
Call me when you get to the station.

❌駅(えき)に着(つ)くと、電話(でんわ)してください is wrong, and not mildly so - it is asking the listener to do something as an automatic physical consequence of arriving, which is not a thing you can ask.

と - conditional · と vs たら (cont.)

この自動販売機(じどうはんばいき)は、お金(かね)を入(い)れるとランプがつきます。
On this vending machine, a light comes on when you put money in.

That is a property of the machine - invariable, no speaker in it. Switch to たら and it becomes a report of one particular time you fed it a coin.

と - conditional · と vs ば

Rule of thumb - と states a link the speaker treats as actual and invariable. ば puts a condition up as a premise and reasons from it.

Both handle general rules, so they overlap widely, and in many sentences either is fine. Where they part company is attitude. と has no speaker in it; ば is the form you use when you are weighing something up.

値段(ねだん)が安(やす)いと、すぐ売(う)り切(き)れます。
When something is cheap, it sells out straight away.

値段(ねだん)が安(やす)ければ、買(か)います。
If it's cheap, I'll buy it.

と - conditional · と vs ば (cont.)

The first is an observation about how this shop behaves. The second is my decision procedure - and a decision is exactly what と cannot host. Two more consequences of this: ば is the form that takes もし comfortably, and ば builds the regret pattern ～ばよかった, for which there is no ～とよかった.

と - conditional · と vs なら

Rule of thumb - なら picks up a premise someone has just supplied, usually the other person. と connects two events with nobody supplying anything.

なら is a reply; と is a description. This shows up most clearly in conversation, where なら can also put its main clause earlier in time than its condition - something と can never do.

「北海道(ほっかいどう)へ行(い)くんです。」「北海道(ほっかいどう)なら、六月(ろくがつ)がいいですよ。」
"I'm going to Hokkaido." "Hokkaido? June's the time to go."

❌北海道(ほっかいどう)へ行(い)くと、六月(ろくがつ)がいいですよ is impossible: going to Hokkaido does not cause June to be good, and the speaker is giving advice, not reporting a mechanism.

と - conditional · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 駅(えき)に着(つ)くと、電話(でんわ)してください。 → ✅ 駅(えき)に着(つ)いたら、電話(でんわ)してください。
    The clause after と is an automatic result, so no request, command, invitation or wish can stand there. This is the single most common と error.

  • ❌ 天気(てんき)がいいと、散歩(さんぽ)しましょう。 → ✅ 天気(てんき)がよかったら、散歩(さんぽ)しましょう。
    Same rule. ～ましょう is a proposal - the speaker's will - and と has no room for it.

  • ❌ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ったと、試合(しあい)は中止(ちゅうし)です。 → ✅ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ると／降(ふ)ったら、試合(しあい)は中止(ちゅうし)です。
    Nothing past ever stands in front of と. The tense is carried by the main clause.

と - conditional · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ もし時間(じかん)があると、手伝(てつだ)います。 → ✅ もし時間(じかん)があれば／あったら、手伝(てつだ)います。
    Two problems at once: もし flags a hypothesis, and 手伝(てつだ)います is an offer. と wants neither.

  • ❌ 学生(がくせい)と安(やす)くなります。 → ✅ 学生(がくせい)だと安(やす)くなります。
    Without だ, this is the N5 particle: "it gets cheaper with a student."

と - conditional · Practice 1

1. 秋(あき)になる____、この公園(こうえん)の木(き)が赤(あか)くなります。

秋(あき)になると、この公園(こうえん)の木(き)が赤(あか)くなります。

Every autumn, without fail, with nobody deciding anything - that is the definition of a と sentence. Note the plain non-past form なる in front of it even though the main clause is polite.

と - conditional · Practice 2

2. 家(いえ)に（着(つ)くと・着(つ)いたら）、メールをください。

家(いえ)に着(つ)いたら、メールをください。

The main clause is a request. と forbids requests outright because its result clause reports an automatic consequence, and you cannot ask someone to do something automatically. たら has no such restriction.

と - conditional · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "When it gets cold, my grandmother's legs hurt."

寒(さむ)くなると、祖母(そぼ)は足(あし)が痛(いた)くなります。

A recurring, involuntary consequence - nobody's choice, every time. と is the natural form. たら would push it towards one specific cold day.

と - conditional · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 時間(じかん)があると、映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)きませんか。

✅ 時間(じかん)があったら（or あれば）、映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)きませんか。

～ませんか is an invitation, so the sentence ends with the speaker's will. と cannot carry that. Both たら and ば work here, because the condition is a state (ある) rather than an action.

と - conditional · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 窓(まど)を開(あ)けると、雪(ゆき)が降(ふ)っていた。 (b) 窓(まど)を開(あ)けると、部屋(へや)が寒(さむ)くなる。

(a) has a past main clause, so it is not a conditional at all. It narrates one occasion: "I opened the window, and - snow." The speaker is reporting a discovery, and this is the standard way to write that in Japanese.

(b) has a non-past main clause, so it is the ordinary と: a general rule about this room. Open the window, and it gets cold - every time.

Same と, and the tense of the main clause is what decides which reading you get.

～ば

Meaning · if A, then B - A offered as the condition that would bring B about

～ば is the conditional of reasoning. It puts a premise on the table and follows it to its consequence, which is why it dominates written Japanese, instructions, arguments - and regrets about what you should have done.

～ば · Formation

Every ば-form ends in -eba. For verbs, change the final -u of the dictionary form to -e and add ば.

～ば · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) final -u → -e + ば 書(か)く → 書(か)けば
Verb (う-verb) final -u → -e + ば 急(いそ)ぐ → 急(いそ)げば
Verb (る-verb) る → れば 見(み)る → 見(み)れば
Verb (する) する → すれば 練習(れんしゅう)する → 練習(れんしゅう)すれば
Verb (来(く)る) irregular 来(く)る → 来(く)れば
Verb (negative) ない → なければ 行(い)かない → 行(い)かなければ
い-adjective い → ければ 忙(いそが)しい → 忙(いそが)しければ
いい irregular いい → よければ
い-adjective (negative) くない → くなければ 高(たか)くない → 高(たか)くなければ

～ば · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
な-adjective stem + なら(ば) 便利(べんり) → 便利(べんり)なら(ば)

Note - the bottom two rows are why なら looks like part of this table. It is: なら(ば) genuinely is the ば-form of the copula. But the standalone なら you meet as its own pattern does a different job, and the two should be kept apart in your head.

～ば · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - if the ば-clause is an action verb with the same subject as the main clause, you cannot end the sentence with a request, a command, an invitation or a statement of intention. Switch to たら. The restriction lifts when the ば-clause is a state: an adjective, ある／いる, a potential form, or a negative.

That single rule explains why 分(わ)からなければ聞(き)いてください is perfectly idiomatic while ❌東京(とうきょう)に着(つ)けば連絡(れんらく)してください is not.

～ば · Examples

急(いそ)げば、間(ま)に合(あ)いますよ。
If we hurry, we'll make it.

天気(てんき)がよければ、山(やま)が見(み)えます。
If the weather's good, you can see the mountains.

時間(じかん)があれば、手伝(てつだ)ってください。
If you have time, please give me a hand.

もっと練習(れんしゅう)すれば、うまくなりますよ。
You'll get better if you practise more.

説明書(せつめいしょ)を読(よ)まなければ、使(つか)い方(かた)が分(わ)かりません。
You won't know how to use it unless you read the manual.

～ば · Examples (cont.)

もう少(すこ)し早(はや)く家(いえ)を出(で)ればよかった。
I should have left home a bit earlier.

明日(あした)雨(あめ)が降(ふ)らなければ、川(かわ)でバーベキューをする予定(よてい)です。
If it doesn't rain tomorrow, we're planning to have a barbecue by the river.

この地図(ちず)を見(み)れば、どこに駐車場(ちゅうしゃじょう)があるかすぐ分(わ)かります。
One look at this map and you'll see straight away where the car parks are.

～ば · Nuance & register

ば itself is neither polite nor casual, but it leans written and considered. Notices, manuals, instructions, mathematical and legal prose, and any sentence that is doing a piece of reasoning all reach for it. In relaxed conversation たら is more frequent - except in the fixed patterns below, which are everywhere in speech.

Patterns built on ば that you will meet constantly:

～ば · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ～ばいい - "you just have to…", "it'd be good if…":
    この紙(かみ)に名前(なまえ)を書(か)けばいいですか。
  • ～ばよかった - regret about the road not taken:
    もっと早(はや)く言(い)えばよかった。 There is no ～とよかった.
  • ～ば～ほど - "the more…, the more…":
    考(かんが)えれば考(かんが)えるほど分(わ)からなくなる。
  • ～なければならない／なければいけない - the written sibling of the N5
    ～なくてはいけない. Same meaning, stiffer feel.

The casual contractions are the part that catches learners out in real conversation:

～ば · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • なければ → なきゃ: 帰(かえ)らなきゃ。 - "I've got to go home."
    Very common, all ages, both genders.
  • なければならない → なきゃ on its own, with the rest swallowed.
  • ～れば → ～りゃ: 見(み)りゃ分(わ)かる。 - blunt, older, and
    male-sounding. Recognise it; don't produce it.

～ば · ～ば vs ～と

Rule of thumb - と reports an actual, invariable link. ば floats a premise and reasons from it, which is why only ば can end in a wish, an offer or a request.

春(はる)になると、この池(いけ)の氷(こおり)が溶(と)けます。
Every spring the ice on this pond melts.

春(はる)になれば、この池(いけ)の氷(こおり)も溶(と)けるでしょう。
Once spring comes, the ice on this pond should melt too.

The と version is a fact about how the pond behaves. The ば version is the speaker working forward from a supposition about this year - note that it needs でしょう, because a prediction is a judgement, and と has no room for judgements.

～ば · ～ば vs ～と (cont.)

Two more ば-only territories: もし sits comfortably in front of it (もし間違(まちが)っていれば直(なお)してください), and counterfactual regret belongs to it alone (～ばよかった, ～ばよかったのに).

～ば · ～ば vs ～たら

Rule of thumb - where ば is blocked, たら steps in: before requests and commands after an action verb, and for anything that actually happened once.

会社(かいしゃ)に着(つ)いたら、すぐ報告(ほうこく)してください。
Report in as soon as you get to the office.

❌会社(かいしゃ)に着(つ)けば、すぐ報告(ほうこく)してください is rejected: 着(つ)く is an action the listener performs, and the main clause is a command aimed at the same person. But make the condition a state and ば returns:

分(わ)からなければ、いつでも聞(き)いてください。
If you don't understand, ask me any time.

～ば · ～ば vs ～たら (cont.)

The other gap is the past. たら can narrate a one-off sequence that really happened - 薬(くすり)を飲(の)んだら熱(ねつ)が下(さ)がった - and ば cannot. A ば sentence ending in the past tense is read as counterfactual instead: 聞(き)けばよかった means "I should have asked", i.e. I didn't.

～ば · ～ば vs ～なら

Rule of thumb - ば conditions on the event happening; なら conditions on a supposition being true. So ば's main clause follows in time, while なら's can come first.

沖縄(おきなわ)に行(い)けば、12月(がつ)でも泳(およ)げますよ。
If you go to Okinawa, you can swim even in December.

沖縄(おきなわ)に行(い)くなら、飛行機(ひこうき)を早(はや)めに予約(よやく)したほうがいいですよ。
If you're going to Okinawa, you should book your flight early.

In the first, the swimming happens once you are there. In the second, the booking happens before you go - the trip is a premise the listener has just announced, and the speaker responds to it. ば cannot reverse the order like that.

～ば · ～ば vs ～なら (cont.)

The second difference is mechanical: なら attaches straight to a bare noun and picks it up as a topic - コーヒーなら台所(だいどころ)にありますよ - a job no ば-form can do.

～ば · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 買(か)うば → ✅ 買(か)えば
    *The ば-form ends in -eba. For a う-verb the final vowel changes: ka-u → ka-eba.*

  • ❌ 高(たか)いければ → ✅ 高(たか)ければ
    The final い of an い-adjective is replaced by ければ, not kept in front of it.

  • ❌ いければ／いいば → ✅ よければ
    いい borrows every conjugated form from よい, here as elsewhere.

  • ❌ 学生(がくせい)ければ → ✅ 学生(がくせい)なら(ば)／学生(がくせい)であれば
    ければ belongs to い-adjectives only. Nouns and な-adjectives take なら(ば), or であれば in formal writing.

～ば · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 国(くに)に帰(かえ)れば、写真(しゃしん)を送(おく)ってください。 → ✅ 国(くに)に帰(かえ)ったら、写真(しゃしん)を送(おく)ってください。
    Action verb plus a request aimed at the same person - the one configuration ば refuses.

～ば · Practice 1

1. もっと野菜(やさい)を食(た)べ____、健康(けんこう)になりますよ。

もっと野菜(やさい)を食(た)べれば、健康(けんこう)になりますよ。

食(た)べる is a る-verb, so the る is replaced by れば. The sentence is a piece of reasoning - eat more vegetables and health follows - which is exactly what ば is for.

～ば · Practice 2

2. 空港(くうこう)に（着(つ)けば・着(つ)いたら）、メールしてください。

空港(くうこう)に着(つ)いたら、メールしてください。

The condition is an action the listener performs, and the main clause is a request to that same listener. ば is blocked in exactly that configuration. Had the condition been a state - 時間(じかん)があれば - ば would have been fine.

～ば · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "If it's cheap, I'll buy two."

安(やす)ければ、2つ買(か)います。

い-adjective: 安(やす)い → 安(やす)ければ. The main clause is the speaker's own decision, not a request to anyone, and the condition is a state - so ば is comfortable here even though the sentence expresses an intention.

～ば · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この本(ほん)が高(たか)いければ、図書館(としょかん)で借(か)ります。

✅ この本(ほん)が高(たか)ければ、図書館(としょかん)で借(か)ります。

い-adjectives lose their final い before ければ. Keeping it produces a form that does not exist. The same trap catches 安(やす)ければ, 寒(さむ)ければ, 忙(いそが)しければ.

～ば · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 窓(まど)を開(あ)ければ、涼(すず)しくなりますよ。 (b) 窓(まど)を開(あ)けると、涼(すず)しくなります。

(b) is a statement about how this room works: open the window and it cools down, every time. There is no speaker in it - it could be printed on a sign.

(a) presents opening the window as a solution the listener might adopt. The speaker is reasoning from a premise and offering the result, which is why the よ fits so naturally on the end. In practice you would say (a) to someone who has just complained about the heat, and (b) to someone asking how the room behaves.

～たら

Meaning · if / when / after - the all-purpose conditional

～たら is the one you reach for when you are not sure which conditional you want, and you will usually be right. It takes any kind of main clause, it handles both "if" and "when", and it is the everyday spoken default.

～たら · Formation

Take the plain past of anything at all and add ら. That is the entire rule, which is a large part of why たら is so easy to use.

～たら · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain past + ら 飲(の)む → 飲(の)んだ → 飲(の)んだら
Verb (る-verb) plain past + ら 届(とど)ける → 届(とど)けた → 届(とど)けたら
Verb (negative) なかった + ら 飲(の)まない → 飲(の)まなかったら
い-adjective かった + ら 寒(さむ)い → 寒(さむ)かったら
い-adjective (negative) くなかった + ら 寒(さむ)くない → 寒(さむ)くなかったら
な-adjective だった + ら 不便(ふべん) → 不便(ふべん)だったら
Noun だった + ら 雨(あめ) → 雨(あめ)だったら
Polite (formal) ました + ら 届(とど)きます → 届(とど)きましたら

～たら · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the past form in front of ら says nothing about past time. It marks the condition as completed at the moment the main clause happens. The tense of the sentence is carried entirely by the final verb.

たら does three jobs, and it is worth naming them separately:

  1. If - a genuine condition: 雨(あめ)だったら中止(ちゅうし)です。 2. When / after - for something certain to happen, where English would not say "if": 20歳(さい)になったら… 3. Discovery - with a past main clause, no conditional meaning survives at all. See the last two examples.

～たら · Examples

寒(さむ)かったら、エアコンを消(け)してください。
If you're cold, turn the air conditioning off.

明日(あした)晴(は)れたら、海(うみ)に行(い)こう。
If it's clear tomorrow, let's go to the sea.

仕事(しごと)が終(お)わったら、飲(の)みに行(い)きませんか。
Shall we go for a drink after work?

20歳(さい)になったら、一人(ひとり)で暮(く)らしたいです。
When I turn twenty, I want to live on my own.

薬(くすり)を飲(の)んだら、熱(ねつ)が下(さ)がりました。
I took the medicine and my fever went down.

～たら · Examples (cont.)

窓(まど)を開(あ)けたら、小(ちい)さな鳥(とり)が入(はい)ってきた。
I opened the window and a little bird flew in.

もし財布(さいふ)を落(お)としたら、すぐ交番(こうばん)に届(とど)けてください。
If you drop your wallet, take it straight to a police box.

来週(らいしゅう)の会議(かいぎ)の資料(しりょう)ができたら、部長(ぶちょう)に見(み)せてから、みんなに送(おく)ってください。
Once next week's meeting materials are ready, show them to the manager and then send them round.

もし道(みち)に迷(まよ)ったら、遠慮(えんりょ)しないで近(ちか)くの人(ひと)に聞(き)いてみてください。
If you get lost, don't hesitate - just ask someone nearby.

～たら · Nuance & register

たら is the spoken workhorse. In ordinary conversation it outnumbers と, ば and なら put together; in formal writing it thins out and 場合(ばあい) and ば take over. It is not casual in itself - the politeness sits on the final verb, and ましたら exists for genuinely formal contexts: 荷物(にもつ)が届(とど)きましたら、ご連絡(れんらく)します。

もし pairs with たら more naturally than with anything else. もし is optional and only reinforces the "this may not happen" reading; it never replaces the たら.

Two constructions built on たら that you meet immediately:

  • ～たらいい／～たらいいですか - "it'd be good if…", "what should I…".
  • ～たらどうですか／～たら? - a suggestion aimed at the listener. Both have
    their own pages; note only that the suggestion reading comes from どう, not from たら.

～たら · Nuance & register (cont.)

The discovery use deserves its own paragraph, because it is the one that makes learners think they have misread the sentence. With a past-tense main clause, たら means "I did X, and then - as it turned out - Y". There is no condition anywhere in it. The Y part has to be something outside the speaker's control: a discovery, a surprise, an event that simply occurred. A planned next action is not allowed there, and needs ～てから instead.

～たら · ～たら vs ～と

Rule of thumb - と needs the link to be automatic and repeatable, and bans any main clause expressing the speaker's will. たら accepts single occasions and every kind of ending.

この窓(まど)を開(あ)けると、海(うみ)が見(み)えます。
Open this window and you can see the sea.

窓(まど)を開(あ)けたら、海(うみ)が見(み)えました。
I opened the window, and there was the sea.

The first is a permanent property of the window; the second is one morning. And where と would refuse outright - ❌窓(まど)を開(あ)けると、写真(しゃしん)を撮(と)りましょう - たら is fine: 窓(まど)を開(あ)けたら、写真(しゃしん)を撮(と)りましょう。

～たら · ～たら vs ～と (cont.)

Note the shapes too: と takes the plain non-past, たら the plain past. If you find yourself writing a past form in front of と, you have picked the wrong one.

～たら · ～たら vs ～ば

Rule of thumb - after an action verb, only たら can be followed by a request or a command. And only たら narrates something that actually happened once.

田中(たなか)さんが来(き)たら、教(おし)えてください。
Let me know when Tanaka gets here.

❌田中(たなか)さんが来(く)れば、教(おし)えてください is wrong. Both work, though, once the condition is a state rather than an action: 分(わ)からなかったら／分(わ)からなければ、聞(き)いてください are equally good.

The second gap is the past. A たら clause with a past main clause reports a real sequence; the ば equivalent flips into counterfactual regret (聞(き)けばよかった = "I should have asked, and didn't"). If you want to say what happened, ば is not available.

～たら · ～たら vs ～なら

Rule of thumb - a たら clause always happens before its main clause. A なら clause need not: なら takes up a supposition, and the main clause can be carried out first.

The clearest illustration is a road-safety slogan you see on posters all over Japan, which uses both in one breath:

飲(の)んだら乗(の)るな、乗(の)るなら飲(の)むな。
If you've drunk, don't drive; if you're going to drive, don't drink.

飲(の)んだら = the drinking is finished, and the driving would come after it. 乗(の)るなら = driving is a plan you are entertaining, and the instruction applies beforehand. Swap the two and the slogan collapses.

The same split in a plain sentence:

～たら · ～たら vs ～なら (cont.)

日本(にほん)に行(い)ったら、温泉(おんせん)に入(はい)りたい。
When I get to Japan, I want to go to a hot spring.

日本(にほん)に行(い)くなら、春(はる)がいちばんいいですよ。
If you're going to Japan, spring is the best time.

～たら · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行(い)くたら → ✅ 行(い)ったら
    たら is built on the past form, never the dictionary form. 行(い)く → 行(い)った → 行(い)ったら.

  • ❌ 高(たか)いたら → ✅ 高(たか)かったら
    い-adjectives go through their past form first: 高(たか)い → 高(たか)かった → 高(たか)かったら.

  • ❌ 日本(にほん)に行(い)ったら、ビザを取(と)ります。 → ✅ 日本(にほん)に行(い)くなら、ビザを取(と)ります。
    A たら clause must come first in time, and you get the visa before you fly. For a plan you are reacting to, なら.

  • ❌ 窓(まど)を開(あ)けたら、部屋(へや)を掃除(そうじ)しました。 → ✅ 窓(まど)を開(あ)けてから、部屋(へや)を掃除(そうじ)しました。
    With a past main clause, たら means the speaker discovered something. A deliberate next action needs ～てから.

～たら · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ このボタンを押(お)したら、お湯(ゆ)が出(で)ます。 → ✅ このボタンを押(お)すと、お湯(ゆ)が出(で)ます。
    Not ungrammatical, but wrong for a manual: たら frames it as one occasion, while a machine's fixed behaviour calls for と.

～たら · Practice 1

1. 安(やす)____、もう一(ひと)つ買(か)います。

安(やす)かったら、もう一(ひと)つ買(か)います。

An い-adjective forms たら through its past: 安(やす)い → 安(やす)かった → 安(やす)かったら. ❌安(やす)いたら does not exist.

～たら · Practice 2

2. 大学(だいがく)を（出(で)ると・出(で)たら）、国(くに)に帰(かえ)るつもりです。

大学(だいがく)を出(で)たら、国(くに)に帰(かえ)るつもりです。

つもり states the speaker's intention, and と cannot host anything that expresses the speaker's will. たら has no such limit - and here it carries the "when", not "if": graduating is going to happen.

～たら · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "When the rain stops, let's go for a walk."

雨(あめ)がやんだら、散歩(さんぽ)に行(い)きましょう。

～ましょう is a proposal, so と is out. The event is expected rather than doubtful, which is why English says "when" and Japanese still uses たら - the same form covers both.

～たら · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 来週(らいしゅう)京都(きょうと)に行(い)ったら、ホテルを予約(よやく)しておきます。

✅ 来週(らいしゅう)京都(きょうと)に行(い)くなら、ホテルを予約(よやく)しておきます。

You book the hotel before leaving, and a たら clause is always the earlier event. なら is the one conditional that lets the main clause come first in time.

～たら · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼(かれ)に電話(でんわ)したら、留守(るす)でした。 (b) 彼(かれ)に電話(でんわ)したら、教(おし)えてください。

(a) has a past main clause, so there is no condition in it at all: "I phoned him, and he was out." The たら clause just sets the scene, and what follows is something the speaker found out.

(b) has a request as the main clause, so it is a genuine conditional: "if (or when) you phone him, let me know." Nothing has happened yet.

The たら is identical in both. The tense of the main clause decides whether you are reading a report or a condition - and this is the single most useful thing to know about たら.

～なら

Meaning · if that's the case - taking up something just said, and replying to it

～なら is the conditional that answers. It picks up a premise - nearly always one the other person has just handed you - and builds the speaker's judgement on top of it: advice, an offer, an opinion, a decision. Nothing about なら reports what will happen; it reports what the speaker thinks about it.

な-adjective stem + なら (no だ) 静(しず)か → 静(しず)かなら

Rule of thumb - だ never appears in front of なら. ❌静(しず)かだなら, ❌雨(あめ)だなら. Compare と, which requires it (雨(あめ)だと). Getting these two the wrong way round is the commonest form error at N4.

Note - だったら, でしたら and それなら are the everyday cousins of this pattern. だったら is the casual "in that case"; でしたら is the polite version you hear across a counter: 明日(あした)でしたら、お席(せき)がございます。

～なら · Examples

ビールなら冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)にありますよ。
Beer? There's some in the fridge.

土曜日(どようび)は無理(むり)だけど、日曜日(にちようび)なら空(あ)いているよ。
Saturday's no good, but Sunday I'm free.

頭(あたま)が痛(いた)いなら、早(はや)く帰(かえ)ったほうがいいですよ。
If your head hurts, you should go home early.

お寿司(すし)なら、駅前(えきまえ)の店(みせ)がおいしいですよ。
For sushi, the place in front of the station is good.

君(きみ)が行(い)かないなら、私(わたし)も行(い)かない。
If you're not going, I'm not going either.

～なら · Examples (cont.)

宿題(しゅくだい)が終(お)わったなら、遊(あそ)びに行(い)ってもいいよ。
If you've finished your homework, you can go out and play.

山(やま)に登(のぼ)るなら、いい靴(くつ)を買(か)っておいたほうがいいですよ。
If you're going to climb a mountain, you'd better buy proper boots first.

中古(ちゅうこ)の車(くるま)を買(か)うなら、一度(いちど)専門(せんもん)の人(ひと)に見(み)てもらったほうが安心(あんしん)です。
If you're buying a used car, it's safer to have a specialist look at it first.

～なら · Nuance & register

なら is conversational glue, and its natural habitat is the second turn of a dialogue. Someone says something; you take their words and hand back a judgement. That is why so many なら sentences begin by repeating a noun the other person just used - ビールなら, 京都(きょうと)なら, その本(ほん)なら. In that use it behaves almost like a topic marker, and it is the only conditional that can attach to a bare noun this way.

The main clause is always the speaker: ～たほうがいい, ～てあげる, ～てください, ～つもりだ, an opinion, an offer. It is never a natural consequence - that is と's territory.

のなら（casual んなら）adds a shade of "given that you're telling me this", which can read as sympathetic or as slightly pointed depending on the tone: そんなに嫌(きら)いなのなら、食(た)べなくていいよ。 Used that way, なら carries a real edge - you are throwing the listener's own premise back at them.

～なら · Nuance & register (cont.)

In writing, なら is replaced by であれば or by 場合(ばあい)は; the stiff ならば survives in formal prose (必要(ひつよう)ならば再度(さいど)ご連絡(れんらく)ください). In speech, ならば sounds bookish.

～なら · ～なら vs ～たら

Rule of thumb - with たら, the main clause comes after the condition in time. With なら, it can come before. And たら reports events, while なら responds to premises.

旅行(りょこう)に行(い)くなら、カメラを貸(か)してあげるよ。
If you're going on a trip, I'll lend you my camera.

旅行(りょこう)に行(い)ったら、写真(しゃしん)をたくさん撮(と)りたい。
When I go on the trip, I want to take lots of photos.

The camera is lent before the trip - the trip is only a plan the listener has mentioned. The photos are taken during it. たら can never do what the first sentence does; that inversion is なら's alone.

～なら · ～なら vs ～たら (cont.)

The second half of the difference is where the condition comes from. たら supposes something itself; なら accepts something already on the table. So なら is at home in replies, and slightly odd as the opening line of a conversation.

～なら · ～なら vs ～と

Rule of thumb - と is a mechanism with no speaker in it. なら is nothing but the speaker, reacting to a premise.

このボタンを押(お)すと、お湯(ゆ)が出(で)ます。
Press this button and hot water comes out.

❌このボタンを押(お)すなら、お湯(ゆ)が出(で)ます is wrong: the water coming out is a fact about the machine, not the speaker's opinion about a supposition. Conversely:

このボタンを押(お)すなら、先(さき)に説明書(せつめいしょ)を読(よ)んでください。
If you're going to press this button, read the manual first.

Now there is a speaker with an instruction, and the instruction applies before the button is pressed - both hallmarks of なら, both impossible for と.

～なら · ～なら vs ～と (cont.)

なら also cannot express a certain future "when": ❌春(はる)になるなら, ❌夜(よる)になるなら. Something guaranteed to occur is not a premise anyone needs to entertain; use と or たら.

～なら · ～なら vs ～ば

Rule of thumb - なら(ば) is literally the ば-form of the copula, which is why they blur. Separate them by what the condition is about: ば conditions on an event occurring, なら on a supposition being true.

部長(ぶちょう)に聞(き)けば、すぐ分(わ)かりますよ。
Ask the manager and you'll know right away.

部長(ぶちょう)に聞(き)くなら、午前中(ごぜんちゅう)がいいですよ。
If you're going to ask the manager, the morning is a good time.

In the first, the asking produces the result. In the second, the asking is a plan the listener has announced, and the advice is about how to carry it out. Note again that the advice applies before the asking happens.

～なら · ～なら vs ～ば (cont.)

The other split is mechanical. ば needs a predicate to attach to; なら fastens straight onto a bare noun and lifts it out as a topic - その辞書(じしょ)なら机(つくえ)の上(うえ)にあります - and no ば-form can do that.

～なら · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 静(しず)かだなら → ✅ 静(しず)かなら
    だ disappears before なら. The same for nouns: ❌雨(あめ)だなら → ✅ 雨(あめ)なら／雨(あめ)だったら.

  • ❌ 家(いえ)に帰(かえ)るなら、シャワーを浴(あ)びます。 → ✅ 家(いえ)に帰(かえ)ったら、シャワーを浴(あ)びます。
    A plain sequence of your own actions is not a premise you are responding to, and the shower comes after you get home. That is a たら sentence.

  • ❌ 春(はる)になるなら、暖(あたた)かくなります。 → ✅ 春(はる)になると、暖(あたた)かくなります。
    Spring is not a supposition, and warming up is not the speaker's judgement. Natural consequences belong to と.

～なら · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 明日(あした)雨(あめ)が降(ふ)るなら、試合(しあい)は中止(ちゅうし)です。 → ✅ 明日(あした)雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ったら／降(ふ)れば…
    If you are the one supposing the rain, use たら or ば. 降るなら becomes correct the moment somebody else says it is going to rain and you reply to them.

  • ❌ 学生(がくせい)のなら割引(わりびき)があります。 → ✅ 学生(がくせい)なら割引(わりびき)があります。
    A noun takes なら directly. The の belongs with 場合(ばあい), not with なら.

～なら · Practice 1

1. 「傘(かさ)を忘(わす)れました。」「傘(かさ)____、私(わたし)のを貸(か)しますよ。」

The second speaker picks up the exact noun the first one used and replies to it with an offer. That is なら at its most typical - attached to a bare noun, no だ, and a main clause that is purely the speaker's own decision.

2. 温泉(おんせん)に（行(い)くなら・行(い)ったら）、平日(へいじつ)のほうがすいていますよ。

The advice is about which day to choose, so it has to reach the listener before the trip. たら would put the advice after arrival, which makes no sense - you cannot pick a weekday once you are already there.

～なら · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "If you're not going to use it, could you lend it to me?"

You are responding to something the other person has implied or said, and the main clause is a request - both squarely なら. たら would be understood, but it would frame not-using as an event that happens first, which is not the point.

～なら · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 大学(だいがく)を卒業(そつぎょう)するなら、国(くに)に帰(かえ)ります。

Graduating is a scheduled certainty and the return happens afterwards, so this is "when", not "if it's the case that" - たら. 卒業するなら would only work as a reply to someone else raising the possibility, with advice attached: 卒業(そつぎょう)するなら、早(はや)めに仕事(しごと)を探(さが)したほうがいい。

～なら · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 引(ひ)っ越(こ)すなら、手伝(てつだ)うよ。 (b) 引(ひ)っ越(こ)したら、新(あたら)しい住所(じゅうしょ)を教(おし)えてね。

(a) responds to news: you have just said you are moving, and the speaker offers help - help that will be given during the move, not after it. That before-or-during timing is only possible with なら.

(b) waits for the move to be over. たら locks the main clause into second place, so the new address can only be requested once there is one.

Both sentences are about the same move. なら reacts to the plan; たら waits for the event.

場合は

Meaning · in the event that X - one case picked out, with the rule that applies to it

場合(ばあい) (read ばあい) is an ordinary noun meaning "case, occasion". Used as a conditional it sets out one possible situation among several and states what applies in it. It is the language of notices, manuals and rules, not of conversation.

場合は · Formation

Everything in front of 場合(ばあい) is a noun-modifying clause - exactly the N5 pattern - because 場合(ばあい) is just a noun.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb dictionary form + 場合(ばあい)は 遅(おく)れる → 遅(おく)れる場合(ばあい)は
Verb (past) た-form + 場合(ばあい)は 忘(わす)れた → 忘(わす)れた場合(ばあい)は
Verb (negative) ない-form + 場合(ばあい)は 届(とど)かない → 届(とど)かない場合(ばあい)は

場合は · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - if you can put it in front of a noun, you can put it in front of 場合(ばあい), using exactly the same shape. That is why な-adjectives take な and nouns take の here, while と demands だ and なら demands nothing at all.

Note - the は is the contrast particle doing its usual job: "in this case (as opposed to the others)". Drop it - 雨(あめ)の場合(ばあい)、中止(ちゅうし)します - and the sentence is flatter and slightly more written. には makes the contrast heavier still. On forms and in headings you often see the bare noun phrase alone: 「団体(だんたい)の場合(ばあい)」.

場合は · Examples

雨(あめ)の場合(ばあい)は、体育館(たいいくかん)で行(おこな)います。
In the event of rain, it will be held in the gym.

遅(おく)れる場合(ばあい)は、必(かなら)ず連絡(れんらく)してください。
If you are going to be late, be sure to get in touch.

If you don't have time, email is fine too.

パスワードを忘(わす)れた場合(ばあい)は、こちらのボタンを押(お)してください。
If you have forgotten your password, press this button.

火事(かじ)の場合(ばあい)は、この階段(かいだん)をお使(つか)いください。
In case of fire, please use these stairs.

場合は · Examples (cont.)

説明(せつめい)が必要(ひつよう)な場合(ばあい)は、いつでもお呼(よ)びください。
If you need an explanation, call me any time.

荷物(にもつ)が1週間(しゅうかん)以内(いない)に届(とど)かない場合(ばあい)は、こちらの番号(ばんごう)までお電話(でんわ)ください。
If your parcel has not arrived within a week, please call this number.

キャンセルは前日(ぜんじつ)まで無料(むりょう)ですが、当日(とうじつ)の場合(ばあい)は料金(りょうきん)の半分(はんぶん)をいただきます。
Cancellation is free up to the day before; on the day itself we charge half the fee.

場合は · Nuance & register

This is the formal, written conditional. Signs at stations, safety notices, insurance documents, school regulations, software error messages, business email - that is where 場合(ばあい)は lives. It has a bureaucratic steadiness to it: here is a contingency, here is the procedure.

Because 場合(ばあい) is a noun, it behaves like one. It takes demonstratives (その場合(ばあい)は - "in that case"), it appears in fixed expressions (場合(ばあい)によって - "depending on the case"; 場合(ばあい)によっては - "in some cases"), and it can head a column on a form. None of the other conditionals can do any of that.

The flip side is that using it with a friend sounds like reading aloud from a manual. ❌お腹(なか)がすいた場合(ばあい)は、何(なに)か作(つく)るよ is grammatical and comic; say お腹(なか)がすいたら、何(なに)か作(つく)るよ. A rough guide: if the sentence could plausibly be printed and laminated, use 場合(ばあい)は; otherwise use たら.

場合は · Nuance & register (cont.)

One more consequence of its formality: the main clause is almost always an instruction, a rule, or a consequence stated impersonally - ～てください, お～ください, ～ことになっています, ～ます. You will rarely see it with a casual ending.

場合は · 場合(ばあい)は vs ときは

Rule of thumb - とき locates an event in time and takes for granted that it happens. 場合(ばあい) presents it as one possibility among others, and usually leads to an instruction.

This is the pair that matters, because both translate as "when".

子(こ)どものときは、よく川(かわ)で泳(およ)ぎました。
When I was a child, I often swam in the river.

❌子(こ)どもの場合(ばあい)は、よく川(かわ)で泳(およ)ぎました is not a sentence about the speaker's childhood at all - it says "in the case of children…", i.e. as opposed to adults. Childhood is a period of time, not a contingency.

地震(じしん)の場合(ばあい)は、エレベーターを使(つか)わないでください。
In the event of an earthquake, do not use the lift.

場合は · 場合(ばあい)は vs ときは (cont.)

Here 場合(ばあい) is right precisely because the earthquake may never come, and because the sentence sits in a list beside 火事(かじ)の場合(ばあい)は… and 停電(ていでん)の場合(ばあい)は….

場合は · 場合(ばあい)は vs ～たら

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different room. たら is what you say; 場合(ばあい)は is what the sign says.

熱(ねつ)が下(さ)がらない場合(ばあい)は、もう一度(いちど)ご来院(らいいん)ください。
If your temperature does not come down, please come in again.

熱(ねつ)が下(さ)がらなかったら、もう一度(いちど)来(き)てくださいね。
If your fever doesn't go down, come back in again, all right?

Identical content; the first is the printed instruction slip, the second is the doctor speaking. Note also that 場合(ばあい) being a noun lets a notice enumerate cases in parallel, each on its own line - a structure たら handles clumsily.

場合は · 場合(ばあい)は vs ～と

Rule of thumb - in a manual the two divide the labour: と says what the machine normally does; 場合(ばあい)は introduces the exception and tells you what you should do.

ボタンを押(お)すと、ランプが光(ひか)ります。光(ひか)らない場合(ばあい)は、電池(でんち)を確(たし)かめてください。
Press the button and the lamp lights up. If it does not light up, check the batteries.

The first half is an automatic consequence with no speaker in it, which is と's whole job - and と cannot be followed by an instruction. The second half needs one, so it switches to 場合(ばあい)は. Almost every instruction manual in Japanese alternates between exactly these two.

場合は · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 子(こ)どもの場合(ばあい)、よく山(やま)に登(のぼ)りました。 → ✅ 子(こ)どものとき、よく山(やま)に登(のぼ)りました。
    A stretch of past time is とき. 場合(ばあい) would mean "as for children (as opposed to adults)".

  • ❌ 必要(ひつよう)だ場合(ばあい) → ✅ 必要(ひつよう)な場合(ばあい)
    場合(ばあい) is a noun, so a な-adjective in front of it takes な - the ordinary noun-modifying rule from N5.

  • ❌ 病気(びょうき)場合(ばあい)は → ✅ 病気(びょうき)の場合(ばあい)は
    A noun needs の to modify another noun. Compare と, which wants だ, and なら, which wants nothing.

  • ❌ 友(とも)だちと会(あ)う場合(ばあい)は、いつも駅(えき)で待(ま)ちます。 → ✅ 友(とも)だちと会(あ)うときは、いつも駅(えき)で待(ま)ちます。
    A regular habit is not a contingency. 場合(ばあい) needs something that might or might not arise.

場合は · Practice 1

1. 都合(つごう)が悪(わる)い____は、前(まえ)の日(ひ)までにお知(し)らせください。

都合(つごう)が悪(わる)い**場合(ばあい)**は、前(まえ)の日(ひ)までにお知(し)らせください。

An い-adjective attaches in its plain form, with nothing in between. The polite お～ください ending confirms the register: this is a written notice, which is where 場合(ばあい)は belongs.

場合は · Practice 2

2. 学生(がくせい)の（場合(ばあい)は・ときは）、料金(りょうきん)が半額(はんがく)になります。

学生(がくせい)の場合(ばあい)は、料金(りょうきん)が半額(はんがく)になります。

The sentence sorts customers into categories - students as against everybody else - which is what 場合(ばあい) does. 学生(がくせい)のときは would mean "back when I was a student", a period of the speaker's life.

場合は · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "If the machine does not start, please contact the office."

機械(きかい)が動(うご)かない場合(ばあい)は、事務所(じむしょ)に連絡(れんらく)してください。

A negative verb attaches in its plain ない-form. This is a printed-notice sentence, so 場合(ばあい)は is the natural choice; 動(うご)かなかったら would be the spoken version.

場合は · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 大学生(だいがくせい)だ場合(ばあい)は、この申(もう)し込(こ)み書(しょ)を使(つか)ってください。

✅ 大学生(だいがくせい)の場合(ばあい)は、この申(もう)し込(こ)み書(しょ)を使(つか)ってください。

A noun modifying another noun takes の, never だ. The だ version is interference from ～だと, where だ really is required.

場合は · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 地震(じしん)のときは、エレベーターを使(つか)わないでください。 (b) 地震(じしん)の場合(ばあい)は、エレベーターを使(つか)わないでください。

Both are correct and you will see both on the same building's signage. (a) frames the earthquake as a moment in time - while it is happening, do not use the lift. (b) frames it as one entry in a list of emergencies, alongside fire and power cuts, and reads more like a clause in a set of regulations.

(b) is the more bureaucratic of the two, and the one that invites a parallel sentence beginning 火事(かじ)の場合(ばあい)は.

～たらどうですか

Meaning · why don't you…? - putting an action to the listener as a suggestion

Literally "if you did X, how would that be?" - the たら is the ordinary conditional and どうですか is the N5 "how about it?". Put together they make a suggestion aimed squarely at the other person, and that aim is what makes the pattern touchier than it looks.

～たらどうですか · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb た-form + らどうですか 休(やす)む → 休(やす)んだらどうですか
Verb (negative) なかった + らどうですか 行(い)かない → 行(い)かなかったらどうですか
Casual た-form + ら? 休(やす)む → 休(やす)んだら?
Softer た-form + らどうでしょうか 休(やす)む → 休(やす)んだらどうでしょうか
Politer た-form + らいかがですか 休(やす)む → 休(やす)んだらいかがですか
Politest (upward) て-form + はいかがでしょうか 休(やす)む → お休(やす)みになってはいかがでしょうか

～たらどうですか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the suggestion lives in どう, not in たら. Change どう and you change the sentence completely: ～たらどうですか = "why don't you…?", but ～たらどうしますか = "what will you do if…?" - a real question about a real condition.

Note - inserting ～てみる softens the whole thing, because it proposes a trial rather than a course of action: 聞(き)いてみたらどうですか lands much more gently than 聞(き)いたらどうですか. This is the single easiest way to take the edge off.

～たらどうですか · Examples

もう少(すこ)し待(ま)ったらどうですか。
Why don't you wait a bit longer?

先生(せんせい)に聞(き)いてみたらどう?
Why not try asking the teacher?

熱(ねつ)があるなら、今日(きょう)は早(はや)く帰(かえ)ったらどうですか。
If you've got a fever, why don't you go home early today?

時間(じかん)がないなら、タクシーで行(い)ったらどうでしょうか。
If you're short of time, how about taking a taxi?

そんなに心配(しんぱい)なら、自分(じぶん)で確(たし)かめたらどう?
If you're that worried, why don't you go and check it yourself?

～たらどうですか · Examples (cont.)

部長(ぶちょう)、こちらの案(あん)も一度(いちど)ご覧(らん)になってはいかがでしょうか。
Might I suggest you also take a look at this proposal?

毎日(まいにち)コンビニのお弁当(べんとう)ばかりじゃなくて、たまには自分(じぶん)で作(つく)ってみたらどうですか。
Instead of convenience-store bentos every single day, why not cook for yourself once in a while?

一人(ひとり)で悩(なや)んでいないで、一度(いちど)ご両親(りょうしん)に相談(そうだん)してみたらいかがですか。
Rather than worrying about it alone, why not talk it over with your parents?

～たらどうですか · Nuance & register

This is not a neutral pattern, and the polite ですか does not make it safe. ～たらどうですか carries an unstated "you are not doing the obvious thing", which is fine from a friend and grating from anyone else. Repeated, it is nagging; delivered flatly, it is criticism.

The register scale, roughly:

～たらどうですか · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 休(やす)んだら? - casual, friendly with rising intonation. With
    falling intonation it is openly annoyed: 自分(じぶん)でやったら? = "then do it yourself."
  • 休(やす)んだらどうですか - polite in form; use downward or sideways, not
    upward.
  • 休(やす)んだらどうでしょうか - hedged, noticeably softer.
  • 休(やす)んだらいかがですか - polite vocabulary, safe with customers and
    acquaintances.
  • お休(やす)みになってはいかがでしょうか - the form to use with a superior.
    Notice it abandons たら entirely for ～ては.

It is a spoken pattern. In writing you would use ～てはいかがでしょうか or ～ことをおすすめします.

Two traps worth keeping apart, both hinging on what follows たら:

～たらどうですか · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ～たらどうですか = a suggestion.
  • ～たらどうしますか = "what will you do if…?" -
    終電(しゅうでん)を逃(のが)したらどうしますか。
  • ～たらどうなりますか = "what happens if…?" - about consequences, not
    people.

～たらどうですか · ～たらどうですか vs ～ほうがいい

Rule of thumb - ～たらどうですか asks; ～ほうがいい tells.

～ほうがいい (N5) weighs one option against the alternative and comes down on one side, often with an unspoken warning attached. ～たらどうですか floats an action and hands the decision back to the listener - formally, at least. It ends in a question, and an answer is expected.

病院(びょういん)に行(い)ったらどうですか。
Why don't you go to the doctor?

病院(びょういん)に行(い)ったほうがいいですよ。
You'd better go to the doctor.

～たらどうですか · ～たらどうですか vs ～ほうがいい (cont.)

The second is stronger and implies a consequence if you don't. The first is softer on the surface - but because it points at something the listener has visibly failed to do, it can end up sounding more pointed. Neither is safe to aim at a superior.

～たらどうですか · ～たらどうですか vs ～ませんか／～ましょう

Rule of thumb - ～ませんか and ～ましょう include the speaker. ～たらどうですか is for the listener alone.

一緒(いっしょ)に昼(ひる)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べませんか。
Shall we have lunch together?

昼(ひる)ご飯(はん)くらい食(た)べたらどうですか。
Why don't you at least eat some lunch?

The second explicitly excludes the speaker: you are telling someone else to eat. Using ～たらどうですか when you mean to come along is one of the more embarrassing N4 errors, because it turns an invitation into a criticism of the listener's habits.

～たらどうですか · ～たらどうですか vs ～たらいいですよ

Rule of thumb - ～たらいいですよ answers a request for advice. ～たらどうですか offers advice nobody asked for.

「どこで買(か)えますか。」「駅前(えきまえ)の店(みせ)で買(か)ったらいいですよ。」
"Where can I buy one?" "You could buy one at the shop by the station."

～たらいい states the good option as information - no question, no pressure. It is the natural reply to どうしたらいいですか. Swapping in 買(か)ったらどうですか would be odd there, because the listener has already asked; suggesting it back at them implies they should have thought of it themselves.

～たらどうですか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 休(やす)むたらどうですか → ✅ 休(やす)んだらどうですか
    たら is built on the past form: 休(やす)む → 休(やす)んだ → 休(やす)んだら.

  • ❌ 社長(しゃちょう)、少(すこ)し休(やす)んだらどうですか。 → ✅ 社長(しゃちょう)、少(すこ)しお休(やす)みになってはいかがでしょうか。
    Grammatical, and rude upward. Telling a superior what they ought to be doing needs the ～てはいかがでしょうか frame and an honorific verb.

  • ❌ 一緒(いっしょ)にコンサートに行(い)ったらどうですか。（meaning "shall we go together?"）→ ✅ 一緒(いっしょ)にコンサートに行(い)きませんか。
    ～たらどうですか never includes the speaker.

  • ❌ Reading 台風(たいふう)が来(き)たらどうしますか as a suggestion → it
    means "what will you do if a typhoon comes?" どうですか suggests; どうしますか asks.

～たらどうですか · Practice 1

1. 眠(ねむ)そうですね。コーヒーでも飲(の)____どうですか。

コーヒーでも飲(の)んだらどうですか。

The pattern is built on the plain past: 飲(の)む → 飲(の)んだ → 飲(の)んだら. The でも softens it further by making the coffee an example rather than a demand.

～たらどうですか · Practice 2

2. 明日(あした)一緒(いっしょ)にテニスを（しませんか・したらどうですか）。

明日(あした)一緒(いっしょ)にテニスをしませんか。

一緒(いっしょ)に puts the speaker in the picture, and ～たらどうですか is strictly about what the listener should do. With したらどうですか the sentence would mean "why don't you play tennis with someone tomorrow" - and would sound faintly critical of how they spend their time.

～たらどうですか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Why don't you try asking the station staff?"

駅(えき)の人(ひと)に聞(き)いてみたらどうですか。

～てみる inside the pattern turns it into "why not give it a try", which is the friendliest version available. Without it, 聞(き)いたらどうですか sounds more like "you should have asked already".

～たらどうですか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 先生(せんせい)、この本(ほん)を読(よ)んだらどうですか。

✅ 先生(せんせい)、この本(ほん)をお読(よ)みになってはいかがでしょうか。

The grammar of the original is fine; the problem is direction. Suggesting an action to a teacher, boss or customer needs ～てはいかがでしょうか with an honorific verb. A slightly lighter option is 読(よ)まれてはいかがでしょうか.

～たらどうですか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 医者(いしゃ)に行(い)ったらどうですか。 (b) 医者(いしゃ)に行(い)ったほうがいいですよ。

(a) is shaped as a question and formally leaves the choice open - but it also points out that the listener has not gone yet, so depending on tone it can come across as impatient: "why don't you just go?"

(b) is a straight recommendation with the speaker's judgement behind it, and the よ adds a note of concern. It is stronger, but paradoxically often kinder, because it reads as looking out for the listener rather than commenting on their inaction.

Neither is appropriate upward; to a superior you would say 一度(いちど)病院(びょういん)へ行(い)かれてはいかがでしょうか.

どうしたらいいですか

Meaning · what should I do? - asking someone to tell you the right course of action

This is a frame, not a single word: a question word + a conditional + いい. "If I did what, would it be good?" Swap the question word and the verb and you can build the whole family - どこに出(だ)せばいいですか, 何時(なんじ)に来(き)たらいいですか - which is why it is worth learning as a shape rather than as a phrase.

どうしたらいいですか · Formation

The たら and ば versions are interchangeable in almost every sentence.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (たら) た-form + らいいですか 出(だ)す → 出(だ)したらいいですか
Verb (ば) ば-form + いいですか 出(だ)す → 出(だ)せばいいですか
する (fixed frame) どう + したら／すれば + いいですか する → どうしたらいいですか
Other question words 何(なに)／どこ／いつ／どう + …たらいいですか どこ → どこで買(か)えばいいですか
Softened …らいいでしょうか どうしたらいいでしょうか
Embedded (no ですか) …らいいか + 分(わ)からない どうしたらいいか分(わ)かりません
Formal / to a superior どのように…らよろしいでしょうか どのようにすればよろしいでしょうか

どうしたらいいですか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - when the question is embedded inside a larger sentence, the ですか disappears and a bare か holds the question together: どうしたらいいか教(おし)えてください. Never ❌どうしたらいいですかを教(おし)えてください.

Note - the いい here is an ordinary い-adjective, so it conjugates: どうしたらよかったんですか ("what should I have done?"), どうしたらいいのか分(わ)かりません, どうしたらよろしいでしょうか (formal, いい → よろしい).

どうしたらいいですか · Examples

切符(きっぷ)をなくしました。どうしたらいいですか。
I've lost my ticket. What should I do?

この書類(しょるい)はどこに出(だ)したらいいですか。
Where should I hand in these documents?

靴(くつ)はここで脱(ぬ)げばいいですか。
Should I take my shoes off here?

漢字(かんじ)がなかなか覚(おぼ)えられません。どうすればいいですか。
I just can't seem to memorise kanji. What should I do?

次(つぎ)はどうしたらいいか、教(おし)えてください。
Please tell me what I should do next.

どうしたらいいですか · Examples (cont.)

パソコンが急(きゅう)に動(うご)かなくなったんですが、どうしたらいいでしょうか。
My computer's suddenly stopped working - what would you suggest I do?

初(はじ)めての出張(しゅっちょう)で、何(なに)を準備(じゅんび)したらいいか全(まった)く分(わ)かりません。
It's my first business trip and I have no idea what I should be getting ready.

保証書(ほしょうしょ)をなくしてしまった場合(ばあい)は、どうしたらよろしいでしょうか。
In the event that the warranty card has been lost, how should we proceed?

どうしたらいいですか · Nuance & register

どうしたらいいですか is the standard polite way to ask for guidance, and it is safe with strangers, shop staff, teachers and colleagues. It is neither stiff nor casual on its own; the register is set by the ending you attach.

  • どうしよう - muttered to yourself. "Oh no, what do I do."
  • どうしたらいい?／どうすればいいの? - casual, to a friend.
  • どうしたらいいですか - the neutral polite default.
  • どうしたらいいでしょうか - softer, and slightly more deferential; it
    presents the question as something you are wondering rather than demanding an answer to.
  • どうしたらよろしいでしょうか／どのようにすればよろしいでしょうか - business
    and customer-facing.

どうしたらいいですか · Nuance & register (cont.)

たら versus ば in this frame: they are close to interchangeable, and no native speaker will correct you either way. どうすればいいですか leans a touch more procedural and written - it fits "what is the correct procedure" - while どうしたらいいですか is marginally more common in speech and a little softer. With other question words the same holds: どこに置(お)いたらいいですか and どこに置(お)けばいいですか are both fine.

One keigo distinction worth knowing early: if you are offering to act for someone - a shop assistant, say - the humble いかがいたしましょうか ("how would you like me to…?") replaces this pattern entirely. どうしたらいいですか asks for instructions; いかがいたしましょうか offers service.

どうしたらいいですか · どうしたらいいですか vs どうしますか

Rule of thumb - どうしたらいいですか asks for advice; どうしますか asks about intention.

雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ってきました。どうしますか。
It's started raining. What do you want to do?

雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ってきました。どうしたらいいですか。
It's started raining. What should we do?

The first hands the decision to the listener and expects them to state their own plan; a waiter or a travel agent would use it of you. The second admits you do not know the right answer and asks to be told. Asking a superior どうしますか when you meant "what should I do?" is a small but real workplace mistake - it sounds like you are asking about their plans.

どうしたらいいですか · どうしたらいいですか vs どうしたんですか

Rule of thumb - these look alike and share nothing. どうしたんですか = "what happened? / what's wrong?"

顔色(かおいろ)が悪(わる)いですね。どうしたんですか。
You look pale. What's the matter?

どうしたんですか is どうした (past of する) plus the N5 explanatory んです - it looks backwards at a situation. どうしたらいいですか looks forwards at an action. The same trap catches どうしましたか, which a doctor uses to mean "what seems to be the problem?" - never "what should I do?"

どうしたらいいですか · どうしたらいいですか vs ～たほうがいいですか

Rule of thumb - どうしたらいいですか is an open question. ～たほうがいいですか names one action and asks yes or no.

この紙(かみ)は捨(す)てたほうがいいですか。
Should I throw this paper away?

この紙(かみ)はどうしたらいいですか。
What should I do with this paper?

Use the first when you already have a candidate action in mind and want it confirmed; use the second when you have nothing. The answer you get back will often be ～たらいいですよ or ～たらどうですか - the mirror image of this pattern, and the reason the two sit next to each other in this book.

どうしたらいいですか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ どうするたらいいですか → ✅ どうしたらいいですか
    たら is built on the plain past. する → した → したら.

  • ❌ どうしたらいいますか → ✅ どうしたらいいですか
    いい is an い-adjective, so it takes です, not ます.

  • ❌ 財布(さいふ)をなくしました。どうしましたか。 → ✅ 財布(さいふ)をなくしました。どうしたらいいですか。
    どうしましたか asks what happened - which you have just said. It cannot mean "what should I do?"

  • ❌ どうすればいいですかを教(おし)えてください。 → ✅ どうすればいいか教(おし)えてください。
    An embedded question drops ですか and ends in bare か. It is not a noun and cannot take を.

どうしたらいいですか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、この件(けん)はどうしますか。（meaning "what should I do?"）→ ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、この件(けん)はどうしたらよろしいでしょうか。
    Asking upward for instructions needs the advice frame, and よろしいでしょうか for the register.

どうしたらいいですか · Practice 1

1. この申込書(もうしこみしょ)は、どこに出(だ)____いいですか。

どこに出(だ)したらいいですか。（or どこに出(だ)せばいいですか）

Either conditional works in this frame. 出したら is the past form plus ら; 出せば is the ば-form. The question word どこ simply replaces どう - the shape of the sentence is unchanged.

どうしたらいいですか · Practice 2

2. パスポートをなくしてしまいました。（どうしましたか・どうしたらいいですか）

パスポートをなくしてしまいました。どうしたらいいですか。

You have already said what happened, so どうしましたか - "what happened?" - would be asking your listener to tell you your own news. The advice frame is the only one that fits.

どうしたらいいですか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "What time should I come tomorrow?"

明日(あした)は何時(なんじ)に来(き)たらいいですか。（or 来(く)ればいいですか）

Question word + conditional + いい. Note the readings of the irregular verb: 来(く)る → 来(き)た → 来(き)たら, but the ば-form is 来(く)る → 来(く)れば.

どうしたらいいですか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: どうすればいいですかを教(おし)えてください。

✅ どうすればいいか教(おし)えてください。

Once the question is embedded in a bigger sentence it loses ですか and keeps only か. The politeness moves to the outer verb, which is already 教(おし)えてください. Adding を treats the question as a noun, which it is not.

どうしたらいいですか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) どうしたらいいですか。 (b) どうしますか。

(a) says "I don't know the right thing to do - please tell me." The speaker is asking to be given a course of action, and the answer is expected to be advice: 係(かかり)の人(ひと)に聞(き)いたらいいですよ.

(b) says "what are you going to do?" The decision belongs to the listener, and the answer will be a plan, not advice. Said to a customer it is a polite "what would you like to do?"; said upward to a boss when you meant (a), it lands as though you were querying their intentions rather than asking for instructions.
