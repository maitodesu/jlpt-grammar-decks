N4 - Doing things for each other, and asking favours (cont.)

  • ～てよかった

～てあげる

Meaning · do something for someone - the action itself is the gift, and it travels away from the speaker

At N5 you learned that あげる hands an object to someone outside your circle. ～てあげる hands over an action instead. The direction rule is identical; what is new is that naming an act as a favour puts it on the record, and that can be generous or it can be smug, depending on who you say it to.

～てあげる · Formation

Attach あげる to the て-form. あげる then conjugates as an ordinary る-verb.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb て-form + あげる 作つくる → 作つくってあげる
Verb (polite) て-form + あげます 貸かす → 貸かしてあげました
Verb (offering) て-form + あげよう／あげましょうか 運はこぶ → 運はこんであげましょうか
To someone above you (humble) て-form + さしあげる 案内あんないする → 案内あんないしてさしあげる
To someone below you / an animal て-form + やる 買かう → 買かってやる

The particle on the person who benefits is not decided by あげる. It is decided by the verb underneath, exactly as it would be without あげる attached:

～てあげる · Formation (cont.)

Underlying verb frame Particle Example
Verb already takes 人ひとに に 友ともだちに道みちを教おしえてあげる
Verb already takes 人ひとを を 子こどもを迎むかえに行いってあげる
Verb acts on the person's possession の 母ははの荷物にもつを持もってあげる

Rule of thumb - the benefit must land outside the speaker. There is no ❌私わたしに教おしえてあげる. If the favour comes toward you or your family, the verb you need is 教おしえてくれる.

～てあげる · Examples

妹いもうとに本ほんを**読よんであげた**。
I read my little sister a book.

荷物にもつが重おもそうだったので、持もってあげました。
The bags looked heavy, so I carried them for her.

お母かあさんに**電話でんわしてあげて**。心配しんぱいしてるよ。
Give your mum a call. She's worried.

道みちが分わからないと言いうので、駅えきまで**案内あんないしてあげた**。
He said he didn't know the way, so I walked him to the station.

～てあげる · Examples (cont.)

息子むすこの自転車じてんしゃを**直なおしてあげたら**、とても喜よろこんだ。
When I fixed my son's bike for him, he was delighted.

留学生りゅうがくせいに漢字かんじの読よみ方かたを**教おしえてあげる**約束やくそくをしました。
I promised to teach the exchange student how to read kanji.

雨あめが降ふってきたから、隣となりのおばあさんの洗濯物せんたくものを**取とり込こんであげました**。
It started raining, so I brought in the laundry for the old lady next door.

自分じぶんで考かんがえさせたほうがいいので、答こたえは**教おしえてあげません**でした。
It's better to make him think for himself, so I didn't give him the answer.

～てあげる · Nuance & register

～てあげる announces the favour. That is its entire function, and it is why the pattern needs handling. 荷物にもつを持もちましょうか is a plain offer to carry a bag. 荷物にもつを持もってあげましょうか adds "…as a kindness to you", and the person hearing it now has a debt on the table they did not ask for. Between friends this is warm. Aimed at a stranger, a customer or anyone senior, it lands as condescending.

The safe habit: do not say ～てあげる to the person receiving the favour. Say it about the favour afterwards, or to a third party.

～てあげる · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • To the person: 手伝てつだいましょうか。／お手伝てつだいしましょうか。
  • About it later: 昨日きのう、田中たなかさんの引ひっ越こしを手伝てつだってあげた。

さしあげる does not rescue the situation. It is grammatically the humble form, but announcing to a superior that you have bestowed something on them is worse, not better - the humility is in the word and the presumption is in the construction. Real Japanese reaches for the honorific-humble frame instead: お持もちします, ご案内あんないします, お貸かしします. Keep さしあげる for narrating in the third person （部長ぶちょうにお土産みやげを買かってさしあげた）and even there it is slightly stiff.

～てあげる · Nuance & register (cont.)

～てやる is the downward member of the family - for animals, plants, small children, one's own younger siblings - and it is blunter and more male-marked.

There is a second, sharper ～てあげる: said with a flat tone about something the listener does not want, it turns sarcastic or threatening. 本当ほんとうのことを言いってあげようか - "shall I do you the favour of telling you the truth?" The mechanism is the same: you are insisting that this is a kindness when both of you know it is not.

In casual speech the contraction is ～たげる: 買かってあげる → 買かったげる, 見みておいてあげる → 見みといたげる. Common in the Kansai area and in relaxed speech generally; never write it.

～てあげる · ～てあげる vs ～てくれる

Rule of thumb - same event, opposite direction. ～てあげる when the favour leaves your side; ～てくれる when it arrives at your side.

The subject of both is the person doing the action. What changes is where the benefit ends up relative to the うち／そと line you learned at N5.

私わたしが山田やまださんの仕事しごとを**手伝てつだってあげた**。
I helped Yamada with his work.

山田やまださんが私わたしの仕事しごとを**手伝てつだってくれた**。
Yamada helped me with my work.

～てあげる · ～てあげる vs ～てくれる (cont.)

The line is your family, not just your body. If a teacher explains something to your daughter, that favour has come toward your side, so it is 娘むすめに説明せつめいしてくださいました - never ❌してあげました, which would push your own daughter into the そと group.

～てあげる · ～てあげる vs the plain verb

Rule of thumb - the plain verb reports what happened. ～てあげる reports who it was for. If the beneficiary is not the point, leave it off.

昨日きのう、弟おとうとの部屋へやを**片かたづけました**。
I tidied my brother's room yesterday.

昨日きのう、弟おとうとの部屋へやを**片かたづけてあげました**。
I tidied my brother's room for him yesterday.

Both are correct. The second one is the version you say when you want credit, or when you are complaining that you had to. This is not a subtlety learners can skip: overusing ～てあげる is one of the clearest markers of a foreign speaker, because English "for him" is neutral and Japanese ～てあげる is not.

～てあげる · ～てあげる vs ～させてあげる

Rule of thumb - ～てあげる means you did it for them. ～させてあげる means you let them do it, as a kindness.

English "let him" and "did it for him" are far apart; in Japanese they are one suffix apart, and learners reach for the wrong one.

子こどもに絵えを**描かいてあげた**。
I drew a picture for my child.

子こどもに自由じゆうに絵えを**描かかせてあげた**。
I let my child draw whatever they liked.

～てあげる · ～てあげる vs ～させてあげる (cont.)

The causative ～させてあげる is extremely common with permission （休やすませてあげる, 遊あそばせてあげる）and carries the same warmth - and the same risk of sounding lofty about your own generosity.

～てあげる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんが私わたしに地図ちずを書かいてあげました。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんが私わたしに地図ちずを書かいてくれました。
    A favour arriving at the speaker can never be ～てあげる. This is the N5 あげる／くれる rule, unchanged.

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、この資料しりょうをコピーしてさしあげましょうか。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、この資料しりょうをコピーしましょうか。／お持もちしましょうか。
    Offering a superior a labelled favour is presumptuous even in the humble form. Drop the giving verb and use the plain offer or the お～します frame.

  • ❌ 友ともだちに手伝てつだってあげました。 → ✅ 友ともだちを手伝てつだってあげました。
    あげる does not assign the particle. 手伝てつだう takes 人ひとを, so the person keeps を.

～てあげる · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 傘かさを貸かしあげます。 → ✅ 傘かさを貸かしてあげます。
    あげる attaches to the て-form, not the ます-stem.

  • ❌ 母ははが妹いもうとにセーターを編あんであげました。（telling an outsider）→ ✅ 母ははが妹いもうとにセーターを編あんでくれました。
    Both are inside your family, so from your chair the favour landed on your side. あげる here would sound as though neither of them were related to you.

～てあげる · Practice 1

1. 荷物にもつが多おおかったので、後輩こうはいのかばんを持もっ____。

持もってあげました。

The favour goes from the speaker outward to a junior colleague, so ～てあげる is the right direction. Note the の on 後輩こうはい: the verb acts on the bag, and the owner of the bag is marked with の.

～てあげる · Practice 2

2. Choose: 課長かちょうが新あたらしいやり方かたを教おしえて（あげました・くださいました）。

課長かちょうが新あたらしいやり方かたを**教おしえてくださいました**。

The section chief is the one acting and the benefit reaches the speaker, so the verb must be the toward-me one, in its honorific form. あげました would reverse the direction and put the speaker above the boss.

～てあげる · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'll look after the dog for you while you're away."

留守るすの間あいだ、犬いぬの世話せわをしてあげるよ。

Said to a close friend this is warm and normal. To an acquaintance you would drop the giving verb - 犬いぬの世話せわをしておきますよ - because announcing the favour to its recipient is what makes ～てあげる sound self-satisfied.

～てあげる · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 先生せんせい、重おもそうですね。荷物にもつを持もってあげましょうか。

✅ 先生せんせい、重おもそうですね。お持もちしましょうか。

The grammar is fine and the effect is rude: you are informing your teacher that you are about to be generous to them. Swapping in さしあげましょうか does not help. The natural offer upward is the humble お + ます-stem + します frame.

～てあげる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 窓まどを開あけました。 (b) 窓まどを開あけてあげました。

(a) is a neutral report of an action: the window got opened, and nothing is said about who wanted it open.

(b) frames the same action as a service performed for somebody - you opened it for them, because they were hot or could not reach. It implies a specific beneficiary who is not you, and depending on tone it can carry either kindness or a hint of "and you're welcome". If the beneficiary is the person you are speaking to, (a) is almost always the better choice.

～てくれる

Meaning · someone does something for me, or for my side - and I am grateful for it

～てくれる is the action counterpart of the くれる you met at N5. Someone else performs the verb, and the good of it arrives at the speaker's うち. Unlike ～てあげる, this one is not risky to say: marking a favour that came to you is simply politeness, and leaving it off can sound cold.

～てくれる · Formation

Attach くれる to the て-form. The doer is the subject; the beneficiary - usually 私わたし - is normally left unsaid, because the verb has already said it.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb て-form + くれる 手伝てつだう → 手伝てつだってくれる
Verb (polite) て-form + くれます 送おくる → 送おくってくれました
Verb (negative) て-form + くれない 返事へんじする → 返事へんじしてくれない
From someone above you (honorific) て-form + くださる → くださいます 直なおす → 直なおしてくださいました
Casual request て-form + くれない？／くれる？ 待まつ → 待まってくれない?

～てくれる · Formation (cont.)

The sentence frame is Aが（私わたしに）～てくれる. As with plain くれる, the particle on the beneficiary comes from the underlying verb - 私わたしに教おしえてくれる but 私わたしを待まってくれる.

Rule of thumb - ～てくれる can never have the speaker as the doer. There is no ❌私わたしが田中たなかさんに貸かしてくれた. Nothing can move toward you while you are the one doing it.

Note - the ます-form of くださる is irregular: くださいます, not ❌くださります. And the ください you have used since your first week is nothing more than the imperative of this verb - 待まってください is literally "give me the favour of waiting".

～てくれる · Examples

父ちちが駅えきまで**送おくってくれました**。
My dad drove me to the station.

だれが窓まどを**閉しめてくれた**の?
Who closed the window for me?

先生せんせいが作文さくぶんを**直なおしてくださいました**。
My teacher kindly corrected my essay.

同おなじ課かの人ひとが仕事しごとを**代かわってくれて**、助たすかりました。
Someone in my section covered my shift, which was a real help.

～てくれる · Examples (cont.)

だれも**教おしえてくれなかった**から、会議かいぎの時間じかんが変かわったのを知しらなかった。
Nobody told me, so I didn't know the meeting time had changed.

妻つまが毎朝まいあさお弁当べんとうを**作つくってくれる**ので、昼ひるごはんに困こまりません。
My wife makes me a packed lunch every morning, so lunch is never a problem.

息子むすこが熱ねつを出だしたとき、隣となりの人ひとが病院びょういんまで車くるまで**運はこんでくださいました**。
When my son had a fever, our neighbour was kind enough to drive us to the hospital.

忙いそがしいのに最後さいごまで話はなしを**聞きいてくれて**、本当ほんとうにありがとう。
Thank you so much for hearing me out to the end even though you were busy.

～てくれる · Nuance & register

～てくれる is gratitude built into the grammar. Japanese has no polite way to report a favour received without it. If a colleague fixes your spreadsheet and you tell someone 田中たなかさんが直なおしました, you have described an event; add くれました and you have acknowledged a kindness. Omitting it where it belongs sounds ungrateful in a way that has no English equivalent, so learners should err toward using it.

The single most useful frame built on it is ～てくれて、ありがとう - the standard way to thank someone for an action（来きてくれてありがとう）, as opposed to ～をありがとう for a thing. Polite version: ～てくださって、ありがとうございます. Also common: ～てくれて助たすかりました, "you saved me."

～てくれる · Nuance & register (cont.)

The negative is where the emotion flips. ～てくれない / ～てくれなかった is a complaint, not a neutral report: it says the favour was owed and did not arrive. 誰だれも手伝てつだってくれなかった means "nobody would help me", with the resentment audible. Compare the bare 誰だれも手伝てつだいませんでした, which is a flat statement of fact.

In the question form ～てくれない? the same negative becomes a soft casual request - the first rung of the request ladder that runs on up through ～てくれませんか, ～てもらえませんか and ～ていただけませんか.

～てくれる · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register notes: くれる is plain, くれます polite, くださる honorific. くださる is used for a superior's kindness toward you and is completely standard in business speech. The plain imperative くれ（待まってくれ）is rough, male-marked and abrupt - fine among close male friends, never to a stranger. In fast speech ～てくれる reduces to ～てくれ + rising tone or, very casually, 見みせてくんない? Do not write that.

One extension worth knowing: ～てくれる can be used with things and weather by personification, and it is warm rather than childish - 天気てんきが持もってくれてよかった, "thankfully the weather held out for us."

～てくれる · ～てくれる vs ～てもらう

Rule of thumb - one event, two chairs. ～てくれる seats the doer as subject; ～てもらう seats the beneficiary as subject.

友ともだちが引ひっ越こしを**手伝てつだってくれました**。
My friend helped me move.

友ともだちに引ひっ越こしを**手伝てつだってもらいました**。
I got my friend to help me move.

Both are natural and describe the same afternoon. The difference is initiative and warmth. くれる presents the friend as acting of his own accord and reads as thanks. もらう presents the speaker as having arranged it, which is why it is the form you use when you asked, hired, or requested. Notice the particle flip on the friend: が as subject, に as source.

～てくれる · ～てくれる vs ～てくださる

Rule of thumb - くださる is くれる with a status gap. Use it when the person doing you the favour is above you, or is a customer.

部長ぶちょうが空港くうこうまで**迎むかえに来きてくださいました**。
The department head came to meet me at the airport.

Using plain くれました about a superior is grammatical but flat, and in front of that superior or their colleagues it sounds disrespectful. In private grumbling to a friend it is perfectly normal - the choice tracks the audience as much as the person.

～てくれる · ～てくれる vs ～てあげる

Rule of thumb - くれる is safe to say to anyone; あげる is not.

They are mirror images grammatically, but not socially. Thanking someone with 手伝てつだってくれてありがとう is warm. Telling that same person 手伝てつだってあげたよ is smug, because you are the one naming your own act a favour.

田中たなかさんが会場かいじょうまで地図ちずを**書かいてくれた**。
Tanaka drew me a map to the venue.

田中たなかさんに会場かいじょうまでの地図ちずを**書かいてあげた**。
I drew Tanaka a map to the venue.

～てくれる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私わたしが友ともだちに自転車じてんしゃを貸かしてくれました。 → ✅ 私わたしが友ともだちに自転車じてんしゃを貸かしてあげました。
    くれる cannot take the speaker as the doer. When the favour leaves you, it is あげる.

  • ❌ 先生せんせいが説明せつめいしてくださりました。 → ✅ 先生せんせいが説明せつめいしてくださいました。
    くださる has an irregular ます-form, like おっしゃる → おっしゃいます.

  • ❌ 来きてくれてありがとうを言いいます。 → ✅ 来きてくれてありがとう。
    Thanks for an action is expressed with ～てくれてありがとう directly; there is no need to build a sentence around the word ありがとう.

～てくれる · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 母ははが私わたしに作つくった料理りょうりはおいしいです。 → ✅ 母ははが作つくってくれた料理りょうりはおいしいです。
    Not ungrammatical, but a Japanese speaker would hear the missing くれる as oddly detached about their own mother's cooking.

  • ❌ 手伝てつだってくれ、お願ねがいします。 → ✅ 手伝てつだってください。／手伝てつだってくれない?
    Bare くれ is a rough male command and clashes with a polite frame.

～てくれる · Practice 1

1. 友ともだちが空港くうこうまで迎むかえに来きて____ので、タクシーに乗のらずにすみました。

迎むかえに来きてくれたので…

The friend acted and the benefit reached the speaker, so くれる is required. It also does real work in the sentence: it is what tells the listener the trip to the airport was a kindness rather than a coincidence.

～てくれる · Practice 2

2. Choose: 部長ぶちょうが推薦状すいせんじょうを書かいて（くれました・くださいました）。You are telling a colleague at work.

部長ぶちょうが推薦状すいせんじょうを書かいてくださいました。

A department head is above you, and you are speaking in a workplace register, so the honorific くださる is the expected choice. くれました is not wrong Japanese, but about a superior at work it sounds careless.

～てくれる · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Thank you for waiting."

待まっていてくれて、ありがとう。／ polite: お待まちくださって、ありがとうございます。

Thanks for an action is built on ～てくれて, not on a noun. Note the ていて: the person was in the state of waiting, so 待まっていて is more natural than the bare 待まって.

～てくれる · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 山田やまださんは私わたしに日本語にほんごを教おしえてあげました。

✅ 山田やまださんは私わたしに日本語にほんごを教おしえてくれました。

The teaching arrives at the speaker, so the direction verb must be くれる. As written, the sentence claims Yamada did the speaker a favour away from the speaker, which is impossible.

～てくれる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 誰だれも手伝てつだいませんでした。 (b) 誰だれも手伝てつだってくれませんでした。

(a) is a neutral fact: no help occurred. It could describe a task nobody was expected to join.

(b) adds the speaker's stake. Because くれる frames help as something that should have come toward them, its negative says the help was owed and withheld - "nobody would lift a finger for me." It is a complaint, and native listeners hear the resentment clearly.

～てもらう

Meaning · get someone to do something for you; have something done for your benefit

～てもらう describes the same favour as ～てくれる, but from the receiving chair. The person who benefits becomes the subject of the sentence, and the person who actually does the work is demoted to a に phrase. That re-arrangement is the whole difficulty: English "I had my hair cut" hides the hairdresser, and Japanese puts them back in with に.

～てもらう · Formation

Attach もらう to the て-form. The frame is Bは Aに ～てもらう - B receives, A does.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb て-form + もらう 直なおす → 直なおしてもらう
Verb (polite) て-form + もらいます 送おくる → 送おくってもらいました
From someone above you (humble) て-form + いただく 見みる → 見みていただきました
Potential (used for requests) て-form + もらえる／いただける 来くる → 来きてもらえますか
Negative potential (polite request) て-form + もらえませんか 待まつ → 待まってもらえませんか

Rule of thumb - the doer takes に. Not が, which would make them the receiver, and not を, which is reserved for the thing acted on.

～てもらう · Formation (cont.)

から is possible instead of に when the favour is information or an object moving from a source, and it is preferred when the giver is an organisation: 大学だいがくから説明せつめいしてもらいました. With most action verbs, に is the default.

Note - the beneficiary is normally the speaker or the speaker's side, exactly as with くれる. 田中たなかさんは山田やまださんに手伝てつだってもらった is fine when you are narrating about a third party, but it quietly puts you in Tanaka's camp for the length of the sentence.

～てもらう · Examples

友ともだちに写真しゃしんを**撮とってもらった**。
I got a friend to take a photo of me.

駅員えきいんさんに行いき方かたを**教おしえてもらいました**。
I had the station attendant tell me how to get there.

パソコンが動うごかなくなったので、弟おとうとに**見みてもらった**。
My laptop stopped working, so I got my brother to take a look at it.

来週らいしゅう、美容院びよういんで髪かみを**切きってもらう**予定よていです。
I'm getting my hair cut at the salon next week.

～てもらう · Examples (cont.)

部長ぶちょうに書類しょるいにサインをしていただきました。
I had the department head sign the documents.

引ひっ越こしの日ひは後輩こうはいに車くるまを**出だしてもらう**つもりです。
I'm planning to get a junior colleague to lend me his car on moving day.

書かき方かたが分わからなかったので、窓口まどぐちの人ひとにゆっくり**説明せつめいしていただきました**。
I didn't know how to fill it in, so I had the clerk at the counter explain it slowly.

自分じぶんで決きめられないなら、一度いちど専門家せんもんかに**相談そうだんに乗のってもらった**ほうがいいですよ。
If you can't decide on your own, you'd do better to get a specialist to talk it through with you.

～てもらう · Nuance & register

～てもらう implies the receiver had a hand in it. Not always a spoken request - sometimes just consent, or having gone somewhere for the service - but the event is not a bolt from the blue. That is why 髪かみを切きってもらった is the normal way to say you had a haircut: you went to the salon and asked. Compare 急きゅうに雨あめがやんでくれた, where nothing was arranged, and only くれる works.

Because the receiver is the subject, ～てもらう is also the natural frame for planning and arranging: 明日あした、田中たなかさんに来きてもらいます means "I'll have Tanaka come" - the speaker is organising it. Said about a person present, this can sound managerial, so it belongs downward or sideways, not upward.

～てもらう · Nuance & register (cont.)

～ていただく is the humble form and is one of the workhorses of business Japanese. Whole registers of office speech are built on it: ご確認かくにんいただけますでしょうか, 少々しょうしょうお待まちいただきます. Note the honorific frame お／ご + stem + いただく （お待まちいただく, ご記入きにゅういただく）, which is politer still than 待まっていただく and very common on signs and announcements.

The potential forms are where this pattern earns its keep in daily life. もらえる means "can receive the favour", which is exactly what a request is:

～てもらう · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ～てもらえる? - casual, to a friend
  • ～てもらえますか - polite, everyday
  • ～てもらえませんか - politer; the negative leaves room to refuse
  • ～ていただけませんか - humble, business standard
  • ～ていただけないでしょうか - the top of the ladder

Register: もらう is neutral in plain and polite speech alike, with no male/female marking. いただく belongs to formal and business situations; using it with close friends sounds like you are being funny on purpose. Casually もらえる? often contracts in speech to もらえん? in western dialects - regional, not standard.

～てもらう · ～てもらう vs ～てくれる

Rule of thumb - ～てもらう says I got them to; ～てくれる says they did it for me. Initiative sits with the subject.

近所きんじょの人ひとに留守るすの間あいだ猫ねこの世話せわをしてもらいました。
I got a neighbour to look after the cat while I was away.

近所きんじょの人ひとが留守るすの間あいだ猫ねこの世話せわをしてくれました。
A neighbour looked after the cat for me while I was away.

～てもらう · ～てもらう vs ～てくれる (cont.)

The first says you arranged it. The second says they stepped in - and it carries more warmth, which is why thanks are almost always phrased with くれる: 世話せわをしてくれてありがとう. してもらってありがとう is occasionally heard in casual speech, but it is not the standard shape: gratitude points at what the other person did, so the giving verb is the one that belongs there.

There is a hard limit on もらう. If the doer acted entirely on their own and the event was not arranged, もらう is unnatural: 知しらない人ひとが財布さいふを拾ひろってくれた - a stranger picked up my dropped wallet - cannot become ❌拾ひろってもらった unless you had asked them to.

～てもらう · ～てもらう vs the causative ～させる

Rule of thumb - both are English "have someone do it". ～てもらう is asking and the doer is willing; ～させる is making and the speaker has authority.

息子むすこに買かい物ものに**行いってもらいました**。
I got my son to go and do the shopping (and thanks, son).

息子むすこに買かい物ものに**行いかせました**。
I made my son go and do the shopping.

～てもらう · ～てもらう vs the causative ～させる (cont.)

Because もらう builds in gratitude, it softens an instruction; because させる asserts power, it is unusable upward and sounds harsh even downward. Combining both is how you ask for permission politely: 説明せつめいさせていただきます, "allow me to explain."

～てもらう · ～てもらう vs ～てほしい

Rule of thumb - ～てもらう reports a favour that happened or is arranged; ～てほしい states a wish that has not.

来週らいしゅうまでに返事へんじをもらいたいんですが。
I'd like to get an answer by next week.

来週らいしゅうまでに返事へんじをしてほしいんですが。
I'd like you to answer by next week.

The desiderative ～てもらいたい sits between them: it is a wish expressed through the receiving frame, and it is a shade more indirect - and therefore softer - than ～てほしい, because it talks about what you would receive rather than about what the other person should do.

～てもらう · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 友ともだちが手伝てつだってもらいました。 → ✅ 友ともだちに手伝てつだってもらいました。
    With もらう the doer takes に. が would make the friend the one receiving the favour.

  • ❌ 先生せんせいに推薦状すいせんじょうを書かいてもらいました。（in a formal report）→ ✅ 先生せんせいに推薦状すいせんじょうを書かいていただきました。
    Receiving a favour from someone above you takes the humble いただく in any formal setting.

  • ❌ 助たすけてもらってありがとうございました。 → ✅ 助たすけてくださってありがとうございました。
    Thanks point at the giver's act, so the giving verb is the one that fits.

～てもらう · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 知しらない人ひとに道みちを教おしえてもらった。（when they volunteered）→ ✅ 知しらない人ひとが道みちを教おしえてくれた。
    Unrequested help is くれる territory. もらう implies you asked.

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょうに会議かいぎに出でてもらいます。（said to the department head）→ ✅ 部長ぶちょうに会議かいぎにご出席しゅっせきいただけますでしょうか。
    Arranging a superior's actions out loud sounds managerial. Turn it into a request with the humble potential.

～てもらう · Practice 1

1. カメラを持もっていなかったので、通とおりかかった人ひと____撮とってもらいました。

通とおりかかった人ひとに撮とってもらいました。

The doer of the favour is marked with に in a もらう sentence. The subject slot belongs to the speaker, who is the one receiving the service.

～てもらう · Practice 2

2. Choose: 荷物にもつが届とどかないので、お店みせに電話でんわして調しらべて（もらいました・くれました）。

お店みせに電話でんわして調しらべてもらいました。

The speaker rang up and asked, so the favour was arranged, and the shop is marked with に as the doer. くれました would need the shop as the が-subject and would suggest they checked without being asked.

～てもらう · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Could I have you explain it once more?" (to a client)

もう一度いちどご説明せつめいいただけますでしょうか。／もう一度いちど説明せつめいしていただけませんか。

Upward, もらう becomes いただく, and the potential いただける in a negative or でしょうか question is what turns the receiving frame into a polite request. The お／ご + noun + いただく version is the more formal of the two.

～てもらう · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昨日きのう、友ともだちが宿題しゅくだいを手伝てつだってもらいました。

✅ 昨日きのう、友ともだちに宿題しゅくだいを手伝てつだってもらいました。 （or ✅ 昨日きのう、友ともだちが宿題しゅくだいを手伝てつだってくれました。）

The sentence mixes the two frames. Either keep もらう and mark the friend with に, or keep が on the friend and switch to the giving verb くれる.

～てもらう · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 山田やまださんに空港くうこうまで送おくってもらいました。 (b) 山田やまださんが空港くうこうまで送おくってくれました。

Both describe Yamada driving the speaker to the airport, and both are natural.

(a) puts the speaker in the subject slot and implies the lift was arranged - you asked, or it had been agreed in advance. (b) puts Yamada in the subject slot and presents the lift as something he did for you, which foregrounds his kindness. If you are thanking him, (b) is the frame you want; if you are explaining your travel plans to someone else, (a) is more informative.

～てやる

Meaning · do something for someone below you - and, in a second life, I'll show them: a declaration of defiant intent

～てやる is the bottom rung of the さしあげる／あげる／やる ladder you met at N5, now carrying an action instead of an object. It points downward: to animals, plants, small children, your own younger family. Alongside that, it has a completely separate use with no beneficiary at all, where it means "just watch me do it" - and that second use is far more common in real speech than textbooks let on.

～てやる · Formation

Attach やる to the て-form. やる is a regular う-verb: やります, やった, やらない, やろう.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (favour, downward) て-form + やる 買かう → 買かってやる
Verb (polite) て-form + やります 読よむ → 読よんでやります
Verb (offer / resolve) て-form + やろう 開あける → 開あけてやろう
Verb (resolve, plain) て-form + やる（no に phrase） 勝かつ → 勝かってやる
Contracted, very casual て-form + たる 買かってやる → 買かったる

In the favour sense the particles come from the underlying verb, exactly as with ～てあげる: 弟おとうとに説明せつめいしてやる, 犬いぬを散歩さんぽに連つれて行いってやる.

～てやる · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - if the sentence has a に phrase naming who benefits, it is the favour ～てやる. If it has no beneficiary and the verb is about the speaker's own achievement, it is the resolve ～てやる.

～てやる · Examples

犬いぬを散歩さんぽに**連つれて行いってやった**。
I took the dog for a walk.

猫ねこが外そとに出でたがっているから、窓まどを**開あけてやろう**。
The cat wants to go out, so I'll open the window for her.

弟おとうとが泣ないていたので、アイスを**買かってやった**。
My little brother was crying, so I bought him an ice cream.

子こどもたちが寝ねる前まえに、毎晩まいばん絵本えほんを**読よんでやります**。
Every night I read the children a picture book before bed.

～てやる · Examples (cont.)

今度こんどこそ絶対ぜったいに**勝かってやる**。
This time I'm definitely going to win.

見みていろよ、いつか**見返みかえしてやる**。
Just you wait - one day I'll show them all.

こんな会社かいしゃ、いつかやめてやると思おもいながら働はたらいている。
I go to work thinking that one day I'm going to walk out of this place.

息子むすこが大学だいがくに受うかったら、好すきな自転車じてんしゃを**買かってやろう**と思おもっています。
If my son gets into university, I'm thinking I'll buy him whatever bike he wants.

～てやる · Nuance & register

The favour sense is rough, and it is age- and gender-marked. やる for people was once the ordinary form; it now sounds blunt, and it is noticeably more common in men's speech and in older speakers. A father saying 宿題しゅくだいを見みてやる about his own child is completely normal and even affectionate. The same sentence to a colleague's child would be presumptuous.

Toward animals and plants it is entirely neutral - 花はなに水みずをやる is the standard phrase, though younger speakers increasingly say あげる there too, and some older speakers find that precious. Both are heard; neither will get you into trouble.

～てやる · Nuance & register (cont.)

Where you must not use it: anyone senior, anyone outside your circle, and anyone you are being polite to. ～てやります exists but does not fix the problem, because the rudeness lives in やる, not in the politeness ending. There is no upward version - the ladder goes やる → あげる → さしあげる, and the top rung has its own difficulties (see ～てあげる, which is presumptuous for a different reason).

The resolve sense is the one you will hear in dramas, sports and complaints. It attaches to a verb about the speaker's own action, has no beneficiary, and means roughly "I'm going to do it, and let that be noted." The emotional colour comes from the same source as the favour sense: you are casting your own act as something imposed on the world.

～てやる · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 絶対ぜったいに合格ごうかくしてやる。 - I will pass.
  • こんな仕事しごと、辞やめてやる。 - I quit. (defiant)
  • 文句もんくを言いってやった。 - I gave them a piece of my mind.

That last type shades into threat: 殴なぐってやる is "I'll hit you", not a kindness. Tone and context do all the disambiguating, and it is almost always obvious.

～てやる · Nuance & register (cont.)

Casually - and especially in Kansai speech - ～てやる contracts to ～たる: 買かってやる → 買かったる, 貸かしてやる → 貸かしたる. You will hear it constantly in comedy and in the west of the country; do not write it, and do not use it in an exam.

Note - ～てやる is also how you tell someone to do something for a third party who is below them: 犬いぬにえさをやってください, 弟おとうとに教おしえてやってください. The polite frame is on the outside and the downward frame on the inside, and this is normal, not contradictory.

～てやる · ～てやる vs ～てあげる

Rule of thumb - same direction, different altitude. ～てあげる is for equals and outsiders; ～てやる is for those clearly below you, and for animals.

娘むすめの髪かみを**結むすんでやった**。
I tied my daughter's hair for her.

同僚どうりょうの仕事しごとを**引ひき受うけてあげた**。
I took on a colleague's work for them.

Swap those and both sentences go wrong in opposite ways: あげる about your own small child sounds oddly formal, as if she were a guest; やる about a colleague sounds contemptuous. When you are not sure of the altitude, あげる is the safer error - but remember that neither should be said to the beneficiary's face.

～てやる · ～てやる (resolve) vs ～てみせる

Rule of thumb - both declare determination. ～てやる aims the declaration at the world; ～てみせる aims it at an audience: I'll show you.

来年らいねんは一位いちいを**取とってやる**。
Next year I'm taking first place.

来年らいねんは一位いちいを**取とってみせる**。
Next year I'll show you I can take first place.

～てやる · ～てやる (resolve) vs ～てみせる (cont.)

～てみせる promises a demonstration to someone who doubts you; ～てやる is closer to spite or grit and does not need a doubter. They combine freely - 必かならず勝かってみせてやる is heavy-handed but grammatical.

～てやる · ～てやる vs ～てさしあげる

Rule of thumb - these are the two ends of one vertical scale, and in modern speech both ends are avoided. The safe middle is ～てあげる, or no giving verb at all.

部長ぶちょうの奥おくさまを駅えきまでお送おくりしました。
I saw the department head's wife to the station.

さしあげる is grammatically the humble upward form, but announcing your own kindness to a superior is presumptuous no matter how humble the verb, so speakers use the お + stem + します frame instead. At the other end, やる is fine within the family and rude outside it. The lesson is the same in both directions: the higher the stakes, the less Japanese wants you to name the favour.

～てやる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 先輩せんぱいのコピーを取とってやりました。 → ✅ 先輩せんぱいのコピーを取とっておきました。
    やる points downward. Used about a senior it is close to insulting, and the polite ending does not soften it.

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、荷物にもつを持もってやりましょうか。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、お荷物にもつをお持もちしましょうか。
    Upward offers use the humble お + ます-stem + します frame, with no giving verb.

  • ❌ 弟おとうとを宿題しゅくだいを見みてやった。 → ✅ 弟おとうとの宿題しゅくだいを見みてやった。
    やる does not assign particles. The verb acts on the homework, so the owner takes の and only one を survives.

～てやる · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 絶対ぜったいに合格ごうかくしてやります。（in a formal speech）→ ✅ 絶対ぜったいに合格ごうかくしたいと思おもっています。
    The resolve sense is emotional and confrontational. It does not belong in formal or public register even with ます on the end.

  • ❌ 母ははが私わたしにセーターを編あんでやりました。 → ✅ 母ははが私わたしにセーターを編あんでくれました。
    やる sends the favour away from the speaker, so it can never land on the speaker. Toward you it is always くれる, however junior the doer.

～てやる · Practice 1

1. 息子むすこが自転車じてんしゃに乗のれないので、後うしろを持もっ____。

後うしろを持もってやった。（or 持もってあげた）

The beneficiary is the speaker's own young child, which is やる territory: downward, inside the family, and affectionate rather than rude. あげた is also perfectly acceptable and is what many younger parents say.

～てやる · Practice 2

2. Choose: 課長かちょうに資料しりょうを届とどけて（やりました・差さし上あげました・きました）。

課長かちょうに資料しりょうを届とどけてきました。

やる is rude upward, and さしあげる, though humble in form, announces your own kindness to a superior. The natural sentence drops the giving verb altogether - here 届とどけてきました, "I went and delivered it."

～てやる · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'll never give up. I'm going to finish this."

絶対ぜったいにあきらめない。やり遂とげてやる。（or 最後さいごまでやってやる）

This is the resolve ～てやる: no beneficiary, the speaker's own action, and a defiant edge. In polite speech you would soften it to やり遂とげたいと思おもいます.

～てやる · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 田中たなかさんが忙いそがしそうだったので、仕事しごとを手伝てつだってやりました。

✅ 田中たなかさんが忙いそがしそうだったので、仕事しごとを手伝てつだいました。

Tanaka is a peer, not someone below you, so やる is out of place and sounds scornful. 手伝てつだってあげました is possible when narrating to a third party, but the plainest version, with no giving verb, is what you would actually say.

～てやる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 必かならず終おわらせます。 (b) 必かならず終おわらせてやる。

(a) is a plain polite commitment: the work will be finished. It is what you say to a manager, and it carries no emotion beyond reliability.

(b) is the resolve ～てやる. It adds defiance - against a deadline, a doubter, or the situation itself - and it is plain, emphatic and slightly aggressive. You mutter it to yourself or say it among friends; saying it to your boss would sound like you were picking a fight.

～てほしい

Meaning · I want you (or someone else) to do something

At N5 you learned two ways to want: ～たい for an action you will perform yourself, and ほしい for an object. ～てほしい fills the remaining square - an action performed by somebody else. The wish is still yours, so the first-person restriction you learned with たい carries over unchanged.

～てほしい · Formation

Attach ほしい to the て-form. ほしい is an い-adjective and conjugates like one.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb て-form + ほしい 手伝てつだう → 手伝てつだってほしい
Verb (negative wish) ない-form + でほしい 言いう → 言いわないでほしい
Polite ～てほしいです 来くる → 来きてほしいです
Past ほしい → ほしかった 分わかる → 分わかってほしかった
Negative ほしい → ほしくない 忘わすれる → 忘わすれてほしくない
Softened request ～てほしいんですが／～てほしいんだけど 見みる → 見みてほしいんですが
Third person's wish ～てほしいと言いっている／～てほしがっている 直なおす → 直なおしてほしいと言いっています

～てほしい · Formation (cont.)

The person you want to act takes に. The wisher is the topic, and as usual is dropped when it is you: （私わたしは）部長ぶちょうに確認かくにんしてほしい。

Rule of thumb - negatives split. "I want you not to do it" is ～ないで**ほしい**; "I don't want you to do it" is ～て**ほしくない**. The first is a request, the second a rejection, and learners routinely swap them.

Note - the subject of ～てほしい does not have to be a person. 雨あめがやんでほしい and 電車でんしゃが時間じかんどおりに来きてほしい are both natural: you can wish an outcome onto the world. With inanimate subjects the particle is が, not に.

～てほしい · Examples

ちょっと**待まってほしい**。
I want you to wait a moment.

明日あしたは**晴はれてほしい**な。
I hope it clears up tomorrow.

これは誰だれにも**言いわないでほしい**。
I don't want you telling anyone about this.

息子むすこにはもっと外そとで**遊あそんでほしい**。
I'd like my son to play outside more.

～てほしい · Examples (cont.)

静しずかにしてほしいんですが、下したの階かいの人ひとに言いいにくくて。
I'd like them to keep it down, but it's hard to say anything to the people downstairs.

上司じょうしに**分わかってほしかった**ことが、うまく伝つたえられなかった。
I couldn't get across the thing I wanted my boss to understand.

会議かいぎの資料しりょうは金曜日きんようびまでに**出だしてほしい**んですが、間まに合あいますか。
I'd like the meeting materials in by Friday - will you manage it?

こんなに楽たのしい時間じかんが**終おわらないでほしい**と心こころから思おもった。
I found myself wishing with all my heart that this happy time would never end.

～てほしい · Nuance & register

～てほしい is direct. It states, out loud, what you want another person to do. Between friends and family, and downward at work, that is ordinary. Aimed upward it is too bald - you are placing a demand on someone senior - and the polite ending does not help, because the directness is in the construction, not in the ending. ❌部長ぶちょう、確認かくにんしてほしいです is grammatical and will make the room uncomfortable.

The standard softeners, from lightest to heaviest:

～てほしい · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ～てほしいんだけど / ～てほしいんですが - trails off and
    leaves the ask implied. Very common.
  • ～てほしいなあ - turns the demand into a wistful wish. Friendly.
  • ～てもらいたい - the receiving frame; a shade more indirect, because
    it talks about what you would receive rather than what they should do.
  • ～ていただきたい - the humble version, and the normal business form:
    ご確認かくにんいただきたいのですが。
  • ～ていただけませんか - a question rather than a statement, and the
    politest of the set.

The negative wish ～ないでほしい is worth practising, because English "I want you not to…" is rare and learners avoid the structure. 心配しんぱいしないでほしい, 無理むりをしないでほしい - both are warm, not scolding.

～てほしい · Nuance & register (cont.)

Third person. As with たい, you cannot flatly assert someone else's wish in a main clause: ❌妹いもうとは父ちちに来きてほしいです. Route it through reported speech or evidence - 来きてほしいと言いっています, 来きてほしいようです. The form ～てほしがっている exists but is much less natural than the simple ほしがる for objects; most speakers avoid it.

Register: ～てほしい is plain, ～てほしいです polite, and neither is gender-marked. In writing - opinion essays, editorials - it is extremely common as a way of stating what society or government ought to do: 国くににはもっと説明せつめいしてほしい。

～てほしい · ～てほしい vs ～たい

Rule of thumb - ～たい is I do it; ～てほしい is you do it. Same wish, different actor.

来週らいしゅう、京都きょうとへ**行いきたい**。
I want to go to Kyoto next week.

来週らいしゅう、京都きょうとへ**行いってほしい**。
I want you to go to Kyoto next week.

Both are い-adjectives and both are restricted to the speaker's own wishes. The difference is only who does the verb - but English "want" collapses the two, so the error is common and the resulting sentence is badly wrong rather than merely odd.

～てほしい · ～てほしい vs ～てもらいたい・～ていただきたい

Rule of thumb - same meaning, rising politeness. てほしい states the wish bare; てもらいたい routes it through receiving; ていただきたい does that humbly.

明日あしたまでに**返事へんじしてほしい**。
I want an answer by tomorrow.

明日あしたまでに**返事へんじしていただきたい**のですが。
I would like to receive your answer by tomorrow.

The second is what you write in an email to a client. Note that もらいたい and いただきたい are the desiderative of もらう／いただく - verbs, so their subject is you, the receiver, while the other person keeps に. The particle pattern is identical to てほしい, which makes them easy to substitute.

～てほしい · ～てほしい vs ～てください

Rule of thumb - ～てください tells someone to do it; ～てほしい says you want it done. A wish is not an instruction, and the two are not interchangeable upward.

ここに名前なまえを**書かいてください**。
Please write your name here.

ここに名前なまえを**書かいてほしい**んです。
I'd like you to write your name here.

ください is polite in form but is still an imperative, which is why it is fine from staff to customer and awkward from junior to superior. ～てほしいんですが is softer because it stops short of telling anyone to do anything - it puts the wish on the table and lets the listener pick it up.

～てほしい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 友ともだちに来きたいです。 → ✅ 友ともだちに**来きてほしいです**。
    たい can only describe an action you perform. When somebody else does the verb, the wish takes ～てほしい.

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんは私わたしに手伝てつだってほしいです。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんは私わたしに手伝てつだってほしいと言いっています。
    A third person's wish cannot be asserted flatly, exactly as with たい.

  • ❌ 心配しんぱいしてほしくない。（meaning "please don't worry"）→ ✅ 心配しんぱいしないでほしい。
    ～てほしくない rejects the wish; ～ないでほしい wishes for the non-action. Only the second one asks them to stop worrying.

～てほしい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 社長しゃちょう、この書類しょるいを見みてほしいです。 → ✅ 社長しゃちょう、この書類しょるいを見みていただけませんか。
    Grammatical, and far too direct upward. The humble request form is what the situation calls for.

  • ❌ 雨あめにやんでほしい。 → ✅ 雨あめがやんでほしい。
    に marks a person you are asking to act. An inanimate subject takes が.

～てほしい · Practice 1

1. うるさいので、もう少すこし静しずかに話はなし____ほしいです。

静しずかに話はなしてほしいです。

ほしい attaches to the て-form when the wish is about somebody else's action. Note that the polite です sits on ほしい, because ほしい is an い-adjective and carries the sentence.

～てほしい · Practice 2

2. Choose: 部長ぶちょう、明日あしたの会議かいぎに（出でてほしいです・ご出席しゅっせきいただけませんか）。

部長ぶちょう、明日あしたの会議かいぎにご出席しゅっせきいただけませんか。

Stating a wish about a superior's behaviour is too direct, however polite the ending. The humble potential turns the same content into a request that leaves the department head free to say no.

～てほしい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I don't want you to worry about me."

私わたしのことは**心配しんぱいしないでほしい**。

The wish is for a non-action, so it takes ～ないでほしい. 心配しんぱいしてほしくない would be understood, but it reads as rejecting the worry rather than asking for it to stop.

～てほしい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 弟おとうとは母ははに新あたらしいゲームを買かってほしいです。

✅ 弟おとうとは母ははに新あたらしいゲームを買かってほしがっています。 （more natural: ✅ …買かってほしいと言いっています。）

ほしい is an い-adjective of desire, and Japanese does not let you assert another person's inner state directly in a plain main clause. Report it, or mark it as observed.

～てほしい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 明日あしたまでに返事へんじをください。 (b) 明日あしたまでに返事へんじしてほしいんですが。

(a) is an instruction. ください is polite in form but imperative in force, so it suits a situation where you are entitled to ask - a shop to a customer, a teacher to a class, a manager to a team.

(b) states a need and stops. Because it never tells the listener to act, it leaves them room to negotiate the deadline, and the trailing んですが invites them to respond. It is what you use sideways or upward, where issuing instructions would be out of place.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか

Meaning · could you do that for me? - the everyday polite request, built on the giving-and-receiving verbs

A Japanese request is not an imperative with "please" stuck on it. It is a question about a favour: either "will you give me this action?" （～てくれませんか）or "could I receive this action?"（～てもらえませんか）. Once you see that, the whole ladder from ～てくれない? up to ～ていただけないでしょうか is one structure with the politeness dial turned.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Formation

Both families take the て-form. くれる is used as it stands; もらう goes into the potential, もらえる, because you are asking whether receiving the favour is possible.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (giving frame) て-form + くれる? 開あける → 開あけてくれる?
Verb (giving frame, negative) て-form + くれない? 貸かす → 貸かしてくれない?
Verb (giving frame, polite) て-form + くれませんか 待まつ → 待まってくれませんか
Verb (receiving frame) て-form + もらえる? 来くる → 来きてもらえる?
Verb (receiving frame, polite) て-form + もらえますか 手伝てつだう → 手伝てつだってもらえますか
Verb (receiving frame, politest) て-form + もらえませんか 見みる → 見みてもらえませんか
Negative request ない-form + でくれませんか／でもらえませんか 入はいる → 入はいらないでもらえませんか

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - two dials, and they turn independently. Dial 1: frame. くれ- asks the listener to give; もらえ- asks whether you may receive. Receiving is more indirect, so もらえ- is politer than くれ- at every matching rung. Dial 2: negative. A negative question - くれない?, くれませんか, もらえませんか - is politer than the affirmative, because it builds in room to say no.

Put the two dials together and you get the ladder learners need memorised as a single sequence:

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Formation (cont.)

Rung Form Who you say it to
1 待まって close friends, family; blunt
2 待まってくれる? friends
3 待まってくれない? friends; softer than rung 2
4 待まってもらえる? friends; softer still
5 待まってくれませんか polite, but the listener is the giver - slightly direct
6 待まってもらえませんか polite, standard with strangers and colleagues
7 待まっていただけませんか humble; superiors, customers, business
8 待まっていただけないでしょうか the top rung; a real imposition

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Examples

その本ほんを**取とってくれない**?
Could you pass me that book?

ペンを**貸かしてもらえませんか**。
Could you lend me a pen?

もう少すこし大おおきい声こえで**話はなしてくれませんか**。
Could you speak up a bit?

すみません、窓まどを**開あけてもらえませんか**。暑あつくて。
Excuse me, could you open the window? It's stifling.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Examples (cont.)

机つくえの上うえに**置おいてくれませんか**。あとで自分じぶんで片かたづけます。
Could you just leave it on the desk? I'll sort it out later myself.

あさっての会議かいぎ、代かわりに**出でてもらえない**かな。
I don't suppose you could go to the meeting the day after tomorrow instead of me?

急きゅうに用事ようじができたので、約束やくそくを来週らいしゅうに**変かえてもらえませんか**。
Something's come up, so could we move our appointment to next week?

申もうし訳わけないんですが、夜よる遅おそい時間じかんに洗濯機せんたくきを**使つかわないでもらえませんか**。
I'm sorry to ask, but could you not run the washing machine late at night?

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Nuance & register

Why negatives are politer. ～てもらえますか asks whether the favour is possible; ～てもらえませんか asks whether it is not possible. The second one presents refusal as the expected answer, so agreeing is a generosity rather than a duty. Japanese politeness runs on exactly this move, and it is why the longest forms are the politest: every extra layer is another step away from a bare demand.

The くれ-／もらえ- split is small but real. Both are polite; くれませんか puts the listener in the subject slot and so points at them a little more directly, while もらえませんか talks about what you would receive. With strangers and in service situations, もらえませんか is the safer default. Inside a team, くれませんか is perfectly normal and sounds slightly warmer, because くれる carries the same gratitude it always does.

Softening prefaces matter as much as the ending. Real requests almost never start cold:

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • すみませんが… - the general-purpose opener
  • 悪わるいんだけど… - casual, "sorry to ask"
  • 申もうし訳わけありませんが… - formal
  • お忙いそがしいところ恐おそれ入いりますが… - business, for a real imposition
  • ちょっとお願ねがいがあるんですが… - announces that a request is coming

Trailing off is also standard: 「手伝てつだってもらえませんか…」 with the sentence left hanging is softer than finishing it crisply.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register warnings. The plain imperative くれ（待まってくれ）is rough and male-marked; do not use it. ～てくれる? and ～てもらえる? with rising intonation are casual and completely normal among friends of any gender. The affirmative ～てくれますか is fine but a touch businesslike - the tone of a form or an announcement.

Note - do not stack. ❌待まってくれてもらえませんか is a beginner error produced by trying to be extra polite. One giving verb per request; climb the ladder instead of doubling up.

～てくれませんか vs ～てもらえませんか

Rule of thumb - same politeness band, different camera. くれませんか asks you to give; もらえませんか asks whether I may receive. The receiving frame is the more distant, and therefore slightly politer.

この漢字かんじの読よみ方かたを**教おしえてくれませんか**。
Would you tell me how to read this kanji?

この漢字かんじの読よみ方かたを**教おしえてもらえませんか**。
Could I get you to tell me how to read this kanji?

Both are perfectly usable to a classmate or a colleague. The gap only becomes important as the social distance grows: to a stranger on the street, choose the もらえ- version, and to your section chief, climb one more rung to いただけませんか.

Affirmative vs negative: ～てもらえますか vs ～てもらえませんか

Rule of thumb - the negative question is the politer one. Adding ません does not make it a refusal; it makes it an offer of one.

すみません、この荷物にもつを上うえまで**運はこんでもらえますか**。
Excuse me, can you carry this luggage upstairs?

すみません、この荷物にもつを上うえまで**運はこんでもらえませんか**。
Excuse me, would you mind carrying this luggage upstairs?

The first is brisk and appropriate when the favour is part of the person's job. The second treats the favour as optional, which is what you want when you are asking for something extra, or from someone who owes you nothing. English does the same thing with "can you" versus "would you mind".

～てもらえませんか vs ～てください

Rule of thumb - ～てください is an instruction dressed politely. The もらえ-／くれ- forms are questions, and questions can be declined.

こちらに**並ならんでください**。
Please queue here.

すみません、こちらに**並ならんでもらえませんか**。
Sorry - would you mind queueing over here?

The first is what a member of staff says to the public: they have the standing to direct people. The second is what one customer says to another, because no such standing exists. Learners overuse ～てください for exactly this reason - it is the first request form taught, and it is the one that carries authority.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 手伝てつだってもらいませんか。 → ✅ 手伝てつだってもらえませんか。
    The request form uses the potential もらえる. もらいませんか would be an invitation - "shall we not receive?" - and does not work as a request.

  • ❌ 待まってくれられませんか。 → ✅ 待まってくれませんか。
    くれる is not put into the potential in this construction. If you want the potential, switch to the receiving frame: 待まってもらえませんか.

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、この書類しょるいを見みてくれませんか。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、この書類しょるいを見みていただけませんか。
    Rungs 5 and 6 are polite but not humble. A superior needs the いただく rung.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 静しずかにしてくれませんでしたか。 → ✅ 静しずかにしてくれませんか。
    The request is about now, not the past. The negative here is a politeness device, not a tense.

  • ❌ ここでたばこを**吸すってもらえませんか**。（meaning "please don't smoke"）→ ✅ ここでたばこを**吸すわないでもらえませんか**。
    To ask for a non-action, negate the verb, not the request ending.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Practice 1

1. すみません、この字じが読よめないので、読よんで____か。

読よんでもらえませんか。（or 読よんでくれませんか）

The potential of もらう plus a negative question is the standard polite request. To a stranger the もらえ- frame is the safer of the two, because it asks about your own receiving rather than pointing at what the listener should do.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Practice 2

2. Choose: 社長しゃちょう、こちらにサインを（してくれませんか・していただけませんか）。

社長しゃちょう、こちらにサインをしていただけませんか。

くれませんか is polite but not humble, and a company president sits well above the rung it occupies. いただく is the humble verb for receiving from above, and its potential-negative question is the normal business request.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Sorry to bother you, but could you take a photo of us?"

すみません、写真しゃしんを**撮とってもらえませんか**。

The opener すみません is not optional politeness padding - a bare request to a stranger sounds abrupt without it. もらえませんか is the right rung for someone you have never met but are not obliged to honour.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: あの、うるさいので、音楽おんがくを止とめてもらいませんか。

✅ あの、うるさいので、音楽おんがくを止とめてもらえませんか。

もらいませんか is the plain negative of もらう and reads as a strange invitation. Requests need the potential form もらえる, which asks whether the favour is available to you.

～てもらえませんか・～てくれませんか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 手伝てつだってくれる? (b) 手伝てつだってもらえませんか。

(a) is rung 2 on the ladder: plain, affirmative, giving frame. It is what you say to a friend or a sibling, and it assumes a relationship where small favours pass back and forth without ceremony.

(b) is rung 6: polite, negative, receiving frame. Every one of those three choices adds distance - ます politeness, a negative that invites refusal, and a frame that talks about your receiving rather than their giving. It is the version for a colleague you do not know well, or for a favour large enough that you want to make saying no easy.

～ていただけませんか

Meaning · could you please do this for me? - the humble, business-standard request

～ていただけませんか is the top of the request ladder that starts at ～てくれない?. Every piece of it is doing work: いただく is the humble verb for receiving, いただける is its potential ("may I receive"), and ませんか is a negative question that hands the listener an easy way out. Stack all three and you have the sentence that runs Japanese offices.

～ていただけませんか · Formation

Attach いただく to the て-form, put it in the potential, and ask the question in the negative.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb て-form + いただけませんか 書かく → 書かいていただけませんか
Verb (lighter) て-form + いただけますか 来くる → 来きていただけますか
Verb (heaviest) て-form + いただけないでしょうか 変かえる → 変かえていただけないでしょうか
Verb (negative request) ない-form + でいただけませんか 入はいる → 入はいらないでいただけませんか
Verb (statement form) て-form + いただきたいのですが 直なおす → 直なおしていただきたいのですが
Honorific-humble frame お + ます-stem + いただけませんか 待まつ → お待まちいただけませんか
する-verbs in that frame ご + noun + いただけませんか 確認かくにんする → ご確認かくにんいただけませんか

～ていただけませんか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the length is the politeness. Each of the four rungs below is longer than the one before, and each is politer: いただけますか → いただけませんか → いただけないでしょうか → いただけますと幸さいわいです.

Note - the person you are asking takes に, exactly as with もらう, and is almost always dropped because it is obvious: （先生せんせいに）推薦状すいせんじょうを書かいていただけませんか。

～ていただけませんか · Examples

もう一度いちど言いっていただけませんか。
Could you say that once more, please?

ここに住所じゅうしょを**書かいていただけますか**。
Could you write your address here?

すみませんが、少々しょうしょうお待まちいただけますか。
I'm sorry - could you wait just a moment?

資料しりょうを明日あしたの朝あさまでに**送おくっていただけないでしょうか**。
Would it be possible for you to send the materials by tomorrow morning?

～ていただけませんか · Examples (cont.)

申もうし訳わけありませんが、日ひにちを**変かえていただけませんか**。
I do apologise, but could we change the date?

工事こうじの音おとがひどいので、夜よる八時はちじ以降いこうはやめていただけませんか。
The construction noise is terrible, so could you stop after eight in the evening?

お忙いそがしいところ恐おそれ入いりますが、この書類しょるいに目めを**通とおしていただけないでしょうか**。
I'm sorry to trouble you when you're busy, but might I ask you to look over this document?

来週らいしゅうの打うち合あわせですが、時間じかんを三時さんじにしていただけると助たすかります。
About next week's meeting - it would be a great help if you could make it three o'clock.

～ていただけませんか · Nuance & register

This is business Japanese's default request, and it is worth over-learning. Emails, phone calls, service counters, and any conversation with a customer or a superior run on it. Used with a friend it sounds like you are joking or being cold.

The pieces are separable, and knowing which piece you are turning lets you fine-tune:

～ていただけませんか · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • いただける vs いただけます - plain vs polite potential. In a request you
    almost always want the polite one.
  • negative vs affirmative - いただけませんか is politer than
    いただけますか because refusal is written into the question.
  • でしょうか instead of か - adds hesitancy. いただけないでしょうか is
    the form for a genuine imposition: a deadline change, a favour outside someone's job.
  • statement instead of question - していただきたいのですが trails
    off and leaves the request unspoken. Softer again, and very common in speech.
  • conditional endings - いただけると助たすかります ("it would
    help"), いただけますと幸さいわいです ("I would be grateful"), いただければと思おもいます. These are email register; the last two are stiff enough that you would rarely say them out loud.

～ていただけませんか · Nuance & register (cont.)

The お／ご frame is politer than the plain て-form version. お待まちいただけませんか outranks 待まっていただけませんか, and ご記入きにゅういただけませんか outranks 記入きにゅうしていただけませんか. Use お with native-Japanese verbs （お書かき, お使つかい）and ご with Sino-Japanese nouns （ご連絡れんらく, ご確認かくにん）. There are exceptions - お電話でんわ, お返事へんじ - that simply have to be learned.

Two things to keep apart. ～ていただけませんか asks the other person to act. ～させていただけませんか asks for permission for you to act: 早はやく帰かえらせていただけませんか, "would you allow me to leave early?" The middle syllables are almost identical and the meanings are opposite.

～ていただけませんか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Spoken register also compresses. In quick service speech you will hear 少々しょうしょうお待まちいただけますでしょうか - technically doubled politeness （ます plus でしょう）that prescriptivists dislike and everyone uses.

～ていただけませんか · ～ていただけませんか vs ～てもらえませんか

Rule of thumb - identical structure, one step apart on the humility scale. もらう is neutral; いただく is humble and marks the other person as above you.

田中たなかさん、この数字すうじを**確たしかめてもらえませんか**。
Tanaka, could you check these figures?

課長かちょう、この数字すうじを**確たしかめていただけませんか**。
Sir, could you check these figures?

Nothing changes but the verb, and the social reading changes completely. Using もらえませんか upward is not rude in the way a bare imperative is - it is more like calling your manager by their first name in a formal meeting: nothing is broken, but you have misjudged the room.

～ていただけませんか vs ～ていただけないでしょうか

Rule of thumb - でしょうか adds doubt, and doubt reads as deference. Save it for requests you would feel awkward making.

会議かいぎの時間じかんを少すこし遅おくらせていただけませんか。
Could we push the meeting back a little?

会議かいぎの時間じかんを少すこし遅おくらせていただけないでしょうか。
Might it be at all possible to push the meeting back a little?

Both are correct and both are polite. The second sounds more apologetic and is what you use when you are asking someone to rearrange their day. Overusing it for small things - asking for a pen - sounds theatrical.

～ていただけませんか · ～ていただけませんか vs お～ください

Rule of thumb - お～ください is honorific but still an instruction; ～ていただけませんか is a question. Instructions suit signs and staff; questions suit asking a favour.

こちらでお待まちください。
Please wait here.

こちらでお待まちいただけませんか。
Would you mind waiting here?

The first is what a receptionist says - perfectly polite, and it presumes compliance because directing visitors is her job. The second asks. Learners default to ください because it is the first request form they meet, and end up issuing gentle orders to their boss.

～ていただけませんか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 待まっていただきませんか。 → ✅ 待まっていただけませんか。
    The request needs the potential いただける. いただきませんか reads as a strange invitation, not a request.

  • ❌ 書かいていただけましたか。（as a request）→ ✅ 書かいていただけませんか。
    The past tense turns it into a question about whether it has already been done. A request stays in the non-past.

  • ❌ 明日あした、休やすんでいただけませんか。（meaning "may I take tomorrow off?"）→ ✅ 明日あした、休やすませていただけませんか。
    ～ていただく asks the other person to act. Asking permission for your own action needs the causative ～させていただく.

～ていただけませんか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ ここで写真しゃしんを**撮とっていただけませんか**。（meaning "please don't take photos"）→ ✅ ここで写真しゃしんを**撮とらないでいただけませんか**。
    To ask someone to refrain, negate the verb. Negating only the ending still asks them to do it.

  • ❌ お待まちしていただけませんか。 → ✅ お待まちいただけませんか。
    お + stem + する is the humble form for your own actions. When the other person is the one waiting, the frame is お + stem + いただく, with no する.

～ていただけませんか · Practice 1

1. 恐おそれ入いりますが、こちらの用紙ようしにご記入きにゅう____か。

ご記入きにゅういただけませんか。（or いただけますでしょうか）

ご + Sino-Japanese noun + いただく is the honorific-humble frame, and the potential-negative question turns it into a polite request. This is the exact sentence you hear at a bank or a clinic counter.

～ていただけませんか · Practice 2

2. Choose: 部長ぶちょう、来週らいしゅうの予定よていを（教おしえてもらえませんか・教おしえていただけませんか）。

部長ぶちょう、来週らいしゅうの予定よていを**教おしえていただけませんか**。

Both are polite requests with the same structure, but only いただく marks the department head as above you. もらえませんか would not offend, yet it would sound like you had misread the workplace.

～ていただけませんか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Would it be possible for you to reply by the end of the week?" (email to a client)

今週中こんしゅうちゅうにご返事へんじいただけないでしょうか。 （or いただけますと幸さいわいです。）

A deadline imposed on a client is a real imposition, so the heavier でしょうか rung fits. The いただけますと幸さいわいです version is email register and sounds slightly warmer while asking for the same thing.

～ていただけませんか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: すみません、ちょっと手伝てつだっていただきませんか。

✅ すみません、ちょっと手伝てつだっていただけませんか。

いただきません is the plain negative of いただく and does not form a request. The construction depends on the potential いただける - you are asking whether the favour is available to you, not whether you will fail to receive it.

～ていただけませんか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 少々しょうしょうお待まちください。 (b) 少々しょうしょうお待まちいただけないでしょうか。

(a) is an honorific instruction. It is entirely polite and is the standard line from staff to customer, because a receptionist directing a visitor is doing her job - compliance is assumed, and no answer is expected.

(b) is a question, and a heavily hedged one. It presents waiting as a favour the listener may decline, which is what you want when you have no standing to direct them: asking a client to hold on while you fix your own mistake, for instance. Said by a receptionist to an ordinary visitor it would sound oddly abject.

～てすみません

Meaning · I'm sorry for doing (or not doing) that - an apology attached to the specific act

Bare すみません apologises for nothing in particular. ～てすみません names the offence: the て-form supplies the cause, すみません supplies the apology, and the two are joined by the ordinary "reason" use of the て-form you already know from 遅おくれて困こまりました. What makes it worth its own page is that Japanese reaches for this construction in places where English would say thank you.

～てすみません · Formation

The て-form of whatever caused the trouble, then the apology word.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb て-form + すみません 遅おくれる → 遅おくれてすみません
Verb (negative) なくて + すみません 行いく → 行いけなくてすみません
Verb (past act) て-form + すみませんでした 休やすむ → 休やすんですみませんでした
い-adjective くて + すみません 遅おそい → 遅おそくてすみません
な-adjective で + すみません 下手へた → 下手へたですみません
Noun で + すみません この字じ → 汚きたない字じですみません
Casual て + ごめん／ごめんね 待またせる → 待またせてごめん
Formal て + 申もうし訳わけありません 遅おくれる → 遅おくれて申もうし訳わけありません

～てすみません · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - すみません for a thing that is still going on, and すみませんでした for a thing that is finished. Arriving late, you say 遅おくれてすみません as you come in; the next day you say 昨日きのうは遅おくれてすみませんでした.

Note - すみません is the standard written and careful-speech form. すいません is the same word as it is actually pronounced in casual speech. Say it, do not write it.

～てすみません · Examples

遅おくれてすみません。
Sorry I'm late.

うるさくしてすみません。
Sorry for the noise.

昨日きのうは急きゅうに**休やすんですみませんでした**。
I'm sorry for taking the day off at short notice yesterday.

電話でんわに**出でられなくてすみません**でした。会議かいぎ中ちゅうだったんです。
Sorry I couldn't pick up - I was in a meeting.

～てすみません · Examples (cont.)

何度なんども**聞きいてすみません**が、もう一度いちど住所じゅうしょを教おしえてください。
Sorry to keep asking, but could you give me the address once more?

わざわざ来きていただいてすみません。近ちかくまで送おくりますよ。
It's very good of you to have come all this way. Let me give you a lift part of the way back.

手伝てつだっていただいたのに、お礼れいも**言いわなくてすみませんでした**。
I'm sorry I never even thanked you after you helped me out.

つまらないことで時間じかんを**取とらせてしまってすみません**でした。
I'm sorry to have taken up your time with something so trivial.

～てすみません · Nuance & register

The apology ladder. Four rungs, and they are not interchangeable:

  • ～てごめん／ごめんね - casual, friends and family. Warm.
  • ～てすみません - the everyday polite apology. Works almost everywhere.
  • ～て申もうし訳わけありません - formal; business, customers,
    real mistakes.
  • ～て申もうし訳わけございません - the most formal; service
    industry and written apologies.

すみません is the safe default. ごめんなさい sits between rungs one and two: it is politer than ごめん but has a slightly childlike, personal quality, and it is odd in a business setting. Note also that ごめん cannot take the past-tense trick - there is no ❌ごめんでした.

～てすみません · Nuance & register (cont.)

Japanese apologises where English thanks. This is the point that catches learners hardest. When someone has gone out of their way for you, Japanese often acknowledges the trouble you caused them rather than the good they did you:

  • 重おもい荷物にもつを持もっていただいてすみません。
  • こんなに遅おそくまで付つき合あわせてすみません。

Both would be "thank you" in English. ありがとうございます is also correct in these situations and is increasingly common among younger speakers; the すみません version sounds more considerate and slightly more formal. Using both together - すみません、ありがとうございます - is entirely normal.

～てすみません · Nuance & register (cont.)

Negatives. For an apology about something you failed to do, the form is ～なくてすみません, built on the plain negative plus て: 返事へんじが遅おそくなってすみません, 手伝てつだえなくてすみません. ～ないですみません is not used.

Common set phrases worth learning whole, because their internal grammar is opaque until you have seen it:

  • お待またせしてすみません - sorry to have kept you waiting (causative + humble)
  • ご迷惑めいわくをおかけしてすみません - sorry for the trouble I've caused
  • 無理むりを言いってすみません - sorry to ask something unreasonable

～てすみません · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register: すみません itself is neutral, used by all speakers. すまない is its plain form and is male-marked and gruff. In Kansai speech すんません and かんにん turn up; both are regional and casual.

～てすみません · ～てすみません vs ～てありがとう

Rule of thumb - ありがとう looks at what the other person gave you; すみません looks at what it cost them. Both can describe the same kindness.

駅えきまで送おくってくれてありがとう。
Thanks for driving me to the station.

駅えきまで送おくっていただいてすみません。
I'm sorry to have made you drive me to the station.

～てすみません · ～てすみません vs ～てありがとう (cont.)

Both are natural and both are grateful. The first is warmer and more direct - the normal choice between friends. The second acknowledges the imposition, which reads as more considerate and fits an unequal relationship or a genuinely large favour. Note the pairing that native speakers keep: くれて with thanks, because thanks point at the giver's act, and いただいて with an apology, because the apology points at your receiving.

～てすみません · ～てすみません vs ～て申し訳ありません

Rule of thumb - 申もうし訳わけありません is for real damage. If nobody was actually harmed, すみません is enough, and the heavier form sounds melodramatic.

ペンをお借かりします。使つかわせてもらってすみません。
Let me borrow your pen - sorry to use your things.

こちらの確認かくにん不足ぶそくでご迷惑めいわくをおかけして申もうし訳わけありませんでした。
I sincerely apologise for the trouble caused by our failure to check properly.

～てすみません · ～てすみません vs ～て申し訳ありません (cont.)

In business the two also split by role: すみません is what you say about your own small slips, while 申もうし訳わけありません is what you say on behalf of your company. A customer-facing apology almost never uses すみません.

～てすみません · ～てすみません vs ～てしまってすみません

Rule of thumb - inserting しまって says the act was unintended or regrettable. It makes the apology sound more sincere, and it is very common.

大切たいせつな本ほんを汚よごしてすみません。
Sorry for getting your important book dirty.

大切たいせつな本ほんを汚よごしてしまってすみません。
I'm so sorry - I've gone and got your important book dirty.

～てすみません · ～てすみません vs ～てしまってすみません (cont.)

～てしまう adds the sense of "and now it's done, and I wish it weren't". Because apologies are exactly the situation where that feeling belongs, the combination is almost formulaic: 遅おくれてしまってすみません, 忘わすれてしまって申もうし訳わけありません.

～てすみません · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 来こないですみません。 → ✅ 来こられなくてすみません。
    A negative reason takes なくて, not ないで. ないで marks manner, not cause.

  • ❌ 遅おくれるすみません。 → ✅ 遅おくれてすみません。
    The apology attaches to the て-form. The dictionary form cannot sit in front of すみません.

  • ❌ 昨日きのうはごめんでした。 → ✅ 昨日きのうはごめんね。／昨日きのうはすみませんでした。
    ごめん has no past form. If you need the past, move up the ladder to すみませんでした.

  • ❌ お客きゃくさま、大変たいへんお待またせしてすみません。 → ✅ お客きゃくさま、大変たいへんお待またせして**申もうし訳わけございません**。
    To a customer, すみません is too light. Service register uses 申もうし訳わけございません.

～てすみません · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 荷物にもつを持もっていただいてすみましたか。 → ✅ 荷物にもつを持もっていただいてすみません。
    すみません is a fixed apology expression here. Do not try to conjugate it like the verb 済すむ it came from.

～てすみません · Practice 1

1. 返事へんじが遅おそく____すみませんでした。

返事へんじが遅おそくなってすみませんでした。

The cause is expressed with the て-form, and here the cause is a change of state: 遅おそくなる → 遅おそくなって. The past でした fits because the delay is over.

～てすみません · Practice 2

2. Choose: 傘かさを貸かして（くれてありがとう・いただいてすみません）。You are talking to a close friend.

傘かさを貸かしてくれてありがとう。

Between friends, thanks are direct and the giving verb is くれる. The いただいてすみません version is not wrong, but its combination of humble verb and apology creates a formal distance that would sound strange with someone close.

～てすみません · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'm sorry I couldn't come to the party yesterday."

昨日きのうはパーティーに**行いけなくてすみませんでした**。

Two things to get right: the negative reason takes なくて, and the event is finished, so the apology is すみませんでした. Japanese normally says 行いけなくて rather than 来こられなくて when talking about a place you did not go to.

～てすみません · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 会議かいぎに遅おくれないですみません。

✅ 会議かいぎに遅おくれてすみません。

Two problems. The negative was inserted by mistake - you are apologising for being late, not for not being late - and even for a genuine negative the reason form is なくて, never ないで.

～てすみません · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 手伝てつだってくれてありがとう。 (b) 手伝てつだっていただいてすみません。

Both respond to the same help, and both are appropriate responses to it.

(a) is gratitude, pure and simple. It points at what the other person did for you, and its plain form makes it friendly and equal.

(b) apologises instead: it points at the trouble your problem caused them. Combined with the humble いただく it marks the helper as senior or as having gone substantially out of their way. Japanese uses this move constantly where English would only ever say thank you, and getting comfortable with it is one of the clearer steps from correct Japanese to natural Japanese.

～てよかった

Meaning · I'm glad I did that; it's a good thing that happened - relief, looking back

～てよかった evaluates something that has actually happened and finds it good. The feeling underneath is relief: it could have gone the other way, and it didn't. That backward glance is what separates it from a plain "I'm happy", and it is why the past tense よかった is fixed even when the good thing is still going on.

～てよかった · Formation

The て-form of the thing that happened, then よかった - the past of いい／よい.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb て-form + よかった 来くる → 来きてよかった
Verb (negative) なくて + よかった 行いく → 行いかなくてよかった
い-adjective くて + よかった 安やすい → 安やすくてよかった
な-adjective で + よかった 元気げんき → 元気げんきでよかった
Noun で + よかった いい天気てんき → いい天気てんきでよかった
Polite ～てよかったです 習ならう → 習ならってよかったです
Reported ～てよかったと思おもう 買かう → 買かってよかったと思おもう

～てよかった · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - よかった is always past, even about the present or the future. 明日あした休やすみでよかった - "I'm glad tomorrow's a day off" - is about tomorrow, and よかった still cannot become いい. What is past is the establishing of the fact, not the fact itself.

Note - ～なくてよかった and ～ないでよかった are both heard. なくて is the standard reason form and the safer answer on a test; ないで turns up in speech, especially where a deliberate choice is meant: 行いかないでよかった, "good thing I decided not to go."

～てよかった · Examples

来きてよかった。
I'm glad I came.

雨あめが**降ふらなくてよかった**。
Good thing it didn't rain.

この映画えいがを**見みてよかった**です。
I'm glad I watched this film.

傘かさを**持もってきてよかった**。急きゅうに降ふりだしたね。
Good thing I brought an umbrella - it's suddenly started pouring.

～てよかった · Examples (cont.)

事故じこにあったと聞ききましたが、けががなくてよかったですね。
I heard you were in an accident - I'm so glad you weren't hurt.

あの日ひ、勇気ゆうきを出だして**声こえをかけてよかった**。
I'm glad I plucked up the courage to say something to her that day.

学生がくせいのうちに日本語にほんごを**習ならっておいてよかった**と今いまになって思おもう。
Now that I'm older I'm glad I learned Japanese while I was still a student.

大学だいがくのときに留学りゅうがくしてよかったと、働はたらき始はじめてから強つよく感かんじるようになった。
Since starting work I've come to feel strongly that studying abroad at university was the right call.

～てよかった · Nuance & register

The relief is the point. ～てよかった implies an alternative that would have been worse. That is why 生うまれてよかった sounds strange out of context - nothing was at stake - while 生いきていてよかった is powerful. If you are simply reporting that something nice occurred, with no sense of a near miss, the pattern is a poor fit.

Uses split three ways, and learners usually only meet the first:

～てよかった · Nuance & register (cont.)

  1. Relief about your own past choice. 早はやめに出でてよかった. 2. Relief about someone else's situation. 間まに合あってよかったですね - said to the person, with ね. This is how Japanese expresses "thank goodness" on someone else's behalf, and the ね is close to obligatory. 3. Relief that something did not happen. 大おおきい地震じしんじゃなくてよかった.

Register. Plain よかった is casual and extremely common as a one-word reaction - a friend tells you their exam went well and you say ああ、よかった. よかったです is polite and fine in ordinary conversation, though in careful or business speech people often prefer よかったと思おもいます or 何なによりです（ご無事ぶじで何なによりです）, because a bare emotional よかったです can sound a little bald upward.

～てよかった · Nuance & register (cont.)

Emphasis is done with 本当ほんとうに, つくづく, or by stretching the vowel in speech: 来きてよかったあ. The written-out long vowel is casual.

～てよかった also lives inside larger structures: ～てよかったと思おもう (reporting your own judgement, common in writing), ～てよかったですね (to someone else), and ～てよかったのに, which flips the meaning to "you needn't have" - 来こなくてよかったのに, "you didn't have to come all this way." The のに does that work, not the よかった.

～てよかった · ～てよかった vs ～ばよかった

Rule of thumb - ～てよかった looks at what you did and approves. ～ばよかった looks at what you didn't and regrets it.

This is the pair that matters most, because they sit one letter apart in a learner's head and mean opposite things.

早はやく**予約よやくしてよかった**。
I'm glad I booked early.

早はやく**予約よやくすればよかった**。
I should have booked early.

～てよかった · ～てよかった vs ～ばよかった (cont.)

The first is a satisfied look back at a real action; the second is regret about an action that never happened. ～たらよかった and ～ておけばよかった work the same way. Note the negatives, which double the confusion: 行いかなくてよかった is relief that you stayed home, 行いかなければよかった is regret that you went.

～てよかった · ～てよかった vs ～てうれしい

Rule of thumb - うれしい reports the emotion; よかった delivers a verdict on the event. よかった carries relief, うれしい carries joy.

合格ごうかくできてうれしいです。
I'm happy I passed.

合格ごうかくできてよかったです。
I'm relieved I passed.

～てよかった · ～てよかった vs ～てうれしい (cont.)

Both are natural here, because passing an exam is both a pleasure and a narrow escape. They come apart at the edges. Pure good news takes うれしい - プレゼントをもらってうれしい, where nothing bad was ever in prospect. Averted disaster takes よかった - 財布さいふが見みつかってよかった, where "うれしい" would understate the panic that preceded it. And only よかった works for a negative event: ❌雨あめが降ふらなくてうれしい is odd.

～てよかった · ～てよかった vs ～て助かった

Rule of thumb - よかった evaluates the situation; 助たすかった says you personally were rescued from trouble by it. 助たすかった needs a helper, usually a person.

道みちが空すいていてよかった。
Good thing the roads were clear.

手伝てつだってくださって**助たすかりました**。
You really saved me by helping out.

～てよかった · ～てよかった vs ～て助かった (cont.)

Because 助たすかる points at a rescuer, it doubles as thanks and is one of the most useful phrases in working Japanese - 代かわってくれて助たすかった. よかった cannot do that job: saying 手伝てつだってくださってよかったです to the person who helped sounds like you are grading their performance rather than thanking them.

～てよかった · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 来きていいです。（meaning "I'm glad I came"）→ ✅ 来きてよかったです。
    The pattern is fixed in the past. いい here would be read as permission - "you may come."

  • ❌ 勉強べんきょうすればよかったと思おもっています。（meaning "I'm glad I studied"）→ ✅ 勉強べんきょうしてよかったと思おもっています。
    ～ばよかった is regret about what you did not do. It reverses your meaning completely.

  • ❌ 元気げんきくてよかった。 → ✅ 元気げんきでよかった。
    元気げんき is a な-adjective, so it connects with で. Only い-adjectives take くて.

～てよかった · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 遅おくれないでよかったですね。（to someone whose train was on time）→ ✅ 遅おくれなくてよかったですね。
    For a fact rather than a decision, なくて is the natural reason form. ないで suggests they chose not to be late.

  • ❌ プレゼントをもらってよかった。（just pleased）→ ✅ プレゼントをもらってうれしい。
    Not ungrammatical, but よかった implies you had feared not getting one, which is not usually what you mean.

～てよかった · Practice 1

1. 台風たいふうが来こなく____、旅行りょこうに行いけました。

台風たいふうが来こなくてよかった。

The negative reason form is 来こなくて, and よかった supplies the relief: the trip could easily have been cancelled, and wasn't. That averted alternative is exactly what the pattern is for.

～てよかった · Practice 2

2. Choose: 昨日きのう、もっと早はやく寝ね（てよかった・ればよかった）。You were exhausted all day today.

昨日きのう、もっと早はやく寝ねればよかった。

You did not go to bed early, and you regret it, so the counterfactual ～ばよかった is required. 寝ねてよかった would claim you went to bed early and are pleased about it - the opposite of the situation described.

～てよかった · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'm glad I asked you before I bought it."

買かう前まえに**聞きいてよかった**。（polite: 聞きいてよかったです）

The action really happened and turned out well, so it is ～てよかった, not ～ばよかった. The relief is implied: had you not asked, you would have bought the wrong thing.

～てよかった · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: パスポートをなくさなくていいでした。

✅ パスポートをなくさなくてよかった。

Two errors. よかった is fixed in the past - the construction has no ❌いいでした form, and いい never takes でした in any case. The polite version is なくてよかったです.

～てよかった · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 会あえてうれしいです。 (b) 会あえてよかったです。

(a) reports emotion: meeting you makes me happy. It is the standard line on being introduced to someone, and it says nothing about whether the meeting was in doubt.

(b) delivers a verdict with relief behind it: I'm glad the meeting happened. It fits when there was a real chance it wouldn't - you almost missed each other, the train was late, you had been trying to arrange it for months. Said at a first introduction with no such backstory, it can sound as though you had been dreading the encounter.
