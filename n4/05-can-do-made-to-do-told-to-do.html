～ことができる

Meaning · can; be able to - the long, formal way of saying that something is possible

～ことができる turns a verb into a noun-like chunk with こと and then says that that chunk "exists as a possibility". It means exactly what the short potential form means, but it is longer, flatter and more official - the shape you meet on signs, in manuals, in contracts and in written explanations.

～ことができる · Formation

Attach ことができる to the dictionary form. Nothing about the verb changes, which is what makes this pattern the safe fallback while the potential form is still settling in.

Note - できる is an ordinary る-verb, so everything after こと conjugates normally: ことができて, ことができれば, ことができるように. The こと itself never changes and is never replaced by の.

～ことができる · Examples

私わたしはピアノを**弾ひくことができます**。
I can play the piano.

ここで写真しゃしんを**撮とることができません**。
You can't take photographs here.

田中たなかさんは中国語ちゅうごくごを**話はなすことができる**。
Tanaka can speak Chinese.

このカードで電車でんしゃにも**乗のることができます**。
You can use this card on the trains as well.

～ことができる · Examples (cont.)

子こどものころは自転車じてんしゃに**乗のることができなかった**。
As a child I couldn't ride a bicycle.

予約よやくは三日みっか前まえまで**変更へんこうすることができます**。
Reservations may be changed up to three days in advance.

インターネットさえあれば、家いえからでも会議かいぎに**参加さんかすることができます**。

On windy days the roof terrace cannot be accessed, so please be aware.

～ことができる · Nuance & register

～ことができる is not casual. It is grammatically fine at any politeness level, but its flavour is written and institutional. A shop puts ご利用りよういただくことができます on a sign; a friend asking whether you can come tonight says 来こられる?, never ❌来くることができる?. Using it in chatty speech is the single clearest way to sound like a textbook.

Three places where it is genuinely the better choice:

～ことができる · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • Rules and notices. 館内かんないでは飲食いんしょくすることができません.
    The length reads as officialdom, which is the point.
  • When the potential form would be ambiguous. For る-verbs,
    食たべられる could be "can eat" or "gets eaten"; 食たべることができる can only be "can eat".
  • In careful writing, where the ら抜ぬき potential
    (食たべれる) is unacceptable and the full 食たべられる feels heavy.

～ことができる · Nuance & register (cont.)

With する-verbs the pattern is often padded out for no reason. 運転うんてんすることができます and 運転うんてんできます mean the same thing, and the short one is what people say. Reserve the long version for the register that wants it.

Finally, note that できる here is a stative verb, so it does not take ～ている for a present ability: ❌泳およぐことができています. Plain 泳およぐことができます already covers the present.

～ことができる · ～ことができる vs the potential form

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register. The potential form is what you say; ～ことができる is what the notice on the wall prints.

私わたしは漢字かんじを書かくことができます。
I can write kanji. (measured, explanatory)

私わたしは漢字かんじが**書かけます**。
I can write kanji. (ordinary spoken Japanese)

～ことができる · ～ことができる vs the potential form (cont.)

Watch the particle. ～ことができる keeps the original を, because the object still belongs to the inner verb. The potential form pulls the object into が. Both 漢字かんじを書かける and 漢字かんじが書かける exist, but ❌漢字かんじが書かくことができる does not - the が has nothing to attach to.

～ことができる · ～ことができる vs Noun + ができる

Rule of thumb - a verb needs こと in front of が. A noun takes が directly and must not have こと.

弟おとうとはスキーができます。
My little brother can ski.

弟おとうとはスキーをすることができます。
My little brother is able to ski. (stiffer, but grammatical)

Nouns of activity - スキー, テニス, 料理りょうり, 運転うんてん, 通訳つうやく - go straight into が + できる. Adding こと to a noun (❌スキーのことができる) is meaningless.

～ことができる · ～ることができる vs ～たことがある

Rule of thumb - the tense before こと decides everything. Dictionary form + ことができる = can. た-form + ことがある = have ever.

These two look almost identical on the page and mean completely different things.

富士山ふじさんに**登のぼることができます**。
You can climb Mt Fuji. (it is possible)

富士山ふじさんに登のぼったことがあります。
I have climbed Mt Fuji. (experience)

The verb at the end differs too - できる for ability, ある for experience. Mixing them (❌登のぼったことができます) produces a sentence that means nothing.

～ことができる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 泳およぐのができます。 → ✅ 泳およぐことができます。
    の nominalises fine before 好すき and 上手じょうず, but this pattern is fixed with こと.

  • ❌ 食たべられることができる → ✅ 食たべることができる／食たべられる
    Pick one. Stacking the potential form inside ことができる says "can" twice.

  • ❌ 日本語にほんごをことができます。 → ✅ 日本語にほんごができます。
    A bare noun takes が + できる with no こと and no を.

  • ❌ 来くることができないでした。 → ✅ 来くることができませんでした。
    できる is a verb, not an adjective; the past negative is できませんでした.

～ことができる · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 漢字かんじが読よむことができます。 → ✅ 漢字かんじを読よむことができます。
    Inside the こと chunk the verb is still ordinary and transitive, so its object keeps を.

A verb has to be nominalised by こと before が できる can take it. The dictionary form 吸すう is what こと attaches to, and the object keeps its を.

フランス語ご is a noun, so it goes straight into が + できる. こと is only needed to turn a verb into something できる can accept.

～ことができる · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, in the register of a museum notice: "Photography is not permitted inside the hall."

This is exactly the register ～ことができません was made for - impersonal, written, institutional. The spoken equivalent 写真しゃしん、撮とれないよ would be badly out of place on a sign.

～ことができる · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 私わたしは一度いちど北海道ほっかいどうへ行いったことができます。

✅ 私わたしは一度いちど北海道ほっかいどうへ行いったことがあります。

The た-form before こと signals experience, and experience pairs with ある, not できる. If the intended meaning were "I can go to Hokkaido", it would be 行いくことができます - dictionary form, and できる.

～ことができる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この部屋へやを使つかうことができます。 (b) この部屋へやが使つかえます。

Both say the room is available. (a) is the announcement version - a receptionist explaining the rules, a sign on the door, a written policy. Its length and the retained を make it sound like an official statement of what is permitted.

(b) is what a colleague says to you in the corridor. The potential form 使つかえる with が is compact and unremarkable, and in ordinary conversation (a) would sound oddly ceremonious for the same piece of information.

～られる - potential, "can do"

Meaning · be able to do it - the ability, or the circumstances, are there

The potential form says that an action is available to the subject. It covers both halves of English can: the skill you have (泳およげる, "I can swim") and the opportunity the world gives you (今日きょうは休やすめる, "I can take today off"). Japanese does not separate the two.

～られる - potential, "can do" · Formation

～られる - potential, "can do" · Nuance & register

The potential form is register-neutral and is the default way to say can in speech. It has no built-in politeness; ～ます on the end handles that.

The particle. が is the textbook answer and the safest one: 日本語にほんごが話はなせます. を is also alive and common, and it is preferred when the object is long, when the clause already contains a が, or when the を was never a direct object to begin with - 道みちを歩あるける, 家いえを出でられる.

～られる - potential, "can do" · Nuance & register (cont.)

ら抜ぬき言葉ことば. In everyday speech many speakers drop the ら from the る-verb and 来くる potentials: 食たべれる, 見みれる, 起おきれる, 来これる. This is extremely widespread - in some regions it is simply the normal form - and it has a real advantage: it separates potential from passive, which the full form cannot do. It is nevertheless still classed as nonstandard. Do not write it in an exam, an essay or a business email, and note that it never touches う-verbs: 書かける is not ら抜ぬき, it is just the potential.

れ足たす言葉ことば is the opposite error and is unambiguously wrong: ❌行いけれる, ❌読よめれる. An extra れ is inserted where nothing belongs.

～られる - potential, "can do" · Nuance & register (cont.)

Verbs with no potential form. Some verbs already contain the idea of ability or spontaneous perception and refuse the potential: わかる, できる, 見みえる, 聞きこえる. ある has none either. And verbs describing something involuntary - 疲つかれる, 降ふる - take one only in odd, marked contexts.

Politeness by potential. Turning a request into a potential question softens it a great deal: 窓まどを開あけてもらえますか is gentler than 開あけてください, and 開あけていただけませんか gentler still. This is one of the main jobs the form does in adult conversation.

～られる - potential, "can do" · ～られる - potential vs passive

Rule of thumb - for う-verbs the two forms are different words (読よめる vs 読よまれる). For る-verbs and 来くる they are identical, and only the particles and the context tell you which one you are looking at.

食たべられる is genuinely two words wearing one coat:

私わたしは納豆なっとうが**食たべられる**。
I can eat nattō. (potential)

ケーキを弟おとうとに**食たべられた**。
My little brother ate my cake (on me). (passive)

～られる - potential, "can do" · ～られる - potential vs passive (cont.)

Three signals separate them. The potential marks its object with が and has no agent; the passive marks its agent with に. The potential is about the subject's own capacity; the passive is about something done to the subject. And the passive very often turns up in the past, reporting a nuisance.

When the sentence would be genuinely ambiguous, Japanese avoids the form rather than tolerating it: speakers reach for 食たべれる in casual speech or 食たべることができる in writing, both of which can only mean "can eat".

～られる - potential, "can do" · the potential form vs ～ことができる

Rule of thumb - identical meaning; the potential form is spoken Japanese, ～ことができる is written and institutional Japanese.

ここから富士山ふじさんが**見みられます**。
You can see Mt Fuji from here.

ここから富士山ふじさんを**見みることができます**。
Mt Fuji can be viewed from here. (a sign, a guidebook)

Note the particle again: the potential form pulls the object to が, while ～ことができる leaves the inner verb intact with its を.

～られる - potential, "can do" · 見られる vs 見える

Rule of thumb - 見みられる is able to see - you have the chance, the permission, the ticket. 見みえる is is visible - the image simply arrives at your eyes.

窓まどから海うみが**見みえる**。
You can see the sea from the window. (it is simply there)

チケットがあれば、リハーサルも**見みられる**。
If you have a ticket, you can watch the rehearsal too. (access)

～られる - potential, "can do" · 見られる vs 見える (cont.)

The same split runs through 聞きこえる (a sound reaches you) and 聞きける (you get the opportunity to listen). Because 見みえる and 聞きこえる already carry the meaning, they have no potential form of their own: ❌見みえられる.

～られる - potential, "can do" · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 話はなせられる → ✅ 話はなせる
    られる is only for る-verbs and 来くる. An う-verb changes its own ending and stops there.

  • ❌ 行いけれる／読よめれる → ✅ 行いける／読よめる
    This is れ足たす言葉ことば - an inserted れ. Unlike ら抜ぬき it is not accepted in any register.

  • ❌ 明日あした来きられますか。 → ✅ 明日あした来こられますか。
    The potential stem of 来くる is こ. き is the ます-stem (来きます), and it does not belong here.

  • ❌ 日本語にほんごがわかれます。 → ✅ 日本語にほんごがわかります。
    わかる already means "is understandable to me". It has no potential form.

～られる - potential, "can do" · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ レポートを食たべれます（in an essay） → ✅ …食たべられます
    ら抜ぬき is fine among friends and wrong in writing. In anything marked or submitted, keep the ら.

～られる - passive, "it was done to me"

Meaning · something is done to the subject - often, in Japanese, something the subject would rather have avoided

The passive rebuilds a sentence around the person or thing on the receiving end. English does this to move an object into the subject slot. Japanese does that too, but it also does something English cannot: it can build a passive on an intransitive verb, to say that an event happened and you were stuck with the consequences.

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · Nuance & register

The passive itself is register-neutral; politeness comes from ～ます as usual. What is not neutral is how often you reach for it. English uses the passive freely to avoid naming an agent; Japanese usually solves that problem by simply leaving the subject out of an active sentence. Translating every English passive into a Japanese one produces stiff, faintly self-pitying prose.

Marking the agent. に is the default. Two alternatives matter:

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • によって for creation, discovery, invention and other one-off acts, especially
    with an inanimate subject: この絵えはピカソによって描かかれた. Saying ピカソに描かかれた makes the painting sound put-upon.
  • から with verbs of giving, telling and teaching, where the thing flows out of
    the agent: 先生せんせいから注意ちゅういされた.

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · Nuance & register (cont.)

The affected subject. A Japanese passive normally wants a person as its subject, because the form is fundamentally about who is affected. Inanimate subjects are fine when the sentence is a neutral report - 会議かいぎが行おこなわれる, 新あたらしい店みせが開ひらかれる, この本ほんはよく読よまれている - and that use is at home in newspapers and formal writing.

られる as honorific. The same form doubles as a light honorific: 社長しゃちょうはもう帰かえられました means "the president has already gone home", not that anyone went home on him. It is weaker than お帰かえりになる and very common in offices precisely because it is easy. Context disambiguates: an honorific られる has no に-marked agent and its subject is someone you are looking up to.

～られる - passive vs potential

Rule of thumb - う-verbs keep them apart (読よまれる vs 読よめる). る-verbs and 来くる do not - 食たべられる, 見みられる and 来こられる are one shape doing two jobs.

夜中よなかに友ともだちに**来こられて**、寝ねられなかった。
A friend turned up in the middle of the night and I couldn't sleep.

That sentence contains both: 来こられて is passive (a friend did it to me, and it was a nuisance), 寝ねられなかった is potential (I lacked the ability). Nothing in the endings tells you apart - the particles do. A passive has an agent in に; a potential has an object in が and no agent.

～られる - passive vs potential (cont.)

Because the collision is real, speakers avoid it. In casual speech the ら抜ぬき potential (食たべれる, 見みれる, 来これる) is unambiguous, which is one honest reason it keeps spreading despite being nonstandard. In writing, ～ことができる does the same job.

the plain passive vs the suffering passive

Rule of thumb - if the active sentence had an object, the passive is the ordinary kind. If it had no object at all, you are looking at the suffering passive, and its whole job is to add a victim who was not in the original sentence.

Start from an intransitive verb with nobody affected:

雨あめが**降ふった**。
It rained.

雨あめに**降ふられた**。
I got rained on. (and it ruined things for me)

the plain passive vs the suffering passive (cont.)

The second sentence has a silent 私わたしは in front. Nothing was done to the rain; the passive exists purely to say that the rain's falling landed on me. The same machinery gives 子こどもに泣なかれる, 前まえの人ひとに立たたれる, 客きゃくに来こられる, and - for one of the heaviest sentences in the language - 父ちちに死しなれる, "my father died on me".

A closely related form takes a transitive verb but puts the owner, not the object, in the subject slot:

誰だれかに自転車じてんしゃを**盗ぬすまれた**。
Someone stole my bicycle.

the plain passive vs the suffering passive (cont.)

Note that を survives: 自転車じてんしゃ is still the object of the stealing. The subject is me, because I am the one out a bicycle. Saying 自転車じてんしゃが盗ぬすまれた is grammatical but sounds like a police report about a bicycle, not a complaint about my morning.

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · ～られる vs ～てもらう

Rule of thumb - same event, opposite feelings. ～てもらう says you are grateful; the passive typically says you are not.

友ともだちに宿題しゅくだいを**手伝てつだってもらった**。
A friend helped me with my homework. (thank you)

友ともだちに日記にっきを**読よまれた**。
A friend read my diary. (thanks for nothing)

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · ～られる vs ～てもらう (cont.)

Both take the other person in に and both make me the subject, so the only thing carrying the attitude is the choice of ending. Using the passive for something kind - ❌先生せんせいに教おしえられた for "my teacher taught me" - reads as a complaint; 教おしえていただきました is what you want.

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 買かあれる → ✅ 買かわれる
    Verbs ending in ～う go to わ, never あ. Same rule as the ない-form: 買かわない.

  • ❌ 雨あめが降ふられた。 → ✅ 雨あめに降ふられた。
    In a suffering passive the cause is the agent, and agents take に. The が slot belongs to the person who suffered.

  • ❌ 私わたしは先生せんせいを褒ほめられた。 → ✅ 私わたしは先生せんせいに褒ほめられた。
    The praiser is the agent, marked に. を here would try to make the teacher the thing being praised.

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この小説しょうせつは有名ゆうめいな作家さっかに書かかれた。 → ✅ …作家さっかによって書かかれた。
    With acts of creation and an inanimate subject, によって is the natural agent marker; plain に makes the novel sound like a victim.

  • ❌ 夜中よなかに友ともだちに来きられた。 → ✅ …友ともだちに**来こられた**。
    The passive stem of 来くる is こ. き is the ます-stem and has no business in a passive.

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · Practice 1

1. 電車でんしゃの中なかで、知しらない人ひとに話はなし____。

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · Practice 2

2. 弟おとうとに私わたしのケーキを（食たべられた・食たべられる）。

Both are the same form, but the に-marked agent and the を-marked object make this a passive, not a potential - and a passive about a completed nuisance goes in the past. A potential reading would need が and no agent: ケーキが食たべられる, "I'm able to eat cake".

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · Practice 3

誘さそう ends in ～う, so the passive stem is 誘さそわ, never ❌誘さそあ. The inviter is the agent and takes に. Note that this is a perfectly pleasant event - the passive is not obliged to be a complaint, it just often is.

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · Practice 4

～られる - passive, "it was done to me" · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 隣となりの人ひとがタバコを吸すいました。 (b) 隣となりの人ひとにタバコを吸すわれました。

(a) is a neutral report of what the person next to me did. It says nothing about how I felt; it could just as easily be an observation about someone across the room.

(b) rebuilds the sentence around me. The smoker becomes an agent in に and I become the silent subject, which is the passive's way of saying and I had to sit in it. There is no English structure that does this, which is why the natural translation has to smuggle the complaint in somewhere else: "the guy next to me went and lit up."

～させる - causative

Meaning · make someone do something, or let someone do something

One form, two meanings that feel like opposites in English. ～させる says that the subject caused the action - and Japanese leaves it to the particles and the situation to say whether that causing was an order or a permission. Learning to read that difference is most of the work on this page.

～させる - causative · Nuance & register

The form is register-neutral, but the social geometry is not. A causative sentence says the subject has enough authority over the causee to bring the action about. That means it is fundamentally a downward or lateral form: a parent over a child, a teacher over a student, a manager over staff. Using it with a superior as the causee is not merely impolite, it is close to nonsense - ❌社長しゃちょうに資料しりょうを作つくらせました claims you gave the president orders. What you want is 作つくっていただきました.

The one place a causative points upward is when you turn it on yourself. ～させていただく frames your own action as something the listener graciously permits, and it is the backbone of business humility: 説明せつめいさせていただきます, お先さきに失礼しつれいさせていただきます.

～させる - causative · Nuance & register (cont.)

The short causative. う-verbs have a contracted causative in ～す: 行いかす, 飲のます, 待またす, やらす. It is casual, common in speech and especially associated with Kansai. It conjugates as an う-verb (行いかした, 行いかして). Keep ～せる in writing and in polite speech.

Emotion causatives are so common they are worth learning as vocabulary: 喜よろこばせる, 困こまらせる, 驚おどろかせる, 安心あんしんさせる, がっかりさせる. They take the person in を, and they are the normal way to say "make someone feel X" - there is no separate construction.

～させる - causative · させる - "make" vs "let"

Rule of thumb - nothing in the verb distinguishes them. Look at three things: the particle on the causee, whether the causee wanted it, and whether a ～てあげる／～てくれる is attached.

With an intransitive verb the particle is a live choice, and it carries weight:

息子むすこを塾じゅくに**通かよわせている**。
I have my son attending cram school. (my decision)

息子むすこに好すきなだけ**遊あそばせている**。
I let my son play as much as he likes. (his wish, I allow it)

～させる - causative · させる - "make" vs "let" (cont.)

を presents the causee as something the subject moves around; に presents them as a person with their own will who is being given room. With a transitive verb the object has already claimed を, so the causee is stuck with に and the particle stops helping - 弟おとうとに皿さらを洗あらわせた is "made" or "let" depending entirely on whether 弟おとうと wanted to.

The clearest signal in real Japanese is the benefactive tail. A causative with ～てくれる, ～てあげる or ～てやる is always let, because those verbs mean the action was a favour:

課長かちょうは会議かいぎに新人しんじんを**出でさせてくれた**。
The section chief let the new hire sit in on the meeting.

～させる - causative · させる - "make" vs "let" (cont.)

Strip the くれた and 新人しんじんを出でさせた tilts straight back towards "made him attend".

～させる - causative · ～させる vs ～てもらう

Rule of thumb - who is holding the power? ～させる puts it with the subject; ～てもらう puts it with the other person, whose cooperation you receive.

部長ぶちょうは山田やまださんに資料しりょうを**集あつめさせた**。
The manager had Yamada gather the materials.

山田やまださんに資料しりょうを**集あつめてもらった**。
I got Yamada to gather the materials (and I'm grateful).

Both English translations can be "had someone do it", which is why the pair is confusing. The Japanese is not ambiguous at all: させる says you had the standing to require it; てもらう says they did you a favour. Since you almost never have standing over a customer or a senior, ～ていただく is the only safe choice upwards.

～させる - causative · ～させる vs a lexical transitive verb

Rule of thumb - if the language already owns a transitive verb for the idea, use it. A causative built on the intransitive twin sounds laboured, and sometimes means something different.

Japanese has hundreds of intransitive/transitive pairs, and the transitive member already contains "cause":

毎朝まいあさ七しち時じに子こどもを**起おこす**。
I wake the children at seven every morning.

～させる - causative · ～させる vs a lexical transitive verb (cont.)

起おきさせる exists, but it means something narrower and stranger - forcing someone to get themselves up. The same holds for 見みせる versus 見みさせる: 写真しゃしんを見みせる is "show someone a photo", while 写真しゃしんを見みさせる is "make them look at it". Other pairs to reach for first: 着きせる (dress someone), 止とめる (stop something), 入いれる (put in), 決きめる (decide).

～させる - causative · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 使つかあせる → ✅ 使つかわせる
    Verbs in ～う take わ, matching 使つかわない and 使つかわれる.

  • ❌ 食たべらせる → ✅ 食たべさせる
    る-verbs add the whole of させる. Only う-verbs get the bare せる.

  • ❌ 子こどもに泣なかせた。 → ✅ 子こどもを泣なかせた。
    Nobody permits a child to cry. Verbs of emotion take the causee in を, because the "letting" reading is unavailable.

  • ❌ 学生がくせいを作文さくぶんを書かかせる → ✅ 学生がくせいに作文さくぶんを書かかせる
    One を per clause. The object has it, so the causee falls to に.

～させる - causative · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょうに説明せつめいさせました。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょうに**説明せつめいしていただきました**。
    A causative claims authority over the causee. Pointed at your boss it is worse than rude - it is a status error.

～させる - causative · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 息子むすこを留学りゅうがくさせた。 (b) 息子むすこに留学りゅうがくさせた。

Both are the causative of an intransitive action, so the particle is doing all the work. (a) with を treats the son as the thing being moved: the decision was the parent's, and it can read as "I sent my son abroad", possibly over his objections.

(b) with に grants the son his own will and reads as permission - he wanted to study abroad and I let him go. Neither is rude; the difference is simply whose idea it was, and a listener will draw exactly that inference from the particle.

～させられる - causative-passive

Meaning · to be made to do something you did not want to do

This is the causative with the passive stacked on top of it, and the meaning is exactly that sum: somebody made me do it, and I am the one it happened to. Almost every sentence built with ～させられる carries an audible and I hated it.

～させられる - causative-passive · Nuance & register

The default colour of ～させられる is grievance. Even without any explicit complaint attached, the form implies the speaker had no say. That makes it the natural way to describe unpaid overtime, childhood piano lessons, drinking-party rituals and school rules, and an odd way to describe anything you enjoyed. If the action was welcome, use ～させてもらう or ～させていただく instead: 好すきな仕事しごとをやらせてもらっている.

Contracted or full? For う-verbs, the contracted ～される is what people actually say: 待またされる, 飲のまされる, 買かわされる. The full ～せられる is not wrong, but it sounds heavy and slightly literary, and outside the ～す verbs you will rarely hear it in conversation.

～させられる - causative-passive · Nuance & register (cont.)

The one warm use. A handful of verbs of thought and feeling take the causative-passive to mean "it moved me", with no unwillingness at all - 考かんがえさせられる, 驚おどろかされる, 感動かんどうさせられる. Here the cause is usually a thing rather than a person, and the に marks that thing: その言葉ことばに考かんがえさせられた. This is common in reviews and essays.

Register. The form is neutral and works at any politeness level - 残業ざんぎょうさせられました is perfectly polite. What it is not is neutral in content: describing your boss's instructions as させられました in front of that boss is a small act of protest, and will be heard as one.

～させられる - causative-passive · ～させられる vs ～させる

Rule of thumb - same event seen from opposite ends. ～させる puts the person with the power in the subject slot; ～させられる puts the person who obeyed there.

先生せんせいは私わたしに教室きょうしつを**片付かたづけさせた**。
The teacher made me tidy the classroom.

私わたしは先生せんせいに教室きょうしつを**片付かたづけさせられた**。
I was made to tidy the classroom by the teacher.

The facts are identical; the sympathy is not. The second sentence is the one you say to a friend afterwards. Note the particle swap: the teacher moves from subject to に-marked agent, exactly as in an ordinary passive.

～させられる vs the plain passive ～られる

Rule of thumb - in the plain passive somebody did the action to you; in the causative-passive you did the action, under orders.

先生せんせいに作文さくぶんを**読よまれた**。
The teacher read my essay. (the teacher's eyes, my writing)

先生せんせいに作文さくぶんを**読よまされた**。
The teacher made me read my essay out. (my mouth, under duress)

The two differ by a single さ, and they attribute the action to different people. Check who physically performs the verb: if it is you, the sentence needs the extra causative layer.

～させられる - causative-passive · ～させられる vs ～なければならない

Rule of thumb - ～させられる needs a person applying pressure. ～なければならない is obligation with no named source - rules, circumstance, common sense.

明日あしたまでにこの書類しょるいを出ださなければならない。
I have to submit this document by tomorrow.

課長かちょうに明日あしたまでにこの書類しょるいを**出ださせられる**。
The section chief is making me submit this document by tomorrow.

The first is a statement about a deadline; the second is a complaint about a man. Because ～させられる points at a person, it is far more loaded - using it where plain obligation was meant sounds like you are picking a fight.

～させられる - causative-passive · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 話はなさされる → ✅ 話はなさせられる
    Verbs ending in ～す do not contract. The same goes for 出ださせられる and 直なおさせられる.

  • ❌ 読よまさせられる → ✅ 読よませられる／読よまされる
    This is さ入いれ言葉ことば - an extra さ in an う-verb. Either the full form or the contraction, never a hybrid of the two.

  • ❌ 食たべさされる → ✅ 食たべさせられる
    る-verbs never contract. Only う-verbs can lose the せら.

  • ❌ 母ははを野菜やさいを食たべさせられた。 → ✅ 母ははに野菜やさいを食たべさせられた。
    The person giving the order is an agent, and agents in a passive take に.

～させられる - causative-passive · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 休やすみの日ひに会社かいしゃに来きさせられた。 → ✅ …来こさせられた。
    The causative stem of 来くる is こ. き belongs only to 来きます.

～させられる - causative-passive · Practice 5

(b) hands the initiative to the book. The causative-passive says the thinking was provoked - I did not set out to reflect, the book made me. Unusually for this form there is no resentment in it at all; with verbs like 考かんがえる, 驚おどろく and 感動かんどうする it is a compliment, and it is the standard way to praise a book or a film in writing.

～させてください

Meaning · please let me do it - asking permission by asking the other person to cause it

Literally this says "please cause me to do X". You are not asking whether you may; you are asking the listener to grant it, which puts the decision squarely on their side of the table. That framing is why ～させてください is so much more deferential than a flat "is it okay if I…".

～させてください · Nuance & register

～させてください is polite, but it is not humble in itself; it is a direct request with ください on the end. In business the humble branch of the ladder does most of the work:

～させてください · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ～させていただきます announces what you are about to do while crediting the
    other side with permitting it: 担当たんとうの田中たなかが 説明せつめいさせていただきます. It is everywhere in Japanese business and broadcasting, and it is also the form that gets criticised for overuse - used where nobody is granting anything (開店かいてんさせていただきます on a shop notice), it sounds hollow.
  • ～させていただけませんか is a genuine question and the safest way to ask a
    superior for something inconvenient.
  • ～させていただきたいのですが trails off deliberately, leaving the listener to
    offer. Very common, very soft.

～させてください · Nuance & register (cont.)

Several fixed phrases live here and are worth memorising whole: お先さきに失礼しつれいさせていただきます (leaving before your colleagues), 遠慮えんりょさせていただきます (a polite refusal - literally "let me hold back"), 検討けんとうさせていただきます (we'll consider it, which in practice often means no).

In casual speech the bare て-form is the everyday version: ちょっと見みさせて, 明日あした使つかわせて. Among friends ～させてよ and ～させてくれ also occur, the latter distinctly masculine and blunt.

One structural point: because the speaker is the one performing the action, ～させてください almost always has an unstated 私わたしに. Adding it explicitly (私わたしにやらせてください) is a way of pushing yourself forward - volunteering, or claiming the job over someone else.

～させてください · ～させてください vs ～てください

Rule of thumb - ～てください asks you to do it. ～させてください asks you to let me do it. The させ flips who acts.

This is the highest-frequency error at N4, because English "please help" hides the distinction entirely.

手伝てつだってください。
Please help me. (you do the helping)

手伝てつだわせてください。
Please let me help. (I do the helping)

～させてください · ～させてください vs ～てください (cont.)

Both are polite, and they are nearly opposite offers. Before you say either one, check whose hands are going to move.

～させてください · ～させてください vs ～てもいいですか

Rule of thumb - ～てもいいですか asks whether a rule allows it. ～させてください asks a person to allow it. The second is warmer and puts you lower.

この席せきに座すわってもいいですか。
Is it okay if I sit here? (checking, neutral)

その仕事しごとを私わたしに**担当たんとうさせてください**。
Please put me on that job. (asking to be granted it)

～させてください · ～させてください vs ～てもいいですか (cont.)

Use ～てもいいですか for small, impersonal permissions - a seat, a window, a photograph. Use ～させてください when the other person genuinely holds the authority, and when you want to sound like you are asking a favour rather than checking the rules. Asking a manager ❌明日あした休やすんでもいいですか is not rude exactly, but 休やすませていただけませんか is what a careful employee says.

～させてください · ～させてください vs ～させていただきます

Rule of thumb - ～させてください asks. ～させていただきます announces, having already assumed the permission.

会議かいぎの前まえに、資料しりょうを**配くばらせてください**。
Please let me hand out the materials before the meeting.

それでは、資料しりょうを**配くばらせていただきます**。
Right then - allow me to hand out the materials.

The first is still open; the second is what you say as you start moving. Because いただきます is a statement, using it for something the listener has not agreed to can come across as presumptuous - that is the real risk with this very useful phrase.

～させてください · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 手伝てつだってください（meaning "let me help"） → ✅ 手伝てつだわせてください
    Without させ you are asking the other person to do the work.

  • ❌ 使つかわさせてください → ✅ 使つかわせてください
    This is さ入いれ言葉ことば. う-verbs take a bare せる, so the causative て-form is 使つかわせて - no extra さ.

  • ❌ 帰かえらさせていただきます → ✅ 帰かえらせていただきます
    Same error, in the phrase where it is heard most often. 帰かえる is an う-verb.

  • ❌ 考かんがえらせてください → ✅ 考かんがえさせてください
    考かんがえる is a る-verb, so it takes the full させる, not せる.

Meaning · a bare order - "do it" - with nothing softening it

The 命令形めいれいけい is the shortest possible way to tell someone to act. It has no politeness of any kind built in, which makes it rude between adults and exactly right in three places: emergencies, signs, and quoting an order that someone else gave.

命令形 - the plain imperative · Nuance & register

Treat the 命令形めいれいけい as a form you must recognise rather than one you use. Pointed at a real person it is harsh - it is what you hear in an argument, from an angry parent, or from a drill instructor. Most Japanese adults go weeks without saying one to another adult.

Where it does live:

命令形 - the plain imperative · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • Emergencies. Politeness costs syllables, and syllables cost lives:
    逃にげろ, 伏ふせろ, 離はなれろ. Nobody hears these as rude.
  • Signs and instructions. 止とまれ, 押おせ, 引ひけ. Impersonal
    and addressed to no one in particular. Exam papers use the よ variant: 答こたえよ, 選えらべ, 述のべよ.
  • Cheering. がんばれ, 勝かて, 行いけ - a stadium is exempt.
  • Sport, the military, fiction. Coaches to players, seniors to juniors in a
    club, and a very large share of what characters shout in manga and anime.
  • Quoting. Reporting an order almost always keeps the imperative inside the
    quote: 「待まて」と言いわれた.

命令形 - the plain imperative · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register colouring. The plain imperative is strongly male-associated in conversation. Women use it in emergencies, in anger and in cheering, but a woman telling a friend 早はやく食たべろ would be marked. The gentler downward orders - ～なさい and the bare て-form - are unmarked for either speaker.

ろ versus よ: ろ is spoken, よ is written and formal. 見みろ in speech, 見みよ in a slogan or on a test. For する the split is しろ／せよ, and せよ has a distinctly stiff, official ring.

Adding a particle does not rescue it. 早はやくしろよ is nagging; 来こいよ is a blunt invitation between close male friends. If you want to be gentle, change the form, not the ending.

命令形 - the plain imperative · 命令形 vs ～なさい

Rule of thumb - both are orders given downward, but ～なさい comes with authority and care, while the 命令形めいれいけい comes with force. A parent uses ～なさい; the same parent uses the 命令形めいれいけい when they have run out of patience.

早はやく**寝ねなさい**。
Go to bed now. (parent, firm but calm)

早はやく**寝ねろ**。
Get to bed. (sharp, angry, or male-casual)

命令形 - the plain imperative · 命令形 vs ～なさい (cont.)

～なさい is also the only one of the two that appears in neutral written instructions to a person - school worksheets, cookery books for children. The 命令形めいれいけい in writing is reserved for signage and for the terse よ form on exams.

命令形 vs ～てください and the bare ～て

Rule of thumb - ～てください is a request and can go upward or sideways. The bare て-form is a casual request between equals. The 命令形めいれいけい is neither - it does not request anything.

窓まどを**閉しめてください**。
Please close the window.

窓まど、閉しめて。
Shut the window, would you.

窓まどを**閉しめろ**。
Shut that window.

命令形 vs ～てください and the bare ～て (cont.)

The bare て-form is the one to reach for in casual speech. It is friendly, it is used by everyone, and it is what learners usually mean when they produce a 命令形めいれいけい by accident.

命令形 - the plain imperative · 命令形 vs ～な - the negative order

Rule of thumb - the positive order changes the verb's ending; the negative order does not. Dictionary form + な is "don't", and it is just as blunt.

そこに**入はいれ**。
Get in there.

そこに**入はいるな**。
Don't go in there.

These two are a matched pair in tone - signs use both (止とまれ, 入はいるな) and so do angry people. Beware the collision with the softened command written the same way: ます-stem + な is a gentle order contracted from ～なさい. 食たべるな means "don't eat"; 食たべな means "eat up". The difference is a single る.

    来くる has no ろ imperative at all; the whole word is replaced.

  • ❌ するろ／しれ → ✅ しろ（written: せよ）
    する is irregular here too. Its imperative is not built from the dictionary form.

  • ❌ くれろ → ✅ くれ
    くれる looks like an ordinary る-verb and is not. Compare 見みせる → 見みせろ, which is regular.

  • ❌ 食たべれ → ✅ 食たべろ
    Applying the う-verb rule to a る-verb produces 食たべれ, which is not a word. る-verbs take ろ.

歩あるく is an う-verb, so the final く moves to the え-row and nothing is added: 歩あるけ. Note how blunt this is - between friends 早はやく歩あるいて would be normal.

命令形 - the plain imperative · Practice 2

2. 試験しけん問題もんだい: 正ただしい答こたえを一ひとつ（選えらべ・選えらんでください）。

正ただしい答こたえを一ひとつ**選えらべ**。

Exam papers address no particular person, so the impersonal 命令形めいれいけい is standard there. ～てください would sound oddly chatty on a test paper.

命令形 - the plain imperative · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, as a shout: "Stop! Don't touch that!"

やめろ！それに**触さわるな**！

The positive order changes the verb ending (やめる → やめろ, a る-verb) and the negative order adds な to the dictionary form (触さわる → 触さわるな). Shouted in an emergency, neither is rude.

命令形 - the plain imperative · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 部長ぶちょう、この書類しょるいにサインしろ。

✅ 部長ぶちょう、この書類しょるいにサインしていただけますか。

しろ is the correct imperative of する, so the grammar is not the problem - the target is. A 命令形めいれいけい aimed at a superior is about as bad as Japanese gets. Upwards, a request has to be a request.

命令形 - the plain imperative · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 静しずかにしろ。 (b) 静しずかにしなさい。

(a) is raw force. It is what someone says when they have lost their temper, or a line in a film; from a stranger it would start a fight, and in conversation it is strongly male-coded.

(b) still comes from above - you could not say it to your boss - but it carries the tone of a person with legitimate authority correcting someone in their care. A teacher says it to a noisy class without any anger at all. Both are downward; only (a) is aggressive.

～なさい

Meaning · a firm instruction given downward - "do it", said with authority rather than anger

～なさい is the imperative of なさる, the honorific verb for "do". The politeness in its ancestry has drained away, and what is left is a command that is softer in tone than the bare 命令形めいれいけい but strictly one-directional: parent to child, teacher to student, exam paper to candidate. You cannot aim it upwards, and pointing it at an adult equal is condescending.

～なさい · Formation

Attach なさい to the ます-stem. If you can build 書かきます, you can build 書かきなさい.

Verb (る-verb) drop る, + なさい 食たべる → 食たべなさい
Verb (する) し + なさい 勉強べんきょうする → 勉強べんきょうしなさい
Verb (来くる) stem き, + なさい 来くる → 来きなさい

Rule of thumb - three different stems of 来くる turn up in this chapter and they are easy to swap by accident. 来きなさい (ます-stem), 来こい (imperative), 来こられる／来こさせる (potential, passive, causative). Only なさい uses き.

～なさい · Examples

もう遅おそいから早はやく**寝ねなさい**。
It's late - go to bed.

ご飯はんの前まえに手てを**洗あらいなさい**。
Wash your hands before dinner.

次つぎの質問しつもんに日本語にほんごで**答こたえなさい**。
Answer the following questions in Japanese.

もっと野菜やさいを**食たべなさい**と母ははに言いわれた。
My mother told me to eat more vegetables.

～なさい · Examples (cont.)

宿題しゅくだいを終おわらせてから**遊あそびなさい**。
Finish your homework before you go and play.

廊下ろうかを走はしらないようにしなさい。
Make sure you don't run in the corridor.

テストの前まえに、教科書きょうかしょをもう一度いちど読よみ返かえしなさい。
Read through the textbook once more before the test.

文句もんくを言いう前まえに、自分じぶんが何なにをしたかよく**考かんがえてみなさい**。
Before you start complaining, have a good think about what you did yourself.

～なさい · Nuance & register

The politeness of ～なさい is a historical fact, not a usable one. Because it descends from the honorific なさる, it sounds educated and controlled rather than crude - but its social direction is fixed downward. A teacher, a parent, an older sibling or a coach can use it. To a colleague it is patronising; to a customer or a superior it is unthinkable.

Three settings where it is completely unmarked:

～なさい · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • Adults to children. This is its home. 早はやく起おきなさい,
    ちゃんと挨拶あいさつしなさい.
  • Written instructions to a reader. Textbook exercises and exam papers use it
    constantly: 正ただしいものを選えらびなさい, 理由りゆうを書かきなさい. Here it addresses nobody in particular and carries no attitude.
  • Set expressions. お休やすみなさい and お帰かえりなさい are frozen
    ～なさい forms that stopped being commands centuries ago. Nobody hears them as orders.

～なさい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The contracted ～な. Dropping さい gives a much warmer, more casual order: 食たべな, 行いきな, 見みな, もう帰かえりな. It is friendly - closer to English "go on, eat up" - and is common from older to younger and among close friends. It leans masculine in some regions but is by no means exclusively so. Never use it in writing.

There is no negative. ❌食たべないなさい does not exist. To forbid something at the same level of authority you switch construction: 食たべてはいけません, 食たべないようにしなさい, or - much blunter - the prohibitive 食たべるな.

～なさい · Nuance & register (cont.)

お～なさい softens it a little further: お入はいりなさい, お食たべなさい. This sounds distinctly old-fashioned and grandmotherly in modern Japanese, and you will meet it more in fiction than in life.

～なさい · ～なさい vs 命令形

Rule of thumb - same direction, different heat. ～なさい is the order of someone with legitimate authority; the 命令形めいれいけい is the order of someone applying force.

自分じぶんの部屋へやを**片付かたづけなさい**。
Tidy your room.

自分じぶんの部屋へやを**片付かたづけろ**。
Tidy your damn room.

～なさい · ～なさい vs 命令形 (cont.)

A parent says the first every week and the second when they have snapped. There is also a difference in who says them: the 命令形めいれいけい is strongly male-coded in conversation, while ～なさい is used freely by everyone.

Note too that only ～なさい is at home in print. A worksheet says 計算けいさんしなさい; a road sign says 止とまれ. Neither would trade places.

～なさい · ～なさい vs ～てください

Rule of thumb - ～てください asks, and can point in any direction. ～なさい tells, and only points down.

すみません、こちらに**座すわってください**。
Please have a seat over here.

そこに**座すわりなさい**。
Sit down there. (teacher to pupil)

～なさい · ～なさい vs ～てください (cont.)

Learners often assume that because ～なさい looks longer and comes from an honorific verb, it must be politer than ～てください. It is not. ～てください is the neutral all-purpose form; ～なさい is what you use precisely when you are not asking. Said to a guest, 座すわりなさい would be startling - お座すわりください is the polite version.

～なさい · 食べな vs 食べるな

Rule of thumb - the same な after two different stems means opposite things. ます-stem + な = do it. Dictionary form + な = don't do it.

冷さめないうちに**食たべな**。
Go on, eat it while it's hot.

まだ**食たべるな**。
Don't eat it yet.

～なさい · 食べな vs 食べるな (cont.)

The only visible difference is the る. The first is ～なさい with its tail bitten off and is friendly; the second is the prohibitive and is as blunt as the 命令形めいれいけい. Intonation helps in speech - the prohibitive な is clipped and stressed - but the stem is the reliable signal.

～なさい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 来こなさい → ✅ 来きなさい
    なさい attaches to the ます-stem, and the ます-stem of 来くる is 来き. The こ stem belongs to 来こい, 来こられる and 来こさせる.

  • ❌ 食たべるなさい → ✅ 食たべなさい
    Never attach なさい to the dictionary form. 食たべます → 食たべ + なさい.

  • ❌ するなさい → ✅ しなさい
    The ます-stem of する is し.

  • ❌ 廊下ろうかを走はしらないなさい。 → ✅ 廊下ろうかを走はしってはいけません／走はしらないようにしなさい。
    ～なさい has no negative form. You have to change construction.

～なさい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、ここに名前なまえを書かきなさい。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、ここにお名前なまえをお書かきください。
    Correctly formed and socially impossible. ～なさい only ever points downward.

～なさい · Practice 1

1. 時間じかんがありませんよ。早はやく準備じゅんび____。

早はやく準備じゅんびしなさい。

準備じゅんびする is a する-verb, and the ます-stem of する is し, so the form is 準備じゅんびしなさい. ❌準備じゅんびするなさい applies なさい to the dictionary form, which never happens.

～なさい · Practice 2

2. （先生せんせいが学生がくせいに）この問題もんだいをもう一度いちど（考かんがえなさい・考かんがえてください）。

この問題もんだいをもう一度いちど考かんがえなさい。

Teacher to student is exactly the downward relationship ～なさい is for. 考かんがえてください is not wrong, but it frames the instruction as a request between equals, which is a different classroom.

～なさい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Get up. You'll be late for school."

起おきなさい。学校がっこうに遅おくれますよ。

起おきる is a る-verb, so drop る and add なさい. This is the classic parent-to-child use, and なさい is warmer here than 起おきろ without being a request.

～なさい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 危あぶないから、道みちで遊あそばないなさい。

✅ 危あぶないから、道みちで**遊あそんではいけません**（or 遊あそばないようにしなさい）。

～なさい has no negative. To forbid something you either use ～てはいけません, or keep なさい on a positive verb by routing the negative through ～ないようにする. The bluntest option is the prohibitive 遊あそぶな.

～なさい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) もう帰かえりなさい。 (b) もう帰かえりな。

(a) is an instruction from someone in charge - a parent sending a child home, a teacher clearing the classroom. It is calm, but it is not negotiable, and the full なさい keeps a certain formality.

(b) is the same order with the ending clipped, and clipping it makes it kind. It sounds like an older friend saying "go on, head home" - casual, a bit warm, and completely unusable in writing. Note that neither can be said upwards: even the friendly (b) assumes you are the senior one.

～ように言う

Meaning · tell someone to do something - reporting an instruction without quoting it

～ように言いう passes on the content of an order or request. The original words are gone: you cannot tell from the report whether the speaker barked 帰かえれ or murmured お帰かえりいただけますか. That flattening is the whole point of the pattern, and it is why learners meet it most often in the passive - 言いわれた, "I was told to".

～ように言う · Formation

ように takes the plain form of the verb, positive or negative. Nothing changes about the verb itself, which is why the four verb classes all behave the same way here.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) dictionary form + ように + 言いう 待まつ → 待まつように言いう
Verb (る-verb) dictionary form + ように + 言いう 片付かたづける → 片付かたづけるように言いう
Verb (する) dictionary form + ように + 言いう 準備じゅんびする → 準備じゅんびするように言いう
Verb (来くる) dictionary form + ように + 言いう 来くる → 来くるように言いう
Negative order ない-form + ように + 言いう 遅おくれる → 遅おくれないように言いう
な-adjective / noun ～にする + ように 静しずか → 静しずかにするように言いう
Asking a favour ～てくれる／～てもらう + ように 手伝てつだう → 手伝てつだってくれるように頼たのむ

～ように言う · Formation (cont.)

The verb at the end is not restricted to 言いう:

Verb Sense Example
言いう tell 来くるように言いう
伝つたえる pass on a message 来くるように伝つたえる
頼たのむ ask a favour 来くるように頼たのむ
お願ねがいする request politely 来くるようにお願ねがいする
注意ちゅういする warn 来こないように注意ちゅういする

～ように言う · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the person receiving the instruction is marked with に, the same as the listener of any speech act: 弟おとうとに片付かたづけるように言いった. In the passive that person becomes the subject and the speaker moves into に: 母ははに言いわれた.

～ように言う · Examples

先生せんせいは学生がくせいに静しずかにするように言いった。
The teacher told the students to be quiet.

母ははに早はやく**帰かえるように言いわれた**。
My mother told me to come home early.

医者いしゃに、お酒さけを**飲のまないように言いわれました**。
The doctor told me not to drink.

受付うけつけで、ここに名前なまえを**書かくように言いわれた**。
At reception they told me to write my name here.

～ように言う · Examples (cont.)

部長ぶちょうに、金曜日きんようびまでに報告書ほうこくしょを**出だすように言いわれている**。
The manager has told me to hand in the report by Friday.

弟おとうとに部屋へやを**片付かたづけるように**何度なんども**言いった**のに、まだそのままだ。
I've told my little brother to tidy his room over and over, and it's still exactly as it was.

田中たなかさんに、会議かいぎの時間じかんが変かわったことをみんなに**伝つたえるように頼たのんだ**。
I asked Tanaka to let everyone know that the meeting time had changed.

子こどもたちには、知しらない人ひとについて**行いかないようにと注意ちゅういして**あります。
We've warned the children not to go off with strangers.

～ように言う · Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral. It appears in family conversation and in business email alike, and any politeness sits on the final verb - 言いいました, 伝つたえていただけますか, お願ねがいしました.

The lost politeness. Because ように reports content rather than words, the report is silent about the tone of the original. If that matters, you say so outside the pattern: 強つよい口調くちょうで言いわれた, 丁寧ていねいに頼たのまれた. Alternatively, quote directly with と.

ようにと. Adding と - 来くるようにと言いわれた - is very common and slightly more formal or written. It changes nothing semantically; think of it as the quotative と reasserting itself.

～ように言う · Nuance & register (cont.)

The passive is the everyday form. In real speech you will hear ～ように言いわれた far more than the active. Reporting what you were told to do is simply a more frequent thing to say than reporting what you told someone else.

Business softening. Upward, 言いう is too blunt. Use ようにお願ねがいします or ようにお伝つたえください - 山田やまださんに折おり返かえし電話でんわをくださるように お伝つたえください is the sort of sentence this pattern was made for.

～ように言う · Nuance & register (cont.)

Dangling ように. A written instruction sometimes stops after ように, with the verb left off entirely: 受付うけつけにお越こしくださいますように. This is formal and a bit old-fashioned, but you will see it on notices.

～ように言う · ～ように言う vs ～と言う

Rule of thumb - と quotes the words; ように reports the instruction. If you can put quotation marks round it, use と.

父ちちは「早はやく寝ねろ」と言いった。
My father said, "Get to bed."

父ちちは早はやく寝ねるように言いった。
My father told me to go to bed early.

～ように言う · ～ように言う vs ～と言う (cont.)

The first preserves the imperative and with it the temper. The second could have been said in any tone at all. Note what happens to the verb: inside a と quote you keep whatever form was actually uttered (寝ねろ, 寝ねなさい, 寝ねてください); with ように the verb goes into the plain dictionary form no matter what.

と can also report a request in indirect speech - 早はやく寝ねるように言いった and 「早はやく寝ねなさい」と言いった are both natural - but ❌「早はやく寝ねなさい」ように言いった mixes the two and is simply ungrammatical.

～ように言う · ～ように頼む vs ～てくださいと頼む

Rule of thumb - same message, different packaging. ～てくださいと reproduces the polite words that were used; ～ように summarises what was asked for.

友ともだちに空港くうこうまで送おくってくれるように頼たのんだ。
I asked my friend to drive me to the airport.

友ともだちに「空港くうこうまで送おくってください」と頼たのんだ。
I asked my friend, "Please take me to the airport."

～ように言う · ～ように頼む vs ～てくださいと頼む (cont.)

When the request is a favour rather than an order, the ように version usually keeps a benefactive verb - ～てくれるように, ～てもらえるように - because without it the sentence sounds like an instruction rather than a request. Compare bare 送おくるように頼たのんだ, which reads oddly high-handed for something a friend is doing for you.

～ように言う · ～ように言う vs ～ようにする・～ようになる

Rule of thumb - same ように, three unrelated jobs. 言いう reports an instruction, する describes an effort you make, なる describes a change that happened.

医者いしゃに毎日まいにち歩あるくように言いわれた。
The doctor told me to walk every day.

毎日まいにち歩あるくようにしている。
I make a point of walking every day.

毎日まいにち歩あるくようになった。
I've got into the habit of walking every day.

～ように言う · ～ように言う vs ～ようにする・～ようになる (cont.)

Learners meet all three within a few weeks of each other and fold them together. Keep the final verb in view: it, not ように, is carrying the meaning.

～ように言う · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 母ははは「早はやく帰かえりなさい」ように言いった。 → ✅ 母ははは早はやく**帰かえるように言いった**。
    ように never follows a quotation. Either quote with と or summarise with ように - not both.

  • ❌ 来きてように言いう → ✅ 来くるように言いう
    ように takes the plain form, not the て-form.

  • ❌ 遅おくれないでように言いう → ✅ 遅おくれないように言いう
    The negative slot wants the plain ない-form. ～ないで belongs with ～ないでください, a different pattern.

  • ❌ 先生せんせいは私わたしを発表はっぴょうするように言いった。 → ✅ 先生せんせいは私わたしに…
    The person told is the addressee of a speech act, so に.

～ように言う · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 静しずかように言いった → ✅ 静しずかにするように言いった
    ように needs a verb in front of it. A な-adjective has to be turned into one with ～にする.

～ように言う · Practice 1

1. 受付うけつけの人ひとに、ここで待ま____ように言いわれた。

ここで待まつように言いわれた。

ように takes the plain dictionary form, so 待まつ goes in unchanged. The receptionist is the source of the instruction and takes に in the passive.

～ように言う · Practice 2

2. 田中たなかさんは「明日あしたは早はやく来きてください」（と・ように）言いいました。

「明日あしたは早はやく来きてください」と言いいました。

Once the words are inside quotation marks, only the quotative と can follow. The ように version would drop the quotes and the politeness: 明日あしたは早はやく来くるように言いいました.

～ように言う · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The doctor told me not to eat anything before the examination."

医者いしゃに、検査けんさの前まえは何なにも**食たべないように言いわれた**。

A negative instruction goes in as the plain ない-form, 食たべない, directly before ように. The passive 言いわれた is the natural way to report an instruction you received.

～ように言う · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 部長ぶちょうに、この書類しょるいをコピーしてように頼たのまれました。

✅ 部長ぶちょうに、この書類しょるいをコピーするように頼たのまれました。

The て-form cannot sit in front of ように; the slot takes the plain form, here コピーする. If you wanted to keep the て-form you would have to quote: 「コピーしてください」と頼たのまれました.

～ように言う · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 先生せんせいは毎日まいにち日記にっきを書かくように言いった。 (b) 先生せんせいは「毎日まいにち日記にっきを書かきなさい」と言いった。

(a) reports only the substance of the instruction. It tells you what the students now have to do and says nothing about how the teacher put it - she may have asked politely or issued an order.

(b) reproduces the teacher's actual words, and those words carry information: the なさい marks her as speaking downward with authority, calmly rather than angrily. Direct quotation is how Japanese keeps that texture, since ように deliberately throws it away.
