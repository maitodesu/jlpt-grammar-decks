～うちに

Meaning · while a state still holds - and with the sense that it will not hold much longer

～うちに opens a window and tells you to act inside it. The window is a state that is currently true - it's hot, you're young, the baby is asleep - and the pattern quietly carries the reason for the hurry: that state is going to end. A second, softer use drops the urgency and reports a change that crept up on you while something was going on: 聞きいているうちに…, "as I listened…".

～うちに · Formation

うちに is the noun うち ("interior, span") plus に, so everything in front of it is in the shape that modifies a noun.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (ている) ている + うちに 待まつ → 待まっているうちに
Verb (stative, dictionary) Plain non-past + うちに いる → 日本にほんにいるうちに
Verb (negative) ない-form + うちに 忘わすれる → 忘わすれないうちに
い-adjective Plain form + うちに 熱あつい → 熱あついうちに
な-adjective な + うちに 元気げんきだ → 元気げんきなうちに
Noun の + うちに 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいのうちに

～うちに · Formation (cont.)

Note - the subject inside the うちに clause takes が, never は: 子こどもが寝ねているうちに. This is the same embedding rule you met with たら and とき clauses at N4.

Note - a plain dictionary-form verb before うちに is limited to verbs that describe a lasting situation - いる, 住すむ, 勤つとめる. For an ordinary action verb you need ている: 読よんでいるうちに, not 読よむうちに.

～うちに · Examples

スープが**熱あついうちに**どうぞ。
Have the soup while it's hot.

明あかるいうちに帰かえろう。
Let's head back while it's still light.

若わかいうちに、いろいろな国くにを見みておきたい。
I want to see plenty of countries while I'm young.

学生がくせいのうちに車くるまの免許めんきょを取とっておいた。
I made sure to get my driving licence while I was still a student.

～うちに · Examples (cont.)

両親りょうしんが**元気げんきなうちに**、一度いちど一緒いっしょに温泉おんせんへ行いきたい。
I'd like to go to a hot spring with my parents once, while they're still in good health.

子こどもが昼寝ひるねをしているうちに、たまった洗濯物せんたくものをたたんだ。
While the kids were napping I folded the laundry that had piled up.

先生せんせいの話はなしを**聞きいているうちに**、だんだん意味いみが分わかってきた。
As I listened to the teacher it gradually began to make sense.

会議かいぎで決きまったことは、覚おぼえているうちにメモしておかないと、夕方ゆうがたには全部ぜんぶ忘わすれてしまいますよ。
If you don't jot down what was decided in the meeting while you still remember it, by evening you'll have lost the lot.

～うちに · Nuance & register

うちに is register-neutral. It takes ます or plain endings without changing character, and it turns up in signage, in advice from your grandmother and in company email alike.

What is not neutral is the shape of the main clause. Because the pattern exists to say "act before the window shuts", it pulls strongly toward volitional and advisory endings - よう, たい, ておく, ておこう, てください, たほうがいい, ないと. A flat past statement with no connection to the window closing sounds off, and that is usually the signal that you wanted 間あいだに instead.

～うちに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The ～ているうちに use is the exception, and it is worth learning as its own thing. There the main clause is a change that happened to you, typically unnoticed and typically expressed with てくる, てしまう or a なる verb: 歩あるいているうちに道みちに迷まよってしまった. Nobody is being advised to hurry; the sentence is reporting drift.

Two fixed expressions to recognise. そのうちに (often just そのうち) is an adverb meaning "before long, one of these days" - そのうち遊あそびに行いきます is a polite, pleasantly vague "I'll drop by sometime." And 知しらないうちに means "without my noticing", not "while I didn't know" in any literal sense.

～うちに · Nuance & register (cont.)

There is a mild literary flavour to 若わかいうちに and 元気げんきなうちに, which show up in proverbs and in the kind of advice older speakers give. Nothing about them is stiff, but they carry a whiff of life-lesson, which is exactly why they are used.

～うちに · ～うちに vs ～間に

Rule of thumb - both put an event inside a window, but 間あいだに only says when, while うちに adds before it's too late. If the window closing does not matter, use 間あいだに.

母ははが買かい物ものに行いっている**間あいだに**、宅配便たくはいびんが届とどいた。
A delivery arrived while my mother was out shopping.

Nothing here is racing the clock; the shopping trip is just a stretch of time that happened to contain the delivery. Swap in うちに and the sentence starts to imply the courier deliberately used her absence, which is not what happened.

母ははが買かい物ものに行いっているうちに、ケーキを隠かくしておこう。
Let's hide the cake while Mum's out shopping.

～うちに · ～うちに vs ～間に (cont.)

Now it fits: she is coming back, and that is the whole point.

The mirror-image restriction is also useful. うちに resists windows defined by the clock or the calendar, because a clock window has no "state" to run out: ❌三時さんじから五時ごじのうちに → ✅三時さんじから五時ごじの間あいだに.

～うちに · ～うちに vs ～前に

Rule of thumb - 前まえに orders two events; うちに judges an opportunity. 前まえに is neutral, and it looks forward at the event; うちに looks around at the situation you are still standing in.

年としを取とる**前まえに**、家いえを建たてるつもりだ。
I intend to build a house before I get old.

若わかいうちに、家いえを建たてるつもりだ。
I intend to build a house while I'm still young.

～うちに · ～うちに vs ～前に (cont.)

The first is a plain schedule: house, then old age. The second says youth is what makes it possible, and that the possibility is draining away. Note also that 前まえに takes a dictionary-form verb naming the event, while うちに takes the state that precedes it - which is why the two versions above look nothing alike.

～うちに · ～のうちに vs ～中に

Rule of thumb - 今日きょう中じゅうに is a deadline; 今日きょうのうちに is an opportunity. Same span, different argument.

この書類しょるいは今日きょう中じゅうに出だしてください。
Please submit these documents by the end of today.

明日あしたは忙いそがしいから、今日きょうのうちに出だしておこう。
Tomorrow's going to be busy, so let's get it submitted while we still have today.

～うちに · ～のうちに vs ～中に (cont.)

中じゅうに states a limit somebody has imposed. のうちに gives a reason, and the reason is always some version of "afterwards will be worse". 中ちゅうに also attaches only to nouns naming a measured span; you cannot say ❌元気げんき中ちゅうに for 元気げんきなうちに.

～うちに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 学生がくせいうちに留学りゅうがくしたい。 → ✅ 学生がくせいのうちに留学りゅうがくしたい。
    うち is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs の. A な-adjective likewise needs な: ❌元気げんきのうちに → ✅元気げんきなうちに.

  • ❌ 九時くじから十時じゅうじのうちに電話でんわします。 → ✅ 九時くじから十時じゅうじの**間あいだに**電話でんわします。
    A window fixed by the clock has no state to lose, so うちに has nothing to work with. Use 間あいだに.

  • ❌ 本ほんを**読よむうちに**眠ねむくなった。 → ✅ 本ほんを**読よんでいるうちに**眠ねむくなった。
    An action verb has to be in ている. The dictionary form is reserved for verbs that already describe a standing situation, like いる or 住すむ.

～うちに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 私わたしは寝ねているうちに地震じしんがあった。 → ✅ 私わたしが寝ねているうちに地震じしんがあった。
    The subject of a subordinate clause takes が. は cannot be trapped inside a clause that only modifies うち.

～うちに · Practice 1

1. 赤あかちゃんが寝ねている____、急いそいで晩ばんご飯はんを作つくってしまおう。

赤あかちゃんが寝ねているうちに、急いそいで晩ばんご飯はんを作つくってしまおう。

The baby is going to wake up, and that is exactly why the cooking has to happen now - the window is closing, which is うちに's job. 間あいだに would also be grammatical but would drop the urgency that いそいで and てしまおう are already signalling.

～うちに · Practice 2

2. Choose: 試験しけんは一時いちじから三時さんじまでです。その（うちに・間あいだに）一度いちどトイレに行いってもいいです。

その**間あいだに**一度いちどトイレに行いってもいいです。

The window is defined by two clock times, not by a state that is about to expire, so うちに has no opportunity to point at. This is the plain "at some point inside the span" meaning, which belongs to 間あいだに.

～うちに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "While I was living in Osaka, I got used to the local accent."

大阪おおさかに住すんでいるうちに、関西弁かんさいべんに慣なれた。

This is the drift use: nobody decided to get used to the accent, it simply happened over the course of the stay. The main clause is a change of state, which is the signature of ～ているうちに. 住すむ also allows the dictionary form here, since it names a lasting situation.

～うちに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 暗くらいのうちに山やまを下おりたほうがいい。

✅ 明あかるいうちに山やまを下おりたほうがいい。

Two problems. An い-adjective attaches directly, with no の - 暗くらいうちに, not ❌暗くらいのうちに. And the meaning was inverted: 暗くらいうちに would mean "while it is still dark", i.e. set off before dawn. The state you want to use up is the daylight.

～うちに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 日本にほんにいる**間あいだに**、富士山ふじさんに登のぼりました。 (b) 日本にほんにいるうちに、富士山ふじさんに登のぼりました。

(a) is a neutral report: the climb took place somewhere inside the period of the stay. It answers "when did you climb it?" and says nothing about why then.

(b) adds a motive. It frames the stay as a limited chance that was running out, so the climb reads as something done deliberately before the opportunity disappeared - closer to "I made sure to climb Fuji while I was still in Japan." With a plain past main clause (b) also sounds slightly more natural if you keep a marker of intent, such as 登のぼっておきました.

～ないうちに

Meaning · before something happens - said from inside the time when it still hasn't

English says "before it rains." Japanese takes the opposite angle and says "while it is not-yet raining": 雨あめが降ふらないうちに. There is no natural English shape for that, which is why the pattern feels strange for a long time and then suddenly feels obvious. The thing in the ない clause is nearly always something you would rather avoid, and the main clause is what you do to get ahead of it.

～ないうちに · Formation

Take the plain negative and put うちに straight after it. The negative does the work; nothing else changes.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ない-form + うちに 降ふる → 降ふらないうちに
Verb (る-verb) ない-form + うちに 冷さめる → 冷さめないうちに
Verb (する) しないうちに 変化へんかする → 変化へんかしないうちに
Verb (来くる) 来こないうちに 来くる → 来こないうちに
い-adjective (via なる) ～く + ならないうちに 暗くらい → 暗くらくならないうちに
な-adjective / Noun (via なる) ～に + ならないうちに 病気びょうき → 病気びょうきにならないうちに

～ないうちに · Formation (cont.)

Note - う-verb / る-verb are the labels used throughout this book; you will also see them as 五段ごだん/Group 1 and 一段いちだん/Group 2. The irregular one to watch is 来くる, whose negative stem is こ: 来こないうちに, never ❌くないうちに.

Note - adjectives almost never attach directly. ❌寒さむくないうちに is not how this is said; route it through ならない - 寒さむくならないうちに. The negative has to name an event that has not started, and adjectives name states, so なる supplies the missing event.

～ないうちに · Examples

雨あめが**降ふらないうちに**帰かえろう。
Let's go home before it rains.

忘わすれないうちにメモしておきます。
I'll write it down before I forget.

暗くらくならないうちに洗濯物せんたくものを取とり込こんだ。
I brought the washing in before it got dark.

コーヒーが**冷さめないうちに**飲のんでしまってください。
Do drink your coffee before it goes cold.

～ないうちに · Examples (cont.)

知しらないうちに財布さいふがなくなっていた。
My wallet had vanished without my noticing.

人ひとが**増ふえないうちに**席せきを取とっておこう。
Let's grab seats before the place fills up.

部長ぶちょうが**戻もどってこないうちに**、机つくえの上うえだけでも片付かたづけておいたほうがいいよ。
You'd better at least clear your desk before the manager gets back.

子こどもは、こちらが**気きづかないうちに**背せが伸のびていて、去年きょねん買かった服ふくがもう入はいらない。
Children grow before you notice, and the clothes we bought last year already don't fit.

～ないうちに · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and common in both speech and writing. What is not free is the shape of the main clause: it is almost always something the speaker chooses to do in response - よう, ておく, てしまう, たほうがいい, てください, なさい, ないと. That is the whole reason the pattern exists.

The ない clause is normally a change for the worse: rain, cold food, darkness, crowds, your boss returning. This is a strong tendency rather than a hard rule, but if the event is welcome the sentence starts to sound odd, and you probably wanted 前まえに.

Three expressions have hardened into set phrases, and they behave like adverbs rather than like clauses:

～ないうちに · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 知しらないうちに - without one's knowledge
  • 気きづかないうちに - without noticing
  • 分わからないうちに - before one has worked out what is going on

In these the main clause is not an action you took but a change you discovered afterwards, usually with ていた or てしまう: 知しらないうちに時間じかんが過すぎていた.

In casual speech the whole thing survives intact - this is not a pattern that gets contracted. What does happen is that the main clause loses its particles: 「雨あめ降ふらないうちに帰かえろ」.

～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs ～前に

Rule of thumb - 前まえに puts two events in order and stops there. ないうちに says the later event is a problem you are outrunning.

出でかける**前まえに**窓まどを閉しめた。
I shut the windows before going out.

雨あめが入はいってこないうちに窓まどを閉しめた。
I shut the windows before the rain could get in.

～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs ～前に (cont.)

The first is a checklist item. The second has a threat in it. Notice too that 前まえに takes a dictionary form and ないうちに takes a negative, even though the English translation is "before" both times - this is the single most reliable source of errors with the pattern.

One more practical difference: 前まえに is happy with a scheduled, neutral event (会議かいぎが始はじまる前まえに), while ないうちに wants something that might land on you at any moment. You would not normally say ❌会議かいぎが始はじまらないうちに for a meeting that starts at ten sharp - unless you mean you are racing it.

～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs the affirmative ～うちに

Rule of thumb - the same situation can be framed from either side. Affirmative うちに names the good state you still have; ないうちに names the bad state that hasn't arrived.

明あかるいうちに洗濯物せんたくものを干ほしてしまおう。
Let's get the washing hung out while it's still light.

暗くらくならないうちに洗濯物せんたくものを干ほしてしまおう。
Let's get the washing hung out before it gets dark.

～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs the affirmative ～うちに (cont.)

These are interchangeable here, and either is natural. The choice matters when only one side of the pair has a name. There is a comfortable word for the good state in 明あかるい, so both work; but for "before the shop closes" there is no adjective for "still open in the sense that matters", so you reach for 店みせが閉しまらないうちに. Conversely, 若わかいうちに has no useful negative twin - ❌年としを取とらないうちに is understandable but distinctly odd.

～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs ～ない間に

Rule of thumb - ない間あいだに is a stretch of non-happening with a start and an end. ないうちに is the run-up before the thing starts.

私わたしが見みていない**間あいだに**、猫ねこが魚さかなを食たべてしまった。
The cat ate the fish while I wasn't looking.

猫ねこが起おきないうちに、魚さかなをしまっておこう。
Let's put the fish away before the cat wakes up.

～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs ～ない間に (cont.)

In the first, "not looking" is a period that has already begun and already ended - the cat operated inside it. In the second, "waking up" has not happened at all yet, and the sentence is about getting in first. A quick test: if you can put ずっと in front of the negative clause, it is ない間あいだに.

～ないうちに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 雨あめが**降ふるないうちに**帰かえります。 → ✅ 雨あめが**降ふらないうちに**帰かえります。
    うちに attaches to the finished ない-form, not to the dictionary form plus ない. Build 降ふらない first, then add うちに.

  • ❌ 暗くらいうちに帰かえりましょう。（meaning "before it gets dark"） → ✅ 暗くらくならないうちに帰かえりましょう。
    暗くらいうちに is perfectly grammatical, but it means "while it is still dark" - the pre-dawn start, not the evening deadline. The two are opposites.

  • ❌ 忘わすれないうちで書かいておきます。 → ✅ 忘わすれないうちに書かいておきます。
    The particle is fixed at に. うちで is not a variant of this pattern.

～ないうちに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 先生せんせいは来こないうちに片付かたづけよう。 → ✅ 先生せんせいが来こないうちに片付かたづけよう。
    The ない clause is embedded, so its subject takes が. And note the stem: 来くる goes to 来こない, not ❌くない.

～ないうちに · Practice 1

1. ご飯はんが____うちに食たべてください。（冷さめる）

ご飯はんが**冷さめない**うちに食たべてください。

English wants "before it gets cold", but Japanese frames the same moment as "while it has not yet gone cold", so the verb goes into the plain negative. Using the affirmative 冷さめるうちに would be ungrammatical here - there is no state of "being in the middle of cooling" to sit inside.

～ないうちに · Practice 2

2. Choose: 台風たいふうが（来くる前まえに・来こないうちに）買かい物ものを済すませておこう。

Both are grammatical, but 来こないうちに is the better fit.

来くる前まえに would simply order the two events, as though the typhoon were an appointment. 来こないうちに treats it as a threat you are outrunning, which is what ておこう and 済すませて are already telling us. Note the irregular negative stem: 来こない.

～ないうちに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The line will get long, so let's buy the tickets before people find out."

列れつが長ながくなるから、みんなに**知しられないうちに**チケットを買かっておこう。

The negative passive 知しられない is what carries "before people find out" - the event you are getting ahead of is other people learning about it. ておこう in the main clause is typical: this pattern nearly always ends in something volitional.

～ないうちに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 子こどもが起おきるないうちに、しずかに出でかけた。

✅ 子こどもが**起おきないうちに**、しずかに出でかけた。

起おきる is a る-verb, so its negative is 起おきない - drop る, add ない. ❌起おきるない stacks the dictionary form and ない together, which never happens in Japanese.

～ないうちに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 留守るすの**間あいだに**、だれかが部屋へやに入はいったようだ。 (b) 帰かえってこないうちに、部屋へやを片付かたづけておこう。

(a) looks backwards at a window that has closed. The absence was a real stretch of time, and the sentence places an event somewhere inside it - a neutral report of when something happened.

(b) looks forwards from inside the window. Nobody has come back yet, and the speaker is using the remaining time to get something done first. The clue is the pairing of a negative clause with a volitional ending: that combination is almost always ないうちに, not 間あいだに.

間 / 間に - while, during

Meaning · a window of time (間あいだ) - and に decides whether the main clause fills it or sits inside it

間あいだ is a noun meaning "the space between". Used with time it marks off a stretch: while the meeting is on, while she was out, all through the summer. The one thing you have to get right is the little に, and it is not decoration:

  • 間あいだ - the main clause is true for the whole window, start to end.
  • 間あいだに - the main clause happens at some point inside the window.

間 / 間に - while, during · Formation

間あいだ is a noun, so whatever comes before it is in noun-modifying shape. The に is then added or not according to the rule above.

間 / 間に - while, during · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (ている) ている + 間あいだ（に） 寝ねる → 寝ねている間あいだ
Verb (stative, dictionary) Plain non-past + 間あいだ（に） いる → 日本にほんにいる間あいだに
Verb (negative) ない-form + 間あいだ（に） 見みる → 見みていない間あいだに
い-adjective Plain form + 間あいだ（に） 若わかい → 若わかい間あいだに
な-adjective な + 間あいだ（に） 静しずかだ → 静しずかな間あいだに
Noun の + 間あいだ（に） 休やすみ → 休やすみの間あいだ
Bare time span Span + の間あいだ 三日みっか → 三日みっかの間あいだ

間 / 間に - while, during · Formation (cont.)

Note - the subject inside the 間あいだ clause takes が, or の in more compact writing: 母ははが寝ねている間あいだ ≒ 母ははの寝ねている間あいだ. は cannot appear there.

Note - do not confuse this 間あいだ with the counter 間かん: 一週間いっしゅうかん, 二時間にじかん, 三日間みっかかん. Those are lengths of time. The あいだ of this page is a window you locate something in. You can stack them: 三日間みっかかんの間あいだ, though 三日みっかの間あいだ is tidier.

間 / 間に - while, during · Examples

夏休なつやすみの**間あいだ**、ずっと田舎いなかにいた。
I was in the countryside for the whole of the summer holiday.

母ははが寝ねている**間あいだに**、部屋へやを片付かたづけた。
I tidied the room while my mother was asleep.

日本にほんにいる**間あいだに**、一度いちど北海道ほっかいどうへ行いきたい。
I want to get to Hokkaido once while I'm in Japan.

授業じゅぎょうの**間あいだ**、彼かれは一言ひとことも話はなさなかった。
He didn't say a single word for the whole lesson.

間 / 間に - while, during · Examples (cont.)

留守るすの**間あいだに**宅配便たくはいびんが届とどいたらしい。
It seems a parcel arrived while I was out.

信号しんごうを待まっている**間あいだ**、弟おとうとはずっとスマホを見みていた。
My brother stared at his phone the entire time we were waiting at the lights.

私わたしが席せきを外はずしている**間あいだに**、課長かちょうが三回さんかいも電話でんわをくださったそうだ。
Apparently the section chief rang three times while I was away from my desk.

工事こうじをしている**間あいだ**、この道みちは車くるまが通とおれませんので、回まわり道みちをお願ねがいします。
For as long as the roadworks are going on this street is closed to traffic, so please take a detour.

間 / 間に - while, during · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. 間あいだ is at home in a train announcement, a contract and a text message alike, and it does not change shape for politeness.

The useful stylistic observations are elsewhere. First, in relaxed speech ている contracts as usual, so you hear 待まってる間あいだに rather than 待まっている間あいだに. Second, writing sometimes prefers の over が for the embedded subject (父ちちの留守るすの間あいだに), which reads as slightly compact and formal.

間 / 間に - while, during · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two adverbial fossils are worth recognising. 間あいだに can appear with no clause at all as そのあいだに, "meanwhile, in the meantime". And 間あいだじゅう - 授業じゅぎょうの間あいだじゅう - is an emphatic version of the no-に reading: "the entire time, without a break". If you find yourself wanting to add ずっと, 間あいだじゅう says it for you.

Finally, a piece of pronunciation. Written 間, this word is あいだ here, かん in counters, ま in 間まに合あう and 居間いま, and けん in some measurements. Kanji alone will not tell you which; the grammar around it will.

間 / 間に - while, during · 間 vs 間に

Rule of thumb - ask what the main clause does with the window. If it occupies the whole window, drop the に. If it lands somewhere inside it, keep the に.

赤あかちゃんが寝ねている**間あいだ**、本ほんを読よんでいました。
I read a book the whole time the baby was asleep.

赤あかちゃんが寝ねている**間あいだに**、本ほんを一冊いっさつ読よんでしまいました。
I got through a whole book while the baby was asleep.

Same nap, two different reports. In the first, reading and sleeping are the same length; in the second, the book was finished at some moment before the baby woke.

間 / 間に - while, during · 間 vs 間に (cont.)

That is why the two versions attract different main verbs. Without に you find durative predicates - ている, ずっと, adjectives, negatives of ongoing action:

旅行りょこうの**間あいだ**、天気てんきはずっとよかった。
The weather was good for the whole trip.

With に you find bounded ones - a completed action, a sudden event, てしまう, a counted quantity:

旅行りょこうの**間あいだに**財布さいふをなくしてしまった。
I lost my wallet at some point during the trip.

間 / 間に - while, during · 間 vs 間に (cont.)

Swap those and both sentences break. ❌旅行りょこうの間あいだ財布さいふをなくした would claim the losing went on for the length of the holiday.

Note - one honest complication. When the main clause is durative but shorter than the window, に comes back: 授業じゅぎょうの間あいだに少すこし寝ねた. The test is not "is the verb punctual" but "does the main clause stretch across the whole window". Punctual verbs simply always fail that test.

間 / 間に - while, during · 間に vs うちに

Rule of thumb - 間あいだに tells you when; うちに tells you when, and hurry. Only うちに implies the window is about to shut.

冬休ふゆやすみの**間あいだに**歯はを治なおした。
I got my teeth seen to over the winter break.

冬休ふゆやすみのうちに歯はを治なおしておこう。
Let's get my teeth seen to while we've still got the winter break.

The second says the holiday is a chance that will not come again soon. うちに pairs naturally with ておく, よう and たほうがいい for that reason, while 間あいだに is content with a plain past.

間 / 間に - while, during · 間に vs うちに (cont.)

The division of labour is clean in one direction: a window defined by clock or calendar takes 間あいだに and refuses うちに - 九時くじから十時じゅうじの間あいだに, not ❌九時くじから十時じゅうじのうちに. A window defined by a state that will end accepts both, with うちに adding the pressure.

間 / 間に - while, during · 間 vs ながら

Rule of thumb - ながら requires one person doing both things at once. 間あいだ does not care who the subjects are, and is the only option when they differ.

姉あねが料理りょうりをしている**間あいだ**、私わたしは皿さらを洗あらっていた。
I washed dishes the whole time my sister was cooking.

Two people, so ながら is impossible: ❌姉あねが料理りょうりをしながら私わたしは… is simply ungrammatical. Where the subject is shared, both work, and the difference becomes one of framing: ながら welds the two actions into one activity, while 間あいだ treats one as a container for the other.

間 / 間に - while, during · 間 vs ながら (cont.)

電車でんしゃに乗のっている**間あいだ**、ずっと音楽おんがくを聞きいていた。
I listened to music the whole time I was on the train.

Here 間あいだ is better than 電車でんしゃに乗のりながら, because riding a train is a situation you are in rather than an action you perform - and ながら wants two actions.

間 / 間に - while, during · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 寝ねている**間あいだに**、ずっといい夢ゆめを見みていた。 → ✅ 寝ねている**間あいだ**、ずっといい夢ゆめを見みていた。
    ずっと…ていた fills the whole window, so the に has to go. に would ask you to locate the dreaming at one moment inside the sleep.

  • ❌ 留守るすの**間あいだ、泥棒どろぼうが入はいった。 → ✅ 留守るすの間あいだに**泥棒どろぼうが入はいった。
    A break-in is a single moment, not a state lasting all day. A bounded event inside the window needs に.

  • ❌ 夏休なつやすみ**間あいだアルバイトをしました。 → ✅ 夏休なつやすみの間あいだ*アルバイトをしました。
    間あいだ is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs の. Compare the counter 一週間いっしゅうかん, which is a single word and takes nothing.*

  • ❌ 私わたしは旅行りょこうしている間あいだに、猫ねこを友ともだちに預あずけた。 → ✅ 私わたしが旅行りょこうしている間あいだに、猫ねこを友ともだちに預あずけた。
    The subject of the embedded clause takes が. A topic は cannot live inside a clause that is only modifying 間あいだ.

間 / 間に - while, during · Practice 1

1. 会議かいぎの____、一言ひとことも発言はつげんしなかった。

Saying nothing at all is a state that covers the entire meeting, so the window is filled and no に appears. Adding に would try to place a moment of not-speaking somewhere inside the meeting, which makes no sense.

間 / 間に - while, during · Practice 2

2. Choose: 私わたしが買かい物ものに行いっている（間あいだ・間あいだに）、犬いぬがソファをかじってしまった。

The chewing is a completed incident located somewhere inside the shopping trip, not something that lasted exactly as long as it. てしまった is a reliable signal: a bounded result takes に.

間 / 間に - while, during · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 二人ふたりが話はなしている**間あいだ、外そとで待まっていました。 (b) 二人ふたりが話はなしている間あいだに**、外そとに出でました。

間 / 間に - between

Meaning · the gap between two things - physically, or between two parties, or across a group

Same word (間あいだ), same core idea of "the space in the middle", but here the two edges are things rather than the start and end of a stretch of time. Between the station and the bank. Between the two of them. Popular among students. The grammar to get right is which particle follows: に for something located in the gap, で for something happening across a group, の when you are modifying a noun.

間 / 間に - between · Formation

間あいだ is a noun. Two nouns joined by と form the two edges, and の links the pair to 間あいだ.

Attaches to Form Example
Two nouns (space) AとBの間あいだ 机つくえ・いす → 机つくえといすの間あいだ
Location of something …の間あいだに + ある/いる 木き → 木きと木きの間あいだに道みちがある
Across a group Noun (plural sense) + の間あいだで 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいの間あいだで

間 / 間に - between · Examples

駅えきと銀行ぎんこうの**間あいだに**花屋はなやがある。
There's a florist between the station and the bank.

ソファとかべの**間あいだ**から鍵かぎが出でてきた。
The keys turned up from between the sofa and the wall.

その歌うたは若わかい人ひとたちの**間あいだで**はやっているらしい。
That song is apparently a hit among young people.

九時くじから五時ごじまでの**間あいだに**電話でんわをください。
Please call me between nine and five.

間 / 間に - between · Examples (cont.)

東京とうきょうと大阪おおさかの**間あいだ**を何度なんども往復おうふくした。
I shuttled back and forth between Tokyo and Osaka more times than I can count.

二人ふたりの**間あいだに**何なにがあったのか、だれも知しらない。
Nobody knows what went on between those two.

両国りょうこくの**間あいだで**新あたらしい貿易ぼうえきの話はなし合あいが始はじまった。
Fresh trade talks have opened between the two countries.

建物たてものと建物たてものの**間あいだに**細ほそい道みちがあって、そこを通とおると駅えきまで五分ごふんで着つきます。
There's a narrow lane between the two buildings, and if you cut through it you reach the station in five minutes.

間 / 間に - between · Nuance & register

The physical sense is entirely everyday. The other two lean slightly formal.

…の間あいだで meaning "among" belongs to news writing, market reports and anything describing a trend: 中高生ちゅうこうせいの間あいだで人気にんきが高たかまっている. In casual speech people usually say …の中なかで or just name the group with は. It is not stiff, but it does sound like something you read.

二人ふたりの間あいだ for a relationship is idiomatic and slightly delicate. 二人ふたりの間あいだに子こどもが生うまれた is a neutral way of saying a couple had a child; 二人ふたりの間あいだに何なにかあった gestures at trouble without naming it, and that discretion is exactly why the phrase gets used. The related noun 間柄あいだがら ("the relationship between parties") is a step more formal again and belongs to writing.

間 / 間に - between · Nuance & register (cont.)

A note on the spatial sense in speech: when the two edges are obvious, Japanese often drops one and says …の間あいだ with a plural or a repeated noun rather than spelling out A と B. 「本ほんの間あいだにはさんでおいた」 - "I slipped it in between the books" - needs no list.

Finally, this 間あいだ is a full noun and can take any particle a noun can: を (間あいだを通とおる), から, まで, は. The time-window use on the previous page is much more restricted.

間 / 間に - between · 間 (between) vs 間 (while)

Rule of thumb - look at what precedes it. Two nouns joined by と, or a から…まで pair, gives you between. A clause, or a single noun naming a period, gives you while.

夏なつと秋あきの**間あいだに**もう一度いちど海うみへ行いきたい。
I want to get to the sea once more between summer and autumn.

夏なつの**間あいだに**もう一度いちど海うみへ行いきたい。
I want to get to the sea once more during the summer.

One と is the entire difference. The first locates the trip in the gap between two seasons; the second locates it inside one of them.

間 / 間に - between · 間 (between) vs 間 (while) (cont.)

The ambiguity is real but rarely a problem, because the two readings need different material in front of them. Where it does bite is with から…まで: 九時くじから五時ごじまでの間あいだに is simultaneously "between nine and five" and "during nine-to-five", and Japanese does not distinguish them - the window is the gap.

間 / 間に - between · 間に vs 間で

Rule of thumb - に puts something in the gap; で makes the gap the arena in which something goes on. Existence and placement take に; activity, exchange and popularity take で.

棚たなと壁かべの**間あいだに**ほこりがたまっていた。
Dust had built up between the shelf and the wall.

社員しゃいんの**間あいだで**その話はなしはもう広ひろまっている。
Word of it has already got around among the staff.

Both particles are possible with two named parties, and then they say different things:

両親りょうしんの**間あいだに**問題もんだいがある。
There is a problem between my parents. (it exists, sitting there)

間 / 間に - between · 間に vs 間で (cont.)

両親りょうしんの**間あいだで**話はなし合あいが続つづいている。
Discussions are going on between my parents. (an activity they are conducting)

Verbs of existence and appearance - ある, いる, できる, 生うまれる - take に. Verbs of doing, spreading and agreeing take で. If in doubt, ask whether the thing sits there or happens there.

間 / 間に - between · 間 vs 中

Rule of thumb - 間あいだ is the gap between things; 中なか is the inside of a container or a set. Comparison and superlatives always take 中なか.

クラスの**中なかで**彼女かのじょが一番いちばん足あしが速はやい。
She's the fastest in the class.

クラスの**間あいだで**そのうわさが広ひろがった。
The rumour spread through the class.

間 / 間に - between · 間 vs 中 (cont.)

中なかで looks into a group and picks a member out; 間あいだで looks at the space between the members and describes what travels across it. That is why ❌三人さんにんの間あいだで一番いちばん背せが高たかい is wrong: choosing one of three is a 中なか job.

Note - the 中 in this contrast is なか, the ordinary noun. It is a different word from the suffix 中 read ちゅう/じゅう, which means "during" or "throughout" and attaches with no の. 会議かいぎの中なかで = "within the meeting's content"; 会議かいぎ中ちゅう = "the meeting is in progress".

間 / 間に - between · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 机つくえとベッドの**間あいだで**本棚ほんだながあります。 → ✅ 机つくえとベッドの**間あいだに**本棚ほんだながあります。
    ある and いる state that something is located somewhere, and location in a gap takes に. で would need an action to host.

  • ❌ 学生がくせいの**間あいだに**人気にんきがあります。 → ✅ 学生がくせいの**間あいだで**人気にんきがあります。
    Popularity, rumour, trends and discussions circulate across a group, and that is the で use. This one is worth memorising as a set: …の間あいだで人気にんきがある.

  • ❌ 四人よにんの間あいだで一番いちばん若わかいのは彼かれです。 → ✅ 四人よにんの**中なか**で一番いちばん若わかいのは彼かれです。
    Picking the extreme member of a set is a 中なか job. 間あいだ would be the space between them, where nobody stands.

間 / 間に - between · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 駅えきと郵便局ゆうびんきょく**間あいだ**に → ✅ 駅えきと郵便局ゆうびんきょく**の間あいだ**に
    The の linking the pair to 間あいだ is not optional. Both edges go in front, joined by と, then の.

間 / 間に - between · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 二人ふたりの**間あいだに**約束やくそくがあった。 (b) 二人ふたりの**間あいだで**約束やくそくをした。

～中

Meaning · a suffix meaning "in the middle of" (read ちゅう) or "all through, all over" (read じゅう)

This is not the noun 中なか. It is a suffix that glues straight onto the back of another noun with no の, and it comes in two flavours that share one kanji:

  • ちゅう - something is in progress, or a period is a deadline you must
    act inside. 会議かいぎ中ちゅう, 午前ごぜん中ちゅうに.
  • じゅう - a span or a space is covered from end to end.
    一日いちにち中じゅう, 世界せかい中じゅう.

～中 · Formation

Nothing conjugates; you are building a compound noun. What varies is the reading and whether に follows.

Attaches to Form Example
Activity noun → in progress Noun + 中ちゅう 工事こうじ → 工事こうじ中ちゅう
Period → deadline Noun + 中ちゅうに / 中じゅうに 午前ごぜん → 午前ごぜん中ちゅうに
Period → whole extent Noun + 中じゅう 一年いちねん → 一年いちねん中じゅう
Place or body → whole extent Noun + 中じゅう 世界せかい → 世界せかい中じゅう
As a predicate Noun + 中ちゅうだ／です 使用しよう → 使用しよう中ちゅうです

～中 · Formation (cont.)

Note - the reading is a property of the word, not something you derive. Learn these in two lists. ちゅう: 会議かいぎ中ちゅう, 食事しょくじ中ちゅう, 授業じゅぎょう中ちゅう, 電話でんわ中ちゅう, 営業えいぎょう中ちゅう, 使用しよう中ちゅう, 工事こうじ中ちゅう, 午前ごぜん中ちゅう, 今月こんげつ中ちゅうに じゅう: 一日いちにち中じゅう, 一年いちねん中じゅう, 今日きょう中じゅうに, 世界せかい中じゅう, 町まち中じゅう, 家いえ中じゅう, 体からだ中じゅう

～中 · Examples

父ちちは今いま電話でんわ**中ちゅう**です。
My father is on the phone at the moment.

昨日きのうは一日いちにち中じゅう雨あめだった。
It rained all day yesterday.

授業じゅぎょう**中ちゅう**はスマホをかばんにしまってください。
Please keep your phone in your bag during class.

この温泉おんせんは一年いちねん中じゅう営業えいぎょうしています。
This hot spring is open for business all year round.

～中 · Examples (cont.)

そのニュースはあっという間まに世界せかい**中じゅう**に広ひろがった。
The news spread around the world in no time.

昨日きのうは体からだ**中じゅう**が痛いたくて、何なにもする気きが起おきなかった。
I ached all over yesterday and couldn't summon the will to do anything.

工事こうじ**中ちゅう**のため、こちらの入いり口ぐちはお使つかいいただけません。
This entrance is out of use owing to construction work.

I'm in meetings all morning, so could I ask you to call again in the afternoon?

～中 · Nuance & register

The suffix itself is neutral, but the words it builds cluster in particular places. 中ちゅう compounds are the language of signs and status messages: 営業えいぎょう中ちゅう on a shop door, 使用しよう中ちゅう on a toilet lock, 準備じゅんび中ちゅう in a restaurant window, 会議かいぎ中ちゅう on a colleague's chat status. Learning to read them is worth more than learning to produce them.

中じゅう compounds lean the other way: they are emphatic and slightly emotional, and they turn up in complaints and enthusiasm alike - 部屋へや中じゅうほこりだらけだ, 町まち中じゅう探さがした. The nuance is "and I mean everywhere", so a flat, factual sentence often reads better with 全部ぜんぶ or ずっと instead.

～中 · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two things about the particle. When 中ちゅう/中じゅう names the window an action must land in, に is required: 今日きょう中じゅうに送おくります. When it merely describes the background, に is absent, and は often appears instead: 授業じゅぎょう中ちゅうは… - this is the same window-filling logic as 間あいだ without に.

One reading to flag honestly: for 今週こんしゅう and 今月こんげつ both readings circulate, and dictionaries generally give ちゅう - 今月こんげつ中ちゅうに. For 今日きょう the reading is solidly じゅう. If you hear a native speaker say it the other way round, they are not wrong; this is a genuinely unsettled corner.

～中 · 中 vs 中

Rule of thumb - 中ちゅう looks at a point inside a period: something is going on, or must be finished, within it. 中じゅう sweeps the whole extent: nothing is left out.

今日きょう中じゅうに見積みつもりをお送おくりします。
I'll send the quote before the day is out.

日曜日にちようびは一日いちにち中じゅう寝ねていた。
On Sunday I was asleep the whole day.

Note that 今日きょう中じゅう takes じゅう even though it is a deadline - the reading follows the word, not the meaning. The pattern is only a tendency.

～中 · 中 vs 中 (cont.)

Where a noun genuinely takes both, the meanings separate cleanly: 試験しけん中ちゅう is "the exam is under way", while 夏なつ中じゅう is "all summer long". You will not find a word that means both at once.

～中 · ～中に vs ～のうちに

Rule of thumb - 中ちゅうに is a limit, usually one somebody else set. のうちに is an opportunity you are choosing to use.

レポートは今月こんげつ中ちゅうに提出ていしゅつしてください。
Please hand the report in by the end of this month.

来月らいげつは出張しゅっちょうが多おおいから、今月こんげつのうちに書かいてしまおう。
I've a lot of business trips next month, so let's get it written while we still have this month.

～中 · ～中に vs ～のうちに (cont.)

The first is an instruction; the second is a plan with a reason attached. There is also a hard structural difference: 中ちゅう takes only nouns naming a measured span, while うちに takes clauses, adjectives and states - 元気げんきなうちに has no 中ちゅう equivalent at all.

～中 · ～中 vs ～の最中に

Rule of thumb - 中ちゅう just says the activity is on. 最中さいちゅうに says you are in the thick of it, and something has cut across it.

会議かいぎ**中ちゅう**ですので、後あとでかけ直なおします。
I'm in a meeting, so I'll call back later.

会議かいぎの**最中さいちゅうに**停電ていでんして、全部ぜんぶやり直なおしになった。
The power went out in the middle of the meeting and we had to start over.

～中 · ～中 vs ～の最中に (cont.)

中ちゅう is a neutral status label and works fine as a whole predicate. 最中さいちゅうに is dramatic and expects an interruption to follow. Note the different attachment too: 会議かいぎ中ちゅう with no の, but 会議かいぎの最中さいちゅう with one.

～中 · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 会議かいぎ**の中ちゅうに電話でんわが来きました。 → ✅ 会議かいぎ中ちゅう**に電話でんわが来きました。
    This 中 is a suffix and takes no の. Writing 会議かいぎの中なかで changes the word to なか and means "within the content of the meeting".

  • ❌ 一日いちにち中ちゅう歩あるきました。 → ✅ 一日いちにち中じゅう歩あるきました。
    "All day" is the whole-extent sense, which is じゅう. The kanji gives you no clue, so the reading has to be learned with the word.

  • ❌ 今日きょう中じゅうで終おわらせます。 → ✅ 今日きょう中じゅうに終おわらせます。
    A window that an action must land inside takes に, exactly as with 間あいだに. で would be marking a place or a means.

  • ❌ 弟おとうとは勉強べんきょう中ちゅうしています。 → ✅ 弟おとうとは勉強べんきょう中ちゅうです。
    中ちゅう makes a noun, so it ends in だ／です, not in a verb. Say either 勉強べんきょう中ちゅうです or 勉強べんきょうしています, never both.

1. すみません、山田やまだは今いま外出がいしゅつ____です。

外出がいしゅつ is an activity currently under way, which is the ちゅう sense, and the suffix attaches with no の. This is the standard way of telling a caller that somebody is out of the office.

The sentence covers the whole of the day from end to end, which is the じゅう sense, and 一日いちにち中じゅう is fixed in that reading. ❌一日いちにち中ちゅう is not an alternative pronunciation of the same phrase - it is simply wrong.

The の has to go: this 中 welds directly onto the noun in front of it. With の the kanji would be read なか and the phrase would mean "inside the preparations", which is not a thing.

～中 · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 旅行りょこう**中ちゅうに財布さいふをなくした。 (b) 旅行りょこうの間あいだ**ずっと天気てんきがよかった。

(a) uses the suffix to name a period the trip occupied and drops a single event into it, so it behaves exactly like 間あいだに: the wallet was lost at one moment somewhere inside the trip. The に is what places the event.

(b) has no に because the good weather lasted the whole trip. You could equally write 旅行りょこう中ちゅうずっと天気てんきがよかった - the suffix works there too, and the absence of に is doing the same job in both patterns.

～最中に

Meaning · right in the thick of it - the peak of an activity, cut across by something else

最中さいちゅう is 中ちゅう with the kanji for "most" bolted on the front, and that is exactly what it adds: not merely during, but at the busiest, most involved point of. Nine times out of ten the main clause is an interruption - the phone rings, the power fails, somebody walks in. If nothing cuts across, you probably wanted 中ちゅう or 間あいだに.

～最中に · Formation

最中さいちゅう is a noun, so what precedes it is in noun-modifying shape. Unlike the suffix 中ちゅう, it requires the の after a noun.

Attaches to Form Example
Activity noun Noun + の + 最中さいちゅうに 試合しあい → 試合しあいの最中さいちゅうに
Verb ている + 最中さいちゅうに 話はなす → 話はなしている最中さいちゅうに
As a predicate …の最中さいちゅうだ／です 準備じゅんび → 準備じゅんびの最中さいちゅうです
Emphatic 真まっ最中さいちゅう 工事こうじ → 工事こうじの真まっ最中さいちゅうだ

～最中に · Formation (cont.)

Note - the noun in front has to name an activity with a middle: 会議かいぎ, 食事しょくじ, 試合しあい, 授業じゅぎょう, 発表はっぴょう, 引ひっ越こし. States and life stages are refused: ❌若わかい最中さいちゅう, ❌留守るすの最中さいちゅう.

Note - the same two kanji, 最中, also spell もなか, a wafer-and-bean-paste sweet. Context separates them without difficulty, but the word does exist and you will meet it in a confectionery shop.

～最中に · Examples

食事しょくじの**最中さいちゅうに**電話でんわが鳴なった。
The phone rang right in the middle of dinner.

授業じゅぎょうの**最中さいちゅうに**おなかが鳴なってしまった。
My stomach rumbled in the middle of class.

試合しあいの**最中さいちゅうに**雨あめが降ふり出だした。
It started raining partway through the match.

会議かいぎの**最中さいちゅうに**居眠いねむりして、部長ぶちょうににらまれた。
I nodded off in the middle of the meeting and got a glare from the manager.

～最中に · Examples (cont.)

人ひとが話はなしている**最中さいちゅうに**口くちをはさむのはやめなさい。
Don't cut in while someone is speaking.

今いま、引ひっ越こしの準備じゅんびの**真まっ最中さいちゅう**なので、散ちらかっていてすみません。
Sorry about the state of the place - I'm right in the middle of packing to move.

発表はっぴょうの**最中さいちゅうに**パソコンが固かたまってしまい、頭あたまが真まっ白しろになった。
My laptop froze in the middle of my presentation and my mind went completely blank.

夕ゆうご飯はんを作つくっている**最中さいちゅうに**停電ていでんして、結局けっきょくカップラーメンで済すませた。
The power went out while I was in the middle of making dinner, and in the end we made do with instant noodles.

～最中に · Nuance & register

Register-neutral in itself, and usable in both ます and plain speech. What marks it is tone: 最中さいちゅうに is vivid. It dramatises the timing, and the speaker is nearly always annoyed, embarrassed or startled. A neutral report of when something happened does not want it - that is 間あいだに's job.

Because of that, the pattern is at home in complaints and anecdotes and comparatively rare in formal writing, where 中ちゅう or 際さいに would be used instead. A company notice says 工事こうじ中ちゅう; a colleague telling you about their week says 工事こうじの最中さいちゅうに.

～最中に · Nuance & register (cont.)

真まっ最中さいちゅう intensifies it further and is common as a whole predicate: 今いま準備じゅんびの真まっ最中さいちゅうです. Note that the intensified form usually appears without に, describing the current state rather than locating an event.

One structural habit worth internalising: the clause before 最中さいちゅう is almost always ている or a noun + の. A bare dictionary form is wrong - ❌話はなす最中さいちゅうに - because the pattern needs the activity to be visibly under way, and only ている says that.

～最中に · 最中に vs 間に

Rule of thumb - 間あいだに puts an event anywhere in the window, including the quiet edges. 最中さいちゅうに puts it at the peak, and implies it should not have happened there.

映画えいがを見みている**間あいだに**二回にかいメールが来きた。
Two emails came in while I was watching the film.

映画えいがの一番いちばんいいところの**最中さいちゅうに**前まえの人ひとが立たち上あがった。
The person in front stood up right at the best bit of the film.

～最中に · 最中に vs 間に (cont.)

The first is a neutral count of when the mail arrived. The second is a grievance. Note also that 最中さいちゅうに resists a durative main clause: ❌映画えいがの最中さいちゅうにずっと寝ねていた does not work, because a peak is a point and cannot host something lasting. Use 間あいだ, with no に, for that.

～最中に · 最中に vs ～中に

Rule of thumb - 中ちゅう is a label: this activity is on. 最中さいちゅう is a judgement: this activity was at full tilt, and look what happened.

運転うんてん**中ちゅう**は電話でんわに出でられません。
I can't take calls while driving.

運転うんてんしている**最中さいちゅうに**タイヤがパンクした。
I had a blowout in the middle of driving.

～最中に · 最中に vs ～中に (cont.)

The forms differ too, and the difference is easy to get wrong: 運転うんてん中ちゅう glues on with no の, whereas 運転うんてんの最中さいちゅう demands one. 中ちゅう also happily takes は for a rule that holds throughout; 最中さいちゅう almost never does, because a peak is not a period you lay rules over.

～最中に · 最中に vs ～ているところに

Rule of thumb - both host interruptions, but 最中さいちゅう emphasises how deep into it you were, while ところ emphasises which stage you were at - about to, just doing, just finished.

ケーキを焼やいている**最中さいちゅうに**客きゃくが来きた。
Guests turned up while I was right in the middle of baking a cake.

ケーキを焼やこうとしているところに客きゃくが来きた。
Guests turned up just as I was about to bake a cake.

～最中に · 最中に vs ～ているところに (cont.)

ところ can slide along the timeline - 焼やくところ, 焼やいているところ, 焼やいたところ - and each position means something different. 最中さいちゅう has only one position: the middle. That is why it cannot be used for "just about to" or "just after".

～最中に · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 会議かいぎ最中さいちゅうに席せきを立たった。 → ✅ 会議かいぎの最中さいちゅうに席せきを立たった。
    A noun in front needs の. It is the suffix 中ちゅう that attaches bare - 会議かいぎ中ちゅう - and mixing the two rules is the commonest slip.

  • ❌ 勉強べんきょうする最中さいちゅうに眠ねむくなった。 → ✅ 勉強べんきょうしている最中さいちゅうに眠ねむくなった。
    The activity has to be shown as under way, which means ている. A dictionary form points at the activity as a whole, not at its middle.

  • ❌ 若わかい最中さいちゅうに海外かいがいに住すみたい。 → ✅ 若わかいうちに海外かいがいに住すみたい。
    Youth is a state, not an activity in progress, so it has no peak for 最中さいちゅう to point at. States take うちに.

～最中に · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 旅行りょこうの最中さいちゅうにずっと天気てんきがよかった。 → ✅ 旅行りょこうの間あいだずっと天気てんきがよかった。
    最中さいちゅうに wants a single intruding event. Something that lasted the whole trip belongs with 間あいだ and no に.

A delivery arriving mid-shower is the textbook case: an activity at full tilt, cut across by something inconvenient. てしまった confirms the speaker's irritation, which is what 最中さいちゅうに exists to convey.

(a) simply locates the message somewhere inside the cleaning. It might have arrived as you were putting the vacuum away, and the sentence carries no attitude at all.

～途中に / ～途中で

Meaning · partway along - on the way from A to B, or partway through something that has a beginning and an end

途中とちゅう is "the middle stretch of a route". Japanese uses the same word for a physical journey (駅えきへ行いく途中とちゅう) and for any process that runs from start to finish (話はなしの途中とちゅう), so "on the way" and "partway through" are one word here. The choice between で and に is not free: で for something that happens, に for something that is there.

～途中に / ～途中で · Formation

途中とちゅう is a noun. A verb in front is in plain non-past form - even when the whole sentence is in the past.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb of motion Plain non-past + 途中とちゅうで／に 帰かえる → 帰かえる途中とちゅうで
Other verb ている + 途中とちゅうで 話はなす → 話はなしている途中とちゅうで
Noun の + 途中とちゅうで／に 会議かいぎ → 会議かいぎの途中とちゅうで
Partial extent 途中とちゅうまで／から 読よむ → 途中とちゅうまで読よんだ
As a predicate …の途中とちゅうだ／です 準備じゅんび → まだ準備じゅんびの途中とちゅうです

～途中に / ～途中で · Formation (cont.)

Note - the plain non-past in front is fixed: 帰かえる途中とちゅうで雨あめに降ふられた, never ❌帰かえった途中とちゅうで. The verb names the journey, and the journey has not finished - that is the whole point of being partway along it.

Note - the bare non-past belongs to verbs of going: 行いく, 来くる, 帰かえる, 向むかう, 登のぼる, 戻もどる. For other activities use ている (読よんでいる途中とちゅうで) or the noun form (読書どくしょの途中とちゅうで).

～途中に / ～途中で · Examples

学校がっこうへ行いく**途中とちゅうで**友ともだちに会あった。
I ran into a friend on the way to school.

駅えきへ行いく**途中とちゅうに**大おおきな公園こうえんがある。
There's a big park on the way to the station.

映画えいがの**途中とちゅうで**寝ねてしまった。
I fell asleep partway through the film.

帰かえる**途中とちゅうで**雨あめに降ふられた。
I got caught in the rain on the way home.

～途中に / ～途中で · Examples (cont.)

人ひとの話はなしの**途中とちゅうで**席せきを立たつのは失礼しつれいだ。
Getting up in the middle of what someone is saying is rude.

この本ほんはまだ**途中とちゅうまで**しか読よんでいません。
I've only read part of this book so far.

空港くうこうへ向むかう**途中とちゅうで**事故じこに巻まき込こまれ、飛行機ひこうきに乗のり遅おくれてしまった。
I got caught up in an accident on the way to the airport and missed my flight.

山やまを登のぼる**途中とちゅうに**小ちいさな茶屋ちゃやがあって、そこで休やすんでから頂上ちょうじょうを目指めざしました。
There's a little teahouse partway up the mountain; we rested there before pressing on for the summit.

～途中に / ～途中で · Nuance & register

Register-neutral and extremely common in everyday speech. There is no polite or plain variant to learn; only the particle choice matters.

The journey sense is so ordinary that it has produced fixed compounds: 帰かえり道みち for the way home (more idiomatic than 帰かえる途中とちゅう when you mean the route rather than the timing), and 通勤つうきん途中とちゅう, 帰宅きたく途中とちゅう in news writing and insurance forms, where the noun attaches with no の.

途中とちゅうまで and 途中とちゅうから are worth learning as separate items. 「途中とちゅうまで一緒いっしょに行いきましょう」 - "let's walk part of the way together" - is a normal, friendly thing to say, and 「途中とちゅうから見みたので、よく分わかりませんでした」 explains that you joined a film or a meeting late.

～途中に / ～途中で · Nuance & register (cont.)

Used as a predicate, まだ途中とちゅうです is a mild, useful hedge: "I'm not finished yet." It sounds considerably softer than まだ終おわっていません, because it presents the work as in motion rather than as incomplete.

～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 途中に

Rule of thumb - で for an event that occurs partway along; に for a thing that sits partway along. If the verb is ある or いる, it is に.

散歩さんぽの**途中とちゅうで**昔むかしの同僚どうりょうに会あった。
I bumped into a former colleague partway through my walk.

散歩さんぽコースの**途中とちゅうに**小ちいさな神社じんじゃがある。
There's a small shrine partway along the walking route.

～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 途中に (cont.)

The distinction mirrors the ordinary で/に split you already know: で marks where an action takes place, に marks where something exists. It follows that the great majority of 途中とちゅう sentences take で, because most of them report events. に shows up mainly with ある, いる, 立たっている and the like.

Note - with a noun naming a process rather than a route, で is effectively the only option: 会議かいぎの途中とちゅうで, 説明せつめいの途中とちゅうで. A meeting is not a road, so nothing can be standing beside it.

～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 最中に

Rule of thumb - 途中とちゅうで measures progress: you had got partway and had not finished. 最中さいちゅうに measures intensity: you were at the busiest point.

説明せつめいの**途中とちゅうで**質問しつもんしてもいいですか。
May I ask a question partway through your explanation?

説明せつめいの**最中さいちゅうに**質問しつもんされて、話はなしが止とまってしまった。
I was asked a question right in the middle of my explanation and lost my thread.

～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 最中に (cont.)

The first is a polite request; the second is a complaint. Only 途中とちゅう works with a journey - 駅えきへ行いく途中とちゅうで, never ❌駅えきへ行いく最中さいちゅうに - and only 途中とちゅう gives you まで and から, because only 途中とちゅう thinks of the activity as a line you can stop on.

～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 間に

Rule of thumb - 間あいだに needs a window with two ends and places an event inside it. 途中とちゅうで needs a process with a direction and says how far along you were.

授業じゅぎょうの**間あいだに**三回さんかい質問しつもんした。
I asked three questions during the lesson.

授業じゅぎょうの**途中とちゅうで**教室きょうしつを出でて行いった。
He walked out of the classroom partway through the lesson.

～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 間に (cont.)

Both are grammatical here, and the difference is one of emphasis: 間あいだに is neutral about position within the window, while 途中とちゅうで insists that the lesson was left unfinished. That is why 途中とちゅうで pairs so naturally with verbs of stopping, leaving and giving up - やめる, 帰かえる, 降おりる, あきらめる.

～途中に / ～途中で · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 家いえに**帰かえった途中とちゅうで**買かい物ものをした。 → ✅ 家いえに**帰かえる途中とちゅうで**買かい物ものをした。
    The verb stays in plain non-past however far in the past the sentence is. A past-tense verb would say the journey was already over, which contradicts 途中とちゅう.

  • ❌ 駅えきへ行いく途中とちゅうでコンビニがあります。 → ✅ 駅えきへ行いく途中とちゅうにコンビニがあります。
    あります states existence, and existence takes に. Use で only when something happens there.

  • ❌ 会議かいぎ途中とちゅうで抜ぬけてもいいですか。 → ✅ 会議かいぎの途中とちゅうで抜ぬけてもいいですか。
    An ordinary noun needs の. The bare-attachment compounds (通勤つうきん途中とちゅう and similar) are fixed vocabulary items, not a general rule.

～途中に / ～途中で · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 本ほんを**読よむ途中とちゅうで**寝ねてしまった。 → ✅ 本ほんを**読よんでいる途中とちゅうで**寝ねてしまった。
    Only verbs of going take the bare non-past. Anything else needs ている, or recast it as a noun: 読書どくしょの途中とちゅうで.

～途中に / ～途中で · Practice 1

1. 銀行ぎんこうへ行いく途中とちゅう____古ふるいお寺てらがあります。

銀行ぎんこうへ行いく途中とちゅうに古ふるいお寺てらがあります。

あります reports that something exists along the route, and existence takes に. で would be looking for an action, and standing there is not something the temple does.

～途中に / ～途中で · Practice 2

2. Choose: 説明せつめいの（途中とちゅうで・最中さいちゅうに）話はなしをやめて、質問しつもんを受うけ付つけます。

説明せつめいの**途中とちゅうで**話はなしをやめて、質問しつもんを受うけ付つけます。

This is a planned pause partway through, not an intrusion, and やめる is exactly the kind of verb 途中とちゅうで attracts. 最中さいちゅうに would frame the speaker's own plan as an unwelcome interruption of themselves.

～途中に / ～途中で · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I noticed on the way to the office that I'd forgotten my wallet."

会社かいしゃへ行いく**途中とちゅうで**財布さいふを忘わすれたことに気きがついた。

Realising something is an event, so the particle is で, and 行いく stays in the plain non-past even though the whole sentence is in the past.

～途中に / ～途中で · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昨日きのう、駅えきに着ついた途中とちゅうで財布さいふを落おとしました。

✅ 昨日きのう、駅えきへ**行いく途中とちゅうで**財布さいふを落おとしました。

Two things are wrong. The verb must be plain non-past, and it must be one that describes the journey rather than its completion - 着つく means "arrive", so 着つく途中とちゅう is a contradiction. 行いく or 向むかう is what you want.

～途中に / ～途中で · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 駅えきへ行いく**途中とちゅうで**本屋ほんやに寄よった。 (b) 駅えきへ行いく**途中とちゅうに**本屋ほんやがある。

(a) reports an action performed partway along the journey: you interrupted the walk to drop into the shop. で is the particle of "where an action happens", and 寄よった is plainly an action.

(b) reports a fact about the route itself: a bookshop stands somewhere along it, regardless of whether anyone goes in. に is the particle of location, and ある is the verb that demands it. The two sentences describe the same street and answer completely different questions.
