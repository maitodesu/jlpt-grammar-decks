～(よ)うとする - try to

Meaning · make a move to do something - the attempt itself, with the result left hanging

The volitional form carries the will; とする turns that will into a physical move. ～(よ)うとする photographs the effort, not the outcome - and Japanese only bothers to photograph the effort when the outcome was not the obvious one. That is why so many of these sentences end in が or けれど.

～(よ)うとする - try to · Formation

The volitional is frozen. する at the end is an ordinary verb and carries all the tense and politeness for the whole phrase.

When the power went out I tried to find a torch, but it was pitch dark and I had no idea where it was.

However hard I try to remember, that one person's name just won't come to me.

～(よ)うとする - try to · Nuance & register

The single most useful fact: in the past tense, the attempt normally failed. If you tried and got there, Japanese reports the arrival - 窓まどを閉しめました - and says nothing about trying. So 閉しめようとしました tells your listener the window is still open. English "I tried to" is often modest understatement for success; this pattern is not.

The て-form is where the pattern earns its keep at N3. ～(よ)うとして plus a following clause says the attempt is what caused what came next: 拾ひろおうとして指ゆびを切きった - the cutting happened in the course of the reaching. This is the natural way to narrate a small accident, and it is far more common in speech than the textbook ～(よ)うとしたが.

～(よ)うとする - try to · Nuance & register (cont.)

The subject has to have a will. That is what separates this sense from the "about to" sense, where a typhoon or a sunset can be the subject. Here, only a creature that can decide something gets to try.

For your own standing effort the plain non-past is wrong: ❌毎日まいにち歩あるこうとしています. A policy you are keeping up is ～ようにしている. The plain non-past ～(よ)うとする is comfortable in general statements about people - 人ひとは楽らくな道みちを選えらぼうとする - where it describes a tendency rather than one person's morning.

～(よ)うとする - try to · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral; する takes whatever politeness the sentence needs. In very casual speech the whole thing is often replaced by ～(し)かけた or by 一応いちおうやってみた, so if you hear 開あけようとしたんだけど in conversation, note that it already sounds a shade more deliberate than the alternatives.

～(よ)うとする - try to · ～(よ)うとする vs ～ようと思う

Rule of thumb - ～ようと思おもう happens in your head; ～(よ)うとする happens with your hands. The first is a decision, the second is a movement.

来年らいねん、車くるまを**買かおうと思おもっています**。
I'm thinking of buying a car next year.

車くるまを**買かおうとした**ら、貯金ちょきんが足たりませんでした。
I went to buy a car and found I didn't have enough saved.

～(よ)うとする - try to · ～(よ)うとする vs ～ようと思う (cont.)

The first sentence is compatible with never going near a dealership. The second one puts you in front of the price tag. This is also why ～ようと思おもう is the polite way to announce a plan and ～(よ)うとする is not: telling your boss 辞やめようとしています sounds like you are halfway out of the building.

～(よ)うとする - try to · ～(よ)うとする vs ～てみる

Rule of thumb - both are "try", but they doubt different things. ～(よ)うとする doubts whether the action can happen at all. ～てみる takes the action for granted and doubts what it will be like.

新あたらしいアプリを**使つかってみました**が、前まえのほうが便利べんりでした。
I gave the new app a go, but the old one was handier.

新あたらしいアプリを**使つかおうとしました**が、登録とうろくできませんでした。
I tried to use the new app, but I couldn't even register.

In the first the app ran. In the second it never opened. Because they are about different things, they stack quite happily: 使つかってみようとした - "I made an attempt at giving it a go."

～(よ)うとする - try to · ～(よ)うとする vs ～ようにする

Rule of thumb - ～(よ)うとする is one moment of trying. ～ようにする is a rule you have set yourself and are keeping to.

エレベーターを**使つかわないようにしています**。
I make a point of not using the lift.

階段かいだんを**上のぼろうとしました**が、足あしが痛いたくて無理むりでした。
I tried to go up the stairs, but my leg hurt too much.

Anything with 毎日まいにち, 最近さいきん or なるべく in it wants ～ようにする. Anything with その時とき in it wants ～(よ)うとする.

～(よ)うとする - try to · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 鍵かぎを探さがすとした → ✅ 鍵かぎを探さがそうとした
    とする here attaches to the volitional, not the dictionary form. Dictionary form + とする exists, but it is a separate written pattern meaning "supposing that", and a learner who mixes them will be misread.

  • ❌ 荷物にもつを運はこびましょうとしました → ✅ 荷物にもつを運はこぼうとしました
    Only the final する gets the polite ending. The volitional inside stays plain - ましょう never appears in front of と.

  • ❌ その映画えいがを見みようとしました。とても面白おもしろかったです。 → ✅ その映画えいがを見みてみました。
    You watched it and formed an opinion, so the attempt succeeded. A successful "try it and see" is てみる.

～(よ)うとする - try to · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 夜よる十一時じゅういちじまでに寝ねようとしています → ✅ 夜よる十一時じゅういちじまでに寝ねるようにしています
    A bedtime you are trying to keep is a standing policy, not a single lunge at the duvet.

  • ❌ 台風たいふうが島しまに近ちかづこうとしました → ✅ 台風たいふうが島しまに近ちかづこうとしていました
    A typhoon cannot attempt anything. With a subject that has no will, only the "on the verge of" reading is available, and that reading needs ている.

～(よ)うとする - try to · Practice 1

1. バスに乗の____としたとき、定期券ていきけんを家いえに忘わすれたことに気きづいた。

バスに乗のろうとしたとき…

乗のる is a う-verb, so the volitional is 乗のろう. ～(よ)うとしたとき pins the sentence to the instant before boarding, which is exactly when a missing pass would announce itself.

～(よ)うとする - try to · Practice 2

2. Choose: 去年きょねんから、朝あさごはんを必かならず（食たべようとしています・食たべるようにしています）。

必かならず**食たべるようにしています**。

去年きょねんから and 必かならず make this a rule you have been keeping for a year - a standing policy, which is ～ようにする. ～(よ)うとしている would describe you at one particular breakfast, fork in hand.

～(よ)うとする - try to · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I tried to open the jar, but the lid wouldn't come off."

びんを**開あけようとしました**が、ふたが取とれませんでした。

The lid stayed on, so the action never completed - that is exactly the job of ～(よ)うとした. Using 開あけてみました would say the jar opened and you looked inside.

～(よ)うとする - try to · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼かれは立たつとして、テーブルに手てをついた。

✅ 彼かれは立たとうとして、テーブルに手てをついた。 "He put a hand on the table as he went to stand up."

立たつ is a う-verb ending in つ, so the volitional is 立たとう. The て-form then links the attempt to what it caused - the hand on the table happened in the course of getting up.

～(よ)うとする - try to · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 窓まどを開あけてみました。 (b) 窓まどを開あけようとしました。

(a) says the window opened. てみる is "do it and see what happens", so the interesting part is what followed - a breeze, a noise from the street, the smell of rain. The action itself was never in doubt.

(b) says the window did not open, or at least that something interrupted you. The interesting part is the obstacle, and a listener will wait for the が clause that explains it. Said with no follow-up, (b) sounds unfinished.

～(よ)うとする - be about to

Meaning · on the very point of happening - the instant before the event tips over

Same three syllables as the "try to" pattern, but a different job. Here nobody is trying anything: ～(よ)うとしている freezes the frame just before a change completes. The winter that has not quite ended, the doors that have not quite shut. Because no will is involved, the subject can be a season, a train or a storm.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · Formation

This sense almost always wears ている or ていた, or else feeds into とき or ところ. The bare non-past ～(よ)うとする does not carry the "about to" reading.

The long winter is drawing to a close.

The ship is about to leave.

The phone rang just as I was about to get in the bath.

The lights in the hall were on the point of going out, so I gathered my things in a hurry.

新あたらしい時代じだいが**始はじまろうとしている**と、その日ひの新聞しんぶんは書かいていた。
That day's paper wrote that a new age was about to begin.

眠ねむりに**落おちようとしていた**ところを、上うえの階かいの物音ものおとに起おこされた。
I was just drifting off when a noise from upstairs woke me.

ホームで最終さいしゅう電車でんしゃが**発車はっしゃしようとしている**のを見みて、走はしるのをあきらめました。
Seeing the last train on the point of pulling out, I gave up on running for it.

会社かいしゃを**辞やめようとしていた**ちょうどその時とき、部長ぶちょうから異動いどうの話はなしが来きた。
Just when I was on the point of quitting, my manager raised the idea of a transfer.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · Nuance & register

The reliable test for which sense you are in is the subject. If it could not possibly decide anything - a season, a candle, a train, a century - you are in the "about to" sense, and no amount of context will make it mean "tried to". With a person, the form is genuinely ambiguous, and this is harmless: both readings point at the same instant, and the sentence is normally interrupted by something anyway.

The register is narrative. This pattern belongs to news copy, novels, essays and the sort of speech that is consciously describing a scene. In ordinary conversation people reach for もうすぐ, ところ or ～そう: もうすぐ着つくよ, not 着つこうとしているよ. Saying ご飯はんが炊たけようとしています to a housemate sounds like you are narrating a documentary about your rice cooker.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · Nuance & register (cont.)

今いまにも pairs with it naturally and raises the tension a notch: 今いまにも倒たおれようとしている. So does まさに in written Japanese.

One boundary worth marking: this is about a moment, not about the near future. Next week's roadworks are not 始はじまろうとしています; they simply 始はじまります. If you can attach a date to it, you have left the pattern's territory.

Finally, the ところ frame is the most conversational corner of this sense. ～(よ)うとしたところに plus an interruption - a phone call, a visitor, rain - is a small, very common storytelling move, and it survives in casual speech where the plain ている form would sound stiff.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · ～(よ)うとしている vs ～ところだ

Rule of thumb - ところだ needs a subject with a schedule; it is the neutral "I'm just about to". ～(よ)うとしている is a camera angle, and it works on things that never planned anything.

今いまから出でかけるところです。
I'm just heading out now.

雪ゆきが屋根やねから**落おちようとしている**。
The snow is on the point of sliding off the roof.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · ～(よ)うとしている vs ～ところだ (cont.)

Swap them and both go wrong. Snow has no ところ of its own to be standing at, and narrating your own departure with 出でかけようとしています makes you sound like a character in a film rather than a person telling a friend where you are.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · ～(よ)うとしている vs ～かけている

Rule of thumb - ～(よ)うとしている means it has not started. ～かけている means it started and stopped partway.

ろうそくの火ひが**消きえようとしている**。
The candle is about to go out.

ろうそくの火ひが**消きえかけている**。
The candle is guttering out.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · ～(よ)うとしている vs ～かけている (cont.)

The first candle is still burning steadily; you can see that it has almost no wax left. The second is already halfway gone - the flame is failing as you watch. The same split explains 読よみかけの本ほん (a book you are partway through) against 読よもうとしている本ほん (one you have not opened).

～(よ)うとする - be about to · ～(よ)うとしている vs 今にも～そうだ

Rule of thumb - both mean "any second now", but ～そうだ is a guess read off the surface of something, while ～(よ)うとしている reports the event as genuinely underway.

棚たなの箱はこが**落おちそうです**。
That box on the shelf looks like it's going to fall.

棚たなの箱はこが**落おちようとしています**。
The box on the shelf is on the point of falling.

The first is your inference from how the box is sitting; it may sit there all year. The second commits: the box is going. In warnings and in speech, そうだ is almost always what you want - it is the one that leaves you room to be wrong.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 月つきが出でようとする。 → ✅ 月つきが出でようとしている。
    The "about to" reading lives in ている. Without it, the plain non-past reads as a general statement about attempting, which the moon cannot do.

  • ❌ バスが来きようとしている。 → ✅ バスが来こようとしている。
    The volitional of 来くる is こよう, not きよう. The ます-stem reading き belongs to 来きます and nowhere near this pattern.

  • ❌ 来週らいしゅう、駅前えきまえの工事こうじが始はじまろうとしています。 → ✅ 来週らいしゅう、駅前えきまえの工事こうじが始はじまります。
    A dated future event is just the plain non-past. This pattern is for the second before something happens, not for next week.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ もうすぐ映画えいがを見みようとしています。 → ✅ もうすぐ映画えいがを見みるところです。
    About yourself, with a plan, ところだ is the natural form. ～(よ)うとしている here narrates you from outside, which is why it sounds oddly grand.

  • ❌ 会議かいぎが終おわろうとしましたとき → ✅ 会議かいぎが終おわろうとしたとき
    A clause in front of とき takes the plain form. Politeness goes on the end of the sentence, not inside it.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · Practice 2

2. Choose: 屋根やねの雪ゆきが（落おちそうです・落おちようとしています）から、下したを通とおらないでください。

A warning is an inference - you are reading the risk off the roof, not announcing that the snow is already moving. ～そうだ is the form for that. 落おちようとしています would claim the fall has begun, which would make "don't walk under it" oddly late advice.

～(よ)うとしたとき is the standard frame for "just as I was about to, something cut in". 出でようとしたところに works equally well and sounds a shade more conversational.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 一年いちねんが終おわろうとしていますので、来年らいねんの計画けいかくを立たてましょう。

The sentence is fine in meaning; the error is placement of politeness. A clause in front of ので normally takes the plain form, with ます kept for the sentence ending. 終おわろうとしていますので is not ungrammatical, but it belongs to very formal announcements, not to a suggestion among colleagues.

～(よ)うとする - be about to · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 花はなが散ちろうとしている。 (b) 花はなが散ちりかけている。

(a) says none of the petals have come down yet, but the moment is here - the tree is at its last full day. It is a narrator's sentence, the kind that opens a paragraph.

(b) says the falling has already begun and is partway through: petals on the ground, gaps in the branches. かける is about an interrupted or incomplete process, so it reports a state you can photograph, while (a) reports a threshold about to be crossed.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない

Meaning · won't even try - the person is unwilling, and the speaker is not pleased about it

Negating the attempt does something the plain negative cannot: it puts the other person's will in the frame. 食たべない reports that no eating is happening. 食たべようとしない says they have decided against it, and you can hear the speaker's patience running out.

The city office would not even attempt to answer the residents' questions.

話はなし合あいは三時間さんじかん続つづきましたが、どちらの側がわも一歩いっぽもゆずろうとしませんでした。
The talks ran for three hours, but neither side would give an inch.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない · Examples (cont.)

新あたらしいやり方かたをどれだけ説明せつめいしても、ベテランの職人しょくにんたちは耳みみを**貸かそうとしなかった**。
No matter how thoroughly the new method was explained, the veteran craftsmen wouldn't hear of it.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない · Nuance & register

This pattern is a complaint, and that is not a side effect - it is the reason the pattern exists. Using it means asserting that you know what is going on inside someone else's head. That makes it comfortable about children, animals, machines and institutions, awkward about equals, and genuinely risky about people you owe deference to. 部長ぶちょうは決きめようとしません is not a neutral status report; it is an accusation of obstruction, and it will be heard as one.

Unlike the "try to" sense, the non-past is completely natural here, because it describes a standing state of refusal rather than one moment of effort. 食たべようとする about a habit is odd; 食たべようとしない about a habit is the normal thing to say.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない · Nuance & register (cont.)

The three degrees are worth learning as a set:

  • ～(よ)うとしない - plain report of unwillingness.
  • ～(よ)うとはしない - the は sets this action against everything
    else the person did do: that one thing, they would not do. More pointed, and commoner in writing.
  • ～(よ)うともしない - "would not so much as". The most dismissive of
    the three, and often paired with どれだけ, いくら or 一度いちども.

Applied to objects, the pattern is a deliberate personification and reads as exasperated rather than mistaken: 車くるまが動うごこうとしない casts the car as sulking. That is a stylistic choice, not the default - a bus that is merely late is なかなか来きません.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない · Nuance & register (cont.)

About yourself, the present tense is strange - you would simply say やりたくない. The past is fine in reflective narration: あのころの私わたしは誰だれの話はなしも聞きこうとしなかった, looking back at your younger self as if at someone else.

～(よ)うとしない vs ～たがらない

Rule of thumb - ～たがらない says they don't want to. ～(よ)うとしない says they won't do it, whatever they may want.

弟おとうとは写真しゃしんを撮とられたがりません。
My little brother doesn't like having his photo taken.

弟おとうとは写真しゃしんの前まえに**立たとうとしません**。
My little brother won't stand in front of the camera.

～(よ)うとしない vs ～たがらない (cont.)

たがる reports a desire you have inferred from the outside; the sentence is about his feelings. ～(よ)うとしない reports behaviour, and it works even where desire is irrelevant - someone may badly want to apologise and still 謝あやまろうとしない. That gap is the useful part: it is how you describe pride, fear or stubbornness overriding what a person actually wants.

～(よ)うとしない vs ～てくれない

Rule of thumb - ～(よ)うとしない criticises the other person's attitude. ～てくれない complains that you did not get what you needed.

店員てんいんが説明せつめいしてくれなかった。
The assistant didn't explain it to me.

店員てんいんが説明せつめいしようとしなかった。
The assistant wouldn't even try to explain.

～(よ)うとしない vs ～てくれない (cont.)

The first is about the loss to you; the assistant might have been busy or new. The second is about the assistant's unwillingness. Stacking them makes the strongest version of the complaint: 説明せつめいしようとしてくれなかった - they would not make the effort for me.

～(よ)うとしない vs ～(よ)うとはしない vs ～(よ)うともしない

Rule of thumb - bare と states it, とは contrasts it against what they did do, とも says they did not even get as far as starting.

彼かれは理由りゆうを**話はなそうとしなかった**。
He wouldn't tell me why.

彼かれは言いい訳わけはしたが、理由りゆうを**話はなそうとはしなかった**。
He made excuses, but the actual reason he would not give.

彼かれは理由りゆうを**話はなそうともしなかった**。
He didn't even attempt to tell me why.

～(よ)うとしない vs ～(よ)うとはしない vs ～(よ)うともしない (cont.)

The middle sentence needs the first clause to earn its は; without something to contrast against, とはしない can sound as though a comparison has gone missing. The third needs no setup at all - も is doing the work of "not even" by itself.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 子こどもが靴くつを履はくとしない → ✅ 子こどもが靴くつを履はこうとしない
    The dictionary form never appears here. It is volitional + としない, so 履はく becomes 履はこう.

  • ❌ 私わたしは朝あさごはんを食たべようとしません。 → ✅ 私わたしは朝あさごはんを食たべません。
    About your own present habit, the pattern is odd - it makes you sound like a bystander disapproving of yourself. Use the plain negative, or 食たべたくないんです if you want the reason in.

  • ❌ バスがなかなか来こようとしません。 → ✅ バスがなかなか来きません。
    A late bus is not refusing anything. Reserve the pattern for subjects with a will, or for a personification you actually mean.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌（to your manager）部長ぶちょうはメールに返事へんじをしようとしませんね。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、メールの件けん、まだお返事へんじをいただいていないのですが。
    Said to the person themselves, this accuses them of deliberate obstruction. When the subject is someone you must be polite to, drop the pattern entirely and report the fact.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない · Practice 1

1. 赤あかちゃんが眠ねむ____としないので、母ははは一晩中ひとばんじゅうだっこしていた。

赤あかちゃんが眠ねむろうとしないので…

眠ねむる is a う-verb, so the volitional is 眠ねむろう. The pattern fits a baby perfectly: it is exactly the sentence of a parent who has decided the child is doing this on purpose.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない · Practice 2

2. Choose: 彼かれは人前ひとまえで歌うたい（たがりません・ようとしません）。カラオケでもずっとすみに座すわっています。

Both are possible, and they say different things. 歌うたいたがりません reports that he has no desire to sing, which fits someone quietly sitting in the corner. 歌うたおうとしません would add that people have been pushing him and he is holding out. Given that the second sentence only describes him sitting there, 歌うたいたがりません is the more natural fit.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I offered several times, but he wouldn't take the money."

何度なんどもすすめましたが、彼かれはお金かねを**受うけ取とろうとしませんでした**。

The point of the sentence is that he could have taken it and chose not to - that is ～(よ)うとしない rather than a plain 受うけ取とりませんでした, which would merely say no money changed hands.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 雨あめがやもうとしないので、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしになりました。

✅ 雨あめがやまないので、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしになりました。

Rain has no will, so it cannot refuse to stop. The plain negative is what you want. やもうとしない is possible only as a conscious personification in literary writing - in a report of a cancelled match it just reads as an error.

～(よ)うとしない・～(よ)うとはしない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼女かのじょはこちらを見みなかった。 (b) 彼女かのじょはこちらを見みようともしなかった。

(a) records that her eyes did not come this way. She may not have known you were there; the sentence takes no view on why.

(b) says she knew perfectly well and would not even turn her head. とも adds "not even", so the sentence measures her refusal against the smallest possible effort and finds she did not make it. It is the harshest of the three degrees, and it tells you as much about the speaker's feelings as about hers.

～べき

Meaning · ought to; is the right thing to do - measured against a principle, not against convenience

べき passes a judgement. It says the action is correct - morally, socially, professionally - and it does so with the weight of a standard behind it. That is what separates it from ほうがいい, which merely says the action would work out better for you.

～べき · Formation

べき is a survival from classical Japanese, which is why it attaches to the dictionary form and why する has two shapes.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb dictionary form + べきだ 守まもる → 守まもるべきだ
Verb (する) するべき / すべき 報告ほうこくする → 報告ほうこくするべきだ・報告ほうこくすべきだ
Verb (来くる) 来くるべき 来くる → 来くるべきだ
Before a noun ～べき + noun 読よむ → 読よむべき本ほん
Regret about the past ～べきだった 言いう → 言いうべきだった
Polite ～べきです・べきでした 守まもるべきです
Noun / な-adjective (written) ～である + べき 公平こうへい → 公平こうへいであるべきだ

～べき · Formation (cont.)

Note - するべき and すべき mean the same thing. すべき is the older form and is preferred in writing, editorials and formal speech; するべき is easier on the ear in conversation. Neither is wrong. In noun-modifying position the だ disappears - 言いうべきこと, not ❌言いうべきだこと.

～べき · Examples

もっと野菜やさいを**食たべるべきだ**。
You ought to eat more vegetables.

約束やくそくは**守まもるべきです**。
Promises should be kept.

こういうことは、早はやめに上司じょうしに**報告ほうこくすべきだ**。
Something like this ought to be reported to your manager early.

あの時とき、はっきり「できません」と**言いうべきだった**。
I should have said "I can't" clearly at the time.

～べき · Examples (cont.)

学生がくせいのうちに**読よむべき**本ほんを、先生せんせいが紹介しょうかいしてくださいました。
My teacher recommended some books one ought to read while still a student.

会議かいぎの前まえに、資料しりょうに目めを**通とおしておくべきでした**。
I should have looked through the handouts before the meeting.

災害さいがいに備そなえて、水みずと食料しょくりょうを**用意よういしておくべきだ**と思おもいます。
I think we ought to keep water and food ready in case of a disaster.

本来ほんらいこの制度せいどは、一番いちばん困こまっている人ひとを**助たすけるべきもの**です。
This system is supposed, by its very nature, to help the people in the greatest difficulty.

～べき · Examples (cont.)

失敗しっぱいしたときにどうするかを、チーム全員ぜんいんで**決きめておくべきだった**と後悔こうかいしています。
I regret that we didn't agree as a team, in advance, what to do if it went wrong.

～べき · Nuance & register

べき is heavy. It appeals to something outside the speaker - a principle, a duty, the way things are done - and that is why it turns up constantly in newspaper editorials, essays, court judgements and meeting minutes, and why it can land badly in a kitchen conversation. Told もっと野菜やさいを食たべるべきだ, a friend hears a small sermon.

Aimed at a listener, therefore, べき needs softening, and Japanese has a standard kit for it:

  • ～べきだと思おもいます - makes it your opinion rather than the
    law.
  • ～べきではないでしょうか - a rhetorical question, the politest form
    of the same claim, very common in meetings.
  • ～べきかもしれません - hedged almost to invisibility.

～べき · Nuance & register (cont.)

Bare ～べきだ pointed at someone senior to you is close to insubordinate. Between friends it is fine if you are half-joking, and it is completely fine when the subject is you - 私わたしが行いくべきでした - or an abstraction: 政治せいじは公平こうへいであるべきだ.

The past form is the one learners under-use. ～べきだった is the everyday way to express regret about a choice you made: 買かうべきだった, 聞きいておくべきだった. It is neither formal nor preachy in that use, because the person being judged is yourself.

～べき · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - you will meet the same word as ～べく ("in order to") and ～べからず ("do not") on signs and in stiff writing: 立たち入いるべからず. Both are frozen classical leftovers; recognise them, but write ～ために and ～ないでください yourself.

～べき · ～べきだ vs ～なければならない

Rule of thumb - なければならない is an obligation imposed on you: a rule, a deadline, a law. べきだ is a judgement you are making: this is the right thing.

外国人がいこくじんは在留ざいりゅうカードを持もち歩あるかなければなりません。
Foreign residents are required to carry their residence card.

留学生りゅうがくせいにもっと情報じょうほうを出だすべきです。
More information ought to be made available to international students.

The first is the law; you have no view about it, you are reporting it. The second is an argument, and someone could disagree with you. Swap them and the first sentence becomes an opinion about immigration policy, while the second sounds as if a regulation exists that you have not cited.

～べき · ～べきだ vs ～なければならない (cont.)

This also explains a common error. If the requirement is already binding, べき is the weaker and stranger choice: a visa application that must include a photo takes 入いれなければなりません.

～べき · ～べきだ vs ～ほうがいい

Rule of thumb - ほうがいい compares outcomes for this person, this situation. べきだ measures the action against a standard and is indifferent to whether it suits you.

熱ねつがあるなら、今日きょうは休やすんだほうがいいですよ。
If you've got a fever, you'd be better off taking today off.

熱ねつがあるのに出勤しゅっきんさせる会社かいしゃのほうが、考かんがえを改あらためるべきです。
It's the company making people come in with a fever that ought to change its thinking.

ほうがいい is advice and is heard as kindness. べき is a verdict and is heard as criticism - which is exactly why the second sentence uses it. For everyday suggestions to a friend, ほうがいい is almost always the safer form.

～べき · ～べきだった vs ～ばよかった

Rule of thumb - both look back at a wrong choice. ばよかった is wishing; べきだった is judging yourself.

もっと早はやく出でればよかった。
I wish I'd left earlier.

もっと早はやく出でるべきだった。
I should have left earlier.

～べき · ～べきだった vs ～ばよかった (cont.)

The first is a sigh; it fits missing a nice sunset. The second accepts fault and fits missing a flight you were responsible for. ばよかった can also be used about things nobody controlled - 晴はれればよかったのに - where べきだった is impossible, because weather cannot be blamed.

～べき · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 教師きょうしは公平こうへいべきだ。 → ✅ 教師きょうしは公平こうへいであるべきだ。
    べき needs a verb in front of it. Nouns and な-adjectives get there through である.

  • ❌ 注意ちゅういしるべき → ✅ 注意ちゅういするべき・注意ちゅういすべき
    する gives two legitimate forms and neither is しる. Pick すべき for writing, するべき for speech.

  • ❌ ビザの申請しんせいには写真しゃしんを入いれるべきです。 → ✅ ビザの申請しんせいには写真しゃしんを入いれなければなりません。
    A published requirement is not your judgement. Reporting a rule takes なければならない.

～べき · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌（to your manager）部長ぶちょう、もっと休やすむべきです。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、少すこしお休やすみになったほうがよろしいのではないでしょうか。
    Bare べきだ aimed upwards is a reprimand. Move to ほうがいい, or at the very least to ～べきではないでしょうか.

  • ❌ 言いうべきだことがあります。 → ✅ 言いうべきことがあります。
    In front of a noun, べき stands alone. The だ only appears at the end of a sentence.

～べき · Practice 1

1. 自分じぶんが悪わるいと分わかっているなら、すぐに謝あやま____だ。

すぐに謝あやまるべきだ。

べき attaches to the dictionary form, so 謝あやまる stays whole. The sentence is a moral judgement - apologising is the right thing - which is precisely べき territory rather than ほうがいい.

～べき · Practice 2

2. Choose: 試験しけんの日ひは受験票じゅけんひょうを持もってこ（なければなりません・るべきです）。

持もってこなければなりません。

The exam board requires the admission slip; this is a rule being reported, not an opinion you are advancing. 持もってくるべきです would suggest you personally think it would be a good idea, which understates a hard requirement.

～べき · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I should have asked her before I decided."

決きめる前まえに、彼女かのじょに**聞きくべきでした**。

～べきだった／べきでした is the standard way to say "I should have", with the fault accepted. 聞きけばよかった is also possible but softer - closer to "I wish I'd asked" than to "that was my mistake".

～べき · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この問題もんだいについては、全員ぜんいんで話はなし合あうべきだことだと思おもいます。

✅ この問題もんだいについては、全員ぜんいんで話はなし合あうべきことだと思おもいます。

べき modifies a noun directly, with no だ in between. The だ you already have at the end of ことだ is doing the sentence-final work.

～べき · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この映画えいがは見みたほうがいいよ。 (b) この映画えいがは見みるべきだよ。

(a) is a friendly recommendation aimed at you: you would enjoy it, so go and see it. Nothing is at stake if you don't.

(b) appeals to a standard outside both of you - the film matters, and anyone serious about films is failing in some small duty by missing it. Between close friends this comes across as enthusiastic overstatement, and that is often exactly the joke; said flatly to someone you do not know well, it sounds like being told off.

～べきではない

Meaning · ought not to; it is wrong to do this - a verdict, not a rule posted on a wall

The negative of べき keeps everything that made べき heavy. It appeals to a principle and finds the action falls short of it. Nobody is forbidding you - べきではない has no authority behind it, only judgement - which is why it feels at once softer than a prohibition and harder to argue with.

～べきではない · Formation

The negative goes on べき, never on the verb inside it.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb dictionary form + べきではない 言いう → 言いうべきではない
Verb (する) するべきではない / すべきではない 優先ゆうせんする → 優先ゆうせんすべきではない
Casual ～べきじゃない 言いうべきじゃない
Polite ～べきではありません 言いうべきではありません
Regret about the past ～べきではなかった 言いうべきではなかった
Polite past ～べきではありませんでした 言いうべきではありませんでした
Noun / な-adjective (written) ～である + べきではない あいまい → あいまいであるべきではない

～べきではない · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ❌言いわないべきだ does not exist. Japanese negates the judgement, not the action: 言いうべきではない. The same is true of the past - 言いうべきではなかった, never ❌言いわないべきだった.

～べきではない · Examples

人ひとの日記にっきを**読よむべきではない**。
You shouldn't read other people's diaries.

そんなことを**言いうべきじゃない**よ。
You shouldn't say things like that.

運転うんてんするなら、お酒さけを**飲のむべきではありません**。
If you're driving, you shouldn't be drinking.

子こどもの前まえで夫婦ふうふげんかをするべきではなかった。
We shouldn't have had that argument in front of the children.

～べきではない · Examples (cont.)

一人ひとりの意見いけんだけで全部ぜんぶを**決きめるべきではない**と思おもいます。
I don't think everything should be decided on one person's opinion alone.

あんな高たかい時計とけいを、給料きゅうりょうが入はいる前まえに**買かうべきではありませんでした**。
I shouldn't have bought a watch that expensive before payday.

会社かいしゃは社員しゃいんの健康けんこうより利益りえきを**優先ゆうせんすべきではない**。
A company should not put profit ahead of its employees' health.

知しらないことを、知しっているふりをして**話はなすべきではありません**。
You shouldn't talk about things you don't know as if you did.

～べきではない · Examples (cont.)

うわさだけで人ひとを**判断はんだんすべきではない*というのが、祖父そふの口くちぐせでした。
"Never judge someone on rumour alone" was my grandfather's favourite saying.*

～べきではない · Nuance & register

The force of べきではない is moral, not institutional. It is what an editorial says about a government, what a parent says about lying, what you say to yourself at three in the morning. There is no penalty attached and no authority cited; the sentence simply asserts that the action is wrong, and invites you to agree.

Because of that, aimed straight at a listener it preaches. Between close friends 言いうべきじゃないよ is normal and even warm - the casual contraction takes most of the weight off - but べきではありません to someone you barely know reads as a dressing-down. Softening works the same way as with the positive: put と思おもいます on it, or move to ないほうがいい.

～べきではない · Nuance & register (cont.)

The past form is the everyday one. ～べきではなかった is how you own a mistake, and it carries no preachiness at all when the subject is yourself: 言いうべきではなかった, 信しんじるべきではなかった.

Note - one trap that catches almost everybody. ～べきではないでしょうか and ～べきではないか are rhetorical questions, and they argue for the action, not against it. もっと話はなし合あうべきではないでしょうか means "shouldn't we talk about it more?" - that is, we should. If you want to argue against something politely, you need ～べきではないと思おもいます.

～べきではない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is otherwise straightforward: すべきではない for writing and formal speech, するべきではない and べきじゃない for conversation. The word is at home in essays, opinion columns and meetings, and slightly stiff in small talk.

～べきではない · ～べきではない vs ～てはいけない

Rule of thumb - てはいけない is a prohibition: someone with authority says no, and there are consequences. べきではない is an opinion about right and wrong, with nobody enforcing it.

館内かんないでは写真しゃしんを撮とってはいけません。
Photography is not permitted inside the building.

相手あいての許可きょかなく写真しゃしんを撮とるべきではありません。
You shouldn't photograph someone without their permission.

The first is the museum's rule; you would find it on a sign, and breaking it gets you stopped. The second is an argument about how people should behave, and it holds in places where no such sign exists. Put べきではない on a notice and it reads oddly - as if the building were expressing a hope rather than setting a rule.

～べきではない · ～べきではない vs ～てはいけない (cont.)

てはいけない also works for immediate, physical danger, where べきではない is far too abstract: 熱あついから触さわってはいけません.

～べきではない · ～べきではない vs ～ないほうがいい

Rule of thumb - ないほうがいい weighs outcomes for this person. べきではない weighs the action against a principle and does not care whether it suits you.

夜よる遅おそくにコーヒーを飲のまないほうがいいですよ。
You're better off not drinking coffee late at night.

人ひとの失敗しっぱいを笑わらうべきではありません。
You should not laugh at other people's mistakes.

～べきではない · ～べきではない vs ～ないほうがいい (cont.)

Swap them and both go wrong in an instructive way. コーヒーを飲のむべきではない turns caffeine into a moral failing. 笑わらわないほうがいい turns cruelty into a tactical error - as though the problem were that you might get caught. For advice to a friend, ないほうがいい is nearly always the right register; save べきではない for when you actually mean to make a judgement.

～べきではない · ～べきではない vs ～べきではないでしょうか

Rule of thumb - adding でしょうか flips the meaning. The statement opposes the action; the question recommends it.

この案あんは今いま決きめるべきではない。
We shouldn't decide this proposal now.

この案あんは今いま決きめるべきではないでしょうか。
Shouldn't we decide this proposal now?

～べきではない · ～べきではない vs ～べきではないでしょうか (cont.)

The second sentence is arguing for a decision today. Japanese does this with negative questions generally - いいんじゃない? is approval, not doubt - but with べき the reversal catches learners because the words look so much like a refusal. In a meeting, hearing 見直みなおすべきではないでしょうか means someone wants a review, and nodding along while believing the opposite is an expensive mistake.

～べきではない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ そのことは言いわないべきだ。 → ✅ そのことは言いうべきではない。
    The negation attaches to べき, not to the verb. There is no ないべき form.

  • ❌（on a notice）ここでたばこを吸すうべきではありません。 → ✅ ここでたばこを吸すってはいけません。
    A posted rule is a prohibition. べきではない on a sign sounds like the building is sharing its personal views.

  • ❌ 明日あしたは雨あめが降ふるべきではない。 → ✅ 明日あしたは雨あめが降ふらないでほしい。
    べき judges a chosen action. Weather chooses nothing, so there is nothing to judge - what you mean is a wish.

～べきではない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌「この計画けいかくはやめるべきではないでしょうか」（intending "we shouldn't cancel this plan"） → ✅ この計画けいかくはやめるべきではないと思おもいます。
    The でしょうか version argues for cancelling. To argue against something politely, keep the statement and soften it with と思おもいます.

  • ❌ 課長かちょう、そんな言いい方かたをするべきではありません。 → ✅ 課長かちょう、少すこし言いい方かたを変かえていただけないでしょうか。
    Aimed upwards, べきではない is a reprimand. Reframe it as a request rather than a verdict.

～べきではない · Practice 1

1. 人ひとから聞きいた秘密ひみつを、勝手かってに他人たにんに話はな____。

The sentence is a judgement about how one ought to behave, so it wants べきではない rather than ないほうがいい, which would only say that letting the secret out tends to backfire. 話はなすべきではない and 話はなすべきではありません differ only in politeness.

～べきではない · Practice 2

A library has rules and enforces them, so this is a prohibition. 話はなすべきではありません would recast a house rule as a moral position, which is both weaker and stranger - it would suit an essay about manners, not a librarian.

～べきではなかった／べきではありませんでした is the standard "I shouldn't have", with fault accepted. Note that the verb stays positive: ❌貸かさないべきでした is not available.

～べきではない · Practice 4

Two things: べき takes the positive dictionary form, and the negation goes on べき itself. 相談そうだんすべきではなかった is the same sentence in a more formal key.

～べきではない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この問題もんだいは来月らいげつまで待まつべきではない。 (b) この問題もんだいは来月らいげつまで待まつべきではないでしょうか。

(a) opposes waiting: the speaker wants the matter dealt with now, and is stating that delay would be wrong.

(b) argues the opposite. It is a rhetorical negative question - "shouldn't we wait until next month?" - and it is a polite way of proposing exactly the delay that (a) rejects. The only difference on the page is でしょうか, which is why this pair is worth memorising as a pair.

～こと - written rules and instructions

Meaning · you are to do this - the sentence-ending こと of notices, house rules and exam papers

A verb followed by a bare こと and a full stop is not a noun phrase that has lost its verb. It is a complete sentence, and it issues an instruction. Nobody in particular is being addressed: the rule simply exists, and anyone it applies to is expected to follow it.

～こと - written rules and instructions · Formation

Nothing follows the こと. No だ, no です, no 。ます. The sentence stops.

～こと - written rules and instructions · Nuance & register

This is a written form, and specifically the written form of an institution talking to whoever is covered by its rules. You meet it on classroom walls, in staff handbooks, on the front page of exam papers, in club regulations and in the list of conditions on an application form. It is often set out as a bulleted list, one こと per line.

It has no politeness level, because it has no addressee. That is worth pausing on: てください is polite because there is a person you are being polite to, whereas こと speaks past everyone to the rule itself. The effect is impersonal and slightly cold, which in a notice is exactly right.

～こと - written rules and instructions · Nuance & register (cont.)

Said to an actual human being, it is another matter. A manager who writes 明日あしたまでに出だすこと in an email to one named person sounds curt and rather military. It is not ungrammatical - some workplaces use it deliberately for terse internal memos - but a learner should assume it will be heard as brusque, and write 出だしてください instead.

Spoken, it survives in one narrow place: a teacher or leader reading a list of rules aloud, essentially quoting the notice. Outside that, it is not conversational Japanese.

Note - the same word ends several other patterns that look identical on the page. ～ことだ gives advice, ～ことがある reports occasional events, ～ことにする announces a decision. The instruction こと is the one with nothing after it.

～こと - written rules and instructions · ～こと vs ～てください

Rule of thumb - てください asks a person; こと states a rule. If you could name who you are talking to, use てください.

すみません、この紙かみにお名前なまえを書かいてください。
Excuse me - please write your name on this form.

受付うけつけで用紙ようしを受うけ取とり、名前なまえを書かくこと。
Collect a form at reception and write your name on it.

～こと - written rules and instructions · ～こと vs ～てください (cont.)

The first is a receptionist speaking to the person in front of her. The second is what the form itself says. Note also that てください can be softened (書かいていただけますか) and こと cannot - there is no polite version, because there is nobody to be polite to.

～こと vs ～ようにしてください

Rule of thumb - こと is a requirement. ～ようにしてください asks people to make an effort in that direction, and leaves room for exceptions.

会議かいぎの十分じゅっぷん前まえまでに集あつまること。
Assemble by ten minutes before the meeting.

なるべく早はやめに集あつまるようにしてください。
Please try to gather a little early if you can.

～こと vs ～ようにしてください (cont.)

The first admits no exceptions and reads as policy; the second is a request from one person to a group and expects some slippage. A written instruction in a customer-facing context - a hotel, a hospital - will usually choose ようにしてください or the still politer お願ねがいいたします, because こと addresses people as rule subjects rather than as guests.

～こと - written rules and instructions · ～こと vs ～なければならない

Rule of thumb - こと issues the requirement. なければならない reports that a requirement exists.

参加者さんかしゃは保険ほけんに入はいること。
Participants are to take out insurance.

参加者さんかしゃは保険ほけんに入はいらなければなりません。
Participants have to take out insurance.

Both appear in regulations, and they are close enough that many documents use them interchangeably. The difference in feel: the first is the rule speaking in its own voice, and belongs in the list of conditions itself; the second is someone explaining the rule, and belongs in the covering letter or in speech. Only the second can be said aloud comfortably to an applicant on the phone.

～こと - written rules and instructions · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 教室きょうしつを出でるときは電気でんきを消けすことです。 → ✅ 電気でんきを消けすこと。
    Adding です turns a notice into personal advice. On a wall, the bare form is what you want.

  • ❌ 辞書じしょを使つかわなくこと → ✅ 辞書じしょを使つかわないこと
    The negative is the plain ない-form. なく belongs to なくて and なくても, not here.

  • ❌（email to one colleague）山田やまださん、水曜日すいようびまでに返事へんじをすること。 → ✅ 山田やまださん、水曜日すいようびまでにお返事へんじをお願ねがいします。
    Aimed at a named person, こと reads as an order barked down a corridor. Use a request form.

～こと - written rules and instructions · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部屋へやはきれいなこと。 → ✅ 部屋へやはきれいにしておくこと。
    Only verbs take the instruction こと. Turn the adjective into an action first.

～こと - written rules and instructions · Practice 5

～ことだ

Meaning · the thing to do is… - advice offered as the obvious answer to a problem

～ことだ hands over a remedy. It does not weigh options or hedge; it names the one action that solves the problem, in the tone of somebody who has seen this situation before. That confidence is the whole flavour of the pattern, and it is also the reason it has to be used carefully.

～ことだ · Formation

Only verbs. The sentence almost always follows a clause that sets up the problem - a たら, a なら, a ければ.

Verb dictionary form + ことだ 休やすむ → 休やすむことだ
Verb (negative) ない-form + ことだ 無理むりをする → 無理むりをしないことだ

Rule of thumb - the だ/です is what makes this advice. Strip it and you get the notice pattern 休やすむこと。, which is a rule on a wall rather than a suggestion to a friend.

～ことだ · Examples

風邪かぜのときは、よく寝ねることです。

If you're in a hurry, take a taxi.

If you want to get good, keep at it a little every day.

心配しんぱいなら、先さきに電話でんわして確たしかめることです。
If you're worried, ring ahead and check.

If you want to lose weight, don't eat late at night.

If kanji won't stick, write them out by hand over and over.

If you get lost in the mountains, don't wander about - stay where you are.

面接めんせつが不安ふあんなら、前まえの日ひに一度いちど声こえに出だして練習れんしゅうしてみることです。
If you're nervous about the interview, run through it out loud the day before.

～ことだ · Examples (cont.)

嫌いやなことがあった日ひは、無理むりに考かんがえないで早はやく寝ねてしまうことだ。
On a day when something horrible has happened, don't force yourself to think it through - just go to bed early.

～ことだ · Nuance & register

The speaker of a ことだ sentence has taken a position: I know the answer to this. That is fine coming from a coach, a teacher, a doctor, a parent, or a friend who has been through the same thing, and it is the standard voice of advice columns and self-help writing. It is much less comfortable between strangers, and it is not appropriate aimed upwards - telling your section chief 休やすむことです puts you in the role of the person who knows better.

ことです is polite in form, but politeness of form does not remove the stance. If you want to advise a senior colleague, leave this pattern alone and use お休やすみになってはいかがでしょうか or ほうがよろしいかと思おもいます.

～ことだ · Nuance & register (cont.)

The advice is general, not a request about one occasion. It answers "what should I do about X?" rather than "will you do X for me?" So 窓まどを開あけることだ is not a way to ask someone to open a window; it is what you say when the room's stuffiness is a recurring problem.

You cannot use it about yourself. ❌私わたしは早はやく寝ねることです is not a plan; for that you want つもりだ or ようにしている.

～ことだ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - a different ことだ turns up in exclamations: なんと静しずかなことだろう, 困こまったことだ. That one takes adjectives and な-adjectives, sits at the end of a written or old-fashioned sentence, and expresses feeling rather than advice. Context separates them easily - advice always has an action verb in front.

～ことだ · ～ことだ vs ～ほうがいい

Rule of thumb - ほうがいい compares: this option beats that one. ことだ prescribes: this is what solves it, and no comparison is offered.

薬くすりを飲のんだほうがいいですよ。
You'd be better off taking something for it.

熱ねつが下さがらないなら、病院びょういんへ行いくことです。
If the fever isn't coming down, go to a doctor.

ほうがいい leaves the decision with the listener and is the gentler everyday choice - it is what you say to a friend at their desk. ことだ closes the question. That is why it fits the second sentence, where the situation has already gone past the point of weighing things up.

～ことだ · ～ことだ vs ～べきだ

Rule of thumb - ことだ is practical: here is the route to the goal you said you wanted. べきだ is moral: here is what is right, whether you want it or not.

試験しけんに受うかりたいなら、過去かこ問もんを解とくことだ。
If you want to pass the exam, work through past papers.

約束やくそくした以上いじょう、最後さいごまでやるべきだ。
Having promised, you ought to see it through.

The first is conditional on your goal: no goal, no advice, and you are free to drop the exam. The second holds regardless of what you want - that is what makes it a judgement. In practice ことだ follows ～たいなら and べきだ follows ～のだから.

～ことだ · ～ことだ vs ～ものだ

Rule of thumb - ことだ advises this person about this problem. ものだ states a general truth or social norm that applies to everyone.

先輩せんぱいに会あったら、自分じぶんから挨拶あいさつするものだ。
When you meet someone senior, you greet them first - that's how it's done.

職場しょくばになじみたいなら、自分じぶんから挨拶あいさつすることだ。
If you want to settle in at work, be the one who says hello first.

The same action, two different arguments. ものだ appeals to custom and would be just as true if you did not care about fitting in; ことだ is a piece of tactical advice tied to the goal in the first clause. ものだ therefore sounds old-fashioned and moralising in a way ことだ does not - and can be dropped into a scolding, which ことだ cannot.

～ことだ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私わたしは明日あした早はやく起おきることです。 → ✅ 私わたしは明日あした早はやく起おきるつもりです。
    ことだ is advice you give someone else. It cannot announce your own plan.

  • ❌ すみません、この書類しょるいをコピーすることです。 → ✅ すみません、この書類しょるいをコピーしていただけますか。
    A request about one occasion is not advice. ことだ prescribes a general solution, not a favour.

  • ❌ 元気げんきなことです。 → ✅ 元気げんきを出だすことです。
    Advice ことだ takes a verb. An adjective has to become an action first.

～ことだ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 課長かちょう、たまには休やすむことですよ。 → ✅ 課長かちょう、たまにはお休やすみになってはいかがでしょうか。
    Polite endings do not change the stance. Advising upwards needs a different pattern altogether.

  • ❌ 遅おくれる場合ばあいは連絡れんらくすることです。（as a posted rule） → ✅ 遅おくれる場合ばあいは連絡れんらくすること。
    A notice drops the です. With it, the wall appears to be offering friendly guidance.

～ことだ · Practice 4

～ことだ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 困こまったときは、すぐに相談そうだんすることだ。 (b) 困こまったときは、すぐに相談そうだんすること。

～ことはない

Meaning · there's no need to; it never happens - two readings of one shape, sorted out by who the subject is

With you as the subject, ～ことはない is reassurance: the effort or the worry is unnecessary, so stop. With somebody or something else as the subject, it is a statement about the world: that does not happen. The words are identical; the subject decides.

There's really no need for you to come all the way to the station to meet me.

あの人ひとが約束やくそくの時間じかんに遅おくれることはない。
He is never late for an appointment.

この辺へんは冬ふゆでも雪ゆきが積つもることはありません。
Round here the snow never settles, even in winter.

部長ぶちょうは厳きびしい人ひとですが、理由りゆうもなく怒おこることはありません。
Our manager is strict, but he never gets angry without a reason.

～ことはない · Examples (cont.)

失敗しっぱいしても誰だれかに笑わらわれることはないので、安心あんしんして挑戦ちょうせんしてみてください。
Nobody is going to laugh at you if it goes wrong, so give it a try without worrying.

～ことはない · Nuance & register

The "no need to" sense is consolation, and it is one of the kindest patterns in the language. Its natural home is comforting someone who is upset, anxious, or apologising too much: 泣なくことないよ, 謝あやまることはありません. In speech the は regularly drops - 気きにすることないよ - and that shortened version is what you will actually hear between friends.

The same words can turn cool. Said flatly about someone else's plan, わざわざ行いくことはない means "there's no point going", and the warmth disappears. Tone and context carry that difference entirely; the grammar does not mark it.

～ことはない · Nuance & register (cont.)

The "never happens" sense is a statement of reliability. It is stronger than あまり～ない and softer than an absolute: it claims a pattern, not a law of physics. It reads well in descriptions, product blurbs and character sketches, and it takes ほとんど comfortably - 故障こしょうすることはほとんどありません.

Two register cautions. Turning down help from someone senior with ことはありません is grammatical but a little blunt; the set humble phrase is ～には及およびません. And ことはない never means "must not" - reading 遅おくれることはない as a warning about lateness reverses the sentence.

～ことはない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - the negative row in the table is a separate, very common idiom. ～ないことはない is a double negative meaning "it's not that I can't": 辛からい物ものが食たべられないことはないんですが、あまり 得意とくいではありません. It is a hedge, and it usually signals a polite reluctance rather than genuine ability.

～ことはない · ～ことはない vs ～なくてもいい

Rule of thumb - なくてもいい permits you to skip it. ことはない says the action would be pointless or unwarranted, and normally comes with a reason.

明日あしたは来こなくてもいいですよ。
You don't have to come in tomorrow.

熱ねつがあるのに来くることはありませんよ。
There's no call for you to come in with a fever.

The first grants permission - the speaker has the authority to excuse you, and is doing so. The second argues that coming would be unreasonable, and it carries concern for the listener. That is why ことはない fits consolation and なくてもいい fits a manager approving a day off. Note also that なくてもいい answers a question; ことはない heads off an intention.

～ことはない · ～ことはない vs ～ことがない

Rule of thumb - ことがない with the た-form is experience: you have never done it. ことはない with the dictionary form is about need or frequency.

北海道ほっかいどうへ行いったことがありません。
I've never been to Hokkaido.

北海道ほっかいどうでは、夏なつに冷房れいぼうを使つかうことはありません。
In Hokkaido nobody uses air conditioning in summer.

～ことはない · ～ことはない vs ～ことがない (cont.)

The first looks back over your life; the second describes a standing state of affairs. The は/が swap is not decorative - が presents the experience as a fact you possess, は sets the situation against expectation ("as for that happening: it doesn't"). If you meet 行いったことはない with は and the た-form, it is still experience, now with a contrastive edge: "been there, no - heard of it, yes."

～ことはない · ～ことはない vs ～はずがない

Rule of thumb - ことはない reports that something does not happen, from observation. はずがない says it could not possibly be so, from reasoning.

彼女かのじょが約束やくそくを忘わすれることはない。
She never forgets an arrangement.

彼女かのじょが約束やくそくを忘わすれるはずがない。
There's no way she's forgotten the arrangement.

The first is a general character reference and could be said about her at any time. The second is about this occasion and is an inference - you are ruling out a possibility that has just been raised. So はずがない answers "do you think she forgot?", while ことはない answers "what's she like?"

～ことはない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 会議かいぎに遅おくれることはない。（intending "you mustn't be late"） → ✅ 会議かいぎに遅おくれてはいけない。
    ことはない never prohibits. As written, the sentence praises someone's punctuality.

  • ❌ 泣ないたことはないよ、大丈夫だいじょうぶ。 → ✅ 泣なくことはないよ、大丈夫だいじょうぶ。
    Consolation takes the dictionary form. With the た-form the sentence flips to experience - "I have never cried" - which is not what a crying friend needs to hear.

  • ❌ この問題もんだいは難むずかしいことはない。 → ✅ この問題もんだいは難むずかしくはない。
    The pattern takes verbs. An い-adjective negates on its own stem.

～ことはない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌（to a senior colleague）部長ぶちょう、わざわざお越こしいただくことはありません。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、お越こしいただくには**及およびません**。
    Declining a superior's offer with ことはありません sounds like you are telling them not to bother. ～には及およびません is the set humble phrase for exactly this.

～ことはない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれは人ひとの悪口わるぐちを言いうことはない。 (b) 彼かれは人ひとの悪口わるぐちを言いったことがない。

～しかない

Meaning · there's nothing for it but to… - the circumstances have closed every other door

しか means "nothing other than", and it always drags a negative along with it. Put it after a verb and the sentence says: of all the things one might do here, exactly one remains. Nobody ordered it and nobody judged it - the situation simply ran out of options.

～しかない · Formation

The verb stays in the dictionary form. しか here does the same work it does after a noun, which you already know from 千円せんえんしかない.

Note - there is no positive form. ❌待まつしかある does not exist; しか is grammatically bound to ない. The negative is doing the work of "other than", not of "not".

～しかない · Examples

バスがないから、歩あるくしかない。
There's no bus, so we'll just have to walk.

こうなったら、やるしかありません。
Now that it's come to this, we just have to do it.

終電しゅうでんを逃のがしたので、タクシーで帰かえるしかなかった。
I missed the last train, so there was nothing for it but to take a taxi home.

雨あめがやむまで、ここで待まつしかないですね。
Looks like we've no choice but to wait here until the rain stops.

～しかない · Examples (cont.)

部品ぶひんがもう作つくられていないので、新あたらしいのを買かうしかありません。
The part isn't made any more, so there's nothing for it but to buy a new one.

誰だれも手伝てつだってくれないなら、一人ひとりで全部ぜんぶやるしかない。
If nobody's going to help, I'll just have to do the whole thing on my own.

説明書せつめいしょを何度なんど読よんでも分わからないので、メーカーに電話でんわして聞きくしかなさそうです。
I've read the manual over and over and still don't understand it, so I'll probably just have to ring the maker.

店みせの売上うりあげがここまで落おちてしまった以上いじょう、営業えいぎょう時間じかんを短みじかくするしかないでしょう。
With takings down this far, we're going to have to cut the opening hours.

～しかない · Examples (cont.)

試合しあいに勝かつには、残のこり五分ごふんで二点にてん取とるしかない。
To win the match, we have to score twice in the last five minutes - there's no other way.

～しかない · Nuance & register

The feeling is resignation, but not always gloom. The same sentence can be a sigh or a squaring of the shoulders, depending on delivery: やるしかない is one of the most common things a Japanese speaker says before starting something difficult, and it is closer to "right, let's get on with it" than to despair. What is constant is that the alternatives have been considered and found gone.

That is the pattern's boundary. しかない reports what circumstances have left you. It does not report a rule, an order, or a moral duty. A deadline set by your boss is 出ださなければならない; a duty you feel is 行いかないわけにはいかない; a situation in which the only remaining option is to hand it in unfinished is 出だすしかない.

～しかない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral, and the polite form is simply しかありません. Two siblings sit above it in formality: ～ほかない and ～よりほかない are written, slightly literary equivalents, and ～ざるを得えない is formal and adds a sense of being compelled against your will - an inspection you must accept, a resignation you must tender. In conversation, しかない covers almost everything those two do.

One small caution. Applied to somebody else's situation, しかない can sound dismissive - あきらめるしかないですよ tells a person their problem is over. Softening it to しかないかもしれませんね leaves them their options.

～しかない · ～しかない vs ～なければならない

Rule of thumb - なければならない comes from outside: a rule, an order, a deadline. しかない comes from the situation: the other options have disappeared.

外国人がいこくじん登録とうろくの書類しょるいは本人ほんにんが出ださなければなりません。
The registration paperwork has to be submitted by the person themselves.

郵便ゆうびんが間まに合あわないので、自分じぶんで持もっていくしかありません。
The post won't get there in time, so I'll have to take it in myself.

The first would still be true if the post were quick, the office were near and you had all week; a regulation does not care. The second exists only because of a timing problem, and it would evaporate if the problem did. Ask yourself: who says so? If the answer is a person or a rulebook, use なければならない.

～しかない · ～しかない vs ～ざるを得ない

Rule of thumb - both mean "have no choice", but しかない is about options running out, while ～ざるを得えない is about being compelled against your own wishes, usually by logic, duty or authority. It is also distinctly formal.

終おわらないので、明日あした続つづきをやるしかない。
It isn't going to be finished, so I'll just carry on tomorrow.

これだけの証拠しょうこがそろえば、事実じじつだと**認みとめざるを得えない**。
With this much evidence, one is forced to accept it as fact.

～しかない · ～しかない vs ～ざるを得ない (cont.)

ざるを得えない belongs to reports, editorials and formal speech, and it carries a note of reluctance that しかない does not require. Saying it about tomorrow's leftover work would sound as though the work had defeated you in some tragic way. Conversely, しかない in the evidence sentence would make the conclusion sound like a practical inconvenience rather than an intellectual surrender.

～しかない · ～しかない vs ～だけだ

Rule of thumb - だけだ says that is all that's left to do, neutrally, and often happily. しかない says there is nothing else you could do, with the other options mourned.

準備じゅんびは終おわった。あとは本番ほんばんを待まつだけだ。
The preparation's done. All that's left is to wait for the day.

準備じゅんびが間まに合あわなかった。このまま本番ほんばんに出でるしかない。
We didn't get everything ready. We'll just have to go on as we are.

～しかない · ～しかない vs ～だけだ (cont.)

だけだ looks forward from a job well done; しかない looks back at options that closed. The two are not interchangeable even where both are grammatical - telling a nervous performer 楽たのしむだけだよ is encouragement, while 楽たのしむしかないよ implies the performance itself is a write-off.

～しかない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ バスがないから歩あるかないしかない。 → ✅ バスがないから歩あるくしかない。
    しかない attaches to the dictionary form. The negative is already inside ない at the end; putting another one on the verb reverses the sentence.

  • ❌ さいふには百円ひゃくえんしかあります。 → ✅ さいふには百円ひゃくえんしかありません。
    しか can never end in a positive. It is one half of a pair, and ない is the other half.

  • ❌ パスポートの申請しんせいは本人ほんにんが行いくしかありません。（reporting the rule） → ✅ パスポートの申請しんせいは本人ほんにんが行いかなければなりません。
    A published requirement is an obligation from outside, not a situation that ran out of options.

～しかない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ このお菓子かしはここにしか売うっています。 → ✅ このお菓子かしはここでしか売うっていません。
    Two things: the sentence needs its negative, and しか follows the particle it attaches to - ここで, since the shop is the location of selling.

～わけにはいかない

Meaning · I can't very well do that - it is perfectly possible, and I still can't

This is not a pattern about ability and not a pattern about rules. Everything わけにはいかない describes is physically available to you: you could take the day off, you could tell the secret, you could go home first. What stops you is a promise, a position, a debt of gratitude, or your own sense of what is decent. It is obligation as you feel it, rather than obligation as somebody imposes it.

～わけにはいかない · Formation

My juniors are still here working - I can't just leave ahead of them.

I'm looking after some important documents, so I can't leave this room unattended.

They went to the trouble of inviting me, so I can't really not go.

I do have a fever, but today's meeting is the one I can't get out of.

～わけにはいかない · Nuance & register

Ask, of any sentence you are tempted to build with this: could I physically do it? If the answer is no, the pattern is wrong. A person who cannot swim does not say 泳およぐわけにはいかない; they say 泳およげません. The pattern's whole content is the gap between could and can't bring myself to.

For the same reason the subject is nearly always the speaker. You are reporting the inside of your own head - the obligation you feel, the awkwardness you would not survive. Said about somebody else it presumes to know their conscience, which is why 君きみは帰かえるわけにはいかない sounds less like an observation than like pressure. Describing a third person's position is possible when you have grounds: 社長しゃちょうとしては認みとめるわけにはいかないだろう - note the だろう doing the hedging.

～わけにはいかない · Nuance & register (cont.)

The も variant is worth having. ～わけにもいかない adds this impossibility to one already mentioned, and it is how the pattern usually appears when you are boxed in from both sides: 断ことわるわけにもいかず、引ひき受うけてしまった.

Register is neutral in itself. The polite わけにはいきません is completely ordinary at work; わけにはまいりません is humble and belongs to formal apologies and customer-facing speech. In casual conversation the は often drops - 寝ねるわけにいかないんだよ.

～わけにはいかない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - the double negative ～ないわけにはいかない means "I have to", and the reluctance is part of the meaning. It is the sentence of someone who has looked for a way out and not found one. Do not use it for things you are glad to do: ❌好すきな人ひとに会あわないわけにはいかない is a strange thing to say about a date.

～わけにはいかない · ～わけにはいかない vs ～てはいけない

Rule of thumb - てはいけない is a prohibition from outside: a rule, a parent, a sign. わけにはいかない is you stopping yourself, for reasons you would have to explain.

館内かんないでは飲食いんしょくしてはいけません。
Eating and drinking are not allowed inside.

会議かいぎの途中とちゅうなので、席せきを立たつわけにはいきません。
We're in the middle of a meeting, so I can hardly get up and leave.

Nothing forbids leaving a meeting; you simply cannot do it and remain the person you want to be at that table. Put てはいけない there and you invent a rule that does not exist. Conversely, わけにはいかない on a museum notice would be peculiar - the building has no feelings to protect.

～わけにはいかない · ～わけにはいかない vs ～てはいけない (cont.)

A quick test: if you can name who forbids it, use てはいけない.

～わけにはいかない · ～ないわけにはいかない vs ～なければならない

Rule of thumb - なければならない is neutral necessity; it says nothing about how you feel. ～ないわけにはいかない says you would rather not, and cannot see a way out.

申込書もうしこみしょには印鑑いんかんを押おさなければなりません。
You have to put your seal on the application form.

部長ぶちょうに直接ちょくせつ頼たのまれたので、引ひき受うけないわけにはいかなかった。
The manager asked me personally, so I couldn't very well refuse.

The first is a form's requirement and carries no emotion at all - it would be identical on a website's help page. The second is a small story about pressure: you did not want the job, you looked for an exit, and the way it was asked closed it. Swap them and the form sentence becomes oddly resentful, while the second becomes a flat report that misses the whole point.

～わけにはいかない · ～ないわけにはいかない vs ～なければならない (cont.)

This is also why ないわけにはいかない almost always has a reason clause in front of it. Without one, the reluctance has nothing to attach to.

～わけにはいかない · ～わけにはいかない vs ～ことができない

Rule of thumb - ことができない is genuine inability: the skill, the permission or the physics is missing. わけにはいかない is unwillingness on principle, with the ability intact.

漢字かんじが読よめないので、この書類しょるいに署名しょめいすることができません。
I can't read the kanji, so I'm unable to sign this document.

中身なかみを確認かくにんしていないので、この書類しょるいに署名しょめいするわけにはいきません。
I haven't checked what's in it, so I'm not prepared to sign this document.

The first speaker cannot sign. The second could sign in a second, and is refusing on principle. That distinction is often the difference between an apology and a stand, and choosing the wrong one misrepresents you badly - the first sentence sounds helpless where you meant to sound careful.

～わけにはいかない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 今日きょうは帰かえるわけにはいけません。 → ✅ 今日きょうは帰かえるわけにはいきません。
    The verb is いく, negated as いかない／いきません. There is no potential form in this pattern.

  • ❌ 泳およげないので、向むこう岸ぎしまで行いくわけにはいかない。 → ✅ 泳およげないので、向むこう岸ぎしまで行いくことができない。
    Real inability is ことができない. わけにはいかない requires that you could do the thing.

  • ❌（on a sign）ここに車くるまを止とめるわけにはいきません。 → ✅ ここに車くるまを止とめてはいけません。
    A notice issues a prohibition. わけにはいかない reports a private conflict, and car parks do not have those.

～わけにはいかない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 君きみは約束やくそくを破やぶるわけにはいかないよ。 → ✅ 君きみは約束やくそくを破やぶってはいけないよ。
    Aimed at someone else, the pattern claims to know what their conscience will allow. If you mean to tell them not to, say so directly.

  • ❌ 大好だいすきなケーキだから、食たべないわけにはいかない。 → ✅ 大好だいすきなケーキだから、食たべずにはいられない。
    ないわけにはいかない carries reluctance. For something you cannot resist, you want the "can't help it" pattern instead.

～わけにはいかない · Practice 1

1. 明日あしたは大事だいじな試験しけんだから、今夜こんや遊あそびに行いく____。

今夜こんや遊あそびに行いくわけにはいかない。

Nothing physically prevents you going out; the exam does. That gap between "could" and "can't bring myself to" is exactly what the pattern expresses, and the reason clause in front supplies the pressure.

～わけにはいかない · Practice 2

2. Choose: 足あしをけがしているので、今日きょうの練習れんしゅうに参加さんかする（ことができません・わけにはいきません）。

参加さんかすることができません。

An injury is real inability, so ことができない is the honest form. わけにはいきません would imply your leg is fine and you are declining on principle, which would puzzle the coach.

～わけにはいかない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "They lent me the money without asking any questions, so I can't not pay it back."

何なにも聞きかずにお金かねを貸かしてくれたのだから、返かえさないわけにはいかない。

The double negative is the point: paying it back is not something you chose, it is something the kindness has made unavoidable. 返かえさなければならない would state the obligation flatly and lose the sense of being morally cornered.

～わけにはいかない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 部長ぶちょうに誘さそわれたので、飲のみ会かいに行いかないわけにはいけませんでした。

✅ 部長ぶちょうに誘さそわれたので、飲のみ会かいに行いかないわけにはいきませんでした。

The only fault is いけません for いきません. The rest is a model use of the double negative: an invitation from a superior is precisely the kind of pressure that removes your options without anyone ordering anything.

～わけにはいかない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 明日あしたは早はやく起おきなければなりません。 (b) 明日あしたは早はやく起おきないわけにはいきません。

(a) is a plain statement of necessity. There is an early train or an early meeting, and the speaker reports the fact without any attitude to it. You could say it cheerfully.

(b) adds a person who does not want to. It implies you have already thought about staying in bed, considered whether anyone would mind, and concluded that you cannot get away with it. The obligation in (a) comes from the timetable; in (b) it comes from your own sense of what you owe someone.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません

Meaning · it's fine either way; I don't mind - permission given by disclaiming any objection

かまう means "to mind, to be bothered". So ～てもかまわない does not say the action is good; it says the speaker has no objection to it. That small difference in angle is why the pattern is at home in written rules and business Japanese, and why it can sound a shade cooler than a warm "go ahead".

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Formation

かまわない sits on top of the same ～ても you already know. Everything before it takes the ordinary ても shapes.

Attaches to Form Example

Note - the kanji is 構かまう, but this pattern is written in kana far more often than not. Both かまいません and 構かまいません are correct; kana is the safer default.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Examples

ここに座すわってもかまいませんか。
Do you mind if I sit here?

試験しけんでは辞書じしょを使つかってもかまいません。
You may use a dictionary in the exam.

時間じかんがなければ、全部ぜんぶやらなくてもかまいません。
If you're short of time, you don't have to do all of it.

部屋へやは狭せまくてもかまわないので、駅えきに近ちかいところがいいです。
I don't mind a small flat - I'd rather have somewhere near the station.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Examples (cont.)

お支払しはらいは現金げんきんでもカードでもかまいません。
You're welcome to pay by cash or by card.

書かき方かたが少すこしくらい違ちがってもかまいませんから、まず出だしてみてください。
It doesn't matter if the format isn't quite right - just hand something in first.

明日あしたの集合しゅうごうは八時はちじでも九時くじでもかまわないと部長ぶちょうは言いっていました。
The manager said eight or nine would both be fine for tomorrow's start.

写真しゃしんを撮とってもかまいませんか。後あとでブログに載のせたいのですが。
Would you mind if I took a photo? I'd like to put it on my blog later.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Examples (cont.)

急いそぎではありませんので、お返事へんじは来週らいしゅうになってもかまいません。
There's no rush, so it's perfectly all right if you reply next week.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Nuance & register

てもいい and てもかまわない are close, but they are not saying quite the same thing. てもいい calls the action good - a green light. てもかまわない says the speaker is not bothered - the light was never red in the first place. Out of that comes the pattern's characteristic flatness: it is generous without being warm.

That flatness is an advantage in writing and business, which is where you meet it most: exam instructions, application forms, hotel policies, emails from suppliers. キャンセルは前日ぜんじつまでにご連絡れんらくいただければかまいません sounds like an institution, and is meant to.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Nuance & register (cont.)

It is also, spoken with the wrong intonation, the sentence of someone who is politely annoyed. 別べつにかまいませんけど ("I don't mind, but…") is a recognisable way of not being fine at all. Learners often reach for かまいません when they mean to be reassuring and land somewhere colder than intended; if warmth is the goal, もちろんいいですよ or 大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ does the job better.

As a question, ～てもかまいませんか is politer and more formal than ～てもいいですか, and slightly less formal than ～てもよろしいでしょうか. All three are usable at work; the middle one is the safe everyday choice with someone you do not know well.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Nuance & register (cont.)

One direction to avoid: granting permission to someone senior. Telling your manager 座すわってもかまいませんよ puts you in charge of the chairs. Answering their question with a bare かまいません is normal and fine - it is the unprompted granting that presumes.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · ～てもかまわない vs ～てもいい

Rule of thumb - てもいい says the action is all right. てもかまわない says the speaker doesn't mind. The first is permission; the second is the absence of objection.

そのペン、使つかってもいいよ。
Sure, use that pen.

どのペンを使つかってもかまいません。
It doesn't matter which pen you use.

The first is a friend handing something over. The second is an invigilator stating that pen choice is not regulated. Notice how naturally かまわない takes どの, どちら, いつ and でも - it is the form of choice for saying "any of these is acceptable", because indifference is exactly what it expresses.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · ～てもかまわない vs ～てもいい (cont.)

In casual conversation between friends, てもいい and simply いいよ are far commoner. かまわないよ is not wrong there, but it sounds a little grown-up and measured.

～てもかまいませんか vs ～てもよろしいでしょうか

Rule of thumb - same request, three rungs of politeness: ～てもいいですか ＜ ～てもかまいませんか ＜ ～てもよろしいでしょうか.

窓まどを開あけてもいいですか。
Can I open the window?

少すこし窓まどを開あけてもかまいませんか。
Would you mind if I opened the window a little?

恐おそれ入いりますが、窓まどを開あけてもよろしいでしょうか。
Excuse me - would it be all right if I opened the window?

～てもかまいませんか vs ～てもよろしいでしょうか (cont.)

Treat these as a ladder rather than as synonyms. The first is what you say to a friend or a shop assistant, the second to a colleague or a stranger, the third to a customer or in a formal setting. Going too far up the ladder is a mild error too: よろしいでしょうか to a close friend sounds like a joke.

～てもかまわない vs ～ても大丈夫

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different worlds. ても大丈夫だいじょうぶ is the spoken, everyday version; てもかまわない is the written and formal one.

明日あした、少すこし遅おくれても大丈夫だいじょうぶですか。
Is it OK if I'm a bit late tomorrow?

提出ていしゅつが明日あしたになってもかまいません。
It is acceptable for submission to be tomorrow.

～てもかまわない vs ～ても大丈夫 (cont.)

大丈夫だいじょうぶ has spread enormously in modern spoken Japanese and now covers most of what かまわない used to do in conversation. The reverse swap is what sounds off: an official notice reading 大丈夫だいじょうぶです feels chatty, and in a contract it would be unusable.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日あした行いくでもかまいません。 → ✅ 明日あした行いってもかまいません。
    Verbs come in on the て-form. でもかまわない is for nouns and な-adjectives: 明日あしたでもかまいません.

  • ❌ 値段ねだんが高たかいでもかまいません。 → ✅ 値段ねだんが高たかくてもかまいません。
    An い-adjective becomes ～くても first. The でも route is not open to it.

  • ❌ 全部ぜんぶ読よまなくでもかまいません。 → ✅ 全部ぜんぶ読よまなくてもかまいません。
    The negative is ～なくても. なくで does not exist.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌（to your manager, unprompted）部長ぶちょう、こちらの席せきに座すわってもかまいませんよ。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、こちらの席せきにどうぞ。
    Handing out permission upwards presumes authority you do not have. Offer, don't permit.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Practice 1

1. コピーの紙かみは裏うらが使つかってあるものでも____。

裏うらが使つかってあるものでもかまいません。

The thing being permitted is a noun - paper of a certain kind - so it takes でもかまいません rather than the て-form. The sentence says the speaker has no objection either way, which is precisely this pattern rather than いいです.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Practice 2

2. Choose: （友達ともだちに）このおかし、一ひとつ食たべ（てもいい?・てもかまいませんか?）

一ひとつ食たべてもいい?

To a friend, てもいい? is the natural register. てもかまいませんか is a step up in formality and would sound as though you had suddenly started addressing them as a client - which is occasionally a joke, but not the default.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "You don't have to write your name if you'd rather not."

書かきたくなければ、お名前なまえは書かかなくてもかまいません。

The negative permission is ～なくてもかまいません. On a survey form this is exactly the register you want - it disclaims any requirement without sounding as if the form is doing you a favour.

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 部屋へやが静しずかくてもかまいませんから、安やすいほうがいいです。

✅ 部屋へやが**静しずかでもかまいません**から、安やすいほうがいいです。

静しずか is a な-adjective, so it takes でも, not ～くても; ❌静しずかくて is not a form. (Note the sentence is odd in meaning too - nobody objects to quiet - so 狭せまくてもかまいません would make a better sentence.)

～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 明日あしたでもいいですよ。 (b) 明日あしたでもかまいませんよ。

(a) offers tomorrow as a positively acceptable option. It is warm and helpful, the sort of thing you say to put someone at ease about a deadline.

(b) says you would not be bothered by tomorrow. The content is the same, but the tone is a step cooler and more businesslike - it disclaims an objection rather than extending an offer. In an email to a client that neutrality is an advantage; to a friend who is apologising for being late, (a) is the kinder sentence.

～てごらん

Meaning · go on, try it; have a look - a warm, gentle command, always aimed downward

ごらん comes from the honorific verb for "look", but do not let that mislead you: in ～てごらん the respect has been worn away completely. What is left is an encouraging imperative that an adult uses to a child, a teacher to a student, a senior to a junior. It is kind. It is also unmistakably from above.

～てごらん · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb て-form + ごらん 食たべる → 食たべてごらん
Verb (する) ～してごらん 想像そうぞうする → 想像そうぞうしてごらん
Fuller, more instructional ～てごらんなさい 食たべてごらんなさい
Softened with a particle ～てごらんよ・～てごらんね 食たべてごらんよ
As a gentle invitation ～てごらん? 読よんでごらん?

Rule of thumb - there is no negative. ❌見みないでごらん does not exist. If you want someone not to do something, you are outside this pattern altogether.

～てごらん · Formation (cont.)

Note - the kanji is ご覧らん, and you will see 見みてご覧らん in print. In the ～てごらん pattern kana is much commoner, and it helps keep this use visually separate from the honorific ご覧らんください.

～てごらん · Examples

これ、食たべてごらん。おいしいよ。
Here, try this. It's good.

窓まどの外そとを見みてごらん。
Have a look outside the window.

自分じぶんで考かんがえてごらん。きっと分わかるから。
Have a think about it yourself. You'll get there.

こわがらないで、一度いちど乗のってごらんなさい。
Don't be frightened - get on and try it once.

～てごらん · Examples (cont.)

お母かあさんに聞きいてごらん。知しっているかもしれないよ。
Go and ask your mum. She might know.

その漢字かんじ、もう一度いちど書かいてごらん。今度こんどはゆっくりね。
Write that kanji once more. Slowly this time.

失敗しっぱいしてもいいから、まず自分じぶんでやってごらん。
It doesn't matter if you get it wrong - have a go on your own first.

静しずかにして、目めを閉とじて鳥とりの声こえを聞きいてごらん。何なん種類しゅるいいるか分わかるかな。
Be quiet, close your eyes and listen to the birds. See if you can tell how many kinds there are.

～てごらん · Examples (cont.)

難むずかしそうに見みえますが、説明書せつめいしょのとおりにやってごらんなさい。十分じゅっぷんでできますよ。
It looks difficult, but try following the instructions - it takes ten minutes.

～てごらん · Nuance & register

The one thing to fix in your head is direction. ～てごらん flows downhill: parent to child, teacher to pupil, coach to player, an older friend to a much younger one. Between adults of equal standing it sounds patronising, and pointed at anyone senior it is close to rude - no amount of politeness elsewhere in the sentence rescues it. This surprises learners, because the word inside it is an honorific one; the honorific is directed at the looking, historically, not at the listener.

Within its range, though, it is genuinely affectionate. Compared with the plain imperative 食たべろ or even with 食たべなさい, ～てごらん invites rather than orders. It very often comes with a reason or a reassurance attached - おいしいよ, 大丈夫だいじょうぶだから - and that is part of its character: it is the grammar of coaxing.

～てごらん · Nuance & register (cont.)

～てごらんなさい is the fuller form. It is a little more formal and a little more instructional, which is why it turns up in children's books, teaching materials and the speech of teachers; ～てごらん is the clipped, homelier version you hear at a kitchen table. Neither is polite in the ます sense - adding です or ます to them is not possible.

You will also meet it addressed to nobody in particular, in lyrics, adverts and narration: 空そらを見みてごらん. There it reads as gentle, slightly poetic, and the downward angle becomes warmth rather than authority.

～てごらん · ～てごらん vs ～てみて

Rule of thumb - てみて is neutral and works in any direction. てごらん adds warmth and a difference in standing, and only works downward.

これ、おいしいので食たべてみてください。
Do try this, it's delicious.

これ、おいしいから食たべてごらん。
Go on, try this - it's delicious.

～てごらん · ～てごらん vs ～てみて (cont.)

To a guest, a colleague or a customer, the first is the only safe option. The second is what you would say to your nephew. Note that they are not doing the same grammatical work either: てみる means "do it and find out", so 食たべてみて is literally "eat it and see", while 食たべてごらん is closer to "let's see you eat it".

～てごらん · ～てごらん vs ～てみなさい

Rule of thumb - both are downward. てみなさい is an instruction and can be stern; てごらん is an invitation and is warm.

もう一度いちど、最初さいしょから読よんでみなさい。
Read it again from the beginning.

もう一度いちど、最初さいしょから読よんでごらん。
Have another go at it from the start.

A teacher who has said the same thing three times uses the first; a teacher sitting beside a struggling child uses the second. なさい carries the authority of an order that expects compliance, which is why parents reach for it when patience is running out. ごらん expects willingness rather than obedience.

～てごらん · ～てごらん vs ご覧ください

Rule of thumb - same word, opposite directions. ご覧らんください and ご覧らんになる are honorific: they raise the listener. ～てごらん does not.

資料しりょうの三さんページ目めをご覧らんください。
Please look at page three of the handout.

ここ、見みてごらん。名前なまえが書かいてあるよ。
Look here - your name's written on it.

～てごらん · ～てごらん vs ご覧ください (cont.)

The first is what you say to an audience, a client or your manager; the second is what you say to a seven-year-old. Because they look so similar, learners produce the fatal hybrid ❌見みてごらんください, which staples an honorific ending onto a downward command. It is not a politer てごらん; it is a sentence that cannot be repaired without changing pattern.

～てごらん · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 先生せんせい、この写真しゃしんを見みてごらんください。 → ✅ 先生せんせい、この写真しゃしんをご覧らんください。
    てごらん cannot be made polite by adding ください. To ask a superior to look, use the honorific verb on its own.

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、こちらの席せきに座すわってごらん。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、こちらの席せきにお座すわりください。
    The pattern is downward only. Aimed at a superior it reads as though you were addressing a child.

  • ❌ その箱はこは開あけないでごらん。 → ✅ その箱はこは開あけないでね。
    There is no negative form. Use an ordinary negative request instead.

  • ❌ もう一度いちど言いうごらん。 → ✅ もう一度いちど言いってごらん。
    ごらん attaches to the て-form, like てみる and てください.

～てごらん · Practice 1

1. 自転車じてんしゃ、こわくないから一人ひとりで乗のっ____。

一人ひとりで乗のってごらん。

The reassurance こわくないから in front of it is the giveaway: this is an adult coaxing a child onto a bike, which is the pattern's home ground. The fuller 乗のってごらんなさい would sound more like an instructor than a parent.

～てごらん · Practice 2

2. Choose: （お客様きゃくさまに）こちらの新製品しんせいひんを試ためし（てごらん・てみてください）。

試ためしてみてください。

A customer must never be spoken to downward, and てごらん is downward by definition. てみてください is neutral and polite; in a shop you would often go further still with お試ためしください.

～てごらん · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, said by a parent to a small child: "Look - the moon's come out."

見みてごらん。お月つきさまが出でてきたよ。

てごらん fits the speaker and the listener exactly, and the final よ matches its warm, coaxing tone. 見みなさい would turn the same sentence into an instruction.

～てごらん · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 社長しゃちょう、こちらのグラフを見みてごらんください。

✅ 社長しゃちょう、こちらのグラフをご覧らんください。

てごらんください combines a downward command with an honorific ending, and the two cannot coexist. The honorific verb ご覧らんになる supplies the polite request on its own.

～てごらん · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) もう少すこし待まってみて。 (b) もう少すこし待まってごらん。

(a) is neutral. You are asking someone - anyone, of any standing - to wait and see what happens. Between adults it is the ordinary way to say it.

(b) adds a difference in position and a note of encouragement. The speaker knows something the listener does not, and is reassuring them that waiting will be worthwhile. Said to a child or a junior colleague it is warm; said to your boss it would sound as though you were patting them on the head.

～ずに

Meaning · without doing - the written, slightly formal twin of ～ないで

ずに and ないで mean the same thing. What separates them is texture: ずに is tighter, older and more literary, and it belongs to writing, to careful speech and to a large family of set expressions. Learn the two irregular stems - せずに and 来こずに - and the rest is straightforward.

Joining clauses (literary) ～ず、 言いわず、去さっていった
Common set forms ～ずにすむ・～ずにおく・～ずにいる 行いかずにすんだ

～ずに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ❌しずに is the commonest error on this page. する does not use its ordinary stem here; the form is せずに, from an older negative. Likewise 来くる gives 来こずに, never ❌きずに.

Note - ある and adjectives have no ずに form. "Because there wasn't any" is なくて, and that is a different pattern with a different job.

～ずに · Examples

朝あさごはんを食たべずに家いえを出でた。
I left the house without eating breakfast.

何なにも言いわずに帰かえってしまった。
He went home without saying a word.

傘かさを持もたずに出でかけて、ずぶぬれになりました。
I went out without an umbrella and got soaked.

誰だれにも相談そうだんせずに、一人ひとりで決きめてしまった。
I decided it on my own, without consulting anybody.

～ずに · Examples (cont.)

息子むすこは靴くつもぬがずに部屋へやに上あがってきた。
My son came into the room without even taking his shoes off.

辞書じしょを使つかわずにこの文章ぶんしょうが読よめたら、なかなかの実力じつりょくです。
If you can read this passage without a dictionary, you're doing rather well.

昨夜さくやは一睡いっすいもせずに原稿げんこうを書かいていました。
I was up writing the manuscript last night without a wink of sleep.

事前じぜんに連絡れんらくせずに訪たずねるのは、日本にほんでは失礼しつれいにあたります。
Calling on someone without letting them know in advance counts as rude in Japan.

～ずに · Examples (cont.)

あきらめずに続つづけていれば、いつか結果けっかはついてくるものです。
If you keep going without giving up, results do come eventually.

～ずに · Nuance & register

ずに is a survival from classical Japanese, and it still carries a faint tightness that ないで does not. In writing - essays, news, instructions, business email - it is the default. In conversation, ないで is the default, and ずに sounds a shade formal or deliberate: 寝ねずに待まっていた is perfectly sayable, just slightly more composed than 寝ねないで待まっていた.

Certain combinations, however, are ずに only or nearly so, and they are worth learning whole:

  • ～ずにすむ - "get away without having to":
    払はらわずにすんだ.
  • ～ずにはいられない - "can't help doing".
  • ～ずじまい - "ended up never doing it":
    会あえずじまいだった.

～ずに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The bare ず without に is a further step into literary Japanese. It links clauses rather than modifying a verb - 振ふり返かえらず、歩あるき続つづけた - and it belongs to novels, speeches and song lyrics. Recognise it; write ずに.

The one thing ずに cannot do is make a request. ないで has a second life as a negative command - 触さわらないでください - and ずに has no share in it. ❌触さわらずにください is not a formal version of anything.

～ずに · ～ずに vs ～ないで

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register: ずに written and formal, ないで spoken and neutral. But only ないで can form a request.

薬くすりを飲のまないで寝ねてしまった。
I fell asleep without taking my medicine.

所定しょていの欄らんに記入きにゅうせずに提出ていしゅつしないでください。
Please do not submit the form without filling in the designated fields.

～ずに · ～ずに vs ～ないで (cont.)

The second sentence shows both at once, and the division of labour is exact: ずに does the "without", ないで does the "please don't". Swapping them produces ❌記入きにゅうしないで提出ていしゅつせずにください, which is not repairable.

Note also that the choice is partly one of rhythm: a formal written sentence that has already used ないで for its request will reach for ずに for the "without", simply to avoid saying ない twice.

～ずに · ～ずに vs ～なくて

Rule of thumb - ずに says without doing it, describing how the main action happened. なくて says because it didn't happen, giving a cause.

地図ちずを見みずに駅えきまで行いけました。
I made it to the station without looking at a map.

地図ちずが読よめなくて、駅えきに着つけませんでした。
I couldn't read the map, so I never got to the station.

～ずに · ～ずに vs ～なくて (cont.)

The first describes your method; the second explains a failure. A quick test: if you could insert "because" into the English, you want なくて. なくて also takes adjectives and ある (お金かねがなくて), which ずに cannot touch at all.

～ずに · ～ずに vs ～ないまま

Rule of thumb - ずに modifies the action: you did B without doing A. ないまま describes a state left unchanged: A never got done, and things stayed that way.

電気でんきを消けさずに出でかけてしまった。
I went out without turning the light off.

電気でんきが消きえないまま、三日みっか留守るすにしていた。
The light stayed on the whole three days I was away.

～ずに · ～ずに vs ～ないまま (cont.)

ずに is tied to a single moment - the moment of leaving. ないまま stretches over a period and emphasises that nothing changed during it. Where both are possible, ないまま adds a sense of something being left unresolved: 理由りゆうを聞きかないまま別わかれた lingers in a way 聞きかずに別わかれた does not.

～ずに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 予約よやくしずに行いったら、満席まんせきでした。 → ✅ 予約よやくせずに行いったら、満席まんせきでした。
    する becomes せずに. This is irregular and there is no way to derive it from the ordinary stem - memorise it.

  • ❌ 昨日きのうは誰だれも**来きずに**、一人ひとりで片付かたづけた。 → ✅ 昨日きのうは誰だれも**来こずに**、一人ひとりで片付かたづけた。
    来くる uses こ- here, the same stem as 来こない. き- belongs to 来きます.

  • ❌ 廊下ろうかを走はしらずにください。 → ✅ 廊下ろうかを走はしらないでください。
    ずに never forms a request. There is no formal-sounding upgrade of ないでください along these lines.

  • ❌ 時間じかんがなずに、全部ぜんぶ見みられなかった。 → ✅ 時間じかんがなくて、全部ぜんぶ見みられなかった。
    ある has no ずに form, and in any case this sentence is giving a reason, which is なくて territory.

～ずに · Practice 1

1. 急いそいでいたので、朝あさごはんも食たべ____会社かいしゃへ向むかった。

朝あさごはんも食たべずに会社かいしゃへ向むかった。

The sentence describes how you set off - without eating - so it wants ずに, not なくて. 食たべないで would be equally correct and simply sound more conversational.

～ずに · Practice 2

2. Choose: レポートは下書したがきを（せずに・しなくて）いきなり書かき始はじめました。

下書したがきをせずにいきなり書かき始はじめました。

This is the method you used, not the reason for something, so ずに is right - and note the irregular せずに for する. しなくて would try to make the missing draft the cause of starting, which is not what the sentence means.

～ずに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "He left without telling anyone where he was going."

彼かれはどこへ行いくのか誰だれにも言いわずに出でていった。

The "without" attaches to the leaving, so ずに (or the more colloquial 言いわないで) is the form. In a novel you might see the bare literary 言いわず、出でていった.

～ずに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 危あぶないですから、工事中こうじちゅうの場所ばしょには近ちかづかずにください。

✅ 危あぶないですから、工事中こうじちゅうの場所ばしょには**近ちかづかないでください**。

ずに cannot carry a request. However formal the notice, a negative instruction takes ないでください - or, in stiffer written style, 近ちかづかないこと.

～ずに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 返事へんじを待またずに出発しゅっぱつした。 (b) 返事へんじが来こなくて出発しゅっぱつできなかった。

(a) describes the manner of departing: you chose to go, and the reply had not come yet. The action succeeded, and the missing reply is background.

(b) gives a cause: no reply arrived, and therefore you could not set off. The action failed, and the missing reply is the reason. ずに never carries that causal weight - it only tells you what accompanied the action, or rather what didn't.

～ずにはいられない

Meaning · can't help doing it; can't stop myself - an urge that overrides you

This is the only "no choice" pattern in which the pressure comes from inside. Nobody has forbidden anything, no rule applies, and the circumstances have not closed off your options - you simply cannot keep yourself from doing it. Laughing, crying, saying the thing you should not say: ずにはいられない is the grammar of being overruled by your own feelings.

～ずにはいられない · Formation

Built on the ずに you already know, plus the potential of いる: literally "cannot be, without doing it".

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (う-verb) ～ない stem + ずにはいられない 笑わらう → 笑わらわずにはいられない
Verb (る-verb) ～ない stem + ずにはいられない 考かんがえる → 考かんがえずにはいられない
Verb (する) せずにはいられない 心配しんぱいする → 心配しんぱいせずにはいられない
Colloquial variant ～ないではいられない 笑わらわないではいられない
Shorter variant ～ずにいられない 笑わらわずにいられない
Polite ～ずにはいられません 笑わらわずにはいられません
Past ～ずにはいられなかった 笑わらわずにはいられなかった

～ずにはいられない · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - する gives せずには, never ❌しずには, exactly as it does in plain ずに. Since half the verbs that fit this pattern are する-verbs - 心配しんぱいする, 同情どうじょうする, 応援おうえんする - this is the form to drill.

～ずにはいられない · Examples

この映画えいがを見みると、泣なかずにはいられない。
I can't watch this film without crying.

息子むすこのことを心配しんぱいせずにはいられません。
I can't help worrying about my son.

あの店みせの前まえを通とおると、パンを買かわずにはいられない。
I can't walk past that shop without buying bread.

彼女かのじょの話はなし方かたがおかしくて、笑わらわずにはいられなかった。
The way she told it was so funny I couldn't keep a straight face.

～ずにはいられない · Examples (cont.)

こんなひどい記事きじを読よむと、一言ひとこと言いわずにはいられません。
When I read an article this bad, I can't let it pass without saying something.

子こどもが一人ひとりで泣ないているのを見みて、声こえをかけずにはいられなかった。
Seeing a child crying on his own, I couldn't not go over and speak to him.

試合しあいの最後さいごの五分間ごふんかんは、立たち上あがって応援おうえんせずにはいられませんでした。
For the last five minutes of the match I simply had to get up and cheer.

災害さいがいのニュースを見みていると、自分じぶんに何なにができるのかを考かんがえずにはいられない。
Watching the news about the disaster, I can't stop asking myself what I could do.

～ずにはいられない · Examples (cont.)

祖母そぼの手紙てがみを読よみ返かえすたびに、あの夏なつの台所だいどころを思おもい出ださずにはいられません。
Every time I reread my grandmother's letters, I find myself back in that kitchen, that summer.

～ずにはいられない · Nuance & register

The compulsion is emotional, and the verbs that appear here reflect that: to laugh, to cry, to worry, to remember, to sympathise, to say something, to cheer. Physical needs and practical necessities do not belong - 傘かさを買かわずにはいられなかった is odd if it merely started raining, and natural if you saw an umbrella so beautiful you had no defence against it.

Because it reports an inner state, the subject is normally you, or a narrator speaking of themselves. About a third person you need a hedge, exactly as with たい: 彼かれも笑わらわずにはいられなかったようだ. Written flat about someone else, it claims access to a mind you cannot see into.

～ずにはいられない · Nuance & register (cont.)

The register leans written and expressive: essays, reviews, letters, blog posts, novels. Spoken, it is entirely usable but a touch dramatic - in casual conversation people say つい笑わらっちゃった instead. That is worth knowing, because a learner who uses ずにはいられない about a mildly amusing thing sounds as though they are narrating their own memoir.

Note - two neighbours, distinguished mainly by register. ～ないではいられない means the same and is a little more conversational. ～ずにいられない, without the は, is a shortened form that turns up in speech and in lyrics. The は adds a touch of emphasis; none of the three differs in meaning.

～ずにはいられない · ～ずにはいられない vs ～てしまう

Rule of thumb - てしまう reports that the action happened despite you. ずにはいられない reports the pressure to do it, and usually says something about you as a person.

おかしくて笑わらってしまいました。
It was so funny I ended up laughing.

彼かれの顔かおを見みるたびに笑わらわずにはいられません。
I can't look at his face without laughing.

てしまう is about one occasion and often carries regret. ずにはいられない states a standing susceptibility - every time, without fail - and carries no regret at all, only recognition. That is why the second sentence takes たびに comfortably and the first does not.

～ずにはいられない · ～ずにはいられない vs ～ないわけにはいかない

Rule of thumb - both come out as "I have to" in English, and they are opposites underneath. ずにはいられない is a want you cannot suppress; ないわけにはいかない is a duty you cannot escape.

友達ともだちの作品さくひんなので、見みに行いかないわけにはいかない。
It's my friend's work, so I can hardly not go and see it.

評判ひょうばんがよすぎて、見みに行いかずにはいられない。
Everyone's raving about it - I can't stay away.

～ずにはいられない · ～ずにはいられない vs ～ないわけにはいかない (cont.)

In the first you would rather stay home; friendship has removed the exit. In the second nothing would keep you away. Choosing wrongly says something unfortunate about your feelings: telling a friend 見みに行いかないわけにはいかなくて admits you went out of obligation.

～ずにはいられない · ～ずにはいられない vs ～しかない

Rule of thumb - しかない is about the world running out of options. ずにはいられない is about you running out of self-control.

終電しゅうでんがないので、歩あるいて帰かえるしかない。
There's no last train, so I'll have to walk home.

あんまり夜よるがきれいだったので、遠回とおまわりせずにはいられなかった。
The night was so lovely I couldn't help taking the long way round.

Both sentences end with the speaker walking, and they explain the walking in opposite ways. If the sentence would still be true of someone with a completely different personality, you want しかない; if it depends on how this person feels, you want ずにはいられない.

～ずにはいられない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 結果けっかが気きになって、確認かくにんしずにはいられない。 → ✅ 結果けっかが気きになって、確認かくにんせずにはいられない。
    する takes the irregular せずに. There is no しずに anywhere in Japanese.

  • ❌ 明日あしたまでにレポートを出ださずにはいられない。 → ✅ 明日あしたまでにレポートを出ださなければならない。
    A deadline is an obligation, not an urge. This pattern reports a feeling you cannot suppress, and nobody is emotionally overwhelmed by a report.

  • ❌ 遅おくれそうだったので、タクシーに乗のらずにはいられなかった。 → ✅ 遅おくれそうだったので、タクシーに乗のるしかなかった。
    Circumstances closing off the alternatives is しかない. ずにはいられない would suggest you have an emotional weakness for taxis.

～ずにはいられない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 山田やまださんは感動かんどうせずにはいられません。 → ✅ 山田やまださんも感動かんどうせずにはいられなかったようです。
    About someone else, the claim needs hedging - ようだ, だろう, らしい - because you cannot see inside their head.

～ずにはいられない · Practice 1

1. あんなに一生懸命いっしょうけんめい練習れんしゅうしていたのを知しっているので、応援おうえん____。

応援おうえんせずにはいられません。

する gives the irregular せずに. The sentence describes an emotional pull rather than a duty, which is what makes this pattern right - nobody has asked the speaker to cheer.

～ずにはいられない · Practice 2

2. Choose: 台風たいふうで電車でんしゃが止とまったので、会社かいしゃに泊とまる（しかなかった・ずにはいられなかった）。

会社かいしゃに泊とまるしかなかった。

A typhoon closing the railway is the world removing your options, which is しかない. 泊とまらずにはいられなかった would claim you were seized by a longing to sleep at the office.

～ずにはいられない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Whenever I hear that song, I can't help remembering my school days."

その曲きょくを聞きくと、学生がくせい時代じだいを思おもい出ださずにはいられません。

Memory arriving uninvited is a textbook use of the pattern. 思おもい出ださないではいられません says the same thing a little more conversationally.

～ずにはいられない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 部長ぶちょうに頼たのまれたので、残業ざんぎょうせずにはいられませんでした。

✅ 部長ぶちょうに頼たのまれたので、残業ざんぎょうしないわけにはいきませんでした。

Being asked by your manager creates a duty, and duty you cannot escape is ないわけにはいかない. As written, the sentence says you were unable to restrain your appetite for overtime.

～ずにはいられない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) その手紙てがみを読よんで、泣ないてしまった。 (b) その手紙てがみを読よむと、泣なかずにはいられない。

(a) reports one occasion. You read the letter, and the tears arrived without your consent; てしまう adds a faint sense of "I didn't mean to", which can shade into embarrassment.

(b) reports a rule about yourself. Note the non-past 読よむと: this happens every time, and the speaker is stating a known feature of their own reaction. (a) is an event, (b) is a disposition.
