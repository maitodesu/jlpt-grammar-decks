～だけ - as much as

Meaning · as much as ～, as many as ～ - up to the limit of what is possible, wanted, or available

This is the same だけ you already know as "only", pointed the other way. Instead of shrinking the amount ("only three"), it names a ceiling and then fills it: as much as you like, as fast as I can, everything there was. The word is identical; what changes it is the kind of word in front of it.

～だけ - as much as · Formation

The extent reading appears when だけ follows a word that names a capacity, a desire, or a stock - a potential form, ～たい, ある, or 好すきな. Very often the same verb is repeated on both sides of it.

～だけ - as much as · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - repeat the verb (買かえるだけ買かう) and the "as much as" reading is unmistakable. Native speakers reach for that doubling precisely because it removes the ambiguity with "only".

Note - が and を behave as they do with ordinary だけ: they are dropped. 好すきなだけ取とってください, not ❌好すきなだけを取とってください. Other particles survive: 話はなしたいだけ話はなしたあとで.

～だけ - as much as · Examples

～だけ - as much as · Examples (cont.)

My little brother complained to his heart's content and then marched back to his room.

They told me to take as much as I wanted, but I didn't have a bag big enough.

～だけ - as much as · Nuance & register

The pattern itself is register-neutral: できるだけ早はやく is at home in a business email and in a text message alike. What varies is the flavour the surrounding words give it.

好すきなだけ and Vたいだけ are generous or indulgent when you say them to someone else - "help yourself" - and slightly self-critical or exasperated when you say them about someone: 言いいたいだけ言いう carries a clear "and never mind the consequences." Tone of voice does the rest.

できるだけ is the workhorse. It attaches to an adverb (できるだけ早はやく, できるだけ安やすく) or to a whole clause (できるだけ参加さんかするようにします) and appears constantly in polite requests and commitments. Its stiffer written cousin is できる限かぎり, and in formal writing you also meet 可能かのうな限かぎり.

～だけ - as much as · Nuance & register (cont.)

あるだけ and 残のこっているだけ imply the stock is now exhausted, which is why they so often pair with 全部ぜんぶ. If you do not mean "and that emptied it", say たくさん instead.

One structural point worth noticing: because だけ here measures an amount, これだけ・それだけ・あれだけ mean "this much" rather than "only this" - あれだけ注意ちゅういしたのに, "after all those warnings." Beginners read those as "only" and get the sentence backwards.

～だけ - as much as · ～だけ "as much as" vs ～だけ "only"

Rule of thumb - look at what sits in front of だけ. A fixed thing (a number, a noun, a plain dictionary verb) gives "only". A capacity or a wish (potential form, ～たい, ある, 好すきな) gives "as much as".

一ひとつだけ買かった。
I bought only one.

買かえるだけ買かった。
I bought as many as I could.

～だけ - as much as · ～だけ "as much as" vs ～だけ "only" (cont.)

The second sentence does not say the amount was small - it may have been a trolley-load. It says the amount ran up against a limit.

The trap is the plain dictionary form, which stays on the "only" side:

見みるだけにします。
I'll just look. (only looking, not buying)

Put that verb into its potential form and the meaning flips completely: 見みられるだけ見みておこう, "let's see as much of it as we can."

～だけ - as much as · できるだけ vs なるべく

Rule of thumb - できるだけ aims at the maximum: as much as is possible. なるべく aims at a preference: if it can be managed. なるべく is the gentler of the two in a request.

なるべく現金げんきんでお願ねがいします。
Cash if at all possible, please.

できるだけ現金げんきんでお願ねがいします。
Please pay in cash as far as you possibly can.

Asking a colleague to hurry, なるべく leaves them room; できるだけ tells them to push. Neither is impolite, but a string of できるだけ requests starts to sound demanding, and なるべく never does.

～だけ - as much as · ～だけ vs ～かぎり

Rule of thumb - they overlap on ability and stock (できるだけ／できる限かぎり), and かぎり is the more formal and more emphatic of the two. But かぎり also works on knowledge and range, where だけ cannot follow at all.

私わたしの知しっている**限かぎり**、その店みせはまだ営業えいぎょうしています。
As far as I know, that shop is still open.

❌知しっているだけ cannot mean "as far as I know" - it means "only knowing (and not acting)". The same goes for 見みわたす限かぎり "as far as the eye can see" and 命いのちのある限かぎり "as long as I live". Keep だけ for countable amounts and effort, and let かぎり handle scope.

～だけ - as much as · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 好すきだけ取とってください。 → ✅ 好すきなだけ取とってください。
    好すき is a な-adjective, so it needs な before だけ - the same rule as ordinary だけ.

  • ❌ 好すきなだけを取とってください。 → ✅ 好すきなだけ取とってください。
    を and が drop after だけ. Leaving them in sounds like a translation.

  • ❌ 走はしるだけ走はしった。（meaning "I ran as far as I could"） → ✅ 走はしれるだけ走はしった。
    A plain dictionary form gives the "only" reading. The extent meaning needs the potential form, the ～たい form, or ある.

～だけ - as much as · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ できるだけに早はやく送おくります。 → ✅ できるだけ早はやく送おくります。
    できるだけ is already adverbial. Nothing goes between it and the word it modifies.

  • ❌ 好すきなほど食たべてください。 → ✅ 好すきなだけ食たべてください。
    ほど measures a degree by comparison, not a helping. An invitation to take as much as you want is always だけ.

～だけ - as much as · Practice 1

1. 時間じかんがないので、____だけ準備じゅんびしておきます。（できる・一ひとつ）

できるだけ準備じゅんびしておきます。

The sentence gives a reason - there is no time - and then promises effort up to a limit, which is the "as much as" reading. 一ひとつだけ would be grammatical but would mean "I'll prepare only one thing", a very different promise.

～だけ - as much as · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural option: 遠慮えんりょしないで、（好すきなだけ・好すきだけ）持もっていってください。

遠慮えんりょしないで、好すきなだけ持もっていってください。

好すき conjugates as a な-adjective, so the modifying form before だけ is 好すきな. The sentence means "take as many as you want", a standard offer when handing out food or samples.

～だけ - as much as · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'll gather as much information as I can and report back tomorrow."

できるだけ情報じょうほうを集あつめて、明日あしたご報告ほうこくします。

できるだけ covers "as much as I can" here and sits in front of the whole verb phrase. 集あつめられるだけ集あつめて is also good, and slightly more emphatic - it foregrounds the limit of what is physically possible.

～だけ - as much as · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 冷蔵庫れいぞうこにある野菜やさいをあるだけを全部ぜんぶ使つかいました。

✅ 冷蔵庫れいぞうこにある野菜やさいを、あるだけ全部ぜんぶ使つかいました。 "I used up every vegetable that was in the fridge."

The を after だけ has to go. One を already marks 野菜やさい as the object; あるだけ is an adverbial amount phrase and takes no particle of its own.

～だけ - as much as · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 話はなしたいだけ話はなしてから帰かえった。 (b) 話はなすだけ話はなして帰かえった。

(a) is the extent reading: they talked until they had had their fill, and only then left. The measure is their own appetite for talking, and the sentence can sound either satisfied or faintly exasperated depending on the context.

(b) is the "only" reading with a common twist: they did the talking and nothing else - no listening, no helping - and left. That 話はなすだけ話はなして shape almost always carries a critical edge: "all they did was talk."

～きり

Meaning · only; just that much - and, after a past-tense verb, "and that was the last of it"

きり limits, like だけ does, but it leaves a mark that だけ does not: a sense of finality. 二人ふたりきり is not merely two people, it is two people and nobody else was let in. 出でかけたきり is not merely "went out", it is "went out, and whatever should have happened next never did."

～きり · Formation

きり follows a noun, a counter, or a past-tense verb. Note the asymmetry: as a limiter it takes nouns; as "and that was the last of it" it takes ～た, never the dictionary form.

Attaches to Form Example

Counter (number of times) Counter + きり 一度いちど → 一度いちどきり
Demonstrative これ／それ／あれ + きり それ → それきり
Noun + きり + で Noun + きりで 一回いっかいきりで終おわる

～きり · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - after Vた, the second clause is nearly always negative. Vたきり exists in order to say that the expected sequel did not arrive: went out → did not come back, borrowed → did not return it, met once → have not met since.

Note - 寝ねたきり has hardened into an ordinary noun meaning "bedridden": 寝ねたきりになる, 寝ねたきりの老人ろうじん. The same freezing happened to つきっきり, "staying right beside someone the whole time."

～きり · Examples

昨日きのうは二人ふたりきりで話はなしました。
Yesterday we talked just the two of us.

彼女かのじょに会あったのは一度いちどきりです。
I've met her exactly once.

兄あには朝あさ出でかけたきり、まだ帰かえってきません。
My brother went out this morning and still isn't back.

貸かした本ほんは、それきり返かえってこなかった。
That book I lent out never came back.

～きり · Examples (cont.)

財布さいふには千円せんえんきりしか入はいっていなかった。
There was only a thousand yen in my wallet.

祖父そふは三年さんねん前まえから寝ねたきりの生活せいかつをしています。
My grandfather has been bedridden for three years.

入学式にゅうがくしきで挨拶あいさつをしたきり、同おなじクラスの人ひととはほとんど話はなしていません。
Apart from saying hello at the entrance ceremony, I've hardly spoken to anyone in my class.

息子むすこは部屋へやに入はいったきり、夕ゆうごはんの時間じかんになっても出でてきませんでした。
My son shut himself in his room and didn't come out even when dinner time came.

～きり · Nuance & register

きり is spoken Japanese first. It is perfectly usable in polite speech - 一度いちどきりです, 出でかけたきりです - but formal writing prefers だけ, のみ, or an explicit それ以来いらい for the "since then" idea.

The doubled form っきり is a straight intensifier and a clear register marker: 二人ふたりっきり, これっきり, それっきり. It is warm and casual with friends and slightly childish or blunt in a work setting. In particular, これっきりにして ("that's the last time - stop it") is a telling-off, not a neutral request.

～きり · Nuance & register (cont.)

The limiting きり on nouns has narrowed over the last century. In modern Japanese it is alive mainly in set combinations - 二人ふたりきり, 一人ひとりきり, 一度いちどきり, 一回いっかいきり, これきり, それきり - and it sounds dated or regional in freer combinations. 千円せんえんきりしかない is still heard, and it is colloquial and a touch old-fashioned; if in doubt, plain 千円せんえんしかない is what most speakers say today.

Emotionally, きり leans toward shortage and loss. 一度いちどきり of a chance means "one shot only, so don't waste it". 二人ふたりきり can be romantic or claustrophobic depending on the story, but it is never neutral - it always says that the rest of the world has been shut out.

～きり · きり vs だけ

Rule of thumb - だけ draws a boundary and reports it. きり draws the same boundary and adds "and nothing further" - that is either not enough, or the end of the matter.

会あったのは一度いちどだけです。
I've met them once.

会あったのは一度いちどきりです。
I've met them just the once - and that was that.

The だけ sentence is a plain count; you could follow it with "so we're meeting again next week". The きり sentence closes the door, so a follow-up meeting would sound like a contradiction.

～きり · きり vs だけ (cont.)

だけ is also far more flexible. It attaches to verbs in the dictionary form, to adjectives, and to desires (好すきなだけ); きり does none of these. Conversely, だけ cannot do the Vたきり job at all: ❌出でかけただけ帰かえってこない is simply broken.

～きり · ～たきり vs ～たまま

Rule of thumb - ～たまま describes a state left unchanged. ～たきり describes a sequel that never came. まま looks at the thing; きり looks at what should have happened next and didn't.

窓まどを開あけたまま出でかけてしまった。
I went out and left the window open.

窓まどを開あけたきり、一度いちども閉しめていない。
I opened the window and haven't closed it once since.

Both are true of the same window, but they are aimed differently. The まま sentence says how the window is; the きり sentence complains about the closing that never happened. That is why a きり clause pulls a negative in behind it, while a まま clause happily takes a positive one.

～きり · きり vs しか～ない

Rule of thumb - しか～ない requires a negative predicate and states a shortfall. きり takes a positive predicate and states a limit. They can be stacked (きりしか～ない) for emphasis, but neither one replaces the other.

部屋へやには二人ふたりしかいませんでした。
There were only two people in the room.

部屋へやで二人ふたりきりで話はなしました。
We talked in the room, just the two of us.

～きり · きり vs しか～ない (cont.)

The しか version counts heads and finds them few. The きり version is about privacy, and it needs a positive verb - ❌二人ふたりきりで話はなしませんでした would have to mean "we didn't talk alone", which is not what the pattern is for.

～きり · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 出でかけるきり、帰かえってこない。 → ✅ 出でかけたきり、帰かえってこない。
    The "and that was the last of it" reading is built on the past form. A dictionary form before きり does not work.

  • ❌ 家いえを出でたきり、三時間さんじかん後ごに帰かえってきた。 → ✅ 家いえを出でたきり、帰かえってこない。
    Vたきり announces that the sequel is missing, so a second clause reporting that it did happen contradicts the pattern. Use ～てから for a plain sequence.

  • ❌ 好すきなきり食たべてください。 → ✅ 好すきなだけ食たべてください。
    きり cannot follow an adjective or express a generous amount. It limits; it does not invite.

～きり · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 二人ふたりきりしか話はなしました。 → ✅ 二人ふたりきりで話はなしました。
    しか demands a negative verb. If the verb is positive, きり stands on its own, usually followed by で.

～きり · Practice 1

The second clause is negative and reports a missing sequel - he went home and the expected contact never came. That is exactly what Vたきり is for, and the verb must be in its past form.

～きり · Practice 2

Both are possible, but きり is the better fit: 一度いちどきり着きて…

The sentence goes on to say the clothes have been in storage ever since, which is the "one time and no more" feeling きり carries. だけ would merely count the wearings and would sit oddly with the "ever since" clause.

貸かしたきり carries both halves of the English - the lending, and the return that never came. Note that the second clause has to be negative; まま here would describe the umbrella's state rather than your grievance.

～きり · Practice 4

～きり · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 電気でんきをつけたまま寝ねてしまった。 (b) 電気でんきをつけたきり、消けしていない。

(a) reports a state: the light was on, and in that condition I fell asleep. The focus is on how things were while something else happened.

(b) reports an omission: I turned the light on, and the switching-off that should have followed has not taken place. The きり clause needs that negative second half, and it carries a faint note of "I really ought to have done it."

～さえ

Meaning · even ～ (of all things); and, with ば, "if only ～ - that alone is enough"

さえ picks the most extreme member of a scale and says the statement holds even there. If even a child knows it, everyone knows it; if she cannot even drink water, she certainly cannot eat. The same word has a second life in さえ～ば, which turns that extreme into a minimum: one condition, and nothing else, is all that is required.

～さえ · Formation

さえ is a focus particle. It sits where the noun's own particle would sit, and が and を are pushed out.

Attaches to Form Example

～さえ · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - さえ on its own means "even". さえ with a ば clause behind it means "if only". The ば is what switches the meaning, so look for it before you translate.

Note - the conditional half of さえ～ば is normally ば, or なら after a noun. Pairing さえ with たら is much rarer and many speakers avoid it, so keep to 時間じかんさえあれば rather than 時間じかんさえあったら until you have heard otherwise.

～さえ · Examples

この漢字かんじは子こどもさえ知しっている。
Even children know this kanji.

I'm so busy I don't even have time to eat.

熱ねつが高たかくて、水みずさえ飲のめなかった。
My fever was so high I couldn't even drink water.

パスワードさえ分わかれば、すぐログインできます。
As long as you know the password, you can log in straight away.

～さえ · Examples (cont.)

名前なまえを書かきさえすれば、申もうし込こみは完了かんりょうです。
Just write your name and the application is done.

引ひっ越こすことを、まだ親おやにさえ話はなしていない。
I haven't told even my parents that I'm moving.

それは専門家せんもんかでさえ説明せつめいできない現象げんしょうなのだそうだ。
Apparently it's a phenomenon that not even specialists can explain.

天気てんきさえよければ、明日あしたの試合しあいは予定よていどおり行おこなわれます。
Provided the weather holds, tomorrow's match will go ahead as scheduled.

～さえ · Nuance & register

さえ is more emphatic and more written than the everyday も. In relaxed conversation people say 子こどもも知しっている; さえ raises the volume and is common in complaints, in news writing, and in argument. It is not stiff, though - nobody will blink at it in speech, and これさえあれば is thoroughly colloquial.

でさえ is さえ with the noun's で kept in place, and it is the usual choice with people presented as authorities or as extreme cases: 先生せんせいでさえ間違まちがえる. The plain 先生せんせいさえ間違まちがえる is also correct; でさえ is a little heavier and a little more formal.

～さえ · Nuance & register (cont.)

すら is the literary sibling. It means the same as this さえ and appears in essays, editorials and formal speeches - 挨拶あいさつすらしない. It sounds bookish in casual conversation, and it has no すら～ば conditional use, so it is not a drop-in replacement.

Emotionally, the "even" さえ almost always leans negative. It turns up with negative verbs and with complaints far more often than with happy news, because its job is to show that a floor has been breached: not even water, not even a greeting, not even the specialists. さえ～ば, by contrast, is optimistic and often relieved - "one thing, and we're fine."

～さえ · さえ vs も

Rule of thumb - both mark an unexpected member, but も is the neutral, everyday word and さえ is the scale-topping one. さえ says "this is the last case you would expect, and even it holds." も can also mean plain "also"; さえ never can.

子こどもも楽たのしめる映画えいがです。
It's a film children can enjoy too.

子こどもさえ楽たのしめる映画えいがです。
It's a film that even children can enjoy.

～さえ · さえ vs も (cont.)

The first adds children to the audience. The second uses children as the hardest case and implies that adults are covered a fortiori. Swap them the wrong way round and you get the classic error: ❌私わたしさえ行いきたいです for "I want to go too" - that says "even I, of all people, want to go."

～さえ · さえ vs まで

Rule of thumb - さえ marks a low bar that was not cleared, or a hardest case that still holds. まで marks a range that was extended further than expected - "went so far as to."

挨拶あいさつさえしてくれなかった。
He didn't even say hello.

彼かれは部長ぶちょうにまで文句もんくを言いった。
He went so far as to complain to the department head.

The さえ sentence points downward: greeting is the minimum, and the minimum was missing. The まで sentence points upward: complaining to colleagues would be normal, and he pushed past that. Where the sentence is about something falling short, さえ is nearly always the one you want.

～さえ · さえ～ば vs plain ～ば

Rule of thumb - plain ば states a condition. さえ～ば states the only condition: satisfy this one thing and nothing else matters.

雨あめが降ふらなければ、出発しゅっぱつします。
If it doesn't rain, we'll set off.

雨あめさえ降ふらなければ、出発しゅっぱつします。
As long as it doesn't rain, we'll set off.

～さえ · さえ～ば vs plain ～ば (cont.)

The second sentence dismisses everything else - wind, cold, a late start - as irrelevant. That is why さえ～ば is the natural pattern for reassurance (体からださえ丈夫じょうぶなら、何なんとかなる) and for regret when the condition was not met (もう少すこし早はやく出でさえすれば、間まに合あったのに).

Note the verb form: with a verb, the pattern splits the verb open - 押おしさえすれば, 言いいさえすれば - putting さえ after the ます-stem and letting する carry the conditional.

～さえ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 水みずをさえ飲のめない。 → ✅ 水みずさえ飲のめない。
    が and を are replaced by さえ, not stacked with it. に, で, と and から are kept: 親おやにさえ.

  • ❌ ボタンを押おすさえすれば動うごきます。 → ✅ ボタンを押おしさえすれば動うごきます。
    With a verb, さえ follows the ます-stem, and する takes the conditional ending.

  • ❌ 私わたしさえ参加さんかしたいです。（meaning "I'd like to join too"） → ✅ 私わたしも参加さんかしたいです。
    さえ is not "too". Using it here says you are the least likely person to want to come.

  • ❌ 一度いちども見みさえいない。 → ✅ 一度いちども見みてさえいない。
    Attached to a verb in this sense, さえ follows the て-form.

～さえ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ お金かねさえあったら、買かいたいです。 → ✅ お金かねさえあれば、買かいます。
    The さえ～ば frame wants ば (or なら after a noun), and its main clause states a result rather than a wish.

Eating lunch is a bare minimum, and the sentence says even that floor was not reached. さえ with a negative predicate is the standard way to show a low bar being missed; も would work but would sound flatter.

～こそ

Meaning · this one and no other; precisely this - emphasis on the word in front of it

こそ has no English word behind it. It does not add information and it does not change what the sentence claims; it takes one element and presses on it, so that "this year I'll do it" becomes "this year I'll do it". The most useful way to learn it is by what it replaces: こそ pushes は and が out of the sentence and stands in their place, carrying their grammatical job plus an insistence they do not have.

～こそ · Formation

こそ goes straight after the element it emphasises. What that element is matters less than what particle it was wearing before.

Attaches to Form Example

Noun that would take は Noun + こそ（は drops） これ → これこそ本物ほんものだ
Noun + another particle に／で／と／から + こそ あなたに → あなたにこそ話はなしたい

～こそ · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - こそ never stacks with が. ❌これがこそ does not exist. With は the picture is softer: は normally drops, but 今度こんどこそは and これこそは are heard when a speaker wants to lean on the point even harder.

Note - ～てこそ is a different shape with a related job: it says the result holds only by way of the action. 自分じぶんでやってこそ意味いみがある, "it only means something if you do it yourself." Its second clause is positive; for the negative version Japanese uses ～なければ.

～こそ · Examples

今度こんどこそ勝かちたい。
This time I really want to win.

「ありがとうございました。」「いいえ、こちらこそ。」
"Thank you very much." "No, thank you."

これこそ私わたしがずっと探さがしていた辞書じしょです。
This is exactly the dictionary I've been looking for all this time.

今いまこそ行動こうどうすべきときだ。
Now is precisely the time to act.

～こそ · Examples (cont.)

失敗しっぱいした人ひとにこそ、話はなを聞きいてみたい。
It's the people who failed that I'd most like to hear from.

親おやは心配しんぱいだからこそ、うるさいことを言いうのです。
Parents nag precisely because they're worried.

毎日まいにち少すこしずつ続つづけてこそ、力ちからがつくのだと思おもいます。
I think it's only by keeping at it a little every day that you build any strength.

来年らいねんこそ家族かぞくそろって旅行りょこうに行いこうと話はなしています。
We keep saying that next year, for sure, we'll all get away together as a family.

～こそ · Nuance & register

こそ is emphatic and slightly formal in free use. Speeches, editorials, slogans and advertising love it - 今いまこそ, これこそ, 本物ほんものこそ - and in ordinary conversation too much of it sounds declamatory, as if you were addressing a room rather than a friend.

That said, several こそ phrases are completely everyday and you should simply learn them whole:

～こそ · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • こちらこそ - the standard reply that turns thanks or an apology back
    on the speaker. It is polite, warm and used at every register.
  • 今度こんどこそ - "this time for real", after previous attempts
    failed.
  • だからこそ - "that is exactly why", used to push back on someone who
    drew the opposite conclusion.
  • 今年ことしこそ - the particle of New Year's resolutions.

～こそ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because こそ insists, it very often arrives in the middle of a disagreement or a correction. It implies that some other candidate was in play and is being set aside: 今度こんどこそ implies the earlier attempts, これこそ implies the things that were not it. If nothing is being set aside, こそ sounds strange and a plain が or は is what you want.

One thing こそ does not do is change the truth of the sentence. 山田やまださんが担当たんとうです and 山田やまださんこそ担当たんとうにふさわしい both leave Yamada in the job; only the speaker's investment differs. Learners who translate こそ as "only" or "must" end up asserting more than the Japanese does.

～こそ · こそ vs が

Rule of thumb - が identifies: it fills the empty slot in "which one?". こそ insists: it argues for one candidate against others already in the listener's mind. Answering a question takes が; arguing takes こそ.

私わたしが担当たんとうです。
I'm the one in charge.

私わたしにこそ責任せきにんがあります。
The responsibility is mine, and mine above all.

～こそ · こそ vs が (cont.)

Asked どなたが担当たんとうですか, you answer with が; replying 私わたしこそ担当たんとうです would sound like a protest against someone else's claim to the role. The reverse holds too: in a heated exchange, あなたこそ - "you're the one who…" - cannot be replaced by が without losing the whole point.

～こそ · こそ vs は

Rule of thumb - は sets its noun quietly against alternatives. こそ sets it loudly against alternatives and rules them out. こそ occupies the same slot, so you never write both.

今年ことしは禁煙きんえんします。
This year I'm going to quit smoking.

今年ことしこそ禁煙きんえんします。
This year I'm really going to quit smoking.

Both sentences make the same promise. The は version compares this year with other years neutrally; the こそ version admits that previous attempts failed and stakes a claim on this one. That admission is why こそ fits resolutions, rematches and second chances so well - and why it sounds odd on a first attempt at anything.

～こそ · ～からこそ vs ～から

Rule of thumb - から gives a reason. からこそ gives the real reason, usually correcting a wrong one the listener has assumed.

時間じかんがないから、手伝てつだえません。
I can't help, because I don't have time.

大切たいせつな友ともだちだからこそ、はっきり言いうんです。
It's precisely because you're a good friend of mine that I'm being blunt.

The second sentence answers an unspoken "if we're friends, why are you being so harsh?" That defensive shape is typical: からこそ almost always appears where the listener might have expected the opposite conclusion. Note also that a からこそ sentence usually closes with のだ／んです, because it is explaining rather than reporting.

～こそ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ これがこそ本物ほんものです。 → ✅ これこそ本物ほんものです。
    こそ takes the place of が; the two never appear together. に, で, と and から do survive in front of it.

  • ❌ どれこそあなたの傘かさですか。 → ✅ どれがあなたの傘かさですか。
    A question word is an unknown, and こそ exists to insist on a known candidate. Question words take が.

  • ❌ 毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうするこそ、上手じょうずになる。 → ✅ 毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうしてこそ、上手じょうずになる。
    With a verb, こそ follows the て-form. A dictionary form plus こそ is not a pattern.

  • ❌ 「どなたが司会しかいですか。」「私わたしこそです。」 → ✅ 「私わたしが司会しかいです。」
    Filling in an unknown is the job of が. こそ in an answer sounds like you are contradicting someone.

～こそ · Practice 1

1. 去年きょねんは一回いっかいも山やまに登のぼれなかった。今年ことし____富士山ふじさんに登のぼりたい。

The first sentence sets up a failed attempt, which is exactly the ground こそ needs: this year, unlike last year, for real. 今年ことしは would be grammatical but would throw away the connection the two sentences have.

～こそ · Practice 5

数量 + は - at least

Meaning · at least this much; this much as a minimum (reading: すうりょう + は)

Put は on a number and the number stops being a measurement and becomes a floor. 三十分さんじゅっぷんかかります reports a journey time; 三十分さんじゅっぷんはかかります says "reckon on thirty minutes - it will not be less, and it may well be more." It is the same contrastive は you already know, doing arithmetic.

数量 + は - at least · Formation

Any expression of amount can take this は - a number plus counter, a money amount, a period of time, or a vague quantity word. The predicate is normally positive.

数量 + は - at least · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Number + counter Quantity + は + predicate 三十分さんじゅっぷん → 三十分さんじゅっぷんはかかる
Money Amount + は + する 五千円ごせんえん → 五千円ごせんえんはする
Number of people Counter + は + いる／来くる 十人じゅうにん → 十人じゅうにんはいる

Rule of thumb - the は goes on the quantity, not on the noun. 水みずを二本にほんは買かっておこう, not ❌水みずは二本にほん買かっておこう, which would be contrasting water with something else instead of setting a minimum.

Note - an approximator and this は can stack, and the order is fixed: ぐらいは, never ❌はぐらい. The combination softens the floor to "about that much at the very least."

数量 + は - at least · Examples

A dictionary like this must cost five thousand yen at least.

There are always at least ten people queuing outside that shop.

I've read that novel at least three times.

数量 + は - at least · Examples (cont.)

引ひっ越こしの準備じゅんびには一週間いっしゅうかんは必要ひつようです。
You'll need a week at the very least to get ready for the move.

台風たいふうの影響えいきょうで、電車でんしゃが動うごくまでに三時間さんじかんはかかりそうです。
Because of the typhoon it looks like it'll be three hours or more before the trains run.

試験しけんに合格ごうかくしたいなら、毎日まいにち二時間にじかんは勉強べんきょうしたほうがいいですよ。
If you want to pass the exam you should be studying two hours a day minimum.

会場かいじょうには百人ひゃくにんは集あつまっていたと思おもいますが、正確せいかくな人数にんずうは分わかりません。
I'd say a hundred people had gathered at the venue, though I don't know the exact number.

数量 + は - at least · Nuance & register

This is a plain contrastive は and it is register-neutral: it appears in casual speech, in polite speech and in writing without changing shape. In conversation the quantity takes the stress - さんじゅっぷんは かかるよ - and that stress is doing much of the work, so listen for it.

What the pattern conveys is confidence about the low end. The speaker is not claiming precision; they are claiming that the true figure will not fall below the one given. That is why it pairs so naturally with hedges: だろう, でしょう, と思おもう, はずだ, そうです. Combining a floor with a hedge is not a contradiction - "at least this much, probably more, I can't say exactly."

数量 + は - at least · Nuance & register (cont.)

The pattern is common in three everyday situations: estimating time and cost (一時間いちじかんはかかる, 一万円いちまんえんはする), asserting effort you have already put in (三回さんかいは確認かくにんした - "I checked it three times, thank you very much"), and setting a requirement for someone else (三日みっかは休やすんだほうがいい). The second of these often carries a defensive edge, because the contrastive は invites the comparison "and that's more than you're giving me credit for."

Beside it stands the adverb 少すくなくとも, which says "at least" explicitly. It is a shade more formal and more logical in tone, it does not replace the は, and the two frequently appear together in the same sentence.

数量 + は - at least · 数量 + は vs 数量 + も

Rule of thumb - は sets a floor: at least this much. も expresses surprise at the size: as much as this. は is sober; も is impressed or appalled.

会議かいぎは二時間にじかんはかかります。
The meeting will take two hours at least.

会議かいぎは二時間にじかんもかかりました。
The meeting dragged on for two whole hours.

数量 + は - at least · 数量 + は vs 数量 + も (cont.)

The first is planning; the second is a complaint. Note the tense that comes with them: は looks forward to an amount not yet known, so it sits with non-past and with hedges, while も reacts to an amount already established, so it sits comfortably in the past. Using も for a forecast - ❌二時間にじかんもかかります as a neutral estimate - makes you sound outraged by your own schedule.

数量 + は - at least · 数量 + は vs 少なくとも

Rule of thumb - 少すくなくとも is an adverb and states the minimum in words; the は marks it grammatically. Use は in speech, add 少すくなくとも when you want the floor spelled out.

少すくなくとも三十分さんじゅっぷんは待またされた。
I was kept waiting at least half an hour.

少すくなくとも can also modify things that are not quantities at all - 少すくなくとも連絡れんらくくらいはしてほしい, "I'd at least like to be told" - where the number-plus-は pattern has nothing to attach to. In return, は is the one that works when no adverb is wanted, which in ordinary conversation is most of the time.

数量 + は with a positive predicate vs with a negative one

Rule of thumb - the same shape does two opposite jobs. Positive predicate: at least this much. Negative predicate: not all of this much - partial negation.

漢字かんじを百ひゃくは覚おぼえた。
I've learned a hundred kanji at least.

漢字かんじを全部ぜんぶは覚おぼえていない。
I haven't learned all the kanji. (I have learned some)

数量 + は with a positive predicate vs with a negative one (cont.)

This is not two grammars; it is one は marking a contrast, with the negative scoping over the quantity rather than over the verb. Still, the practical effect is that a careless negative reverses your meaning. If you want to say "it takes at least thirty minutes", the verb must be positive: 三十分さんじゅっぷんはかかります. 三十分さんじゅっぷんはかかりません tells the listener the opposite - it will not take a full thirty minutes.

数量 + は - at least · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 一時間いちじかんもかかると思おもうので、早はやめに出でましょう。 → ✅ 一時間いちじかんはかかると思おもうので、早はやめに出でましょう。
    For a forward-looking minimum you need は. も would make the hour sound outrageous, which is odd in a sentence that is calmly planning around it.

  • ❌ 三十分さんじゅっぷんはかかりません。（meaning "it takes at least thirty minutes"） → ✅ 三十分さんじゅっぷんはかかります。
    With a negative predicate the pattern flips to partial negation: "it doesn't take a full thirty minutes."

  • ❌ 一時間いちじかんはぐらい待まった。 → ✅ 一時間いちじかんぐらいは待まった。
    The approximator goes next to the number and は goes outside it. The order is fixed.

数量 + は - at least · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 水みずは二本にほん買かっておきます。（meaning "I'll buy at least two bottles"） → ✅ 水みずを二本にほんは買かっておきます。
    The は has to land on the quantity. On the noun it contrasts water with the other shopping instead.

  • ❌ 少すくなくとも三日みっかも必要ひつようです。 → ✅ 少すくなくとも三日みっかは必要ひつようです。
    少すくなくとも already announces a minimum, so the particle that goes with it is は. も pulls in the opposite direction.

数量 + は - at least · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 昨日きのうは三時間さんじかんは寝ねた。 (b) 昨日きのうは三時間さんじかんしか寝ねていない。

(a) treats three hours as a floor and, by implication, as enough to get by on: "I got a good three hours." The speaker is arguing that the amount was not as bad as it might sound.

(b) treats the same three hours as a shortfall: "three hours and no more." The しか frame needs its negative verb and carries an unmistakable complaint. The number is identical in both sentences - only the particle decides whether it counts as sufficient or as scandalous.

くらい / ぐらい

Meaning · about, roughly; to the extent that ～; and - dismissively - "no more than that"

くらい names a degree. Attached to a number it makes the number approximate ("about thirty minutes"); attached to a clause it measures one thing against another ("so tired I couldn't stand"); attached to a small task it belittles it ("it's only washing up"). Those three uses look unrelated in English, but they are one idea: this is the level we are talking about.

くらい / ぐらい · Formation

くらい and ぐらい are the same word. Both attach the same way, and nothing about the attachment decides which of the two you say.

くらい / ぐらい · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Number + counter Quantity + くらい／ぐらい 三十分さんじゅっぷん → 三十分さんじゅっぷんぐらい

くらい / ぐらい · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - くらい measures how much, so it goes with spans, amounts and degrees. It does not name a point on the calendar or the clock; that is ごろ. 三時間さんじかんぐらい but 三時さんじごろ.

Note - ～くらいなら is a related pattern worth recognising: it rejects one option in favour of another. 待まつくらいなら、歩あるいて帰かえる - "if it comes to waiting, I'd rather walk home." The first clause names the level the speaker refuses to sink to.

くらい / ぐらい · Examples

駅えきまで歩あるいて十分じゅっぷんぐらいです。
It's about ten minutes on foot to the station.

パーティーには二十人にじゅうにんぐらい来きました。
Around twenty people came to the party.

どのくらい日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうしていますか。
How long have you been studying Japanese?

お皿さらを洗あらうくらい、私わたしがやりますよ。
Washing a few dishes - leave that to me.

くらい / ぐらい · Examples (cont.)

昨日きのうは立たっていられないくらい疲つかれていた。
Yesterday I was so tired I could barely stay on my feet.

弟おとうとは私わたしと同おなじぐらい背せが高たかくなった。
My little brother has grown about as tall as me.

忙いそがしいのは分わかるけれど、メールの返事へんじぐらいしてほしかった。
I know you're busy, but you could at least have answered my email.

会場かいじょうまではバスで一時間いちじかんぐらいかかりますので、余裕よゆうをもって出でてください。
The bus to the venue takes about an hour, so please leave yourself plenty of time.

くらい / ぐらい · Examples (cont.)

久ひさしぶりに聞きいたその曲きょくは、涙なみだが出でるくらい懐なつかしかった。
Hearing that song again after so long brought back memories so strongly it almost made me cry.

くらい / ぐらい · Nuance & register

くらい and ぐらい mean exactly the same thing and both are correct everywhere. The tendency - and it is only a tendency - is that ぐらい is a little more frequent in speech and after the demonstratives (これぐらい, どれぐらい), while くらい turns up slightly more often in careful writing. No listener will notice which you chose, so pick one and be consistent within a sentence.

The approximate use is completely neutral and extremely common; Japanese conversation prefers a hedged number to a bare one, and a bare 三十分さんじゅっぷんかかります can sound oddly exact where 三十分さんじゅっぷんぐらい is expected.

くらい / ぐらい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The belittling use is the one to handle with care. Applied to your own effort it is modest and generous: それくらい、喜よろこんでやります - "that's nothing, happy to." Applied to someone else's effort it says their contribution is trivial, and in a request it says the bar you are setting is embarrassingly low: 挨拶あいさつくらいしなさい is something a parent says to a child. Aimed upward at a manager or a customer it is rude, so keep it for peers, family and yourself.

There is also a superlative frame worth recognising: ～くらい～はない, "nothing is as ～ as this." 夏なつの試験しけんくらいつらいものはない - "there's nothing as grim as summer exams." It behaves the same way as the more formal ～ほど～はない.

くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ごろ

Rule of thumb - ごろ marks a point in time; くらい marks a span or an amount. If the word answers "when?", use ごろ. If it answers "how long?" or "how many?", use くらい.

七時しちじごろ家いえを出でます。
I leave the house around seven.

七時間しちじかんぐらい寝ねました。
I slept for about seven hours.

くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ごろ (cont.)

The counter tells you which is which: 三時さんじ is a clock time and takes ごろ; 三時間さんじかん is a duration and takes ぐらい. Dates and days work like clock times - 来週らいしゅうの水曜日すいようびごろ. Money, distance, weight and headcount are all amounts, so they can only take くらい: ❌千円せんえんごろ is not Japanese.

Note - you will hear 三時さんじぐらいに in casual speech, and it is not an error, but ごろ is the standard choice for a clock time and the safe one in writing.

くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ほど

Rule of thumb - for approximation they overlap, with ほど sounding more formal. For degree, ほど points up the scale and くらい can point down it. Only くらい can belittle.

一時間いちじかんほどお待まちいただけますか。
Could I ask you to wait for about an hour?

一時間いちじかんぐらい待まってよ。
Come on, wait an hour, would you?

Those two sentences carry the same number and completely different manners: ほど belongs to the polite register and shows up constantly in service Japanese, while the second sentence is a friend being pushy.

くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ほど (cont.)

The split is sharper with degrees. 死しぬほど疲つかれた and 死しぬくらい疲つかれた are both "dead tired", but move to the bottom of the scale and ほど stops working: お茶ちゃくらい出だしてください ("you could at least offer tea") cannot become ❌お茶ちゃほど出だしてください. Remember also that ほど owns two frames くらい does not share: ～ば～ほど and comparative ～ほど～ない.

くらい / ぐらい · Belittling くらい vs だけ

Rule of thumb - だけ states a limit neutrally. くらい states the same limit and adds a judgement: that little is not worth making a fuss about.

漢字かんじを五いつつだけ覚おぼえました。
I learned just five kanji.

漢字かんじを五いつつぐらい覚おぼえてください。
Learn five kanji at least, would you.

くらい / ぐらい · Belittling くらい vs だけ (cont.)

だけ reports; くらい evaluates. That is why the second sentence works as a complaint and the first does not, and why swapping them in a request changes what you are doing socially. 一人ひとりだけ来こなかった counts the absentees; 一人ひとりぐらい来こなくてもいい waves them away as unimportant.

くらい / ぐらい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 三時間さんじかんごろ勉強べんきょうしました。 → ✅ 三時間さんじかんぐらい勉強べんきょうしました。
    ごろ needs a point in time. A duration counter such as 三時間さんじかん can only take くらい／ぐらい.

  • ❌ 大変たいへんくらい忙いそがしかった。 → ✅ 大変たいへんなくらい忙いそがしかった。
    A な-adjective keeps its な before くらい, exactly as it does before だけ and ので.

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、確認かくにんぐらいしてください。 → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、ご確認かくにんをお願ねがいできますか。
    Not a grammar error but a social one. Belittling くらい aimed at a superior is openly rude; save it for yourself and for people you are close to.

  • ❌ 千円せんえんごろの店みせ → ✅ 千円せんえんぐらいの店みせ
    Money is an amount, never a point in time.

くらい / ぐらい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この問題もんだいは難むずかしいぐらいです。（meaning "it's really hard"） → ✅ この問題もんだいは解とけないぐらい難むずかしいです。
    くらい needs something to measure the degree against. On its own after an adjective it produces the opposite, dismissive reading: "it's merely hard."

くらい / ぐらい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 自分じぶんの部屋へやだけ掃除そうじしました。 (b) 自分じぶんの部屋へやぐらい掃除そうじしなさい。

～あまり

Meaning · out of too much ～; so ～ that … - an excess of feeling or effort that produces an unintended result

～あまり names a cause that went too far. Worry, nerves, delight, haste or overwork build past a reasonable level, and the second half of the sentence reports what that excess brought about - usually something the person did not intend. English reaches for "so worried that…" or "in his haste…"; Japanese puts the excess in a noun-like phrase and hangs the consequence off it.

～あまり · Formation

The excess comes first, あまり closes it, and the result follows after a comma. Almost anything before あまり has to name a feeling, a state or a degree.

Attaches to Form Example
Emotion noun Noun + の + あまり 心配しんぱい → 心配しんぱいのあまり
Degree noun in ～さ ～さ + の + あまり 忙いそがしさ → 忙いそがしさのあまり
Verb, non-past Vる + あまり 急いそぐ → 急いそぐあまり
Verb, past Vた + あまり 緊張きんちょうした → 緊張きんちょうしたあまり
Fronted degree あまりの + Noun／～さ + に 寒さむさ → あまりの寒さむさに
Result clause ～あまり、（unintended outcome） 驚おどろきのあまり、声こえが出でなかった

～あまり · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the second clause reports something beyond the speaker's control: could not sleep, dropped it, forgot, burst into tears, missed the deadline. If the outcome was planned and ordinary, the sentence wants ので or から, not あまり.

Note - あまりの～に is the same idea rearranged, with the degree word turned into a さ-noun: あまりの暑あつさに、外そとに出でられなかった. Learn both shapes; news writing uses them about equally.

～あまり · Examples

心配しんぱいのあまり、昨夜さくやは眠ねむれませんでした。
I was so worried I couldn't sleep last night.

緊張きんちょうのあまり、声こえが出でなかった。
I was so nervous my voice wouldn't come out.

驚おどろきのあまり、手てに持もっていたコップを落おとしてしまった。
I was so startled that I dropped the glass I was holding.

急いそぐあまり、財布さいふを家いえに忘わすれてきてしまった。
In my hurry I went and left my wallet at home.

～あまり · Examples (cont.)

喜よろこびのあまり、彼女かのじょはその場ばで泣なき出だした。
Overcome with joy, she burst into tears on the spot.

忙いそがしさのあまり、友人ゆうじんの誕生日たんじょうびをすっかり忘わすれていました。
Things were so hectic that I completely forgot my friend's birthday.

完璧かんぺきを求もとめるあまり、締しめ切きりに間まに合あわなくなることがある。
Chasing perfection can go so far that you end up missing the deadline.

息子むすこの成績せいせきを気きにするあまり、母親ははおやは毎晩まいばん同おなじ小言こごとを繰くり返かえすようになった。
So preoccupied did she become with her son's grades that his mother started repeating the same scolding every night.

～あまり · Nuance & register

This is a written pattern. It belongs to novels, newspaper reports, formal speeches and essays; in ordinary conversation people say 心配しんぱいで眠ねむれなかった or 心配しんぱいしすぎて眠ねむれなかった. Using 心配しんぱいのあまり with a friend is not wrong, but it sounds as if you are narrating your own life.

The nouns that fit in front of it form a fairly closed set - emotions and pressures: 心配しんぱい, 緊張きんちょう, 驚おどろき, 喜よろこび, 悲かなしみ, 怒いかり, 疲つかれ, 忙いそがしさ, 恥はずかしさ. A neutral noun does not work: ❌雨あめのあまり is not Japanese, because rain is not a degree of anything.

～あまり · Nuance & register (cont.)

The tone is nearly always regretful or cautionary. Even with a positive emotion, the result is an overreaction: joy that ends in tears, enthusiasm that ends in a missed deadline. If the result is simply good news, the sentence needs a different connector - 喜よろこびのあまり合格ごうかくした is incoherent, because passing an exam is not something excess joy causes.

Grammatically, the phrase behaves like an adverbial and does not take は or が. It sits at the head of the sentence, or right after the subject: 母親ははおやは心配しんぱいのあまり….

～あまり · ～あまり vs ～ので

Rule of thumb - ので gives a plain reason. あまり gives a reason that was excessive, and its result is correspondingly abnormal.

心配しんぱいだったので、何度なんども電話でんわしました。
I was worried, so I rang several times.

心配しんぱいのあまり、夜中よなかに何度なんども電話でんわしてしまった。
I was so worried that I ended up ringing over and over in the middle of the night.

The ので version reports reasonable behaviour. The あまり version admits the behaviour got out of hand - which is why てしまう is such a frequent companion. Swap them and you either lose the excess (ので) or accidentally confess to one (あまり).

～あまり · ～あまり vs ～すぎて

Rule of thumb - すぎて is the everyday word for "too much" and attaches to any verb or adjective. あまり is literary and restricted to degrees of feeling and effort.

食たべすぎて、お腹なかが痛いたくなった。
I ate too much and got a stomach ache.

考かんがえすぎる****あまり、何なにも決きめられなくなった。
He thought about it so hard that he could no longer decide anything.

～あまり · ～あまり vs ～すぎて (cont.)

Notice that the second sentence uses both: すぎる supplies the excess and あまり frames it as the cause of the paralysis. That stacking is common and perfectly natural. What you cannot do is put an everyday physical action straight in front of あまり: ❌食たべるあまり asks the reader to treat eating as an emotion.

～あまり · ～あまり vs あまり～ない

Rule of thumb - same word, opposite structures. Something + の／verb + あまり at the head of a clause means "so much that". Bare あまり in front of a negative means "not very". The の and the negative are the tells.

疲つかれのあまり、食欲しょくよくがなくなった。
I was so exhausted that I lost my appetite.

今日きょうはあまり食欲しょくよくがない。
I don't have much appetite today.

～あまり · ～あまり vs あまり～ない (cont.)

The first あまり follows its noun; the second precedes the whole predicate and demands a negative. In speech the two are also stressed differently, and the "not very" one has the casual variants あんまり and あんま, which the ～あまり pattern never takes: ❌疲つかれのあんまり.

～あまり · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 心配しんぱいあまり、電話でんわした。 → ✅ 心配しんぱいのあまり、電話でんわした。
    A noun needs の to join あまり. Only verbs attach directly.

  • ❌ 忙いそがしいあまり、返事へんじが遅おくれました。 → ✅ 忙いそがしさのあまり、返事へんじが遅おくれました。
    An い-adjective has to become a さ-noun first. The pattern wants a degree, and ～さ is what turns an adjective into one.

  • ❌ 嬉うれしさのあまり、予定よていどおり準備じゅんびを進すすめた。 → ✅ 嬉うれしさのあまり、大声おおごえを上あげてしまった。
    The result clause has to be something the excess pushed the person into. An orderly, planned outcome contradicts the pattern.

～あまり · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 時間じかんがあまりのなかった。 → ✅ 時間じかんがあまりなかった。
    The "not very" adverb takes no の and no following noun. If you find yourself writing のあまり in a negative sentence, you have mixed the two patterns.

  • ❌ 雨あめのあまり、試合しあいが中止ちゅうしになった。 → ✅ 雨あめのため、試合しあいが中止ちゅうしになった。
    あまり needs a degree of feeling or effort, not an ordinary event. For a plain cause in written Japanese, use のため.

あまりに

Meaning · too ～; excessively ～ - so far past the normal level that something follows from it

あまりに is a degree adverb, like とても, but with a verdict attached. とても says the level is high; あまりに says the level has gone past where it should be, and the sentence usually goes on to say what that cost. Too expensive to buy, too sudden to answer, too detailed for anyone to remember.

あまりに · Formation

あまりに stands in front of the word whose degree it is measuring. It never follows it.

Attaches to Form Example
い-adjective あまりに + い-adjective 高たかい → あまりに高たかい
な-adjective あまりに + な-adjective 静しずかだ → あまりに静しずかだ
な-adjective + noun あまりに + ～な + Noun 急きゅうな話はなし → あまりに急きゅうな話はなし

Rule of thumb - あまりに is an adverb, so it takes no の and no な of its own. If you want a の after it, the following word must become a noun: あまりに寒さむくて／あまりの寒さむさに.

Note - a bare あんまりだ is a fixed colloquial complaint meaning "that's not fair", used of someone's behaviour: それはあんまりじゃない?. It comes from this same word but works as a な-adjective predicate, not as an adverb.

It was all so sudden that I couldn't find any words.

あまりの人ひとの多おおさに、会場かいじょうに入はいるのをあきらめた。
There were so many people that I gave up on getting into the venue.

新あたらしい規則きそくはあまりにも細こまかくて、全部ぜんぶ覚おぼえられる人ひとはいないでしょう。
The new rules are so fiddly that nobody is going to remember all of them.

あまりに急きゅうなお知しらせだったので、参加さんかできない人ひとも多おおかったようです。
The announcement came at such short notice that a lot of people apparently couldn't attend.

あまりに · Nuance & register

あまりに is emphatic and a little formal. In casual conversation the usual words are すごく, めっちゃ and the ～すぎる ending; あまりに sounds deliberate, and it is at home in writing, in complaints made carefully, and in speech where you want the excess to register.

あまりにも adds another notch of emphasis and is, if anything, more common than the bare form in modern speech. There is no difference in meaning; も simply presses harder.

あまりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The word carries an evaluation, and that evaluation is usually negative: too expensive, too sudden, too strict. It can front a positive adjective, but even then the sentence is reporting a reaction that overwhelmed the speaker - あまりに美うつくしくて、言葉ことばが出でなかった. What it cannot do is state a plain, contented high degree. For "the cake was really delicious", say とても; あまりにおいしかった without a consequence sounds unfinished, as though a "so I…" clause had been cut off.

That expectation of a consequence is the practical point. Most あまりに sentences have a second half - ～て, ～ので, ～ため - or end in an exclamation. When you write one, ask yourself what followed from the excess; if nothing did, you probably wanted とても.

あまりに · あまりに vs とても

Rule of thumb - とても measures. あまりに judges. とても says "very"; あまりに says "more than it should be".

この部屋へやはとても広ひろいですね。
This room is really spacious, isn't it.

この部屋へやはあまりに広ひろくて、掃除そうじが大変たいへんです。
This room is so big that cleaning it is a chore.

あまりに · あまりに vs とても (cont.)

Both sentences agree about the size of the room; only the second one has a problem with it. That is why とても fits compliments and あまりに fits complaints, warnings and excuses. Note too that とても has a second life in front of a negative (とても信しんじられない, "I simply can't believe it") which あまりに does not share.

あまりに · あまりに vs ～すぎる

Rule of thumb - ～すぎる is a verb ending attached to the word itself; あまりに is a separate adverb placed before it. They mean much the same thing, and Japanese happily uses both at once.

この服ふくは大おおきすぎます。
These clothes are too big.

この服ふくはあまりに大おおきすぎます。
These clothes are far too big.

あまりに · あまりに vs ～すぎる (cont.)

すぎる is the everyday choice and the one to reach for in conversation. あまりに is what you add when you want to underline the excess or when the sentence is written. One structural difference matters: すぎる conjugates and can end a sentence on its own, while あまりに cannot - ❌この服ふくはあまりにです is not a sentence.

あまりに · あまりに vs ～あまり

Rule of thumb - あまりに precedes the word it modifies and is an adverb. ～あまり follows a noun (with の) or a verb and introduces a clause. Word order is the whole test.

あまりに緊張きんちょうして、手てが震ふるえた。
I was so nervous my hands shook.

緊張きんちょうのあまり、手てが震ふるえた。
Out of sheer nerves, my hands shook.

あまりに · あまりに vs ～あまり (cont.)

The two sentences mean nearly the same thing, which is why the pair is so easily muddled. The difference is in what each one attaches to: あまりに needs a verb or adjective after it, while あまり needs a noun or verb before it. Get the order backwards - ❌緊張きんちょうにあまり, ❌あまりの緊張きんちょうして - and neither pattern survives.

あまりに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 寒さむいあまりに、外そとに出でなかった。 → ✅ あまりに寒さむくて、外そとに出でなかった。
    あまりに is an adverb and comes first. Putting it after the adjective produces neither pattern.

  • ❌ あまりの寒さむくて眠ねむれなかった。 → ✅ あまりに寒さむくて眠ねむれなかった。／✅ あまりの寒さむさに眠ねむれなかった。
    の has to be followed by a noun. Either keep the adjective and use に, or turn the adjective into a さ-noun and use の.

  • ❌ 時間じかんがあまりにありません。（meaning "I don't have much time"） → ✅ 時間じかんがあまりありません。
    The "not very" adverb is the bare あまり. Adding に switches you to the "too much" word, which does not belong in front of a plain negative.

あまりに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ あまりに静しずか部屋へやで、落おち着つかなかった。 → ✅ あまりに静しずかな部屋へやで、落おち着つかなかった。
    あまりに changes nothing about how the な-adjective attaches to its noun.

  • ❌ ケーキがあまりにおいしかったです。（as a simple compliment） → ✅ ケーキがとてもおいしかったです。
    あまりに expects a consequence. With nothing following, a listener waits for the rest of the sentence.

あまりに · Practice 1

1. 昨日きのうのテストは____難むずかしくて、半分はんぶんも答こたえられなかった。

あまりに（あまりにも）難むずかしくて、半分はんぶんも答こたえられなかった。

The second clause reports the damage the difficulty caused, which is exactly the frame あまりに sets up. とても would be possible but flatter - it would not present the difficulty as beyond what was reasonable.

あまりに · Practice 2

2. Choose: この辞書じしょは（とても・あまりに）重おもくて、持もち歩あるけません。

この辞書じしょはあまりに重おもくて、持もち歩あるけません。

The sentence is a complaint: the weight has crossed the line into unusable. とても would simply rate the dictionary as heavy and would sit oddly with a clause about what you therefore cannot do.

あまりに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The news was so unexpected that I couldn't say anything for a while."

その知しらせはあまりに意外いがいで、しばらく何なにも言いえなかった。

あまりに goes in front of the な-adjective, and the second clause supplies the consequence. あまりにも意外いがいで would be a slightly stronger version of the same sentence.

あまりに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 仕事しごとが忙いそがしいあまりに、返事へんじを書かく時間じかんもなかった。

✅ 仕事しごとがあまりに忙いそがしくて、返事へんじを書かく時間じかんもなかった。 （or ✅ 仕事しごとの忙いそがしさのあまり、…）

あまりに must precede the adjective. If you want the phrase to come after, you need the other pattern - a さ-noun plus の plus あまり.

あまりに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この映画えいがはとても長ながいです。 (b) この映画えいがはあまりにも長ながいです。

(a) reports the running time as a fact and can be perfectly neutral - a long film may be exactly what you wanted.

(b) passes judgement: the length has gone beyond what an audience should be asked to sit through. The も sharpens it further, and a listener will expect you to go on and say what was wrong with sitting there.

～ことか

Meaning · how ～! - an exclamation shaped like a question, for a feeling too big to state plainly

～ことか ends a sentence that looks like a question and expects no answer. The speaker is not asking how worried they were; they are saying the amount cannot be put into words. English does the same trick with "how many times have I told you!" - a question mark would be wrong there too.

～ことか · Formation

The pattern needs two halves: a degree word near the front, and ことか at the end. Without the degree word the sentence collapses into an ordinary question.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb, plain Vる／Vた／Vている + ことか 待まっていた → どれほど待まっていたことか
い-adjective ～い + ことか 嬉うれしい → どんなに嬉うれしいことか
な-adjective ～な + ことか 大変たいへんだ → どんなに大変たいへんなことか
Noun predicate ～な + ことか 幸しあわせ → なんと幸しあわせなことか
Degree words used どんなに／どれほど／どれだけ／何度なんど／何回なんかい／なんと 何度なんど言いったことか
Softer variants ～ことだろう／～ことでしょう どんなに喜よろこぶことでしょう

～ことか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the clause before ことか takes the plain form, however polite the rest of the passage is. Politeness is expressed by switching to ことでしょう, not by inserting です or ます.

Note - ことだろう and ことでしょう are the same exclamation with a softer edge, and they point outward at someone else's feelings: ご家族かぞくはどんなに安心あんしんされたことでしょう. Use ことか for your own feelings and ことでしょう when imagining another person's.

～ことか · Examples

どんなに心配しんぱいしたことか。
You have no idea how worried I was.

この日ひをどれほど待まっていたことか。
How long I've waited for this day.

何度なんど同おなじことを注意ちゅういしたことか。
How many times have I told you the same thing!

一人ひとりで暮くらすのがどんなに大変たいへんなことか、やってみて初はじめて分わかった。
Only when I tried it did I realise how hard living alone is.

～ことか · Examples (cont.)

合格ごうかくの知しらせを聞きいて、両親りょうしんはどんなに喜よろこんだことか。
When they heard I'd passed, how delighted my parents must have been.

留学中りゅうがくちゅう、家族かぞくからの手紙てがみにどれだけ励はげまされたことか。
While I was studying abroad, those letters from home kept me going more than I can say.

あのとき助たすけてもらえなかったら、今いまごろどうなっていたことか。
If nobody had helped me then, goodness knows where I'd be now.

山やまの上うえから見みえる景色けしきの、なんと美うつくしいことか。
How beautiful the view from the mountaintop is.

～ことか · Nuance & register

ことか is emotive and literary. It lives in essays, memoirs, letters, speeches, drama and song lyrics; in everyday conversation it sounds heightened, and younger speakers use it sparingly. That is not a reason to avoid it - it is a reason to notice what you are doing when you say it. Dropping a どんなに心配しんぱいしたことか into a conversation makes the moment weightier, which is sometimes exactly right.

The everyday equivalents are worth having side by side:

～ことか · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • 本当ほんとうに心配しんぱいしたよ - plain and warm.
  • どれだけ心配しんぱいしたと思おもってるの - colloquial, and the
    usual spoken way of scolding someone with the same content.
  • どんなに心配しんぱいしたことか - the written, weightier version.

The emotion is usually one of two kinds: long-suffering (worry, waiting, repeated warnings ignored) or overflowing gratitude and relief. Both are retrospective, which is why the verb before ことか is so often in the past or the ～ている form.

～ことか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Punctuation is a small giveaway. Because the sentence is not a real question, it is normally written with a full stop rather than a question mark, and it is said with falling intonation. If you catch yourself waiting for an answer, you have misread it.

～ことか · ～ことか vs ～だろう

Rule of thumb - だろう guesses. ことか exclaims. だろう is uncertain about a fact; ことか is certain about a feeling and only uncertain about how to measure it.

彼かれはもう着ついただろう。
He's probably arrived by now.

彼かれの到着とうちゃくをどれほど待まったことか。
How long we waited for him to arrive.

～ことか · ～ことか vs ～だろう (cont.)

The halfway house is ことだろう, which keeps the exclamation and adds a guess about someone else's inner state: お母かあさんはどんなに悲かなしんだことだろう - "how his mother must have grieved." Note that a plain conjecture cannot take ことか: ❌明日あしたは雨あめが降ふることか is not Japanese, because there is no emotion being measured.

～ことか · ～ことか vs ～ものか

Rule of thumb - both are rhetorical questions, and they do opposite things. ことか says "so much!". ものか says "not a chance!" - it is a flat refusal or denial.

どんなに感謝かんしゃしていることか。
I'm more grateful than I can say.

あんな店みせ、二度にどと行いくものか。
As if I'd ever go back to that place.

ものか attaches to the plain non-past and needs no degree word; its casual spoken form is もんか, and it is distinctly rough. Mixing the two up reverses your meaning entirely, so check which of "how much!" and "no way!" you mean before you choose.

～ことか · ～ことか vs なんて

Rule of thumb - same feeling, different register and different position. なんて fronts the exclamation and is ordinary spoken Japanese; ことか closes the sentence and is written.

なんてきれいな海うみなんだろう。
What a beautiful sea.

なんときれいな海うみであることか。
How beautiful this sea is.

～ことか · ～ことか vs なんて (cont.)

The first is what you would actually say standing on the shore. The second belongs in a travel essay about standing on the shore. Note also that なんて has a second, unrelated use meaning "things like ～" (運動うんどうなんて嫌きらいだ), which ことか never has.

～ことか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 心配しんぱいしたことか。 → ✅ どんなに心配しんぱいしたことか。
    Without a degree word there is nothing for the exclamation to measure, and the sentence reads as a broken question.

  • ❌ どんなに嬉うれしかったですことか。 → ✅ どんなに嬉うれしかったことか。／✅ どんなに嬉うれしかったことでしょう。
    The clause before ことか stays plain. For a polite register, switch the ending to ことでしょう.

  • ❌ どんなに大変たいへんだことか。 → ✅ どんなに大変たいへんなことか。
    A な-adjective keeps its な in front of こと, as it does before any noun.

～ことか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 来週らいしゅうは忙いそがしいことか。（meaning "next week will probably be busy"） → ✅ 来週らいしゅうは忙いそがしいだろう。
    ことか is not a conjecture ending. Use it only where a strong feeling is being exclaimed over.

  • ❌ 「どんなに心配しんぱいしたことか。」「三日みっかぐらいです。」 → the question is rhetorical; it takes no answer.
    Treat it as a statement with an exclamation behind it, not as something to reply to.

ことか needs a degree word to exclaim over, and どんなに is the most common one. The sentence says the longing was beyond measuring, which the following line confirms.

The speaker is refusing outright - "I'd never ask him again" - which is the job of ものか. ことか would turn the sentence into an exclamation about how much they ask him, the opposite of what is meant.

何度なんど supplies the degree, and the past-tense verb before ことか gives the pattern its long-suffering tone. In casual speech the same complaint comes out as 何度なんど言いったと思おもってるの.

～ことか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 子こどもが生うまれたとき、両親りょうしんはどんなに幸しあわせだことか。

✅ 子こどもが生うまれたとき、両親りょうしんはどんなに幸しあわせなことか。 （more natural still: …どんなに幸しあわせだったことでしょう。）

幸しあわせ is a な-word, so it needs な before こと. Because the feeling belongs to someone else, ことでしょう fits the sentence even better than ことか.

～ことか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 祖母そぼにどれだけ助たすけられたことか。 (b) 祖母そぼにとても助たすけられました。

(a) presents the help as beyond counting and asks the reader to feel it. It is written, emotional and slightly formal, and it works well in a letter or a piece of reflective writing.

(b) simply reports that the help was considerable. It is polite, plain and unremarkable - the right choice in conversation, where (a) would sound as if you were delivering a eulogy.

～ことに

Meaning · to my surprise / delight / regret - the speaker's reaction, stated before the news itself

～ことに puts your feeling first and the fact second. 驚おどろいたことに、彼かれはもう帰かえっていた - "to my astonishment, he had already gone home." The listener is told how to receive the news before they hear it, which is exactly what English does with "sadly," "remarkably," "to my relief."

～ことに · Formation

The slot in front of ことに takes a word of evaluation or emotion, in the form it would use to modify a noun. The clause after the comma states a fact.

Attaches to Form Example
い-adjective ～い + ことに 嬉うれしい → 嬉うれしいことに
な-adjective ～な + ことに 残念ざんねんだ → 残念ざんねんなことに
Verb of reaction, past Vた + ことに 驚おどろく → 驚おどろいたことに
With a topic marker ～ことに + は 困こまった → 困こまったことには
Usual い-adjectives 嬉うれしい／悲かなしい／ありがたい／面白おもしろい／おかしい ありがたいことに
Usual な-adjectives 残念ざんねん／不思議ふしぎ／幸さいわい／意外いがい 幸さいわいなことに
Usual verbs 驚おどろいた／困こまった／がっかりした がっかりしたことに

～ことに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the second clause must be a statement of fact. A request, an invitation or a command cannot follow: ❌嬉うれしいことに、一緒いっしょに行いきましょう. The pattern comments on news; it does not introduce a proposal.

Note - the reaction belongs to the speaker (or, in a story, to the narrator or the viewpoint character). You cannot use it to report someone else's feeling about their own news; for that, Japanese says 彼かれは驚おどろいたようで、….

～ことに · Examples

驚おどろいたことに、彼かれはもう帰かえっていた。
To my surprise, he had already gone home.

残念ざんねんなことに、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしになりました。
Sadly, the match was called off.

不思議ふしぎなことに、同おなじ夢ゆめを三日みっか続つづけて見みた。
Strangely enough, I had the same dream three nights running.

ありがたいことに、近所きんじょの人ひとが荷物にもつを預あずかってくれていた。
Thankfully, a neighbour had taken the parcel in for me.

～ことに · Examples (cont.)

困こまったことに、会場かいじょうの鍵かぎを持もっている人ひとと連絡れんらくが取とれません。
Awkwardly, we can't get hold of the person who has the key to the venue.

面白おもしろいことに、同おなじ質問しつもんをしても年代ねんだいによって答こたえが全まったく違ちがった。
Interestingly, the same question got completely different answers depending on the age group.

幸さいわいなことに、地震じしんのとき家族かぞくは全員ぜんいん外そとにいて、けがはありませんでした。
Fortunately my whole family was outside when the earthquake struck, and nobody was hurt.

情なさけないことに、一年いちねんかけて準備じゅんびしたのに、本番ほんばんでは名前なまえすら言いえなかった。
Embarrassingly, after a year of preparation I couldn't even get my name out on the day.

～ことに · Nuance & register

The pattern leans written. It is a staple of essays, reports, travel writing and news features, where it does the work of an English sentence adverb. In speech it is perfectly usable and does not sound stiff, but conversation more often reaches for the plain adverbs 幸さいわい, あいにく, 残念ざんねんながら, or simply for びっくりした! followed by the news.

Two combinations have drifted into everyday speech on their own: 困こまったことに, which introduces a problem you are still standing in, and ありがたいことに, which is a modest, slightly formal way of acknowledging help. Both are safe at work.

～ことに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Adding は - 困こまったことには - makes the phrase heavier and more literary. It is the same pattern with the contrastive は doing its usual job of setting this circumstance against the rest, and you will meet it far more often in print than in conversation.

Because the emotion is stated up front, the pattern has a mild framing effect: it tells the listener how to take what follows, which is why journalists like it and why it can sound editorial. If you want the facts to speak for themselves, leave it out.

～ことに · ～ことに vs ～ことにする / ～ことになる

Rule of thumb - look at what こと is followed by. ことに、 with a comma and a following clause is a comment. ことにする is a decision and ことになる is an arrangement - both of them verbs, both attached to a plain-form verb rather than to an emotion word.

来月らいげつから大阪おおさかで働はたらくことになりました。
It's been decided that I'll be working in Osaka from next month.

嬉うれしいことに、来月らいげつから大阪おおさかで働はたらけることになりました。
Happily, it's been settled that I can work in Osaka from next month.

～ことに · ～ことに vs ～ことにする / ～ことになる (cont.)

The second sentence contains both, which is the clearest way to see the difference: 嬉うれしいことに is the speaker's reaction, and ことになりました is the arrangement being reacted to. An emotion word can never take ことにする: ❌嬉うれしいことにしました would have to mean "I decided on happiness."

～ことに · ～ことに vs 幸い・あいにく・残念ながら

Rule of thumb - the adverbs are shorter, more spoken, and fixed in form. The ことに frame is open: any evaluation word can go into it, including ones that have no adverb of their own.

あいにく、担当者たんとうしゃは席せきを外はずしております。
Unfortunately the person in charge is away from their desk.

情なさけないことに、自分じぶんの傘かさをまた電車でんしゃに忘わすれた。
Pathetically, I've left my umbrella on the train again.

～ことに · ～ことに vs 幸い・あいにく・残念ながら (cont.)

There is no single adverb for "pathetically" in this sense, and that is the frame's value: it manufactures one on demand. Where a ready-made adverb exists, prefer it in speech - あいにく品切しなぎれです is what a shop assistant says, not 残念ざんねんなことに品切しなぎれです.

～ことに · ～ことに vs ～て

Rule of thumb - 驚おどろいて makes the surprise a cause of what follows. 驚おどろいたことに makes it a comment on what follows. The て-form joins two events; ことに joins a feeling to a fact.

驚おどろいて、声こえを上あげてしまった。
I was so startled that I cried out.

驚おどろいたことに、窓まどが開あいたままだった。
To my surprise, the window had been left open.

In the first sentence the surprise causes the shout; in the second it causes nothing at all - it merely tells you how the open window struck the speaker. That is why the て-form sentence needs an action after it, while the ことに sentence needs a state of affairs.

～ことに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 残念ざんねんことに、来こられませんでした。 → ✅ 残念ざんねんなことに、来こられませんでした。
    A な-adjective keeps its な before こと. The same goes for 不思議ふしぎな and 幸さいわいな.

  • ❌ 驚おどろくことに、値段ねだんが半分はんぶんになっていた。 → ✅ 驚おどろいたことに、値段ねだんが半分はんぶんになっていた。
    With a verb of reaction the form is the past. The reaction has already happened by the time you report it.

  • ❌ 嬉うれしいことに、一緒いっしょに飲のみに行いきませんか。 → ✅ 嬉うれしいことに、彼かれも来きてくれるそうです。
    The clause after the comma has to be a fact. Invitations, requests and commands cannot follow.

～ことに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんは嬉うれしいことに、試験しけんに合格ごうかくしました。 → ✅ 嬉うれしいことに、田中たなかさんが試験しけんに合格ごうかくしました。
    The feeling is the speaker's, so the phrase should not be tucked in behind another person's topic where it looks like theirs.

  • ❌ 来週らいしゅう休やすむ嬉うれしいことにしました。 → ✅ 来週らいしゅう休やすむことにしました。
    ことにする is a separate pattern that attaches to a plain verb and means "decide to". Nothing emotional goes in front of it.

～ことに · Practice 1

1. ____ことに、財布さいふは交番こうばんに届とどけられていました。

ありがたい（幸さいわいな）ことに、財布さいふは交番こうばんに届とどけられていました。

The news is good, so the slot takes a word of relief or gratitude. Any evaluation word fits the frame as long as it is in its noun-modifying form - ありがたい unchanged, 幸さいわい with な.

～ことに · Practice 2

2. Choose: 来年らいねんから支店してんに移うつる（ことに・ことになり）ました。

来年らいねんから支店してんに移うつることになりました。

This is the arrangement pattern attached to a plain verb, not the comment pattern. ことに as taught on this page follows an emotion word and is followed by a comma and a clause, never by ます.

～ことに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Unfortunately, the shop had already closed."

残念ざんねんなことに、店みせはもう閉しまっていました。

残念ざんねんなことに puts the speaker's disappointment in front of the fact. In speech あいにく店みせはもう閉しまっていました is the more idiomatic version of the same sentence.

～ことに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 驚おどろくことに、みんなで写真しゃしんを撮とりましょう。

✅ 驚おどろいたことに、みんながもう集あつまっていました。

Two things were wrong. The verb needs its past form, 驚おどろいたことに, and the second clause cannot be an invitation - the pattern comments on facts, so 撮とりましょう has to be replaced by a statement.

～ことに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 困こまったことに、電車でんしゃが止とまっている。 (b) 電車でんしゃが止とまっていて、困こまっている。

(a) frames the news: before you hear about the trains, you are told that this is a problem. It reads as narration or as a report to a colleague, and it keeps a little distance from the speaker's own state.

(b) puts the same content the other way round - the stopped trains are the cause and the speaker's difficulty is the result. It is plainer, more conversational, and more directly about how the speaker is doing right now.
