～だけ - as much as

Meaning · as much as ～, as many as ～ - up to the limit of what is possible, wanted, or available

This is the same だけ you already know as "only", pointed the other way. Instead of shrinking the amount ("only three"), it names a ceiling and then fills it: as much as you like, as fast as I can, everything there was. The word is identical; what changes it is the kind of word in front of it.

～だけ - as much as · Formation

The extent reading appears when だけ follows a word that names a capacity, a desire, or a stock - a potential form, ～たい, ある, or 好(す)きな. Very often the same verb is repeated on both sides of it.

～だけ - as much as · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - repeat the verb (買(か)えるだけ買(か)う) and the "as much as" reading is unmistakable. Native speakers reach for that doubling precisely because it removes the ambiguity with "only".

Note - が and を behave as they do with ordinary だけ: they are dropped. 好(す)きなだけ取(と)ってください, not ❌好(す)きなだけを取(と)ってください. Other particles survive: 話(はな)したいだけ話(はな)したあとで.

～だけ - as much as · Nuance & register

The pattern itself is register-neutral: できるだけ早(はや)く is at home in a business email and in a text message alike. What varies is the flavour the surrounding words give it.

好(す)きなだけ and Vたいだけ are generous or indulgent when you say them to someone else - "help yourself" - and slightly self-critical or exasperated when you say them about someone: 言(い)いたいだけ言(い)う carries a clear "and never mind the consequences." Tone of voice does the rest.

できるだけ is the workhorse. It attaches to an adverb (できるだけ早(はや)く, できるだけ安(やす)く) or to a whole clause (できるだけ参加(さんか)するようにします) and appears constantly in polite requests and commitments. Its stiffer written cousin is できる限(かぎ)り, and in formal writing you also meet 可能(かのう)な限(かぎ)り.

～だけ - as much as · Nuance & register (cont.)

あるだけ and 残(のこ)っているだけ imply the stock is now exhausted, which is why they so often pair with 全部(ぜんぶ). If you do not mean "and that emptied it", say たくさん instead.

One structural point worth noticing: because だけ here measures an amount, これだけ・それだけ・あれだけ mean "this much" rather than "only this" - あれだけ注意(ちゅうい)したのに, "after all those warnings." Beginners read those as "only" and get the sentence backwards.

～だけ - as much as · ～だけ "as much as" vs ～だけ "only"

Rule of thumb - look at what sits in front of だけ. A fixed thing (a number, a noun, a plain dictionary verb) gives "only". A capacity or a wish (potential form, ～たい, ある, 好(す)きな) gives "as much as".

一(ひと)つだけ買(か)った。
I bought only one.

買(か)えるだけ買(か)った。
I bought as many as I could.

The second sentence does not say the amount was small - it may have been a trolley-load. It says the amount ran up against a limit.

The trap is the plain dictionary form, which stays on the "only" side:

～だけ - as much as · ～だけ "as much as" vs ～だけ "only" (cont.)

見(み)るだけにします。
I'll just look. (only looking, not buying)

Put that verb into its potential form and the meaning flips completely: 見(み)られるだけ見(み)ておこう, "let's see as much of it as we can."

～だけ - as much as · できるだけ vs なるべく

Rule of thumb - できるだけ aims at the maximum: as much as is possible. なるべく aims at a preference: if it can be managed. なるべく is the gentler of the two in a request.

なるべく現金(げんきん)でお願(ねが)いします。
Cash if at all possible, please.

できるだけ現金(げんきん)でお願(ねが)いします。
Please pay in cash as far as you possibly can.

Asking a colleague to hurry, なるべく leaves them room; できるだけ tells them to push. Neither is impolite, but a string of できるだけ requests starts to sound demanding, and なるべく never does.

～だけ - as much as · ～だけ vs ～かぎり

Rule of thumb - they overlap on ability and stock (できるだけ／できる限(かぎ)り), and かぎり is the more formal and more emphatic of the two. But かぎり also works on knowledge and range, where だけ cannot follow at all.

私(わたし)の知(し)っている限(かぎ)り、その店(みせ)はまだ営業(えいぎょう)しています。
As far as I know, that shop is still open.

❌知(し)っているだけ cannot mean "as far as I know" - it means "only knowing (and not acting)". The same goes for 見(み)わたす限(かぎ)り "as far as the eye can see" and 命(いのち)のある限(かぎ)り "as long as I live". Keep だけ for countable amounts and effort, and let かぎり handle scope.

～だけ - as much as · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 好(す)きだけ取(と)ってください。 → ✅ 好(す)きなだけ取(と)ってください。
    好(す)き is a な-adjective, so it needs な before だけ - the same rule as ordinary だけ.

  • ❌ 好(す)きなだけを取(と)ってください。 → ✅ 好(す)きなだけ取(と)ってください。
    を and が drop after だけ. Leaving them in sounds like a translation.

  • ❌ 走(はし)るだけ走(はし)った。（meaning "I ran as far as I could"） → ✅ 走(はし)れるだけ走(はし)った。
    A plain dictionary form gives the "only" reading. The extent meaning needs the potential form, the ～たい form, or ある.

  • ❌ できるだけに早(はや)く送(おく)ります。 → ✅ できるだけ早(はや)く送(おく)ります。
    できるだけ is already adverbial. Nothing goes between it and the word it modifies.

～だけ - as much as · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 好(す)きなほど食(た)べてください。 → ✅ 好(す)きなだけ食(た)べてください。
    ほど measures a degree by comparison, not a helping. An invitation to take as much as you want is always だけ.

The sentence gives a reason - there is no time - and then promises effort up to a limit, which is the "as much as" reading. 一(ひと)つだけ would be grammatical but would mean "I'll prepare only one thing", a very different promise.

～だけ - as much as · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 話(はな)したいだけ話(はな)してから帰(かえ)った。 (b) 話(はな)すだけ話(はな)して帰(かえ)った。

(a) is the extent reading: they talked until they had had their fill, and only then left. The measure is their own appetite for talking, and the sentence can sound either satisfied or faintly exasperated depending on the context.

(b) is the "only" reading with a common twist: they did the talking and nothing else - no listening, no helping - and left. That 話(はな)すだけ話(はな)して shape almost always carries a critical edge: "all they did was talk."

～きり

Meaning · only; just that much - and, after a past-tense verb, "and that was the last of it"

きり limits, like だけ does, but it leaves a mark that だけ does not: a sense of finality. 二人(ふたり)きり is not merely two people, it is two people and nobody else was let in. 出(で)かけたきり is not merely "went out", it is "went out, and whatever should have happened next never did."

～きり · Formation

きり follows a noun, a counter, or a past-tense verb. Note the asymmetry: as a limiter it takes nouns; as "and that was the last of it" it takes ～た, never the dictionary form.

Attaches to Form Example

Counter (number of times) Counter + きり 一度(いちど) → 一度(いちど)きり
Demonstrative これ／それ／あれ + きり それ → それきり

～きり · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - after Vた, the second clause is nearly always negative. Vたきり exists in order to say that the expected sequel did not arrive: went out → did not come back, borrowed → did not return it, met once → have not met since.

Note - 寝(ね)たきり has hardened into an ordinary noun meaning "bedridden": 寝(ね)たきりになる, 寝(ね)たきりの老人(ろうじん). The same freezing happened to つきっきり, "staying right beside someone the whole time."

～きり · Examples

彼女(かのじょ)に会(あ)ったのは一度(いちど)きりです。
I've met her exactly once.

兄(あに)は朝(あさ)出(で)かけたきり、まだ帰(かえ)ってきません。
My brother went out this morning and still isn't back.

貸(か)した本(ほん)は、それきり返(かえ)ってこなかった。
That book I lent out never came back.

財布(さいふ)には千円(せんえん)きりしか入(はい)っていなかった。
There was only a thousand yen in my wallet.

～きり · Examples (cont.)

My grandfather has been bedridden for three years.

Apart from saying hello at the entrance ceremony, I've hardly spoken to anyone in my class.

My son shut himself in his room and didn't come out even when dinner time came.

～きり · Nuance & register

きり is spoken Japanese first. It is perfectly usable in polite speech - 一度(いちど)きりです, 出(で)かけたきりです - but formal writing prefers だけ, のみ, or an explicit それ以来(いらい) for the "since then" idea.

The doubled form っきり is a straight intensifier and a clear register marker: 二人(ふたり)っきり, これっきり, それっきり. It is warm and casual with friends and slightly childish or blunt in a work setting. In particular, これっきりにして ("that's the last time - stop it") is a telling-off, not a neutral request.

～きり · Nuance & register (cont.)

The limiting きり on nouns has narrowed over the last century. In modern Japanese it is alive mainly in set combinations - 二人(ふたり)きり, 一人(ひとり)きり, 一度(いちど)きり, 一回(いっかい)きり, これきり, それきり - and it sounds dated or regional in freer combinations. 千円(せんえん)きりしかない is still heard, and it is colloquial and a touch old-fashioned; if in doubt, plain 千円(せんえん)しかない is what most speakers say today.

Emotionally, きり leans toward shortage and loss. 一度(いちど)きり of a chance means "one shot only, so don't waste it". 二人(ふたり)きり can be romantic or claustrophobic depending on the story, but it is never neutral - it always says that the rest of the world has been shut out.

～きり · きり vs だけ

Rule of thumb - だけ draws a boundary and reports it. きり draws the same boundary and adds "and nothing further" - that is either not enough, or the end of the matter.

会(あ)ったのは一度(いちど)だけです。
I've met them once.

会(あ)ったのは一度(いちど)きりです。
I've met them just the once - and that was that.

The だけ sentence is a plain count; you could follow it with "so we're meeting again next week". The きり sentence closes the door, so a follow-up meeting would sound like a contradiction.

～きり · きり vs だけ (cont.)

だけ is also far more flexible. It attaches to verbs in the dictionary form, to adjectives, and to desires (好(す)きなだけ); きり does none of these. Conversely, だけ cannot do the Vたきり job at all: ❌出(で)かけただけ帰(かえ)ってこない is simply broken.

～きり · ～たきり vs ～たまま

Rule of thumb - ～たまま describes a state left unchanged. ～たきり describes a sequel that never came. まま looks at the thing; きり looks at what should have happened next and didn't.

窓(まど)を開(あ)けたまま出(で)かけてしまった。
I went out and left the window open.

窓(まど)を開(あ)けたきり、一度(いちど)も閉(し)めていない。
I opened the window and haven't closed it once since.

Both are true of the same window, but they are aimed differently. The まま sentence says how the window is; the きり sentence complains about the closing that never happened. That is why a きり clause pulls a negative in behind it, while a まま clause happily takes a positive one.

～きり · きり vs しか～ない

Rule of thumb - しか～ない requires a negative predicate and states a shortfall. きり takes a positive predicate and states a limit. They can be stacked (きりしか～ない) for emphasis, but neither one replaces the other.

部屋(へや)には二人(ふたり)しかいませんでした。
There were only two people in the room.

部屋(へや)で二人(ふたり)きりで話(はな)しました。
We talked in the room, just the two of us.

The しか version counts heads and finds them few. The きり version is about privacy, and it needs a positive verb - ❌二人(ふたり)きりで話(はな)しませんでした would have to mean "we didn't talk alone", which is not what the pattern is for.

～きり · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 出(で)かけるきり、帰(かえ)ってこない。 → ✅ 出(で)かけたきり、帰(かえ)ってこない。
    The "and that was the last of it" reading is built on the past form. A dictionary form before きり does not work.

  • ❌ 家(いえ)を出(で)たきり、三時間(さんじかん)後(ご)に帰(かえ)ってきた。 → ✅ 家(いえ)を出(で)たきり、帰(かえ)ってこない。
    Vたきり announces that the sequel is missing, so a second clause reporting that it did happen contradicts the pattern. Use ～てから for a plain sequence.

  • ❌ 好(す)きなきり食(た)べてください。 → ✅ 好(す)きなだけ食(た)べてください。
    きり cannot follow an adjective or express a generous amount. It limits; it does not invite.

  • ❌ 二人(ふたり)きりしか話(はな)しました。 → ✅ 二人(ふたり)きりで話(はな)しました。
    しか demands a negative verb. If the verb is positive, きり stands on its own, usually followed by で.

～きり · Practice 5

～さえ

Meaning · even ～ (of all things); and, with ば, "if only ～ - that alone is enough"

さえ picks the most extreme member of a scale and says the statement holds even there. If even a child knows it, everyone knows it; if she cannot even drink water, she certainly cannot eat. The same word has a second life in さえ～ば, which turns that extreme into a minimum: one condition, and nothing else, is all that is required.

～さえ · Formation

さえ is a focus particle. It sits where the noun's own particle would sit, and が and を are pushed out.

～さえ · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - さえ on its own means "even". さえ with a ば clause behind it means "if only". The ば is what switches the meaning, so look for it before you translate.

Note - the conditional half of さえ～ば is normally ば, or なら after a noun. Pairing さえ with たら is much rarer and many speakers avoid it, so keep to 時間(じかん)さえあれば rather than 時間(じかん)さえあったら until you have heard otherwise.

Provided the weather holds, tomorrow's match will go ahead as scheduled.

～さえ · Nuance & register

さえ is more emphatic and more written than the everyday も. In relaxed conversation people say 子(こ)どもも知(し)っている; さえ raises the volume and is common in complaints, in news writing, and in argument. It is not stiff, though - nobody will blink at it in speech, and これさえあれば is thoroughly colloquial.

でさえ is さえ with the noun's で kept in place, and it is the usual choice with people presented as authorities or as extreme cases: 先生(せんせい)でさえ間違(まちが)える. The plain 先生(せんせい)さえ間違(まちが)える is also correct; でさえ is a little heavier and a little more formal.

すら is the literary sibling. It means the same as this さえ and appears in essays, editorials and formal speeches - 挨拶(あいさつ)すらしない. It sounds bookish in casual conversation, and it has no すら～ば conditional use, so it is not a drop-in replacement.

～さえ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Emotionally, the "even" さえ almost always leans negative. It turns up with negative verbs and with complaints far more often than with happy news, because its job is to show that a floor has been breached: not even water, not even a greeting, not even the specialists. さえ～ば, by contrast, is optimistic and often relieved - "one thing, and we're fine."

～さえ · さえ vs も

Rule of thumb - both mark an unexpected member, but も is the neutral, everyday word and さえ is the scale-topping one. さえ says "this is the last case you would expect, and even it holds." も can also mean plain "also"; さえ never can.

子(こ)どもも楽(たの)しめる映画(えいが)です。
It's a film children can enjoy too.

子(こ)どもさえ楽(たの)しめる映画(えいが)です。
It's a film that even children can enjoy.

The first adds children to the audience. The second uses children as the hardest case and implies that adults are covered a fortiori. Swap them the wrong way round and you get the classic error: ❌私(わたし)さえ行(い)きたいです for "I want to go too" - that says "even I, of all people, want to go."

～さえ · さえ vs まで

Rule of thumb - さえ marks a low bar that was not cleared, or a hardest case that still holds. まで marks a range that was extended further than expected - "went so far as to."

挨拶(あいさつ)さえしてくれなかった。
He didn't even say hello.

彼(かれ)は部長(ぶちょう)にまで文句(もんく)を言(い)った。
He went so far as to complain to the department head.

The さえ sentence points downward: greeting is the minimum, and the minimum was missing. The まで sentence points upward: complaining to colleagues would be normal, and he pushed past that. Where the sentence is about something falling short, さえ is nearly always the one you want.

～さえ · さえ～ば vs plain ～ば

Rule of thumb - plain ば states a condition. さえ～ば states the only condition: satisfy this one thing and nothing else matters.

雨(あめ)が降(ふ)らなければ、出発(しゅっぱつ)します。
If it doesn't rain, we'll set off.

雨(あめ)さえ降(ふ)らなければ、出発(しゅっぱつ)します。
As long as it doesn't rain, we'll set off.

The second sentence dismisses everything else - wind, cold, a late start - as irrelevant. That is why さえ～ば is the natural pattern for reassurance (体(からだ)さえ丈夫(じょうぶ)なら、何(なん)とかなる) and for regret when the condition was not met (もう少(すこ)し早(はや)く出(で)さえすれば、間(ま)に合(あ)ったのに).

～さえ · さえ～ば vs plain ～ば (cont.)

Note the verb form: with a verb, the pattern splits the verb open - 押(お)しさえすれば, 言(い)いさえすれば - putting さえ after the ます-stem and letting する carry the conditional.

～さえ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 水(みず)をさえ飲(の)めない。 → ✅ 水(みず)さえ飲(の)めない。
    が and を are replaced by さえ, not stacked with it. に, で, と and から are kept: 親(おや)にさえ.

  • ❌ ボタンを押(お)すさえすれば動(うご)きます。 → ✅ ボタンを押(お)しさえすれば動(うご)きます。
    With a verb, さえ follows the ます-stem, and する takes the conditional ending.

  • ❌ 私(わたし)さえ参加(さんか)したいです。（meaning "I'd like to join too"） → ✅ 私(わたし)も参加(さんか)したいです。
    さえ is not "too". Using it here says you are the least likely person to want to come.

  • ❌ 一度(いちど)も見(み)さえいない。 → ✅ 一度(いちど)も見(み)てさえいない。
    Attached to a verb in this sense, さえ follows the て-form.

～さえ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ お金(かね)さえあったら、買(か)いたいです。 → ✅ お金(かね)さえあれば、買(か)います。
    The さえ～ば frame wants ば (or なら after a noun), and its main clause states a result rather than a wish.

～こそ

Meaning · this one and no other; precisely this - emphasis on the word in front of it

こそ has no English word behind it. It does not add information and it does not change what the sentence claims; it takes one element and presses on it, so that "this year I'll do it" becomes "this year I'll do it". The most useful way to learn it is by what it replaces: こそ pushes は and が out of the sentence and stands in their place, carrying their grammatical job plus an insistence they do not have.

～こそ · Formation

こそ goes straight after the element it emphasises. What that element is matters less than what particle it was wearing before.

Attaches to Form Example

Noun + another particle に／で／と／から + こそ あなたに → あなたにこそ話(はな)したい

Rule of thumb - こそ never stacks with が. ❌これがこそ does not exist. With は the picture is softer: は normally drops, but 今度(こんど)こそは and これこそは are heard when a speaker wants to lean on the point even harder.

Note - ～てこそ is a different shape with a related job: it says the result holds only by way of the action. 自分(じぶん)でやってこそ意味(いみ)がある, "it only means something if you do it yourself." Its second clause is positive; for the negative version Japanese uses ～なければ.

～こそ · Examples

This time I really want to win.

「ありがとうございました。」「いいえ、こちらこそ。」
"Thank you very much." "No, thank you."

これこそ私(わたし)がずっと探(さが)していた辞書(じしょ)です。
This is exactly the dictionary I've been looking for all this time.

今(いま)こそ行動(こうどう)すべきときだ。
Now is precisely the time to act.

失敗(しっぱい)した人(ひと)にこそ、話(はな)を聞(き)いてみたい。

～こそ · Examples (cont.)

親(おや)は心配(しんぱい)だからこそ、うるさいことを言(い)うのです。
Parents nag precisely because they're worried.

毎日(まいにち)少(すこ)しずつ続(つづ)けてこそ、力(ちから)がつくのだと思(おも)います。
I think it's only by keeping at it a little every day that you build any strength.

来年(らいねん)こそ家族(かぞく)そろって旅行(りょこう)に行(い)こうと話(はな)しています。
We keep saying that next year, for sure, we'll all get away together as a family.

～こそ · Nuance & register

こそ is emphatic and slightly formal in free use. Speeches, editorials, slogans and advertising love it - 今(いま)こそ, これこそ, 本物(ほんもの)こそ - and in ordinary conversation too much of it sounds declamatory, as if you were addressing a room rather than a friend.

That said, several こそ phrases are completely everyday and you should simply learn them whole:

  • こちらこそ - the standard reply that turns thanks or an apology back
    on the speaker. It is polite, warm and used at every register.
  • 今度(こんど)こそ - "this time for real", after previous attempts
    failed.
  • だからこそ - "that is exactly why", used to push back on someone who
    drew the opposite conclusion.
  • 今年(ことし)こそ - the particle of New Year's resolutions.

～こそ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because こそ insists, it very often arrives in the middle of a disagreement or a correction. It implies that some other candidate was in play and is being set aside: 今度(こんど)こそ implies the earlier attempts, これこそ implies the things that were not it. If nothing is being set aside, こそ sounds strange and a plain が or は is what you want.

One thing こそ does not do is change the truth of the sentence. 山田(やまだ)さんが担当(たんとう)です and 山田(やまだ)さんこそ担当(たんとう)にふさわしい both leave Yamada in the job; only the speaker's investment differs. Learners who translate こそ as "only" or "must" end up asserting more than the Japanese does.

～こそ · こそ vs が

Rule of thumb - が identifies: it fills the empty slot in "which one?". こそ insists: it argues for one candidate against others already in the listener's mind. Answering a question takes が; arguing takes こそ.

私(わたし)が担当(たんとう)です。
I'm the one in charge.

私(わたし)にこそ責任(せきにん)があります。
The responsibility is mine, and mine above all.

Asked どなたが担当(たんとう)ですか, you answer with が; replying 私(わたし)こそ担当(たんとう)です would sound like a protest against someone else's claim to the role. The reverse holds too: in a heated exchange, あなたこそ - "you're the one who…" - cannot be replaced by が without losing the whole point.

～こそ · こそ vs は

Rule of thumb - は sets its noun quietly against alternatives. こそ sets it loudly against alternatives and rules them out. こそ occupies the same slot, so you never write both.

今年(ことし)は禁煙(きんえん)します。
This year I'm going to quit smoking.

今年(ことし)こそ禁煙(きんえん)します。
This year I'm really going to quit smoking.

Both sentences make the same promise. The は version compares this year with other years neutrally; the こそ version admits that previous attempts failed and stakes a claim on this one. That admission is why こそ fits resolutions, rematches and second chances so well - and why it sounds odd on a first attempt at anything.

～こそ · ～からこそ vs ～から

Rule of thumb - から gives a reason. からこそ gives the real reason, usually correcting a wrong one the listener has assumed.

時間(じかん)がないから、手伝(てつだ)えません。
I can't help, because I don't have time.

大切(たいせつ)な友(とも)だちだからこそ、はっきり言(い)うんです。
It's precisely because you're a good friend of mine that I'm being blunt.

The second sentence answers an unspoken "if we're friends, why are you being so harsh?" That defensive shape is typical: からこそ almost always appears where the listener might have expected the opposite conclusion. Note also that a からこそ sentence usually closes with のだ／んです, because it is explaining rather than reporting.

～こそ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ これがこそ本物(ほんもの)です。 → ✅ これこそ本物(ほんもの)です。
    こそ takes the place of が; the two never appear together. に, で, と and から do survive in front of it.

  • ❌ どれこそあなたの傘(かさ)ですか。 → ✅ どれがあなたの傘(かさ)ですか。
    A question word is an unknown, and こそ exists to insist on a known candidate. Question words take が.

  • ❌ 毎日(まいにち)練習(れんしゅう)するこそ、上手(じょうず)になる。 → ✅ 毎日(まいにち)練習(れんしゅう)してこそ、上手(じょうず)になる。
    With a verb, こそ follows the て-form. A dictionary form plus こそ is not a pattern.

  • ❌ 「どなたが司会(しかい)ですか。」「私(わたし)こそです。」 → ✅ 「私(わたし)が司会(しかい)です。」
    Filling in an unknown is the job of が. こそ in an answer sounds like you are contradicting someone.

～こそ · Practice 1

～こそ · Practice 5

数量 + は - at least

Meaning · at least this much; this much as a minimum (reading: すうりょう + は)

Put は on a number and the number stops being a measurement and becomes a floor. 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)かかります reports a journey time; 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)はかかります says "reckon on thirty minutes - it will not be less, and it may well be more." It is the same contrastive は you already know, doing arithmetic.

数量 + は - at least · Formation

Any expression of amount can take this は - a number plus counter, a money amount, a period of time, or a vague quantity word. The predicate is normally positive.

数量 + は - at least · Formation (cont.)

Note - an approximator and this は can stack, and the order is fixed: ぐらいは, never ❌はぐらい. The combination softens the floor to "about that much at the very least."

数量 + は - at least · Examples

駅(えき)まで三十分(さんじゅっぷん)はかかります。
It takes a good thirty minutes to the station.

この辞書(じしょ)なら五千円(ごせんえん)はするでしょう。
A dictionary like this must cost five thousand yen at least.

あの店(みせ)の前(まえ)にはいつも十人(じゅうにん)は並(なら)んでいる。
There are always at least ten people queuing outside that shop.

その小説(しょうせつ)なら三回(さんかい)は読(よ)みました。
I've read that novel at least three times.

引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの準備(じゅんび)には一週間(いっしゅうかん)は必要(ひつよう)です。
You'll need a week at the very least to get ready for the move.

数量 + は - at least · Examples (cont.)

台風(たいふう)の影響(えいきょう)で、電車(でんしゃ)が動(うご)くまでに三時間(さんじかん)はかかりそうです。
Because of the typhoon it looks like it'll be three hours or more before the trains run.

試験(しけん)に合格(ごうかく)したいなら、毎日(まいにち)二時間(にじかん)は勉強(べんきょう)したほうがいいですよ。
If you want to pass the exam you should be studying two hours a day minimum.

会場(かいじょう)には百人(ひゃくにん)は集(あつ)まっていたと思(おも)いますが、正確(せいかく)な人数(にんずう)は分(わ)かりません。
I'd say a hundred people had gathered at the venue, though I don't know the exact number.

数量 + は - at least · Nuance & register

This is a plain contrastive は and it is register-neutral: it appears in casual speech, in polite speech and in writing without changing shape. In conversation the quantity takes the stress - さんじゅっぷんは かかるよ - and that stress is doing much of the work, so listen for it.

What the pattern conveys is confidence about the low end. The speaker is not claiming precision; they are claiming that the true figure will not fall below the one given. That is why it pairs so naturally with hedges: だろう, でしょう, と思(おも)う, はずだ, そうです. Combining a floor with a hedge is not a contradiction - "at least this much, probably more, I can't say exactly."

数量 + は - at least · Nuance & register (cont.)

The pattern is common in three everyday situations: estimating time and cost (一時間(いちじかん)はかかる, 一万円(いちまんえん)はする), asserting effort you have already put in (三回(さんかい)は確認(かくにん)した - "I checked it three times, thank you very much"), and setting a requirement for someone else (三日(みっか)は休(やす)んだほうがいい). The second of these often carries a defensive edge, because the contrastive は invites the comparison "and that's more than you're giving me credit for."

Beside it stands the adverb 少(すく)なくとも, which says "at least" explicitly. It is a shade more formal and more logical in tone, it does not replace the は, and the two frequently appear together in the same sentence.

数量 + は - at least · 数量(すうりょう) + は vs 数量(すうりょう) + も

Rule of thumb - は sets a floor: at least this much. も expresses surprise at the size: as much as this. は is sober; も is impressed or appalled.

会議(かいぎ)は二時間(にじかん)はかかります。
The meeting will take two hours at least.

会議(かいぎ)は二時間(にじかん)もかかりました。
The meeting dragged on for two whole hours.

The first is planning; the second is a complaint. Note the tense that comes with them: は looks forward to an amount not yet known, so it sits with non-past and with hedges, while も reacts to an amount already established, so it sits comfortably in the past. Using も for a forecast - ❌二時間(にじかん)もかかります as a neutral estimate - makes you sound outraged by your own schedule.

数量 + は - at least · 数量(すうりょう) + は vs 少(すく)なくとも

Rule of thumb - 少(すく)なくとも is an adverb and states the minimum in words; the は marks it grammatically. Use は in speech, add 少(すく)なくとも when you want the floor spelled out.

少(すく)なくとも三十分(さんじゅっぷん)は待(ま)たされた。
I was kept waiting at least half an hour.

少(すく)なくとも can also modify things that are not quantities at all - 少(すく)なくとも連絡(れんらく)くらいはしてほしい, "I'd at least like to be told" - where the number-plus-は pattern has nothing to attach to. In return, は is the one that works when no adverb is wanted, which in ordinary conversation is most of the time.

数量(すうりょう) + は with a positive predicate vs with a negative one

Rule of thumb - the same shape does two opposite jobs. Positive predicate: at least this much. Negative predicate: not all of this much - partial negation.

漢字(かんじ)を百(ひゃく)は覚(おぼ)えた。
I've learned a hundred kanji at least.

漢字(かんじ)を全部(ぜんぶ)は覚(おぼ)えていない。
I haven't learned all the kanji. (I have learned some)

This is not two grammars; it is one は marking a contrast, with the negative scoping over the quantity rather than over the verb. Still, the practical effect is that a careless negative reverses your meaning. If you want to say "it takes at least thirty minutes", the verb must be positive: 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)はかかります. 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)はかかりません tells the listener the opposite - it will not take a full thirty minutes.

数量 + は - at least · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 一時間(いちじかん)もかかると思(おも)うので、早(はや)めに出(で)ましょう。 → ✅ 一時間(いちじかん)はかかると思(おも)うので、早(はや)めに出(で)ましょう。
    For a forward-looking minimum you need は. も would make the hour sound outrageous, which is odd in a sentence that is calmly planning around it.

  • ❌ 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)はかかりません。（meaning "it takes at least thirty minutes"） → ✅ 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)はかかります。
    With a negative predicate the pattern flips to partial negation: "it doesn't take a full thirty minutes."

  • ❌ 一時間(いちじかん)はぐらい待(ま)った。 → ✅ 一時間(いちじかん)ぐらいは待(ま)った。
    The approximator goes next to the number and は goes outside it. The order is fixed.

数量 + は - at least · Common mistakes (cont.)

    The は has to land on the quantity. On the noun it contrasts water with the other shopping instead.

  • ❌ 少(すく)なくとも三日(みっか)も必要(ひつよう)です。 → ✅ 少(すく)なくとも三日(みっか)は必要(ひつよう)です。

くらい / ぐらい

Meaning · about, roughly; to the extent that ～; and - dismissively - "no more than that"

くらい names a degree. Attached to a number it makes the number approximate ("about thirty minutes"); attached to a clause it measures one thing against another ("so tired I couldn't stand"); attached to a small task it belittles it ("it's only washing up"). Those three uses look unrelated in English, but they are one idea: this is the level we are talking about.

くらい / ぐらい · Formation

くらい and ぐらい are the same word. Both attach the same way, and nothing about the attachment decides which of the two you say.

くらい / ぐらい · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - くらい measures how much, so it goes with spans, amounts and degrees. It does not name a point on the calendar or the clock; that is ごろ. 三時間(さんじかん)ぐらい but 三時(さんじ)ごろ.

Note - ～くらいなら is a related pattern worth recognising: it rejects one option in favour of another. 待(ま)つくらいなら、歩(ある)いて帰(かえ)る - "if it comes to waiting, I'd rather walk home." The first clause names the level the speaker refuses to sink to.

くらい / ぐらい · Examples

Around twenty people came to the party.

How long have you been studying Japanese?

Washing a few dishes - leave that to me.

My little brother has grown about as tall as me.

I know you're busy, but you could at least have answered my email.

久(ひさ)しぶりに聞(き)いたその曲(きょく)は、涙(なみだ)が出(で)るくらい懐(なつ)かしかった。
Hearing that song again after so long brought back memories so strongly it almost made me cry.

くらい / ぐらい · Nuance & register

くらい and ぐらい mean exactly the same thing and both are correct everywhere. The tendency - and it is only a tendency - is that ぐらい is a little more frequent in speech and after the demonstratives (これぐらい, どれぐらい), while くらい turns up slightly more often in careful writing. No listener will notice which you chose, so pick one and be consistent within a sentence.

The approximate use is completely neutral and extremely common; Japanese conversation prefers a hedged number to a bare one, and a bare 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)かかります can sound oddly exact where 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)ぐらい is expected.

くらい / ぐらい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The belittling use is the one to handle with care. Applied to your own effort it is modest and generous: それくらい、喜(よろこ)んでやります - "that's nothing, happy to." Applied to someone else's effort it says their contribution is trivial, and in a request it says the bar you are setting is embarrassingly low: 挨拶(あいさつ)くらいしなさい is something a parent says to a child. Aimed upward at a manager or a customer it is rude, so keep it for peers, family and yourself.

There is also a superlative frame worth recognising: ～くらい～はない, "nothing is as ～ as this." 夏(なつ)の試験(しけん)くらいつらいものはない - "there's nothing as grim as summer exams." It behaves the same way as the more formal ～ほど～はない.

くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ごろ

Rule of thumb - ごろ marks a point in time; くらい marks a span or an amount. If the word answers "when?", use ごろ. If it answers "how long?" or "how many?", use くらい.

七時(しちじ)ごろ家(いえ)を出(で)ます。
I leave the house around seven.

七時間(しちじかん)ぐらい寝(ね)ました。
I slept for about seven hours.

The counter tells you which is which: 三時(さんじ) is a clock time and takes ごろ; 三時間(さんじかん) is a duration and takes ぐらい. Dates and days work like clock times - 来週(らいしゅう)の水曜日(すいようび)ごろ. Money, distance, weight and headcount are all amounts, so they can only take くらい: ❌千円(せんえん)ごろ is not Japanese.

くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ごろ (cont.)

Note - you will hear 三時(さんじ)ぐらいに in casual speech, and it is not an error, but ごろ is the standard choice for a clock time and the safe one in writing.

くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ほど

Rule of thumb - for approximation they overlap, with ほど sounding more formal. For degree, ほど points up the scale and くらい can point down it. Only くらい can belittle.

一時間(いちじかん)ほどお待(ま)ちいただけますか。
Could I ask you to wait for about an hour?

一時間(いちじかん)ぐらい待(ま)ってよ。
Come on, wait an hour, would you?

Those two sentences carry the same number and completely different manners: ほど belongs to the polite register and shows up constantly in service Japanese, while the second sentence is a friend being pushy.

くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ほど (cont.)

The split is sharper with degrees. 死(し)ぬほど疲(つか)れた and 死(し)ぬくらい疲(つか)れた are both "dead tired", but move to the bottom of the scale and ほど stops working: お茶(ちゃ)くらい出(だ)してください ("you could at least offer tea") cannot become ❌お茶(ちゃ)ほど出(だ)してください. Remember also that ほど owns two frames くらい does not share: ～ば～ほど and comparative ～ほど～ない.

くらい / ぐらい · Belittling くらい vs だけ

Rule of thumb - だけ states a limit neutrally. くらい states the same limit and adds a judgement: that little is not worth making a fuss about.

漢字(かんじ)を五(いつ)つだけ覚(おぼ)えました。
I learned just five kanji.

漢字(かんじ)を五(いつ)つぐらい覚(おぼ)えてください。
Learn five kanji at least, would you.

だけ reports; くらい evaluates. That is why the second sentence works as a complaint and the first does not, and why swapping them in a request changes what you are doing socially. 一人(ひとり)だけ来(こ)なかった counts the absentees; 一人(ひとり)ぐらい来(こ)なくてもいい waves them away as unimportant.

くらい / ぐらい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 三時間(さんじかん)ごろ勉強(べんきょう)しました。 → ✅ 三時間(さんじかん)ぐらい勉強(べんきょう)しました。
    ごろ needs a point in time. A duration counter such as 三時間(さんじかん) can only take くらい／ぐらい.

  • ❌ 大変(たいへん)くらい忙(いそが)しかった。 → ✅ 大変(たいへん)なくらい忙(いそが)しかった。
    A な-adjective keeps its な before くらい, exactly as it does before だけ and ので.

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、確認(かくにん)ぐらいしてください。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、ご確認(かくにん)をお願(ねが)いできますか。
    Not a grammar error but a social one. Belittling くらい aimed at a superior is openly rude; save it for yourself and for people you are close to.

  • ❌ 千円(せんえん)ごろの店(みせ) → ✅ 千円(せんえん)ぐらいの店(みせ)
    Money is an amount, never a point in time.

くらい / ぐらい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この問題(もんだい)は難(むずか)しいぐらいです。（meaning "it's really hard"） → ✅ この問題(もんだい)は解(と)けないぐらい難(むずか)しいです。
    くらい needs something to measure the degree against. On its own after an adjective it produces the opposite, dismissive reading: "it's merely hard."

～あまり

Meaning · out of too much ～; so ～ that … - an excess of feeling or effort that produces an unintended result

～あまり names a cause that went too far. Worry, nerves, delight, haste or overwork build past a reasonable level, and the second half of the sentence reports what that excess brought about - usually something the person did not intend. English reaches for "so worried that…" or "in his haste…"; Japanese puts the excess in a noun-like phrase and hangs the consequence off it.

～あまり · Formation

The excess comes first, あまり closes it, and the result follows after a comma. Almost anything before あまり has to name a feeling, a state or a degree.

Attaches to Form Example
Emotion noun Noun + の + あまり 心配(しんぱい) → 心配(しんぱい)のあまり
Degree noun in ～さ ～さ + の + あまり 忙(いそが)しさ → 忙(いそが)しさのあまり
Verb, non-past Vる + あまり 急(いそ)ぐ → 急(いそ)ぐあまり
Verb, past Vた + あまり 緊張(きんちょう)した → 緊張(きんちょう)したあまり

Rule of thumb - the second clause reports something beyond the speaker's control: could not sleep, dropped it, forgot, burst into tears, missed the deadline. If the outcome was planned and ordinary, the sentence wants ので or から, not あまり.

Note - あまりの～に is the same idea rearranged, with the degree word turned into a さ-noun: あまりの暑(あつ)さに、外(そと)に出(で)られなかった. Learn both shapes; news writing uses them about equally.

～あまり · Examples

心配(しんぱい)のあまり、昨夜(さくや)は眠(ねむ)れませんでした。
I was so worried I couldn't sleep last night.

緊張(きんちょう)のあまり、声(こえ)が出(で)なかった。
I was so nervous my voice wouldn't come out.

I was so startled that I dropped the glass I was holding.

In my hurry I went and left my wallet at home.

～あまり · Examples (cont.)

息子(むすこ)の成績(せいせき)を気(き)にするあまり、母親(ははおや)は毎晩(まいばん)同(おな)じ小言(こごと)を繰(く)り返(かえ)すようになった。
So preoccupied did she become with her son's grades that his mother started repeating the same scolding every night.

～あまり · Nuance & register

This is a written pattern. It belongs to novels, newspaper reports, formal speeches and essays; in ordinary conversation people say 心配(しんぱい)で眠(ねむ)れなかった or 心配(しんぱい)しすぎて眠(ねむ)れなかった. Using 心配(しんぱい)のあまり with a friend is not wrong, but it sounds as if you are narrating your own life.

The nouns that fit in front of it form a fairly closed set - emotions and pressures: 心配(しんぱい), 緊張(きんちょう), 驚(おどろ)き, 喜(よろこ)び, 悲(かな)しみ, 怒(いか)り, 疲(つか)れ, 忙(いそが)しさ, 恥(は)ずかしさ. A neutral noun does not work: ❌雨(あめ)のあまり is not Japanese, because rain is not a degree of anything.

～あまり · Nuance & register (cont.)

The tone is nearly always regretful or cautionary. Even with a positive emotion, the result is an overreaction: joy that ends in tears, enthusiasm that ends in a missed deadline. If the result is simply good news, the sentence needs a different connector - 喜(よろこ)びのあまり合格(ごうかく)した is incoherent, because passing an exam is not something excess joy causes.

Grammatically, the phrase behaves like an adverbial and does not take は or が. It sits at the head of the sentence, or right after the subject: 母親(ははおや)は心配(しんぱい)のあまり….

～あまり · ～あまり vs ～ので

Rule of thumb - ので gives a plain reason. あまり gives a reason that was excessive, and its result is correspondingly abnormal.

心配(しんぱい)だったので、何度(なんど)も電話(でんわ)しました。
I was worried, so I rang several times.

心配(しんぱい)のあまり、夜中(よなか)に何度(なんど)も電話(でんわ)してしまった。
I was so worried that I ended up ringing over and over in the middle of the night.

The ので version reports reasonable behaviour. The あまり version admits the behaviour got out of hand - which is why てしまう is such a frequent companion. Swap them and you either lose the excess (ので) or accidentally confess to one (あまり).

～あまり · ～あまり vs ～すぎて

Rule of thumb - すぎて is the everyday word for "too much" and attaches to any verb or adjective. あまり is literary and restricted to degrees of feeling and effort.

食(た)べすぎて、お腹(なか)が痛(いた)くなった。
I ate too much and got a stomach ache.

考(かんが)えすぎる****あまり、何(なに)も決(き)められなくなった。
He thought about it so hard that he could no longer decide anything.

Notice that the second sentence uses both: すぎる supplies the excess and あまり frames it as the cause of the paralysis. That stacking is common and perfectly natural. What you cannot do is put an everyday physical action straight in front of あまり: ❌食(た)べるあまり asks the reader to treat eating as an emotion.

～あまり · ～あまり vs あまり～ない

Rule of thumb - same word, opposite structures. Something + の／verb + あまり at the head of a clause means "so much that". Bare あまり in front of a negative means "not very". The の and the negative are the tells.

疲(つか)れのあまり、食欲(しょくよく)がなくなった。
I was so exhausted that I lost my appetite.

今日(きょう)はあまり食欲(しょくよく)がない。
I don't have much appetite today.

The first あまり follows its noun; the second precedes the whole predicate and demands a negative. In speech the two are also stressed differently, and the "not very" one has the casual variants あんまり and あんま, which the ～あまり pattern never takes: ❌疲(つか)れのあんまり.

～あまり · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 心配(しんぱい)あまり、電話(でんわ)した。 → ✅ 心配(しんぱい)のあまり、電話(でんわ)した。
    A noun needs の to join あまり. Only verbs attach directly.

  • ❌ 忙(いそが)しいあまり、返事(へんじ)が遅(おく)れました。 → ✅ 忙(いそが)しさのあまり、返事(へんじ)が遅(おく)れました。
    An い-adjective has to become a さ-noun first. The pattern wants a degree, and ～さ is what turns an adjective into one.

  • ❌ 嬉(うれ)しさのあまり、予定(よてい)どおり準備(じゅんび)を進(すす)めた。 → ✅ 嬉(うれ)しさのあまり、大声(おおごえ)を上(あ)げてしまった。
    The result clause has to be something the excess pushed the person into. An orderly, planned outcome contradicts the pattern.

  • ❌ 時間(じかん)があまりのなかった。 → ✅ 時間(じかん)があまりなかった。
    The "not very" adverb takes no の and no following noun. If you find yourself writing のあまり in a negative sentence, you have mixed the two patterns.

～あまり · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 雨(あめ)のあまり、試合(しあい)が中止(ちゅうし)になった。 → ✅ 雨(あめ)のため、試合(しあい)が中止(ちゅうし)になった。
    あまり needs a degree of feeling or effort, not an ordinary event. For a plain cause in written Japanese, use のため.

あまりに

Meaning · too ～; excessively ～ - so far past the normal level that something follows from it

あまりに is a degree adverb, like とても, but with a verdict attached. とても says the level is high; あまりに says the level has gone past where it should be, and the sentence usually goes on to say what that cost. Too expensive to buy, too sudden to answer, too detailed for anyone to remember.

あまりに · Formation

あまりに stands in front of the word whose degree it is measuring. It never follows it.

Rule of thumb - あまりに is an adverb, so it takes no の and no な of its own. If you want a の after it, the following word must become a noun: あまりに寒(さむ)くて／あまりの寒(さむ)さに.

Note - a bare あんまりだ is a fixed colloquial complaint meaning "that's not fair", used of someone's behaviour: それはあんまりじゃない?. It comes from this same word but works as a な-adjective predicate, not as an adverb.

It was all so sudden that I couldn't find any words.

It was far too cold outside, so we gave up on the walk.

The offer sounded too good, which in fact made me suspicious.

His explanation went on far too long and I got sleepy halfway through.

あまりに · Examples (cont.)

There were so many people that I gave up on getting into the venue.

The new rules are so fiddly that nobody is going to remember all of them.

あまりに · Nuance & register

あまりに is emphatic and a little formal. In casual conversation the usual words are すごく, めっちゃ and the ～すぎる ending; あまりに sounds deliberate, and it is at home in writing, in complaints made carefully, and in speech where you want the excess to register.

あまりにも adds another notch of emphasis and is, if anything, more common than the bare form in modern speech. There is no difference in meaning; も simply presses harder.

The word carries an evaluation, and that evaluation is usually negative: too expensive, too sudden, too strict. It can front a positive adjective, but even then the sentence is reporting a reaction that overwhelmed the speaker - あまりに美(うつく)しくて、言葉(ことば)が出(で)なかった. What it cannot do is state a plain, contented high degree. For "the cake was really delicious", say とても; あまりにおいしかった without a consequence sounds unfinished, as though a "so I…" clause had been cut off.

あまりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

That expectation of a consequence is the practical point. Most あまりに sentences have a second half - ～て, ～ので, ～ため - or end in an exclamation. When you write one, ask yourself what followed from the excess; if nothing did, you probably wanted とても.

あまりに · あまりに vs とても

Rule of thumb - とても measures. あまりに judges. とても says "very"; あまりに says "more than it should be".

この部屋(へや)はとても広(ひろ)いですね。
This room is really spacious, isn't it.

この部屋(へや)はあまりに広(ひろ)くて、掃除(そうじ)が大変(たいへん)です。
This room is so big that cleaning it is a chore.

Both sentences agree about the size of the room; only the second one has a problem with it. That is why とても fits compliments and あまりに fits complaints, warnings and excuses. Note too that とても has a second life in front of a negative (とても信(しん)じられない, "I simply can't believe it") which あまりに does not share.

あまりに · あまりに vs ～すぎる

Rule of thumb - ～すぎる is a verb ending attached to the word itself; あまりに is a separate adverb placed before it. They mean much the same thing, and Japanese happily uses both at once.

この服(ふく)は大(おお)きすぎます。
These clothes are too big.

この服(ふく)はあまりに大(おお)きすぎます。
These clothes are far too big.

すぎる is the everyday choice and the one to reach for in conversation. あまりに is what you add when you want to underline the excess or when the sentence is written. One structural difference matters: すぎる conjugates and can end a sentence on its own, while あまりに cannot - ❌この服(ふく)はあまりにです is not a sentence.

あまりに · あまりに vs ～あまり

Rule of thumb - あまりに precedes the word it modifies and is an adverb. ～あまり follows a noun (with の) or a verb and introduces a clause. Word order is the whole test.

あまりに緊張(きんちょう)して、手(て)が震(ふる)えた。
I was so nervous my hands shook.

緊張(きんちょう)のあまり、手(て)が震(ふる)えた。
Out of sheer nerves, my hands shook.

The two sentences mean nearly the same thing, which is why the pair is so easily muddled. The difference is in what each one attaches to: あまりに needs a verb or adjective after it, while あまり needs a noun or verb before it. Get the order backwards - ❌緊張(きんちょう)にあまり, ❌あまりの緊張(きんちょう)して - and neither pattern survives.

あまりに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 寒(さむ)いあまりに、外(そと)に出(で)なかった。 → ✅ あまりに寒(さむ)くて、外(そと)に出(で)なかった。
    あまりに is an adverb and comes first. Putting it after the adjective produces neither pattern.

  • ❌ あまりの寒(さむ)くて眠(ねむ)れなかった。 → ✅ あまりに寒(さむ)くて眠(ねむ)れなかった。／✅ あまりの寒(さむ)さに眠(ねむ)れなかった。
    の has to be followed by a noun. Either keep the adjective and use に, or turn the adjective into a さ-noun and use の.

  • ❌ 時間(じかん)があまりにありません。（meaning "I don't have much time"） → ✅ 時間(じかん)があまりありません。
    The "not very" adverb is the bare あまり. Adding に switches you to the "too much" word, which does not belong in front of a plain negative.

あまりに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ あまりに静(しず)か部屋(へや)で、落(お)ち着(つ)かなかった。 → ✅ あまりに静(しず)かな部屋(へや)で、落(お)ち着(つ)かなかった。
    あまりに changes nothing about how the な-adjective attaches to its noun.

  • ❌ ケーキがあまりにおいしかったです。（as a simple compliment） → ✅ ケーキがとてもおいしかったです。
    あまりに expects a consequence. With nothing following, a listener waits for the rest of the sentence.

～ことか

Meaning · how ～! - an exclamation shaped like a question, for a feeling too big to state plainly

～ことか ends a sentence that looks like a question and expects no answer. The speaker is not asking how worried they were; they are saying the amount cannot be put into words. English does the same trick with "how many times have I told you!" - a question mark would be wrong there too.

～ことか · Formation

The pattern needs two halves: a degree word near the front, and ことか at the end. Without the degree word the sentence collapses into an ordinary question.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb, plain Vる／Vた／Vている + ことか 待(ま)っていた → どれほど待(ま)っていたことか
い-adjective ～い + ことか 嬉(うれ)しい → どんなに嬉(うれ)しいことか
な-adjective ～な + ことか 大変(たいへん)だ → どんなに大変(たいへん)なことか
Noun predicate ～な + ことか 幸(しあわ)せ → なんと幸(しあわ)せなことか
Degree words used どんなに／どれほど／どれだけ／何度(なんど)／何回(なんかい)／なんと 何度(なんど)言(い)ったことか
Softer variants ～ことだろう／～ことでしょう どんなに喜(よろこ)ぶことでしょう

～ことか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the clause before ことか takes the plain form, however polite the rest of the passage is. Politeness is expressed by switching to ことでしょう, not by inserting です or ます.

Note - ことだろう and ことでしょう are the same exclamation with a softer edge, and they point outward at someone else's feelings: ご家族(かぞく)はどんなに安心(あんしん)されたことでしょう. Use ことか for your own feelings and ことでしょう when imagining another person's.

～ことか · Examples

どんなに心配(しんぱい)したことか。
You have no idea how worried I was.

この日(ひ)をどれほど待(ま)っていたことか。
How long I've waited for this day.

何度(なんど)同(おな)じことを注意(ちゅうい)したことか。
How many times have I told you the same thing!

一人(ひとり)で暮(く)らすのがどんなに大変(たいへん)なことか、やってみて初(はじ)めて分(わ)かった。
Only when I tried it did I realise how hard living alone is.

合格(ごうかく)の知(し)らせを聞(き)いて、両親(りょうしん)はどんなに喜(よろこ)んだことか。
When they heard I'd passed, how delighted my parents must have been.

～ことか · Examples (cont.)

留学中(りゅうがくちゅう)、家族(かぞく)からの手紙(てがみ)にどれだけ励(はげ)まされたことか。
While I was studying abroad, those letters from home kept me going more than I can say.

あのとき助(たす)けてもらえなかったら、今(いま)ごろどうなっていたことか。
If nobody had helped me then, goodness knows where I'd be now.

山(やま)の上(うえ)から見(み)える景色(けしき)の、なんと美(うつく)しいことか。
How beautiful the view from the mountaintop is.

～ことか · Nuance & register

ことか is emotive and literary. It lives in essays, memoirs, letters, speeches, drama and song lyrics; in everyday conversation it sounds heightened, and younger speakers use it sparingly. That is not a reason to avoid it - it is a reason to notice what you are doing when you say it. Dropping a どんなに心配(しんぱい)したことか into a conversation makes the moment weightier, which is sometimes exactly right.

The everyday equivalents are worth having side by side:

  • 本当(ほんとう)に心配(しんぱい)したよ - plain and warm.
  • どれだけ心配(しんぱい)したと思(おも)ってるの - colloquial, and the
    usual spoken way of scolding someone with the same content.
  • どんなに心配(しんぱい)したことか - the written, weightier version.

～ことか · Nuance & register (cont.)

The emotion is usually one of two kinds: long-suffering (worry, waiting, repeated warnings ignored) or overflowing gratitude and relief. Both are retrospective, which is why the verb before ことか is so often in the past or the ～ている form.

Punctuation is a small giveaway. Because the sentence is not a real question, it is normally written with a full stop rather than a question mark, and it is said with falling intonation. If you catch yourself waiting for an answer, you have misread it.

～ことか · ～ことか vs ～だろう

Rule of thumb - だろう guesses. ことか exclaims. だろう is uncertain about a fact; ことか is certain about a feeling and only uncertain about how to measure it.

彼(かれ)はもう着(つ)いただろう。
He's probably arrived by now.

彼(かれ)の到着(とうちゃく)をどれほど待(ま)ったことか。
How long we waited for him to arrive.

The halfway house is ことだろう, which keeps the exclamation and adds a guess about someone else's inner state: お母(かあ)さんはどんなに悲(かな)しんだことだろう - "how his mother must have grieved." Note that a plain conjecture cannot take ことか: ❌明日(あした)は雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ることか is not Japanese, because there is no emotion being measured.

～ことか · ～ことか vs ～ものか

Rule of thumb - both are rhetorical questions, and they do opposite things. ことか says "so much!". ものか says "not a chance!" - it is a flat refusal or denial.

どんなに感謝(かんしゃ)していることか。
I'm more grateful than I can say.

あんな店(みせ)、二度(にど)と行(い)くものか。
As if I'd ever go back to that place.

ものか attaches to the plain non-past and needs no degree word; its casual spoken form is もんか, and it is distinctly rough. Mixing the two up reverses your meaning entirely, so check which of "how much!" and "no way!" you mean before you choose.

～ことか · ～ことか vs なんて

Rule of thumb - same feeling, different register and different position. なんて fronts the exclamation and is ordinary spoken Japanese; ことか closes the sentence and is written.

なんてきれいな海(うみ)なんだろう。
What a beautiful sea.

なんときれいな海(うみ)であることか。
How beautiful this sea is.

The first is what you would actually say standing on the shore. The second belongs in a travel essay about standing on the shore. Note also that なんて has a second, unrelated use meaning "things like ～" (運動(うんどう)なんて嫌(きら)いだ), which ことか never has.

～ことか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 心配(しんぱい)したことか。 → ✅ どんなに心配(しんぱい)したことか。
    Without a degree word there is nothing for the exclamation to measure, and the sentence reads as a broken question.

  • ❌ どんなに嬉(うれ)しかったですことか。 → ✅ どんなに嬉(うれ)しかったことか。／✅ どんなに嬉(うれ)しかったことでしょう。
    The clause before ことか stays plain. For a polite register, switch the ending to ことでしょう.

  • ❌ どんなに大変(たいへん)だことか。 → ✅ どんなに大変(たいへん)なことか。
    A な-adjective keeps its な in front of こと, as it does before any noun.

  • ❌ 来週(らいしゅう)は忙(いそが)しいことか。（meaning "next week will probably be busy"） → ✅ 来週(らいしゅう)は忙(いそが)しいだろう。
    ことか is not a conjecture ending. Use it only where a strong feeling is being exclaimed over.

～ことか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 「どんなに心配(しんぱい)したことか。」「三日(みっか)ぐらいです。」 → the question is rhetorical; it takes no answer.
    Treat it as a statement with an exclamation behind it, not as something to reply to.

～ことに

Meaning · to my surprise / delight / regret - the speaker's reaction, stated before the news itself

～ことに puts your feeling first and the fact second. 驚(おどろ)いたことに、彼(かれ)はもう帰(かえ)っていた - "to my astonishment, he had already gone home." The listener is told how to receive the news before they hear it, which is exactly what English does with "sadly," "remarkably," "to my relief."

Usual verbs 驚(おどろ)いた／困(こま)った／がっかりした がっかりしたことに

～ことに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the second clause must be a statement of fact. A request, an invitation or a command cannot follow: ❌嬉(うれ)しいことに、一緒(いっしょ)に行(い)きましょう. The pattern comments on news; it does not introduce a proposal.

Note - the reaction belongs to the speaker (or, in a story, to the narrator or the viewpoint character). You cannot use it to report someone else's feeling about their own news; for that, Japanese says 彼(かれ)は驚(おどろ)いたようで、….

～ことに · Examples

驚(おどろ)いたことに、彼(かれ)はもう帰(かえ)っていた。
To my surprise, he had already gone home.

残念(ざんねん)なことに、試合(しあい)は中止(ちゅうし)になりました。
Sadly, the match was called off.

不思議(ふしぎ)なことに、同(おな)じ夢(ゆめ)を三日(みっか)続(つづ)けて見(み)た。
Strangely enough, I had the same dream three nights running.

ありがたいことに、近所(きんじょ)の人(ひと)が荷物(にもつ)を預(あず)かってくれていた。
Thankfully, a neighbour had taken the parcel in for me.

困(こま)ったことに、会場(かいじょう)の鍵(かぎ)を持(も)っている人(ひと)と連絡(れんらく)が取(と)れません。
Awkwardly, we can't get hold of the person who has the key to the venue.

～ことに · Examples (cont.)

面白(おもしろ)いことに、同(おな)じ質問(しつもん)をしても年代(ねんだい)によって答(こた)えが全(まった)く違(ちが)った。
Interestingly, the same question got completely different answers depending on the age group.

幸(さいわ)いなことに、地震(じしん)のとき家族(かぞく)は全員(ぜんいん)外(そと)にいて、けがはありませんでした。
Fortunately my whole family was outside when the earthquake struck, and nobody was hurt.

情(なさ)けないことに、一年(いちねん)かけて準備(じゅんび)したのに、本番(ほんばん)では名前(なまえ)すら言(い)えなかった。
Embarrassingly, after a year of preparation I couldn't even get my name out on the day.

～ことに · Nuance & register

The pattern leans written. It is a staple of essays, reports, travel writing and news features, where it does the work of an English sentence adverb. In speech it is perfectly usable and does not sound stiff, but conversation more often reaches for the plain adverbs 幸(さいわ)い, あいにく, 残念(ざんねん)ながら, or simply for びっくりした! followed by the news.

Two combinations have drifted into everyday speech on their own: 困(こま)ったことに, which introduces a problem you are still standing in, and ありがたいことに, which is a modest, slightly formal way of acknowledging help. Both are safe at work.

Adding は - 困(こま)ったことには - makes the phrase heavier and more literary. It is the same pattern with the contrastive は doing its usual job of setting this circumstance against the rest, and you will meet it far more often in print than in conversation.

～ことに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because the emotion is stated up front, the pattern has a mild framing effect: it tells the listener how to take what follows, which is why journalists like it and why it can sound editorial. If you want the facts to speak for themselves, leave it out.

～ことに · ～ことに vs ～ことにする / ～ことになる

Rule of thumb - look at what こと is followed by. ことに、 with a comma and a following clause is a comment. ことにする is a decision and ことになる is an arrangement - both of them verbs, both attached to a plain-form verb rather than to an emotion word.

来月(らいげつ)から大阪(おおさか)で働(はたら)くことになりました。
It's been decided that I'll be working in Osaka from next month.

嬉(うれ)しいことに、来月(らいげつ)から大阪(おおさか)で働(はたら)けることになりました。
Happily, it's been settled that I can work in Osaka from next month.

The second sentence contains both, which is the clearest way to see the difference: 嬉(うれ)しいことに is the speaker's reaction, and ことになりました is the arrangement being reacted to. An emotion word can never take ことにする: ❌嬉(うれ)しいことにしました would have to mean "I decided on happiness."

～ことに · ～ことに vs 幸(さいわ)い・あいにく・残念(ざんねん)ながら

Rule of thumb - the adverbs are shorter, more spoken, and fixed in form. The ことに frame is open: any evaluation word can go into it, including ones that have no adverb of their own.

あいにく、担当者(たんとうしゃ)は席(せき)を外(はず)しております。
Unfortunately the person in charge is away from their desk.

情(なさ)けないことに、自分(じぶん)の傘(かさ)をまた電車(でんしゃ)に忘(わす)れた。
Pathetically, I've left my umbrella on the train again.

There is no single adverb for "pathetically" in this sense, and that is the frame's value: it manufactures one on demand. Where a ready-made adverb exists, prefer it in speech - あいにく品切(しなぎ)れです is what a shop assistant says, not 残念(ざんねん)なことに品切(しなぎ)れです.

～ことに · ～ことに vs ～て

Rule of thumb - 驚(おどろ)いて makes the surprise a cause of what follows. 驚(おどろ)いたことに makes it a comment on what follows. The て-form joins two events; ことに joins a feeling to a fact.

驚(おどろ)いて、声(こえ)を上(あ)げてしまった。
I was so startled that I cried out.

驚(おどろ)いたことに、窓(まど)が開(あ)いたままだった。
To my surprise, the window had been left open.

In the first sentence the surprise causes the shout; in the second it causes nothing at all - it merely tells you how the open window struck the speaker. That is why the て-form sentence needs an action after it, while the ことに sentence needs a state of affairs.

～ことに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 残念(ざんねん)ことに、来(こ)られませんでした。 → ✅ 残念(ざんねん)なことに、来(こ)られませんでした。
    A な-adjective keeps its な before こと. The same goes for 不思議(ふしぎ)な and 幸(さいわ)いな.

  • ❌ 驚(おどろ)くことに、値段(ねだん)が半分(はんぶん)になっていた。 → ✅ 驚(おどろ)いたことに、値段(ねだん)が半分(はんぶん)になっていた。
    With a verb of reaction the form is the past. The reaction has already happened by the time you report it.

  • ❌ 嬉(うれ)しいことに、一緒(いっしょ)に飲(の)みに行(い)きませんか。 → ✅ 嬉(うれ)しいことに、彼(かれ)も来(き)てくれるそうです。
    The clause after the comma has to be a fact. Invitations, requests and commands cannot follow.

～ことに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 田中(たなか)さんは嬉(うれ)しいことに、試験(しけん)に合格(ごうかく)しました。 → ✅ 嬉(うれ)しいことに、田中(たなか)さんが試験(しけん)に合格(ごうかく)しました。
    The feeling is the speaker's, so the phrase should not be tucked in behind another person's topic where it looks like theirs.

  • ❌ 来週(らいしゅう)休(やす)む嬉(うれ)しいことにしました。 → ✅ 来週(らいしゅう)休(やす)むことにしました。
    ことにする is a separate pattern that attaches to a plain verb and means "decide to". Nothing emotional goes in front of it.

～ことに · Practice 1

1. ____ことに、財布(さいふ)は交番(こうばん)に届(とど)けられていました。

ありがたい（幸(さいわ)いな）ことに、財布(さいふ)は交番(こうばん)に届(とど)けられていました。

The news is good, so the slot takes a word of relief or gratitude. Any evaluation word fits the frame as long as it is in its noun-modifying form - ありがたい unchanged, 幸(さいわ)い with な.

～ことに · Practice 2

2. Choose: 来年(らいねん)から支店(してん)に移(うつ)る（ことに・ことになり）ました。

来年(らいねん)から支店(してん)に移(うつ)ることになりました。

This is the arrangement pattern attached to a plain verb, not the comment pattern. ことに as taught on this page follows an emotion word and is followed by a comma and a clause, never by ます.

～ことに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Unfortunately, the shop had already closed."

残念(ざんねん)なことに、店(みせ)はもう閉(し)まっていました。

残念(ざんねん)なことに puts the speaker's disappointment in front of the fact. In speech あいにく店(みせ)はもう閉(し)まっていました is the more idiomatic version of the same sentence.

～ことに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 驚(おどろ)くことに、みんなで写真(しゃしん)を撮(と)りましょう。

✅ 驚(おどろ)いたことに、みんながもう集(あつ)まっていました。

Two things were wrong. The verb needs its past form, 驚(おどろ)いたことに, and the second clause cannot be an invitation - the pattern comments on facts, so 撮(と)りましょう has to be replaced by a statement.

～ことに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 困(こま)ったことに、電車(でんしゃ)が止(と)まっている。 (b) 電車(でんしゃ)が止(と)まっていて、困(こま)っている。

(a) frames the news: before you hear about the trains, you are told that this is a problem. It reads as narration or as a report to a colleague, and it keeps a little distance from the speaker's own state.

(b) puts the same content the other way round - the stopped trains are the cause and the speaker's difficulty is the result. It is plainer, more conversational, and more directly about how the speaker is doing right now.
