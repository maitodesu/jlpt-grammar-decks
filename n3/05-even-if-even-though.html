たとえ～ても

Meaning · even if - the speaker grants a supposition and says the outcome would not change

たとえ is an adverb parked at the head of the clause. It warns the listener in advance: what follows is a supposition, and I am about to throw it away. The grammar is carried by the ても you learned at N4; たとえ supplies the emphasis and the sense of "however extreme this gets."

This is the calm end of the concession family. のに and くせに react to a gap between expectation and reality that has already opened up, and they mind about it. たとえ～ても looks forward at a gap that has not opened yet, and announces that the speaker will not mind when it does.

たとえ～ても · Formation

たとえ never changes shape and never attaches to anything. Everything conjugates on the ても side, exactly as at N4.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (literary) たとえ + volitional + と（も） 言(い)われる → たとえ何(なに)を言(い)われようと

Rule of thumb - たとえ is decoration you can delete; ても is not. Take たとえ out of たとえ寒(さむ)くても行(い)く and you still have a sentence. Take ても out and you have nothing.

Note - write it in kana. The kanji spelling 例(たと)え collides with 例(たと)えば ("for example") and with 例(たと)える ("to liken"), which are different words entirely. The older form たとい still turns up in legal and literary writing.

たとえ～ても · Examples

たとえ安(やす)くても、これは買(か)わない。
Even if it were cheap, I wouldn't buy this.

たとえ冗談(じょうだん)でも、そんなことは言(い)わないで。
Don't say things like that, even as a joke.

たとえ一人(ひとり)になっても、ぼくはこの案(あん)に賛成(さんせい)します。
Even if I end up the only one, I'm backing this proposal.

たとえ親(おや)に反対(はんたい)されても、来年(らいねん)留学(りゅうがく)するつもりだ。
Even if my parents are against it, I intend to go abroad to study next year.

たとえ忙(いそが)しくても、返事(へんじ)だけは今日(きょう)中(じゅう)に出(だ)します。
However busy things get, I'll at least send a reply before the day is out.

たとえ～ても · Examples (cont.)

たとえ失敗(しっぱい)したとしても、挑戦(ちょうせん)したことは無駄(むだ)にならないよ。
Even supposing it doesn't work out, having tried won't have been for nothing.

たとえ社長(しゃちょう)の指示(しじ)でも、安全(あんぜん)を後回(あとまわ)しにするわけにはいきません。
Even if the order comes from the president, we can't put safety second.

たとえ何(なに)を言(い)われようと、祖父(そふ)の店(みせ)は閉(し)めないと決(き)めている。
Whatever anyone says to me, I've made up my mind not to close my grandfather's shop.

たとえ～ても · Nuance & register

たとえ is not casual, but it is not stiff either. It is emphatic, and emphasis has its own register: you hear it in speeches, in resolutions, in job interviews, and in the moments when someone is stating what they will not compromise on. In an ordinary exchange about the weather, people just use bare ても and leave たとえ out.

Because たとえ announces a supposition, it pulls the main clause toward will and judgement rather than plain report. The natural endings are つもりだ, ～たい, ～なければならない, ～わけにはいかない, a negative, or a command. A flat factual ending sounds like the sentence lost its point halfway through.

Three shapes, roughly by formality:

たとえ～ても · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • たとえ～ても - the everyday one.
  • たとえ～としても - a step more formal and more clearly hypothetical; common in
    writing and in careful speech.
  • たとえ～（よ）うと（も） - literary and declamatory. Fine in a written pledge or a
    dramatic line, overblown in conversation.

たとえ also survives in fixed emotional phrasing: たとえ一(いち)パーセントでも可能性(かのうせい)があるなら - "if there's even a one percent chance." Notice the pattern: たとえ plus a deliberately tiny quantity plus でも is how Japanese says "even so much as."

たとえ～ても · たとえ～ても vs bare ～ても

Rule of thumb - bare ても covers both "even if" (a supposition) and "even when" (a habit). たとえ selects the supposition reading and turns the volume up.

熱(ねつ)があっても、兄(あに)は練習(れんしゅう)に出(で)ている。
My brother turns up to practice even when he has a temperature.

That is a report of what actually keeps happening. Adding たとえ there would be odd, because there is nothing to suppose - it is already true. Now the other reading:

たとえ熱(ねつ)があっても、明日(あした)の練習(れんしゅう)は休(やす)まない。
Even if I'm running a temperature, I'm not missing practice tomorrow.

Nothing has happened; the speaker is pre-committing. That is たとえ's home ground.

たとえ～ても · たとえ～ても vs いくら／どんなに～ても

Rule of thumb - たとえ concedes whether; いくら and どんなに concede how much. If there is no scale of degree or repetition, いくら has nothing to measure.

たとえ一円(いちえん)でも、人(ひと)のお金(かね)に手(て)をつけてはいけない。
Even one yen of someone else's money is not yours to touch.

いくら説明(せつめい)しても、彼(かれ)は納得(なっとく)してくれなかった。
No matter how much I explained, he wouldn't accept it.

The first is a single yes-or-no supposition, so たとえ. The second is about repeated effort piling up with no result, so いくら. Where a degree really is at stake both can appear together: たとえどんなに時間(じかん)がかかっても。

たとえ～ても · たとえ～ても vs ～のに

Rule of thumb - たとえ～ても is about a gap that has not happened yet and the speaker does not mind. のに is about a gap that has happened and the speaker does mind. They never swap.

たとえ会社(かいしゃ)が遠(とお)くても、自転車(じてんしゃ)で通(かよ)います。
Even if the office turns out to be far, I'll cycle in.

会社(かいしゃ)が遠(とお)いのに、毎日(まいにち)自転車(じてんしゃ)で通(かよ)っている。
The office is a long way off and yet he cycles in every day.

The first speaker is deciding something in advance. The second is looking at a fact and reacting to it - with surprise here, but のに can just as easily carry a complaint. That is the axis this whole group of patterns is sorted on: how much the speaker minds.

たとえ～ても · Common mistakes

  • ❌ たとえ雨(あめ)だても → ✅ たとえ雨(あめ)でも
    Nouns and な-adjectives take で + も. だ never survives in front of ても.

  • ❌ たとえ降(ふ)ったても → ✅ たとえ降(ふ)っても
    No tense goes in front of ても. The final verb decides whether the sentence is about the future or the past.

  • ❌ たとえば高(たか)くても買(か)います → ✅ たとえ高(たか)くても買(か)います
    たとえば is "for example" - a different word that happens to start the same way. There is no ば in this pattern.

  • ❌ たとえ謝(あやま)っても、姉(あね)はまだ怒(おこ)っている → ✅ 謝(あやま)ったのに、姉(あね)はまだ怒(おこ)っている
    If the apology actually happened, it is not a supposition any more. Reporting a real, disappointing outcome is のに's job.

たとえ～ても · Practice 1

1. たとえ子(こ)ども____、この約束(やくそく)は守(まも)らせます。

たとえ子(こ)どもでも、この約束(やくそく)は守(まも)らせます。

子(こ)ども is a noun, so the て-form it needs is で, giving でも. The sentence says the speaker will hold even a child to the promise - a small, extreme case granted and then dismissed, which is what たとえ is for.

たとえ～ても · Practice 2

2. Choose: 台風(たいふう)が来(き)た（たとえ・のに）、試合(しあい)は予定(よてい)どおり行(おこな)われた。

台風(たいふう)が来(き)たのに、試合(しあい)は予定(よてい)どおり行(おこな)われた。

The typhoon actually arrived, so this is a real event, not a supposition - and たとえ cannot attach to a past plain form anyway. The forward-looking version would be たとえ台風(たいふう)が来(き)ても、試合(しあい)はやります。

たとえ～ても · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Even if nobody agrees with me, I'm going to say what I think."

たとえだれも賛成(さんせい)してくれなくても、思(おも)っていることは言(い)います。

The negative て-form is なくて, so the concessive is なくても. Note how naturally the main clause states an intention: たとえ sets up a supposition precisely so the speaker can declare that it changes nothing.

たとえ～ても · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: たとえ給料(きゅうりょう)が安(やす)いだても、この仕事(しごと)を続(つづ)けたい。

✅ たとえ給料(きゅうりょう)が安(やす)くても、この仕事(しごと)を続(つづ)けたい。

安(やす)い is an い-adjective, so its て-form is 安(やす)くて and the concessive is 安(やす)くても. The だ is a noun-sentence ending and has no business here; even with a noun the form would be でも, never だても.

たとえ～ても · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) たとえ一人(ひとり)でやることになっても、この企画(きかく)は進(すす)めます。 (b) 一人(ひとり)でやることになったのに、この企画(きかく)を進(すす)めています。

(a) is a pledge made before the fact. The speaker imagines the worst version of the situation - being left to do it alone - grants it, and says the plan goes ahead regardless. There is no complaint in it; if anything it sounds determined.

(b) reports what did happen. The speaker was in fact left alone with the project and is carrying on, and のに colours that with feeling: admiration if said about someone else, and something close to a grumble if said about oneself.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも

Meaning · no matter how much - turn the amount up as far as you like, the result still does not follow

いくら is the question word for a price: "how much?" Put it in front of a ても clause and it stops asking and starts conceding. Whatever quantity of effort, time, money, patience or intensity you want to imagine, the sentence says the outcome is unaffected.

On this batch's axis - how much the speaker minds the gap between expectation and outcome - いくら～ても sits in the middle. たとえ～ても grants one supposition and shrugs. いくら～ても grants an unlimited amount of effort and usually reports that it was wasted, so frustration leaks in even when no complaint is stated.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Formation

いくら is an adverb. It stands at the head of the clause and the ても does the conjugating, exactly as at N4. でも is not a separate word to learn: it is simply what ても looks like after a noun or a な-adjective.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - でも here is the noun's て-form plus も, nothing else. い-adjectives already have their own て-form, so they take くても and never でも: ✅いくら寒(さむ)くても, ❌いくら寒(さむ)いでも.

Note - いくら counts. It needs something that can be piled up: repetitions, hours, yen, or a degree on a scale. That is why it fits いくら待(ま)っても ("however long I wait") but not a one-shot yes-or-no supposition, where たとえ is the word you want.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Examples

いくら呼(よ)んでも、猫(ねこ)は出(で)てこなかった。
No matter how much I called, the cat wouldn't come out.

いくら寒(さむ)くても、弟(おとうと)は上着(うわぎ)を着(き)ない。
However cold it gets, my little brother won't put a coat on.

いくら好(す)きでも、毎日(まいにち)カレーだと飽(あ)きるよ。
However much you like it, curry every single day gets old.

いくら考(かんが)えても、いい題名(だいめい)が思(おも)いつかなかった。
However long I thought about it, no good title came to me.

いくら練習(れんしゅう)しても、発音(はつおん)だけはなかなか直(なお)らない。
No matter how much I practise, my pronunciation is the one thing that won't improve.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Examples (cont.)

いくら急(いそ)いでも、この渋滞(じゅうたい)では終電(しゅうでん)に間(ま)に合(あ)いそうにない。
However much we hurry, in this traffic we're not going to make the last train.

いくら部長(ぶちょう)に頼(たの)まれても、土曜日(どようび)の出勤(しゅっきん)はお断(ことわ)りするつもりです。
However hard the manager presses me, I intend to say no to working Saturday.

いくらなんでも、三日(みっか)で百(ひゃく)ページの翻訳(ほんやく)は無理(むり)ですよ。
A hundred pages of translation in three days is asking too much, surely.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Nuance & register

いくら～ても is thoroughly colloquial and completely neutral as to politeness. It appears in ます speech and in plain speech unchanged, and nothing about it is male, female, young or old.

What it is marked for is futility. The commonest ending by far is a negative or a disappointing result: 来(こ)なかった, 直(なお)らない, 分(わ)からない, ダメだった. A learner who uses いくら～ても with a happy outcome will usually be misunderstood, because the pattern has been carrying resignation since the first word. When the ending is positive it is nearly always a statement of refusal or resolve - as in the Saturday example above - which is still the speaker pushing back against pressure.

Two set expressions worth memorising whole:

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • いくらなんでも - "surely, that's beyond reason." Not "no matter what": it is a
    protest against something excessive, and it needs a judgement after it (ひどい, 無理だ, ～すぎる).
  • いくら～からといって - "just because ~". いくら安(やす)いからといって、三(さん)つも買(か)うことはない。

In writing, どんなに and the stiffer いかに often replace いくら; a formal report is more likely to say いかに努力(どりょく)しても than いくら努力(どりょく)しても. In speech the proportion reverses.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · いくら～ても vs どんなに～ても

Rule of thumb - いくら counts an amount (money, time, tries, effort); どんなに measures an intensity (how hard, how painful, how much someone cared). They overlap in most sentences; where a literal quantity is in view, いくら is the natural word, and where feeling is the point, どんなに is.

いくらお金(かね)を出(だ)しても、あの絵(え)は手(て)に入(はい)らないだろう。
However much money you put up, that painting isn't going to be yours.

どんなに悔(くや)しくても、判定(はんてい)に文句(もんく)を言(い)ってはいけない。
However bitter you feel about it, you don't argue with the decision.

Swap them and both sentences still parse; the second just loses some of its warmth. One thing only どんなに can do is head an exclamation: どんなに心配(しんぱい)したことか - "you have no idea how worried I was." いくら has no such use.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · いくら～ても vs たとえ～ても

Rule of thumb - たとえ concedes whether something is so; いくら concedes how much of it there is. Ask whether the clause has a dial you can turn up.

たとえ中止(ちゅうし)になっても、会場(かいじょう)の準備(じゅんび)はしておきます。
Even if it gets cancelled, I'll have the venue set up.

Cancellation has no degrees - it either happens or it does not - so いくら would be strange there. Compare a clause with a dial:

いくら準備(じゅんび)しても、当日(とうじつ)は必(かなら)ず何(なに)か起(お)きる。
However much you prepare, something always goes wrong on the day.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · いくら～ても vs いくらでも / いくらか

Rule of thumb - いくらでも on its own, with no ても clause around it, is not concessive at all. It means "as much as you like."

おかわりはいくらでもどうぞ。
Help yourself to as many refills as you want.

薬(くすり)を飲(の)んだら、いくらか楽(らく)になりました。
After the medicine I felt somewhat better.

Three different words sharing a stem: いくら～ても concedes, いくらでも offers an unlimited amount, and いくらか means "some, a bit." Only the first has a verb in ても form after it.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Common mistakes

  • ❌ いくら高(たか)いでも買(か)います → ✅ いくら高(たか)くても買(か)います
    でも is the noun form. い-adjectives make their own て-form in くて.

  • ❌ いくら走(はし)ったても → ✅ いくら走(はし)っても
    Nothing is ever marked for tense in front of ても; the final verb carries the time.

  • ❌ いくら練習(れんしゅう)しても、上手(じょうず)になりました → ✅ たくさん練習(れんしゅう)したので、上手(じょうず)になりました
    いくら～ても promises that the effort did not pay off. With a result that matches the effort, the pattern contradicts itself.

  • ❌ いくらでも説明(せつめい)しても、伝(つた)わらなかった → ✅ いくら説明(せつめい)しても、伝(つた)わらなかった
    でも and ても are the same slot; you cannot have both. いくらでも belongs to a different construction entirely.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Practice 1

1. いくら待(ま)っ____、バスは来(こ)なかった。

いくら待(ま)っても、バスは来(こ)なかった。

待(ま)つ has the て-form 待(ま)って, so the concessive is 待(ま)っても. The negative ending is typical: いくら～ても almost always reports that the effort came to nothing.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Practice 2

2. Choose: （いくら・たとえ）一日(いちにち)遅(おく)れても、品質(ひんしつ)は落(お)としたくない。

"One day late" is a single fixed supposition, not an amount being turned up, so たとえ fits. いくら would need something gradable - いくら遅(おく)れても would mean "however late it ends up being", which is a different sentence.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "No matter how many times I read it, I don't understand this contract."

いくら読(よ)んでも、この契約書(けいやくしょ)は理解(りかい)できない。

"No matter how many times" is a count, which is いくら's natural territory - 何回(なんかい)読(よ)んでも works too and is a shade more concrete. The negative main clause is what the pattern expects.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: いくらなんでも、彼(かれ)は来(こ)なかった。

✅ いくら待(ま)っても、彼(かれ)は来(こ)なかった。

いくらなんでも is a fixed protest meaning "surely that's too much", and it has to be followed by a judgement such as ひどい or 無理(むり)だ. It cannot serve as the concessive clause of an ordinary report.

いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) いくら説明(せつめい)しても、母(はは)は納得(なっとく)しなかった。 (b) 説明(せつめい)したのに、母(はは)は納得(なっとく)しなかった。

(a) piles up the effort: the speaker explained again and again, at length, and none of it landed. The frustration is aimed at the sheer waste of effort, and the sentence implies several attempts.

(b) reports a single explanation and a disappointing response, and のに puts the speaker's dissatisfaction on the mother rather than on the effort. One explanation should have been enough - that expectation is exactly what のに says was betrayed.

どんなに～ても

Meaning · no matter how - push the intensity as far as it will go, and the result still stands

どんなに asks "to what degree?" and then refuses to wait for an answer. Whatever level of cold, pain, effort, skill or worry you care to imagine, the ても clause grants it and the main clause carries on regardless.

Placed on this batch's axis - how much the speaker minds the gap between expectation and outcome - どんなに～ても is the emotional member of the "even if" branch. たとえ～ても grants a supposition coolly; いくら～ても counts up wasted effort; どんなに～ても reaches for the feeling behind the effort, which is why it is the one that shows up in pledges, song lyrics and speeches.

どんなに～ても · Formation

どんなに is an adverb of degree standing at the head of the clause. All the conjugation happens on the ても, as at N4.

Rule of thumb - どんなに modifies a degree, so it wants a verb or an adjective after it, not a bare noun. ❌どんなに医者(いしゃ)でも → ✅どんなに経験(けいけん)のある医者(いしゃ)でも. When you really do want "even a doctor", いくら or plain でも handles it more comfortably.

どんなに～ても · Examples

どんなに眠(ねむ)くても、歯(は)だけは磨(みが)く。
However sleepy I am, I at least brush my teeth.

どんなに急(いそ)いでも、ここから空港(くうこう)までは一時間(いちじかん)かかります。
However much we rush, it's an hour from here to the airport.

No matter how hard we try to talk her round, my grandmother won't go to the hospital.

どんなに辛(つら)くても、最後(さいご)まで走(はし)り切(き)ります。
However much it hurts, I'm running this to the finish.

However often he's told off, that child repeats the same prank.

どんなに～ても · Examples (cont.)

どんなに高価(こうか)な道具(どうぐ)でも、使(つか)わなければただの荷物(にもつ)だ。
However expensive the tools, unused they're just so much luggage.

どんなに忙(いそが)しくても、娘(むすめ)の話(はなし)を聞(き)く時間(じかん)だけは作(つく)るようにしている。

However good the terms, I can't take a job that means living apart from my family.

どんなに～ても · Nuance & register

どんなに is neutral for politeness - it sits happily in ます speech and in plain speech - but it is not neutral for temperature. It reaches for feeling. A weather forecast says いくら, a wedding speech says どんなに.

That warmth gives it a use いくら does not have: the bare exclamation, with no concessive clause at all.

合格(ごうかく)したと聞(き)いて、どんなに嬉(うれ)しかったことか。
You can't imagine how glad I was to hear you'd passed.

どんなに～ことか and どんなに～だろう are both this exclamatory use. They are distinctly written or heartfelt-spoken; nobody says them while ordering lunch.

Register neighbours, roughly from stiff to slangy - treat these as tendencies rather than fixed rules:

どんなに～ても · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • いかに～ても - formal writing, reports, editorials.
  • どんなに～ても - the general-purpose word, speech and writing alike.
  • どれだけ～ても - colloquial, and slightly more about quantity.
  • どんだけ～ても - markedly casual, common in younger speech, out of place at work.

Finally, note the main clauses どんなに attracts. Roughly half are resigned (無理(むり)だ, 変(か)わらない) and half are defiant (やめない, 続(つづ)ける). What they have in common is that the speaker has already taken a position on the outcome.

どんなに～ても · どんなに～ても vs いくら～ても

Rule of thumb - いくら counts; どんなに feels. Where the clause is about a literal amount of money, time or repetitions, いくら is the plainer choice; where it is about how intensely something is experienced, どんなに is.

いくら払(はら)っても、この古(ふる)い車(くるま)は直(なお)らなかった。
However much I paid, the old car never got fixed.

どんなに悲(かな)しくても、人前(ひとまえ)では泣(な)かない人(ひと)だった。
However sad she was, she was not one to cry in front of others.

Most sentences accept either word. The two places they part company are the literal price tag, where いくら is idiomatic, and the exclamation どんなに～ことか, which いくら cannot form at all.

どんなに～ても · どんなに～ても vs どんな～でも

Rule of thumb - どんなに + degree + ても = "no matter how much." どんな + noun + でも = "any ~ at all." One syllable apart, completely different sentences.

姉(あね)はどんな料理(りょうり)でも作(つく)れます。
My sister can cook anything you like.

どんなに料理(りょうり)を習(なら)っても、母(はは)の味(あじ)にはならない。
However many cooking lessons I take, it never tastes the way my mother's did.

The first ranges over kinds of thing; the second ranges over degrees of effort. Remember that どんな needs a noun immediately after it, so if there is no noun there, the word you want is どんなに.

どんなに～ても · どんなに～ても vs question word + ても

Rule of thumb - どんなに concedes degree only. To concede who, what, when or where, put that question word in front of the ても clause instead.

だれが来(き)ても、この部屋(へや)には入(い)れないでください。
Whoever turns up, don't let them into this room.

何(なに)を言(い)われても、決(き)めたことは変(か)えません。
Whatever I'm told, I'm not changing what I've decided.

The frame is identical - question word, ても, unmoved main clause - and どんなに is simply the member of the set that handles "how much." They stack, too: だれが何(なに)を言(い)おうと。

どんなに～ても · Common mistakes

  • ❌ どんなに人(ひと)でも間違(まちが)える → ✅ どんなに優秀(ゆうしゅう)な人(ひと)でも間違(まちが)える
    どんなに needs something gradable to modify, and a bare noun gives it nothing to measure. Either supply an adjective, or drop to plain どんな人(ひと)でも, which means "anyone at all."

  • ❌ どんなに大変(たいへん)くても → ✅ どんなに大変(たいへん)でも
    大変(たいへん) is a な-adjective, so its て-form is 大変(たいへん)で. Only い-adjectives take くても.

  • ❌ どんなに頑張(がんば)るても → ✅ どんなに頑張(がんば)っても
    も attaches to the て-form, never to the dictionary form.

  • ❌ どんなに説明(せつめい)しても、やっと分(わ)かってくれた → ✅ 何度(なんど)も説明(せつめい)して、やっと分(わ)かってくれた
    The whole point of どんなに～ても is that the effort fails. If it finally worked, you need an ordinary て-form linking cause to result.

どんなに～ても · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) どんなに待(ま)っても、返事(へんじ)は来(こ)なかった。 (b) どんなに待(ま)ったことか。

～にしても

Meaning · even if; even granting that - the speaker accepts the other side's point and objects anyway

にしても picks up a reason, an excuse or a possibility that is already on the table, grants it, and then says it is not enough. Almost every にしても sentence has an argument going on inside it: fine, suppose that is true - even so, this still does not follow.

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, にしても is the reasonable complainer. のに blurts out its dissatisfaction; くせに sneers; にしても concedes first and criticises second, which is why it is the concession you hear in meetings.

～にしても · Formation

にしても follows a plain form - a whole clause, with its own tense - and nouns and な-adjectives attach bare, with no だ and no な.

Rule of thumb - no だ and no な before にしても: ✅学生(がくせい)にしても, ❌学生(がくせい)だにしても. If you want the copula visible, the formal option is である: 学生(がくせい)であるにしても.

I'd have kept quiet too, in the same position.

Even if I do buy a new computer, I'm going to wait until next month's bonus.

All the same - isn't this summer dragging on rather?

安全(あんぜん)を優先(ゆうせん)するにしても、この規則(きそく)は厳(きび)しすぎるのではないでしょうか。
Granting that safety comes first, isn't this rule far too strict?

～にしても · Nuance & register

にしても is the vocabulary of argument conducted politely. It concedes in order to earn the right to object, which makes it a staple of meetings, negotiations, letters of complaint and mild parental scolding. Because the concession comes first, the objection that follows lands as reasonable rather than hostile - this is a genuinely useful piece of adult Japanese.

The main clause is where the speaker's feeling lives, and it is usually one of:

  • a criticism - ～すぎる, ひどい, おかしい;
  • a minimum demand - ぐらいは～てほしい, ～べきだ;
  • a limit on a plan - それでも～は変(か)えない.

A flat, neutral report after にしても sounds unfinished, because the listener is waiting for the objection the concession promised.

～にしても · Nuance & register (cont.)

Its family, roughly by register:

  • としても - the most clearly hypothetical: "even supposing." Neutral.
  • にしても - concedes something already raised, real or supposed. Neutral.
  • にしろ - the same, a shade curt; common in spoken instructions.
  • にせよ - formal and written; documents and speeches.

それにしても is worth learning as a single word. It re-opens a topic to comment on its degree: 「遅(おそ)いね。」「うん、それにしても遅(おそ)すぎるよ。」 It is not conceding anything specific - it just says "granting everything we have said, still."

～にしても · ～にしても vs ～ても

Rule of thumb - ても attaches to a te-form and states a bare concession. にしても attaches to a whole plain clause, so it can carry past tense and nouns, and it concedes a claim someone has actually made.

雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っても、畑(はたけ)の水(みず)やりは必要(ひつよう)です。
Even if it rains, the field still needs watering.

雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ったにしても、これだけしか降(ふ)らなければ同(おな)じことです。
Granted it rained - but this little makes no difference.

Notice what the second one does that the first cannot: it keeps the past tense (降(ふ)った) and it answers somebody. ても cannot take tense at all, and it does not imply a conversation.

～にしても · ～にしても vs ～にしては

Rule of thumb - one syllable, two different sentences. にしても = "even granting X, Y is still not acceptable." にしては = "measured against X, Y is surprising." にしても complains; にしては evaluates.

初心者(しょしんしゃ)にしては、ずいぶん上手(じょうず)に削(けず)れていますね。
For a beginner, that's remarkably well planed.

初心者(しょしんしゃ)にしても、安全(あんぜん)な持(も)ち方(かた)は習(なら)っておくべきだ。
Beginner or not, he ought to have learned how to hold it safely.

The first is a compliment: the standard "beginner" predicts little, and the result beat it. The second is a reproach: being a beginner is offered as an excuse, accepted, and ruled insufficient.

～にしても · ～にしても vs ～のに

Rule of thumb - のに reports a fact that disappointed the speaker. にしても concedes a premise - which may be a fact or merely a supposition - and then argues from it.

熱(ねつ)があったのに、最後(さいご)まで司会(しかい)を続(つづ)けた。
She had a temperature and still chaired the whole thing.

熱(ねつ)があったにしても、一言(ひとこと)言(い)ってくれればよかったのに。
Even with a temperature, she could have said something.

The first admires a fact. The second takes the same fact as an excuse and pushes back on it. If you find yourself simply reporting something surprising with no argument attached, のに is the word.

～にしても · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 学生(がくせい)だにしても → ✅ 学生(がくせい)にしても
    Nouns and な-adjectives attach bare. だ never appears before にしても; the formal alternative is である.

  • ❌ 忙(いそが)しいにしても、妹(いもうと)は手伝(てつだ)いに来(き)てくれた → ✅ 忙(いそが)しいのに、妹(いもうと)は手伝(てつだ)いに来(き)てくれた
    にしても sets up an objection. When you are grateful rather than dissatisfied, the fact-reporting のに is what you want.

  • ❌ この字(じ)は子(こ)どもが書(か)いたにしても、きれいだ → ✅ この字(じ)は子(こ)どもが書(か)いたにしては、きれいだ
    You are measuring the handwriting against the standard "written by a child", so the particle is は, not も.

  • ❌ 行(い)くにしても、行(い)かないにも、返事(へんじ)をください → ✅ 行(い)くにしても、行(い)かないにしても、返事(へんじ)をください
    In the paired "whether A or B" frame, both halves take the full にしても.

～といっても

Meaning · although I say ~ - the word just used promises more than the reality delivers

といっても takes the label that has just been put on something - "I can cook", "it's near the station", "he's a company president" - and immediately cuts it down to size. The listener has drawn a conclusion from the label, and the speaker heads that conclusion off before it settles.

On this batch's axis, といっても is not really about minding at all. のに and くせに are unhappy about a gap between expectation and outcome. といっても is correcting one, usually the speaker's own, and it is more often modest or wry than annoyed.

～といっても · Formation

といっても follows a plain form or a bare noun. The と is the quotation particle, so what precedes it is treated as a quoted claim.

Attaches to Form Example

Verb (past) plain past + といっても 優勝(ゆうしょう)した → 優勝(ゆうしょう)したといっても
い-adjective plain form + といっても 寒(さむ)い → 寒(さむ)いといっても
な-adjective stem (+ だ) + といっても 便利(べんり) → 便利(べんり)（だ）といっても
Noun Noun (+ だ) + といっても 旅行(りょこう) → 旅行(りょこう)（だ）といっても

Rule of thumb - the clause after といっても must shrink what came before it, so it very often contains だけ, ぐらい, ほんの, たった, or たいしたことはない. If your second clause adds new information instead of scaling the first one down, といっても is the wrong join.

Note - the same shape appears as a stand-alone connector at the head of a sentence. 「来週(らいしゅう)引(ひ)っ越(こ)します。といっても、同(おな)じ 町(まち)の中(なか)ですが。」 - "We're moving next week. Only within the same town, mind."

I say I can cook, but it's about as far as frying an egg.

It's close to the station, but that still means a fifteen-minute walk.

～といっても · Nuance & register

といっても is register-neutral and equally at home in speech and writing, but it is strongly associated with one social move: modest self-presentation. Japanese conversation makes a habit of naming an achievement and deflating it in the same breath, and といっても is the hinge that deflation turns on. Learners who say 「日本語(にほんご)が話(はな)せます」 and stop can sound like they are boasting; 「話(はな)せるといっても、まだ簡単(かんたん)な会話(かいわ)だけですが」 lands much better.

Turned outward, it becomes a gentle correction of somebody else's overstatement, as in the "you say you're busy" example above. Said sharply it can needle, so keep the tone light - a ね or けど at the end helps.

Casual contractions are everywhere in speech:

～といっても · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • っていっても - the everyday spoken shape after a vowel:
    「無理(むり)っていっても、やるしかないよ。」
  • つっても - very casual, more common in men's relaxed speech.

Two neighbours to keep straight. そうはいっても ("even so") pushes back on what was said instead of shrinking it. とはいえ does much the same work as といっても but belongs to written and formal register - you will meet it in editorials, not in chat.

～といっても · ～といっても vs ～けど

Rule of thumb - けど simply joins two clauses that lean opposite ways. といっても says something more specific: the word I just used was too big.

給料(きゅうりょう)は上(あ)がったけど、残業(ざんぎょう)も増(ふ)えた。
My pay went up, but so did the overtime.

給料(きゅうりょう)が上(あ)がったといっても、月(つき)に二千円(にせんえん)ほどです。
My pay went up - by about two thousand yen a month.

The けど sentence adds a second, separate fact. The といっても sentence stays on the first fact and shows you it is smaller than it sounded. Swap them and the けど version loses nothing; the といっても version stops making sense, because "the overtime increased" does not measure the pay rise.

～といっても · ～といっても vs ～というより

Rule of thumb - といっても keeps the label and shrinks it. というより rejects the label and offers a better one.

趣味(しゅみ)といっても、月(つき)に一度(いちど)触(さわ)る程度(ていど)です。
It's a hobby, if you can call something I touch once a month a hobby.

あれは趣味(しゅみ)というより、もう仕事(しごと)ですね。
That's not so much a hobby as a job by now.

In the first the thing is still a hobby, just a thin one. In the second the word "hobby" is withdrawn and replaced.

～といっても · ～といっても vs そうはいっても

Rule of thumb - といっても deflates the claim. そうはいっても accepts the claim and refuses the conclusion drawn from it.

安(やす)いといっても、中古(ちゅうこ)なら当然(とうぜん)でしょう。
Cheap, sure - but it's second-hand, so of course it is.

忙(いそが)しいのはわかります。そうはいっても、締(し)め切(き)りは動(うご)かせません。
I know you're busy. Even so, the deadline can't move.

そうはいっても needs something to point back at, so it opens a sentence rather than joining one. In formal writing the equivalent is とはいえ.

～といっても · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っているといっても、傘(かさ)をささない → ✅ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っているのに、傘(かさ)をささない
    といっても needs a claim or label in front of it to cut down. A plain observed fact contradicted by someone's behaviour is のに territory.

  • ❌ 英語(えいご)が話(はな)せるといっても、毎日(まいにち)勉強(べんきょう)しています → ✅ 英語(えいご)が話(はな)せるといっても、挨拶(あいさつ)ぐらいです
    The second clause has to measure the first one downward. Adding an unrelated fact leaves the pattern with nothing to do.

  • ❌ 高(たか)いといっても、思(おも)い切(き)って買(か)いました → ✅ 高(たか)かったけど、思(おも)い切(き)って買(か)いました
    "It was expensive but I bought it anyway" is a plain adversative join. といっても would mean "expensive, but not as expensive as you think."

  • ❌ 忙(いそが)しいいっても → ✅ 忙(いそが)しいといっても
    The と is the quotation particle and cannot be dropped. In fast speech it fuses into っていっても, but it is never simply absent.

～といっても · Practice 1

1. 新築(しんちく)____、建(た)ってから三年(さんねん)経(た)っています。

新築(しんちく)といっても、建(た)ってから三年(さんねん)経(た)っています。

A bare noun attaches directly. "Newly built" is the label; the second clause shows the label is generous, which is exactly the shrinking move といっても exists for.

～といっても · Practice 2

2. Choose: 彼(かれ)のスピーチは説明(せつめい)（といっても・というより）自慢(じまん)だった。

彼(かれ)のスピーチは説明(せつめい)というより自慢(じまん)だった。

The sentence throws the word "explanation" out and puts "boasting" in its place, so it needs というより. といっても would keep it an explanation and merely say it was a small one.

～といっても · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I lived in Kyoto - though only for six months."

京都(きょうと)に住(す)んでいました。といっても、半年(はんとし)だけですが。

Here といっても opens a second sentence as a connector, which is very common in speech. You could also write it as one sentence: 京都(きょうと)に住(す)んでいたといっても、半年(はんとし)だけです。

～といっても · Practice 4

～といっても · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部長(ぶちょう)といっても、部下(ぶか)は二人(ふたり)だけです。 (b) 部長(ぶちょう)なのに、部下(ぶか)は二人(ふたり)だけです。

(a) is modest and matter-of-fact. The speaker anticipates that "department manager" sounds grander than it is and trims the picture themselves; there is no complaint in it, and it is a natural thing to say about one's own job.

(b) is dissatisfied. のに treats the small team as something that should not go with the title - the speaker feels short-changed, or is criticising whoever made that decision.

ところが

Meaning · however; but then - what actually happened defeated what everyone was expecting

ところが starts a new sentence and drops the floor out from under the previous one. The first sentence sets up an expectation; ところが announces that reality went the other way. Crucially, the thing that went the other way has already happened - ところが reports outcomes, never plans.

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, ところが is close to neutral. It marks surprise, not dissatisfaction. The turn can be for the worse, as it usually is in a complaint, but it can equally be for the better - a cheap hotel that turned out to be lovely takes ところが just as happily.

ところが · Formation

ところが is a conjunction, not a suffix, so there is nothing for it to attach to in the usual sense. What matters is the shape of the sentences on either side of it.

Position Form Example
Between two sentences Sentence。ところが、Sentence。 出(で)かけた。ところが、閉(し)まっていた。
Head of a spoken turn ところが、… ところが、それが違(ちが)ったんです
Inside one sentence (written) Verb plain past + ところが 頼(たの)んだ → 頼(たの)んだところが
What may follow it a realized fact only ✅閉(し)まっていた ❌閉(し)めましょう

ところが · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - nothing forward-looking can follow ところが. No volitional, no request, no invitation, no command. ❌ところが、明日(あした)行(い)きましょう is simply not available; that sentence needs しかし or でも.

Note - the written ～たところが packs the same reversal into a single sentence: 問(と)い合(あ)わせたところが、担当者(たんとうしゃ)は 退職(たいしょく)していた。 It always follows a past verb, and it belongs to reports and essays rather than conversation.

ところが · Examples

傘(かさ)を持(も)って出(で)かけた。ところが、一滴(いってき)も降(ふ)らなかった。
I took an umbrella out with me. Not a drop fell.

楽(たの)しみにしていました。ところが、当日(とうじつ)熱(ねつ)を出(だ)してしまいました。
I'd been looking forward to it. Then on the day itself I came down with a fever.

簡単(かんたん)な仕事(しごと)だと聞(き)いていた。ところが、実際(じっさい)には三日(みっか)かかった。
I'd been told it was an easy job. In fact it took three days.

安(やす)いホテルを予約(よやく)した。ところが、部屋(へや)は想像(そうぞう)よりずっと広(ひろ)かった。
I booked a cheap hotel. The room turned out to be far bigger than I'd pictured.

ところが · Examples (cont.)

開店(かいてん)前(まえ)から並(なら)んだ。ところが、目当(めあ)てのパンはもう売(う)り切(き)れていた。
I queued from before opening time. The bread I'd come for was already sold out.

洗濯機(せんたくき)を静(しず)かなものに買(か)い替(か)えました。ところが、音(おと)は前(まえ)のより大(おお)きいんです。
I replaced the washing machine with a quiet model. It's noisier than the old one.

山田(やまだ)さんなら知(し)っているだろうと思(おも)って聞(き)いてみた。ところが、彼(かれ)も首(くび)をかしげるばかりだった。
I asked Yamada, thinking he was bound to know. All he did was tilt his head at me.

課長(かちょう)に相談(そうだん)してみたところが、その場(ば)ですぐに決(き)めてくださった。
When I took it to the section chief, he settled it on the spot.

ところが · Nuance & register

ところが is the storyteller's word. It carries no politeness of its own - it appears unchanged before ます sentences and plain ones - but it does presuppose a narrative: something was set up, and now it collapses. That is why it so often turns up with のだ／んです after it. The speaker is not just reporting the reversal, they are explaining it to you.

Because the reversal has to be real, ところが is overwhelmingly followed by past tense or by ている. A present-tense fact is possible when it is a discovered state (音(おと)は前(まえ)のより大(おお)きいんです), but a future or a decision is not.

Neighbours in the "but" family:

ところが · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • しかし - neutral, all-purpose, no surprise required; fine in writing and speech.
  • でも - the casual everyday one, and the only one that comfortably opens a
    disagreement with the person you are talking to.
  • ところが - narrative reversal, expectation defeated, outcome already in.
  • それが - the colloquial cousin of ところが, used to answer a question whose
    premise has failed: 「来(き)ましたか。」「それが、来(こ)なかったんです。」

One more thing ところが does not do: it does not disagree with your listener. If someone has just stated an opinion and you want to push back, でも or そうはいっても is what you need. ところが argues with the world, not with people.

ところが · ところが vs しかし・でも

Rule of thumb - しかし and でも can be followed by anything, including a plan or a request. ところが can only be followed by a fact that has already turned out.

雨(あめ)が降(ふ)り出(だ)した。でも、予定(よてい)どおり出発(しゅっぱつ)しましょう。
It's started raining. Let's set off as planned anyway.

雨(あめ)が降(ふ)り出(だ)した。ところが、誰(だれ)も傘(かさ)を持(も)っていなかった。
It started raining. Nobody had an umbrella.

Replace でも with ところが in the first and the sentence breaks: a suggestion is not something that "turned out." The second works with either word, but ところが adds the click of an expectation failing, which でも leaves out.

ところが · ところが vs ところで

Rule of thumb - one syllable apart, and unrelated. ところが = "but then." ところで = "by the way", a change of topic.

会議(かいぎ)は終(お)わりました。ところで、この後(あと)時間(じかん)ありますか。
The meeting's over. By the way, are you free after this?

会議(かいぎ)は三時(さんじ)に終(お)わるはずだった。ところが、五時(ごじ)まで続(つづ)いた。
The meeting was supposed to finish at three. It ran until five.

Note also the unrelated ～たところで ("even if you were to…"), which is a concessive and belongs to a later level. Three similar strings, three separate words: check the final syllable and whether a full stop precedes.

ところが · ところが vs ～のに

Rule of thumb - のに lives inside a sentence and carries the speaker's dissatisfaction. ところが stands between sentences and carries surprise, which may be pleasant.

一時間(いちじかん)も待(ま)ったのに、連絡(れんらく)ひとつなかった。
I waited a whole hour and didn't get so much as a message.

一時間(いちじかん)待(ま)つ覚悟(かくご)で店(みせ)に行(い)った。ところが、すぐに座(すわ)れた。
I went to the restaurant braced for an hour's wait. We were seated straight away.

Try putting のに in the second one and it turns sour - のに would suggest the speaker resents being seated quickly. That is the difference in one line: のに minds, ところが merely did not see it coming.

ところが · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 道(みち)が混(こ)んでいる。ところが、電車(でんしゃ)で行(い)きましょう。 → ✅ 道(みち)が混(こ)んでいる。でも、電車(でんしゃ)で行(い)きましょう。
    A suggestion cannot follow ところが, which reports only what has already turned out.

  • ❌ ところが、コーヒーでも飲(の)みませんか。 → ✅ ところで、コーヒーでも飲(の)みませんか。
    Changing the subject is ところで. The が version announces a reversal, which is not what a change of topic is.

  • ❌ 安(やす)いところが、買(か)わなかった。 → ✅ 安(やす)かったけれど、買(か)わなかった。
    As a reversal marker ところが needs a full stop in front of it. The only in-sentence form is ～たところが, which requires a past verb and belongs to written style.

ところが · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 姉(あね)は朝型(あさがた)だ。ところが、妹(いもうと)は夜型(よるがた)だ。 → ✅ 姉(あね)は朝型(あさがた)だ。一方(いっぽう)、妹(いもうと)は夜型(よるがた)だ。
    Two things merely differing is a comparison, not a defeated expectation. ところが needs something to have gone wrong against a prediction.

ところが · Practice 1

1. 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)前(まえ)に着(つ)いた。____、会場(かいじょう)はまだ開(あ)いていなかった。

三十分(さんじゅっぷん)前(まえ)に着(つ)いた。ところが、会場(かいじょう)はまだ開(あ)いていなかった。

Arriving early sets up the expectation of getting in early, and the second sentence reports that reality defeated it. Both halves are finished facts in the past, which is what ところが requires.

資料(しりょう)はそろいました。ところで、印刷(いんさつ)は何部(なんぶ)必要(ひつよう)ですか。

The second sentence opens a new topic and asks a question - nothing has been contradicted. A question about a plan also cannot follow ところが, which admits only outcomes that have already happened.

ところが · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I saved for a year. But then the price went up."

一年(いちねん)貯金(ちょきん)しました。ところが、値段(ねだん)が上(あ)がってしまいました。

Saving up sets an expectation of being able to buy; the price rise cancels it. The てしまいました ending suits ところが well, because it adds the speaker's dismay at an outcome they did not control.

ところが · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 予約(よやく)が取(と)れなかった。ところが、来週(らいしゅう)また電話(でんわ)してみるつもりだ。

✅ 予約(よやく)が取(と)れなかった。でも、来週(らいしゅう)また電話(でんわ)してみるつもりだ。

つもりだ states an intention, and intentions are forward-looking, so ところが cannot introduce them. Swapping in でも or しかし fixes it; alternatively keep ところが and follow it with what actually happened.

ところが · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 説明書(せつめいしょ)のとおりに組(く)み立(た)てた。ところが、部品(ぶひん)が一(ひと)つ余(あま)った。 (b) 説明書(せつめいしょ)のとおりに組(く)み立(た)てたのに、部品(ぶひん)が一(ひと)つ余(あま)った。

(a) tells it as a small story: here is what I did, and here is the turn nobody saw coming. The two halves stand as separate sentences, so the reversal gets its own beat, and the tone can be amused as easily as annoyed.

(b) packs the same content into one sentence and adds feeling. のに says the speaker followed the instructions and feels let down by the result - there is a hint of blame, aimed at the instructions or at themselves.

～どころか

Meaning · far from ~; never mind ~ - the word just offered is not merely wrong, it points the wrong way

どころか rejects a description and replaces it with one further along the scale, or on the other side of it altogether. Somebody expects an apology and gets laughter; somebody expects a ten-minute wait and waits an hour. どころか says: that is not the half of it.

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, どころか is the emphatic end. のに reports a gap between expectation and outcome; どころか says the gap runs in the opposite direction from the one you assumed, and it usually says so with feeling.

～どころか · Formation

どころか attaches to a plain form or a bare noun. Nouns and な-adjectives take no だ and no な.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + どころか 謝(あやま)る → 謝(あやま)るどころか
Verb (negative) ない-form + どころか 減(へ)らない → 減(へ)らないどころか
い-adjective plain form + どころか 安(やす)い → 安(やす)いどころか
な-adjective stem + どころか 不便(ふべん) → 不便(ふべん)どころか
Noun Noun + どころか 休憩(きゅうけい) → 休憩(きゅうけい)どころか
"not even" frame A どころか B + も／さえ + negative 漢字(かんじ)どころかひらがなも読(よ)めない

～どころか · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - whatever follows どころか must contradict or overshoot what precedes it. A second clause that simply agrees with the first leaves the pattern with nothing to reject: ❌下(さ)がるどころか少(すこ)し下(さ)がった, ✅下(さ)がるどころかかえって上(あ)がった.

Note - in the "not even" frame the order looks backwards to English eyes. The bigger item goes before どころか and the smaller one after it, with a negative at the end: 漢字(かんじ)どころか、ひらがなも読(よ)めない - "never mind kanji, he can't even read hiragana."

～どころか · Examples

彼(かれ)は謝(あやま)るどころか、笑(わら)っていた。
Far from apologising, he was laughing.

漢字(かんじ)どころか、ひらがなもまだ覚(おぼ)えていません。
Never mind kanji - I haven't even learned hiragana yet.

休(やす)むどころか、昼(ひる)ごはんも食(た)べていないんです。
Rest? I haven't even had lunch.

十分(じゅっぷん)どころか、一時間(いちじかん)以上(いじょう)待(ま)たされました。
Ten minutes? I was kept waiting over an hour.

薬(くすり)を飲(の)んだのに、よくなるどころか熱(ねつ)が上(あ)がってしまった。
I took the medicine, and instead of getting better my temperature went up.

～どころか · Examples (cont.)

あの店(みせ)は不便(ふべん)どころか、駅(えき)の真(ま)ん前(まえ)にありますよ。
Inconvenient? That shop is right in front of the station.

台風(たいふう)のせいで、旅行(りょこう)どころか、外(そと)に出(で)ることもできなかった。
Because of the typhoon we couldn't go outside, let alone take our trip.

後輩(こうはい)に注意(ちゅうい)したら、反省(はんせい)するどころか、口(くち)を利(き)いてくれなくなった。
I had a word with my junior colleague, and rather than take it on board he stopped speaking to me.

～どころか · Nuance & register

どころか is neutral for politeness and appears in です・ます speech and in writing without changing, but it is far from neutral in temperature. It is a correction delivered with force, and it nearly always carries one of three feelings: indignation ("far from apologising…"), astonishment ("an hour, not ten minutes"), or self-deprecation ("never mind kanji…"). Said flatly it can sound like you are contradicting the listener, so in careful conversation people soften it with よ, ね, or an explanatory んです.

Both directions are available and it is worth noticing which one you are in:

  • Downward - the reality falls short of the label:
    上達(じょうたつ)するどころか、下手(へた)になった気(き)がする。
  • Upward - the label is too modest for the reality:
    上手(じょうず)どころか、プロと間違(まちが)えられるほどだ。

～どころか · Nuance & register (cont.)

The related どころではない looks similar and does something else: it says the situation rules the thing out entirely. 「今(いま)は旅行(りょこう)どころではない」 - "this is no time for travelling." Notice that it ends the sentence, while どころか is a join.

～どころか · ～どころか vs ～ばかりか・～だけでなく

Rule of thumb - ばかりか and だけでなく accept the first item and add a second. どころか rejects the first item as an inadequate description.

祖父(そふ)は英語(えいご)だけでなく、ロシア語(ご)も話(はな)します。
My grandfather speaks not only English but Russian too.

祖父(そふ)は英語(えいご)どころか、自分(じぶん)の名前(なまえ)もローマ字(じ)で書(か)けません。
Never mind English - my grandfather can't even write his own name in roman letters.

In the first, the English is real and the Russian is extra. In the second, the English is thrown out as absurdly optimistic. That is why どころか in the "not even" frame almost always ends in a negative, and だけでなく almost never does.

～どころか · ～どころか vs ところが

Rule of thumb - どころか joins two descriptions inside one sentence and denies the first. ところが joins two sentences and reports that events took an unexpected turn.

新(あたら)しい靴(くつ)を買(か)った。ところが、一日(いちにち)で靴(くつ)ずれができた。
I bought new shoes. Within a day they'd given me blisters.

その靴(くつ)は楽(らく)になるどころか、履(は)くたびに痛(いた)くなる。
Far from breaking in, those shoes hurt more every time I wear them.

ところが needs a full stop before it and a finished event after it; どころか sits mid-sentence and needs two competing descriptions of the same thing.

～どころか · ～どころか vs ～どころではない

Rule of thumb - どころか continues the sentence and offers the truth. どころではない ends it and says the thing is out of the question.

引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの準備(じゅんび)で、映画(えいが)を見(み)るどころではない。
With the move to get ready, watching a film is out of the question.

引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの準備(じゅんび)で、映画(えいが)どころか、買(か)い物(もの)にも行(い)けていない。
What with the move, I haven't even managed the shopping, never mind a film.

The first is a verdict on the situation. The second still describes what is and is not happening, and it needs its second half.

～どころか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 漢字(かんじ)どころか、ひらがなも読(よ)めます → ✅ 漢字(かんじ)どころか、ひらがなも読(よ)めません
    The "never mind A" frame is built on a negative. With an affirmative ending there is nothing being ruled out and the sentence collapses.

  • ❌ 値段(ねだん)が下(さ)がるどころか、少(すこ)し下(さ)がった → ✅ 値段(ねだん)が下(さ)がるどころか、かえって上(あ)がった
    What follows どころか has to run counter to what precedes it. Repeating the same direction, even weakly, gives the pattern nothing to reject.

  • ❌ 一時間(いちじかん)待(ま)った。どころか、彼(かれ)は来(こ)なかった。 → ✅ 一時間(いちじかん)待(ま)った。ところが、彼(かれ)は来(こ)なかった。
    どころか cannot start a sentence. A narrative reversal between two sentences is ところが.

～どころか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 今日(きょう)は残業(ざんぎょう)で、飲(の)み会(かい)どころか。 → ✅ 今日(きょう)は残業(ざんぎょう)で、飲(の)み会(かい)どころではない。
    どころか is a join and needs a second half. To end the sentence with "that's out of the question", use どころではない.

～どころか · Practice 1

1. 貯金(ちょきん)____、今月(こんげつ)の家賃(やちん)も払(はら)えない。

貯金(ちょきん)どころか、今月(こんげつ)の家賃(やちん)も払(はら)えない。

The bigger, more ambitious item - saving - goes first and is dismissed; the smaller one follows with も and a negative. That ordering is the fixed shape of the "never mind A" frame.

～どころか · Practice 2

2. Choose: 弟(おとうと)はギター（どころか・だけでなく）ピアノも弾(ひ)けます。

弟(おとうと)はギターだけでなくピアノも弾(ひ)けます。

Both instruments are genuinely played, so the first item is being accepted and the second added - that is だけでなく. With どころか the sentence would have to deny the guitar, and it would need a negative ending.

～どころか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Far from thanking me, she got angry."

彼女(かのじょ)はお礼(れい)を言(い)うどころか、怒(おこ)り出(だ)した。

The plain form 言(い)う takes どころか directly, and the second clause runs in the opposite direction to the first. This is the indignant use, and it is the commonest one in speech.

～どころか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 試験(しけん)が近(ちか)いので、遊(あそ)びに行(い)くどころか。

✅ 試験(しけん)が近(ちか)いので、遊(あそ)びに行(い)くどころではない。

どころか is a connector: something has to follow it. When you want to end on "that is out of the question", the form is どころではない (or the more casual どころじゃない).

～どころか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部屋(へや)が片付(かたづ)くどころか、前(まえ)より散(ち)らかってしまった。 (b) 部屋(へや)を片付(かたづ)けたのに、また散(ち)らかってしまった。

(a) denies the expected direction outright: the tidying did not merely fail, it made things worse. どころか is doing the work of "far from", and the sentence carries a note of exasperation at how completely the expectation was inverted.

(b) reports two facts - the room was tidied, and it is messy again - and colours the mismatch with the speaker's dissatisfaction. Nothing is being denied; のに simply says the second half should not have followed the first.

～くせに

Meaning · even though; and yet - said about a person, with a curl of the lip

くせに marks the same expectation-versus-outcome gap that のに marks, and then adds something のに does not have: a judgement on the person. Whoever the sentence is about should have behaved otherwise, and the speaker is holding it against them.

Two restrictions come with that. The clause has to be about a person, and both halves of the sentence have to be about the same person. Those two facts are most of what there is to learn here, and they are what stops くせに from being a free substitute for のに.

～くせに · Formation

くせに follows a plain form. It began life as the noun くせ ("a habit, a trait"), which is why nouns link to it with の and な-adjectives with な - the same links a noun takes.

～くせに · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + くせに 知(し)っている → 知(し)っているくせに
Verb (past) plain past + くせに 断(ことわ)った → 断(ことわ)ったくせに
Verb (negative) ない-form + くせに できない → できないくせに
い-adjective plain form + くせに 若(わか)い → 若(わか)いくせに
な-adjective な + くせに 下手(へた) → 下手(へた)なくせに
Noun の + くせに 新人(しんじん) → 新人(しんじん)のくせに
Sentence-final jab …くせに。 分(わ)かっているくせに。
Blunter colloquial variant …くせして 知(し)らないくせして

～くせに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - one person, both clauses. ✅ 彼(かれ)は手伝(てつだ)ってもらったくせに、お礼(れい)も言(い)わない。 ❌ 私(わたし)が手伝(てつだ)ったくせに、彼(かれ)はお礼(れい)も言(い)わない。 The second sentence has two different subjects, so it must use のに. のに never cares who is doing what; くせに is passing judgement on one individual and cannot split its attention.

Note - the subject also has to be a person. Textbook Japanese does not allow ❌この道(みち)は広(ひろ)いくせに暗(くら)い. In casual grumbling you will hear people stretch it to a shop, a phone or a piece of software they are cross with, which works precisely because it treats the object as somebody misbehaving. Keep it for people until you have a feel for that.

～くせに · Examples

知(し)っているくせに、何(なに)も教(おし)えてくれない。
He knows perfectly well and won't tell me a thing.

下手(へた)なくせに、文句(もんく)ばかり言(い)っている。
He's no good at it himself, and all he does is complain.

自分(じぶん)も遅(おく)れてきたくせに、人(ひと)の遅刻(ちこく)には厳(きび)しい。
He turned up late himself, yet he's merciless about other people being late.

甘(あま)い物(もの)は嫌(きら)いだと言(い)っていたくせに、ケーキを三(みっ)つも食(た)べた。
He said he didn't like sweet things - then ate three slices of cake.

運転(うんてん)できないくせに、助手席(じょしゅせき)でうるさく指示(しじ)を出(だ)す。
He can't drive himself, but he barks directions from the passenger seat.

～くせに · Examples (cont.)

あんなに反対(はんたい)していたくせに、今(いま)では誰(だれ)よりも楽(たの)しんでいる。
She was so against it, and now she's enjoying it more than any of us.

本当(ほんとう)は寂(さび)しいくせに、一人(ひとり)のほうが楽(らく)だなんて言(い)っている。
He's lonely really, and there he is saying he prefers being on his own.

自分(じぶん)では一円(いちえん)も出(だ)していないくせに、店(みせ)の選(えら)び方(かた)にだけは口(くち)を出(だ)してくる。
He hasn't put in a single yen, and yet he has plenty to say about which restaurant we pick.

～くせに · Nuance & register

くせに is never neutral. Where のに ranges from mild surprise to real irritation, くせに starts at criticism and goes down from there: blame, scorn, resentment. Its politeness ceiling is low. You do not use it about a customer, a teacher, a manager or anyone else you owe deference to, and it does not belong in written Japanese outside dialogue.

Within that range it has two quite different lives:

  • Aimed at someone absent - a complaint, and it can sound genuinely mean.
  • Aimed at a friend, face to face - teasing, and it softens almost to affection.
    「泣(な)いてたくせに。」 said with a grin between friends is nothing worse than "you were crying, though."

The sentence-final version, with the main clause simply left off, is common in casual speech: 「本当(ほんとう)は行(い)きたいくせに。」 It works like a raised eyebrow - the listener knows what was left unsaid.

～くせに · Nuance & register (cont.)

くせして is the same word, blunter and more colloquial. くせに is already strong; くせして is stronger, and rarer in mixed company.

A note about 子(こ)どものくせに and 新人(しんじん)のくせに. These fixed scoldings are still heard, but they land as high-handed and are drifting out of acceptable workplace speech. Recognise them; think twice before producing them.

Finally, くせに can be turned on yourself, and then it becomes self-mockery: 泳(およ)げないくせに、深(ふか)いところまで行(い)ってしまった。 The contempt is still there - the speaker has simply aimed it at their own behaviour.

～くせに · ～くせに vs ～のに

Rule of thumb - のに reports a gap and expresses the speaker's dissatisfaction. くせに blames a person for the gap, and needs the same person in both clauses. Every くせに sentence could be のに; the reverse is not true.

私(わたし)が何度(なんど)も誘(さそ)ったのに、弟(おとうと)は一度(いちど)も来(こ)なかった。
I invited him again and again, and my brother never came once.

Two subjects - I invited, he didn't come - so くせに is unavailable no matter how annoyed the speaker is. Now put both halves on one person:

弟(おとうと)は自分(じぶん)から誘(さそ)ったくせに、当日(とうじつ)になって断(ことわ)ってきた。
My brother was the one who suggested it, and then cancelled on the day.

～くせに · ～くせに vs ～のに (cont.)

Even where both are possible, the feeling differs. 遠(とお)いのに来(き)てくれた is gratitude; ❌遠(とお)いくせに来(き)てくれた is not a sentence anyone would say, because you cannot sneer and thank someone in the same breath.

～くせに · ～くせに vs ～にもかかわらず

Rule of thumb - same gap, opposite register. にもかかわらず is formal, written and emotionally flat; くせに is spoken and hostile. のに sits between them.

にもかかわらず takes a plain form or a bare noun: 雨天(うてん)にもかかわらず、大勢(おおぜい)が集(あつ)まった。

悪天候(あくてんこう)にもかかわらず、開会式(かいかいしき)は予定(よてい)どおり行(おこな)われた。
Despite the poor weather, the opening ceremony went ahead as scheduled.

天気(てんき)が悪(わる)いと文句(もんく)を言(い)っていたくせに、一番(いちばん)早(はや)く来(き)ていた。
For all his moaning about the weather, he was the first one there.

～くせに · ～くせに vs ～にもかかわらず (cont.)

Notice that the formal version happily takes a non-human subject - the weather - which くせに cannot. Reports, notices and speeches use にもかかわらず precisely because it registers the contradiction without any attitude attached.

～くせに · ～くせに vs ～ながら（も）

Rule of thumb - ながらも concedes something about a person without attacking them, and often about the speaker themselves. It is literary where くせに is colloquial.

狭(せま)い部屋(へや)ながらも、一人(ひとり)で暮(く)らせるのが嬉(うれ)しかった。
Cramped as the room was, I was glad to be living on my own.

狭(せま)い部屋(へや)に住(す)んでいるくせに、家具(かぐ)ばかり買(か)っている。
He lives in a cramped little room and still keeps buying furniture.

ながらも accepts the drawback and moves on; くせに uses it as ammunition. ながらも also has no same-subject-must-be-human requirement of the kind くせに imposes, which is one reason it survives in formal writing.

～くせに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 学生(がくせい)くせに → ✅ 学生(がくせい)のくせに
    くせ is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs の. な-adjectives need な: ✅下手(へた)なくせに, ❌下手(へた)だくせに.

  • ❌ 母(はは)が作(つく)ったくせに、兄(あに)は食(た)べなかった → ✅ 母(はは)が作(つく)ったのに、兄(あに)は食(た)べなかった
    Two different people, so くせに is out. This is the single commonest くせに error, and it produces a sentence native speakers cannot parse.

  • ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)はお忙(いそが)しいくせに、話(はなし)を聞(き)いてくださった → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)はお忙(いそが)しいのに、話(はなし)を聞(き)いてくださった
    くせに sneers, so it cannot coexist with honorific language or with gratitude. Aimed upward it is close to an insult.

  • ❌ この電車(でんしゃ)は新(あたら)しいくせに遅(おそ)い → ✅ この電車(でんしゃ)は新(あたら)しいのに遅(おそ)い
    Standard くせに wants a person to blame. A train can be disappointing, but it cannot be shameless.

～くせに · Practice 1

1. 山田(やまだ)さんは料理(りょうり)ができない____、レストランの味(あじ)に厳(きび)しい。

Both clauses describe Yamada, and the speaker clearly thinks he has no right to be fussy - a person, one subject, and a judgement. That is exactly the shape くせに wants. The ない-form attaches directly.

2. Choose: 台風(たいふう)が近(ちか)づいていた（くせに・にもかかわらず）、試合(しあい)は中止(ちゅうし)にならなかった。

The subject of the first clause is a typhoon, and the two clauses have different subjects besides. にもかかわらず accepts both, and its flat, formal tone suits a report of what the organisers decided.

～くせに · Practice 3

One person does both things - claims disinterest, then reads it all - and the English "and yet" carries a knowing dig. のに would also be grammatical; くせに is what gives it the teasing edge.

～くせに · Practice 4

The breaking and the telling-off belong to different people, and くせに cannot span two subjects. If you want to keep くせに, rewrite so that one person carries both halves: 弟(おとうと)は自分(じぶん)で割(わ)ったくせに、黙(だま)っていた。

～くせに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 新入社員(しんにゅうしゃいん)なのに、よく気(き)がつく。 (b) 新入社員(しんにゅうしゃいん)のくせに、口(くち)の利(き)き方(かた)を知(し)らない。

(a) is のに used for pleasant surprise: newness predicted less, and the speaker is impressed. くせに could not replace it here, because you cannot sneer at somebody while praising them.

(b) is くせに doing what it does - using the person's junior status as grounds for looking down on them. It is grammatical and it is also the kind of remark that dates the speaker; のに would report the same gap without the contempt.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも

Meaning · although; despite - a quality is granted, and something that does not fit it is asserted anyway

This is the second ながら. The one you met at N4 stacks two actions on top of each other ("while eating"); this one concedes. It grants a fact - the room is small, he knew the risk, she is only a child - and then states something that the fact should have ruled out.

The two are told apart by shape, not by guesswork. Concessive ながら attaches to い-adjectives, な-adjective stems, nouns and ている forms, none of which the "while" ながら can take. And ながらも, with the extra も, is always concessive.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Overview

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, concessive ながら minds least of all. It is dignified rather than annoyed, and it is often outright warm: 狭(せま)いながらも楽(たの)しい我(わ)が家(や) is a compliment to a small flat.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb of knowing／thinking ます-stem + ながら 知(し)る → 知(し)りながら
Verb in ている てい + ながら（も） 分(わ)かっている → 分(わ)かっていながら
い-adjective plain form + ながら（も） 狭(せま)い → 狭(せま)いながらも
な-adjective stem + ながら（も） 残念(ざんねん) → 残念(ざんねん)ながら
Noun Noun + ながら（も） 子(こ)ども → 子(こ)どもながら
Noun + に (adverbial) Noun + ながらに 涙(なみだ) → 涙(なみだ)ながらに
Existence, fixed いながらにして 家(いえ)にいながらにして

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - な-adjectives and nouns attach bare: ✅ 残念(ざんねん)ながら, ❌残念(ざんねん)なながら, ❌子(こ)どもだながら. There is no な and no だ in front of this ながら.

Note - いながら is just ～ている plus ながら with the て swallowed in speech and writing alike: 知(し)っていながら. It is the standard way to concede that somebody was aware and acted anyway, and it is noticeably sharper than a plain のに because awareness is built into it.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Examples

狭(せま)いながらも、居心地(いごこち)のいい部屋(へや)です。
It's a small room, but a comfortable one.

残念(ざんねん)ながら、今回(こんかい)は見送(みおく)らせていただきます。
Regrettably, we'll be passing on it this time.

危険(きけん)だと知(し)りながら、誰(だれ)も注意(ちゅうい)しようとしなかった。
Everyone knew it was dangerous, and no one would say anything.

体(からだ)に悪(わる)いと分(わ)かっていながら、なかなかやめられません。
I know full well it's bad for me, and still I can't quite stop.

小(ちい)さな店(みせ)ながら、常連(じょうれん)が全国(ぜんこく)にいるそうだ。
Small as it is, the shop apparently has regulars all over the country.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Examples (cont.)

子(こ)どもながらに、母(はは)が無理(むり)をしていることは感(かん)じていた。
Young as I was, I could tell my mother was pushing herself too hard.

貧(まず)しいながらも、家族(かぞく)全員(ぜんいん)がよく笑(わら)う家(いえ)だった。
Poor though we were, ours was a house where everybody laughed a lot.

家(いえ)にいながらにして、世界中(せかいじゅう)の授業(じゅぎょう)が受(う)けられる時代(じだい)になりました。
We've reached an age where you can take classes from anywhere in the world without leaving home.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Nuance & register

Concessive ながら is written and formal. In an essay, a notice, a speech or a carefully worded email it is entirely ordinary; in relaxed conversation it survives mainly as a handful of set phrases, and using it freely in chat sounds like you have swallowed a textbook.

The set phrases are worth learning as vocabulary, because they are extremely common:

  • 残念(ざんねん)ながら - "regrettably", the standard soft refusal.
  • 失礼(しつれい)ながら - "if I may be so bold", before a delicate question.
  • 恥(は)ずかしながら - "I'm ashamed to say."
  • 我(われ)ながら - "even if I say so myself."
  • いつもながら - "as ever."
  • 生(う)まれながら（に） - "from birth, innately."

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Nuance & register (cont.)

ながらも is the emphatic version. The も makes the concession unmistakable, which is useful precisely because bare ながら could be read as "while". It also adds a faint note of admiration, which is why it collocates so happily with things that are small, poor or difficult but turn out well.

いながらにして is a fixed expression meaning "without stirring from where you are", and it has drifted into technology writing: 自宅(じたく)にいながらにして 診察(しんさつ)が受(う)けられる.

Concessive ～ながら vs simultaneous ～ながら

Rule of thumb - look at what is in front of it. An adjective, a noun or a ている form can only be the concessive one. A plain action verb in ます-stem form is normally the "while" one, unless the verb is about knowing or thinking.

音楽(おんがく)を聞(き)きながら、洗(あら)い物(もの)をしています。
I do the washing up with music on.

失敗(しっぱい)すると思(おも)いながら、止(と)めなかった。
I thought it would go wrong, and I didn't stop him.

Both take a ます-stem, and the second is concessive only because 思(おも)う is a verb of mental attitude - you cannot "do thinking while" in the way you can do listening while. When in doubt, add も: 思(おも)いながらも can only mean "although."

Concessive ～ながら vs simultaneous ～ながら (cont.)

The "while" ながら also demands two activities that last long enough to overlap; the concessive one has no such requirement, because nothing is overlapping.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · ～ながらも vs ～のに

Rule of thumb - same contradiction, different voice. のに is spoken and carries the speaker's dissatisfaction. ながらも is written and carries composure, and often respect.

初(はじ)めてながらも、落(お)ち着(つ)いて司会(しかい)を務(つと)めていた。
It was his first time, and yet he chaired it calmly.

初(はじ)めてなのに、ずいぶん落(お)ち着(つ)いていたね。
For a first go you were awfully calm, weren't you.

The ながらも version would suit a written report of the event; the のに version is what you say to the person afterwards. Note the small formal difference too: のに needs な after a noun, ながらも needs nothing.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · ～ながらも vs ～つつも

Rule of thumb - near twins, both literary. つつも attaches only to a ます-stem, so it cannot concede an adjective or a noun; ながらも can.

無理(むり)だと思(おも)いつつも、もう一度(いちど)応募(おうぼ)してみた。
Even while thinking it was hopeless, I applied one more time.

不安(ふあん)ながらも、自分(じぶん)で決(き)めた道(みち)を選(えら)んだ。
Uneasy though I was, I chose the path I'd decided on myself.

つつも is the more bookish of the two and is almost always about an inner conflict - the speaker knew better and did it anyway. ながらも covers a much wider range, including plain external facts like a room being small.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 知(し)るながら → ✅ 知(し)りながら
    ながら takes the ます-stem, exactly as the "while" version does. The dictionary form never appears before it.

  • ❌ 残念(ざんねん)なながら → ✅ 残念(ざんねん)ながら
    な-adjectives attach by the bare stem. The same goes for nouns: ✅子(こ)どもながら, ❌子(こ)どもだながら.

  • ❌ 残念(ざんねん)ながらも、今回(こんかい)は中止(ちゅうし)です → ✅ 残念(ざんねん)ながら、今回(こんかい)は中止(ちゅうし)です
    残念(ざんねん)ながら is a frozen adverb meaning "regrettably" and does not take the extra も. The も version belongs to live concessions such as 狭(せま)いながらも.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 毎日(まいにち)勉強(べんきょう)しながら、成績(せいせき)が上(あ)がらない → ✅ 毎日(まいにち)勉強(べんきょう)しているのに、成績(せいせき)が上(あ)がらない
    With an ordinary action verb, ながら is read as "while", and "while studying, my grades don't rise" is not a sentence. Use のに, or make the awareness explicit with 勉強(べんきょう)していながら.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Practice 1

い-adjectives attach in their plain form, and the も makes the concessive reading unmistakable. The sentence has the classic ながらも flavour: a drawback granted, and something admirable asserted in spite of it.

2. Choose: 悪(わる)いと（知(し)りながら・知(し)っていながら）、彼(かれ)は報告(ほうこく)しなかった。

Both are possible, and 知(し)っていながら is the stronger choice.

知(し)りながら concedes the knowledge in passing; the ている version says the knowledge was already settled in his mind before he acted, which is what makes the failure to report culpable. Written reports and criticism favour ～ていながら for exactly that reason.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Regrettably, the concert has been cancelled."

残念(ざんねん)ながら、コンサートは中止(ちゅうし)になりました。

残念(ざんねん)ながら is the fixed adverbial phrase used to deliver bad news politely, and it opens the sentence. Note that no な and no も appear - adding either breaks the set phrase.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この町(まち)は不便(ふべん)なながら、人(ひと)が温(あたた)かい。

✅ この町(まち)は不便(ふべん)ながらも、人(ひと)が温(あたた)かい。

不便(ふべん) is a な-adjective and attaches by its bare stem, so the な has to go. Adding も is optional but natural here, since the sentence is conceding a drawback in order to praise something else.

～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 体(からだ)に悪(わる)いと知(し)っていながら、夜中(よなか)にラーメンを食(た)べてしまう。 (b) 体(からだ)に悪(わる)いと知(し)っているくせに、夜中(よなか)にラーメンを食(た)べている。

(a) is measured and slightly literary. The speaker concedes their own awareness and reports the lapse; てしまう adds rueful self-awareness rather than blame. It could appear in an essay unchanged.

(b) is spoken and accusing. くせに turns the same awareness into grounds for contempt, so this version is aimed at somebody else - and if it is aimed at the speaker, it is deliberate self-mockery.

～にしては

Meaning · for ~; considering it's ~ - measured against this standard, the result is not what you would predict

にしては names a yardstick and then reports that the thing being judged does not fit it. A first-year student who writes a firm hand; a March day that feels like May; a first attempt that came out well. The yardstick is real and specific, and the result overshoots or undershoots it.

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, にしては sits at the mild end alongside わりに. It is an evaluation, not a complaint - most often a compliment, occasionally a gentle dig, and almost never anything sharper.

～にしては · Formation

にしては attaches to a noun or to a plain-form verb clause. Nothing intervenes: no だ, no な.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + にしては 一年生(いちねんせい) → 一年生(いちねんせい)にしては
Noun of time／season Noun + にしては 三月(さんがつ) → 三月(さんがつ)にしては
Verb plain form + にしては 習(なら)う → 習(なら)うにしては
Verb (past) plain past + にしては 作(つく)った → 作(つく)ったにしては
Verb (ている) ている + にしては 練習(れんしゅう)している → 練習(れんしゅう)しているにしては
Referring back それにしては - → それにしては元気(げんき)だ

～にしては · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the standard must be specific enough to predict something. ✅この値段(ねだん)にしては, ❌値段(ねだん)にしては. A bare "price" predicts nothing; this price predicts a great deal. When the standard is general rather than definite, the pattern you want is わりに.

Note - い-adjectives and な-adjectives do not attach to にしては directly. ❌寒(さむ)いにしては → ✅寒(さむ)い日(ひ)にしては, or switch to 寒(さむ)いわりに, which takes adjectives happily.

～にしては · Examples

一年生(いちねんせい)にしては、しっかりした字(じ)を書(か)きますね。
For a first-year, she writes a very steady hand.

三月(さんがつ)にしては暖(あたた)かい日(ひ)が続(つづ)いています。
It's been unusually warm for March.

初(はじ)めて作(つく)ったにしては、よくできていますよ。
For a first attempt, that's turned out well.

この値段(ねだん)にしては、生地(きじ)がしっかりしている。
For this price, the fabric is remarkably solid.

十年(じゅうねん)乗(の)ったにしては、傷(きず)が少(すく)ないですね。
For a car that's been driven ten years, there are hardly any scratches.

～にしては · Examples (cont.)

日本(にほん)に来(き)たばかりだと言(い)っていたにしては、敬語(けいご)がとても自然(しぜん)だ。
Considering he said he'd only just arrived in Japan, his honorific language is very natural.

大(おお)きな地震(じしん)だったにしては、被害(ひがい)は少(すく)なくて済(す)んだ。
For an earthquake that size, the damage was mercifully limited.

急(きゅう)に決(き)めたにしては、旅行(りょこう)の計画(けいかく)が細(こま)かいところまでよくできている。
For something decided at the last minute, the trip is planned down to the details.

～にしては · Nuance & register

にしては is register-neutral. It appears unchanged in ます speech, in plain speech and in writing, and nothing about it is stiff or slangy. It pairs naturally with degree adverbs - ずいぶん, なかなか, けっこう, 意外(いがい)に - because the whole point of the sentence is that the result is off the predicted mark.

What you do have to watch is the social arithmetic. にしては says out loud that your expectations for the standard were low. That is fine for a beginner, a first attempt or the weather, and it is not fine aimed upward at a person: 「先生(せんせい)にしてはお上手(じょうず)ですね」 tells your teacher you assumed teachers were bad at this. With superiors, praise them without the yardstick, or use さすが - 「さすが先生(せんせい)ですね」 - which says the opposite: exactly what one would expect.

～にしては · Nuance & register (cont.)

それにしては is the stand-alone version, pointing back at something already said: 「徹夜(てつや)したんです。」「それにしては元気(げんき)ですね。」 Note that this is それにしても with one particle changed, and the meanings diverge completely - see the next section.

～にしては · ～にしては vs ～わりに

Rule of thumb - にしては needs a definite standard; わりに accepts a general one and attaches to adjectives as well. Where both fit, they mean much the same thing.

このかばんは値段(ねだん)のわりにじょうぶだ。
This bag is sturdy for what it cost.

三千円(さんぜんえん)のかばんにしてはじょうぶだ。
For a three-thousand-yen bag, it's sturdy.

Both are natural. The difference is that わりに can leave "price" as an abstract scale, while にしては wants an actual figure or an actual category to measure against. That is also why わりに handles adjectives - 安(やす)いわりに, 若(わか)いわりに - and にしては does not.

～にしては · ～にしては vs ～にしても

Rule of thumb - は evaluates, も concedes. にしては says "measured against X, this is surprising." にしても says "even granting X, this still is not good enough."

初日(しょにち)にしては、よく売(う)れたほうだと思(おも)います。
For an opening day, I'd say it sold well.

初日(しょにち)にしても、この数字(すうじ)は少(すく)なすぎませんか。
Opening day or not, isn't this figure far too low?

Same noun, same day, opposite verdicts. One particle decides whether you are praising the result or refusing to accept the excuse.

～にしては · ～にしては vs ～のに

Rule of thumb - のに needs a whole clause and carries the speaker's dissatisfaction. にしては takes a bare noun as well, and carries mild surprise at how the measurement came out.

一週間(いっしゅうかん)しか練習(れんしゅう)していないにしては、上手(じょうず)に弾(ひ)けている。
For someone who's only practised a week, she plays well.

一週間(いっしゅうかん)も練習(れんしゅう)したのに、まだうまく弾(ひ)けない。
I practised for a whole week and I still can't play it properly.

The first weighs the result against the effort and finds it impressive. The second weighs the same two things and feels cheated. If the sentence is a compliment, にしては is nearly always the safer choice; のに used for praise needs the rest of the sentence to make the warmth obvious.

～にしては · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 学生(がくせい)だにしては → ✅ 学生(がくせい)にしては
    Nouns attach bare. The だ here comes from thinking of にしては as following a complete sentence, which it does not have to.

  • ❌ プロ野球(やきゅう)選手(せんしゅ)にしては足(あし)が速(はや)い → ✅ プロ野球(やきゅう)選手(せんしゅ)だけあって足(あし)が速(はや)い
    A professional athlete being fast is exactly what the standard predicts, and にしては requires the result to defy the standard. When the result confirms it, the pattern is だけあって.

  • ❌ 値段(ねだん)にしては安(やす)い → ✅ この値段(ねだん)にしては安(やす)い ／ 値段(ねだん)のわりに安(やす)い
    A bare "price" is not a definite standard. Either pin it down with この, or switch to わりに.

  • ❌ 寒(さむ)いにしては、人(ひと)が多(おお)い → ✅ 寒(さむ)いわりに、人(ひと)が多(おお)い
    い-adjectives do not attach to にしては. Use わりに, or supply a noun: 寒(さむ)い日(ひ)にしては.

～にしては · Practice 1

1. 三日(みっか)で覚(おぼ)えた____、よく発音(はつおん)できていますね。

三日(みっか)で覚(おぼ)えたにしては、よく発音(はつおん)できていますね。

The plain past attaches directly. Three days of learning predicts shaky pronunciation, the result beats that prediction, and the sentence lands as a compliment - the standard use of にしては.

～にしては · Practice 2

2. Choose: 新人(しんじん)（にしては・にしても）、落(お)ち着(つ)いて話(はな)していた。

新人(しんじん)にしては、落(お)ち着(つ)いて話(はな)していた。

The sentence praises the person: judged by the low bar "new employee", the composure is surprising. にしても would set up an objection instead, and would need a critical main clause such as 説明(せつめい)が雑(ざつ)すぎる.

～にしては · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "For a Sunday, the station is quiet."

日曜日(にちようび)にしては、駅(えき)が静(しず)かですね。

日曜日(にちようび) is a definite standard that predicts crowds, and the station fails to live up to it. Days of the week and months are among the most common nouns to appear with にしては.

～にしては · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 高(たか)いにしては、この椅子(いす)は座(すわ)り心地(ごこち)がよくない。

✅ 高(たか)いわりに、この椅子(いす)は座(すわ)り心地(ごこち)がよくない。

An い-adjective cannot attach to にしては. わりに takes adjectives directly and carries the same "measured against that, this is unexpected" meaning. Keeping にしては would mean naming a definite standard instead: この値段(ねだん)にしては.

～にしては · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 子(こ)どもが作(つく)ったにしては、よくできている。 (b) 子(こ)どもが作(つく)ったのに、よくできている。

(a) is the natural sentence. It sets "made by a child" as the yardstick, and reports that the result clears it. The praise is measured against a deliberately low bar, which is exactly what にしては announces.

(b) is odd, and if anything faintly rude. のに treats the two facts as being in conflict and adds the speaker's feeling about that conflict - which here comes out as "a child made it, and it has no business being this good." Where a comparison against a standard is what you mean, にしては is the pattern.

～わりに

Meaning · for ~; considering ~ - the result is out of proportion to what the premise leads you to expect

The わり in わりに is the noun meaning "proportion, ratio", and that is exactly what the pattern measures. Set one fact against another - the price against the quality, the age against the speed, the hours put in against the marks that came back - and わりに says the two do not scale together.

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, わりに is mild, like にしては. It notices a mismatch and comments on it. Whether the comment is admiring or slightly disappointed depends entirely on which way the mismatch runs.

～わりに · Formation

わりに behaves like the noun it came from: nouns link with の, な-adjectives with な. Adding は - わりには - is optional and very common.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + の + わりに（は） 年(とし) → 年(とし)のわりに
Verb plain form + わりに（は） よく食(た)べる → よく食(た)べるわりに
Verb (past) plain past + わりに（は） 練習(れんしゅう)した → 練習(れんしゅう)したわりに
Verb (negative) ない-form + わりに（は） 寝(ね)ていない → 寝(ね)ていないわりに
い-adjective plain form + わりに（は） 安(やす)い → 安(やす)いわりに
な-adjective な + わりに（は） 地味(じみ) → 地味(じみ)なわりに
Adverb (different word) わりに／わりと + adjective わりとおいしい

～わりに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the two halves must be out of proportion. If the result is what the premise predicts, わりに has nothing to say: ❌たくさん練習(れんしゅう)したわりに上手(じょうず)になった. Effort matching result is だけあって.

Note - usually written in kana, though 割(わり)に is not wrong. Adding は sharpens the contrast a little: 安(やす)いわりに simply notes the mismatch, while 安(やす)いわりには leans on it. Both are entirely ordinary.

～わりに · Examples

この店(みせ)は安(やす)いわりに、味(あじ)がいい。
This place is good for what it charges.

父(ちち)は年(とし)のわりに足(あし)が速(はや)い。
My father is quick on his feet for his age.

妹(いもうと)はよく食(た)べるわりに、全然(ぜんぜん)太(ふと)らないんです。
My sister eats a lot and never puts on any weight.

机(つくえ)に向(む)かっている時間(じかん)が長(なが)いわりには、点数(てんすう)が伸(の)びない。
For all the hours he spends at his desk, his marks aren't going up.

この部屋(へや)は駅(えき)から遠(とお)いわりに、家賃(やちん)が高(たか)すぎませんか。
Isn't the rent rather high for somewhere this far from the station?

～わりに · Examples (cont.)

準備(じゅんび)に半年(はんとし)かけたわりには、当日(とうじつ)はあっという間(ま)に終(お)わってしまった。
For six months of preparation, the day itself was over in no time.

あの映画(えいが)は評判(ひょうばん)がいいわりに、席(せき)がずいぶん空(す)いていた。
For a film with such good word of mouth, the cinema was pretty empty.

山(やま)が好(す)きだと言(い)っているわりに、道具(どうぐ)は一(ひと)つも持(も)っていない。
For someone who says he loves the mountains, he doesn't own a single piece of kit.

～わりに · Nuance & register

わりに is colloquial-neutral: at home in conversation, in reviews, in casual writing, and unremarkable in ます speech. It carries no politeness of its own and no strong emotion. What it does carry is a faint air of appraisal - the speaker has weighed two things against each other and is reporting the ratio.

Which direction the appraisal runs is decided by the content, and both are common:

  • In the thing's favour - 安(やす)いわりにおいしい, 年(とし)のわりに元気(げんき)だ.
  • Against it - 高(たか)いわりに量(りょう)が少(すく)ない,
    時間(じかん)をかけたわりに中身(なかみ)がない.

Because the second use is so easy, わりに aimed at a person can sting a little more than にしては does: 「若(わか)いわりに体力(たいりょく)がないね」 is not a compliment. As with にしては, avoid pointing it at someone senior to you.

～わりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Then there is the adverb わりに, more often said as わりと, meaning "fairly, rather": 「今日(きょう)はわりと暖(あたた)かいね。」 It has no second half and compares nothing in particular - it is just a hedge, roughly the strength of けっこう. Same string, different word, and context separates them easily once you know both exist.

～わりに · ～わりに vs ～にしては

Rule of thumb - にしては needs a definite yardstick and usually flatters. わりに takes a general one, attaches to adjectives, and is just as happy pointing out that something under-delivers.

三年(さんねん)習(なら)ったにしては、よく話(はな)せるほうだと思(おも)う。
For three years of lessons, I'd say he speaks well.

三年(さんねん)習(なら)ったわりに、まだ自己(じこ)紹介(しょうかい)しかできない。
For three years of lessons, he can still only introduce himself.

Both take the same clause, and both are grammatical. The give-away is the direction of the verdict: にしては sets a low bar and reports it cleared, so a disappointed にしては sentence sounds strange, while わりに is neutral about which way the imbalance falls. And only わりに can attach to a bare adjective: ✅安(やす)いわりに, ❌安(やす)いにしては.

～わりに · ～わりに vs ～のに

Rule of thumb - わりに weighs two things and finds the ratio off. のに says two facts should not go together at all, and adds the speaker's dissatisfaction. わりに needs a scale; のに does not.

この辞書(じしょ)は厚(あつ)いわりに、軽(かる)い。
This dictionary is light for its thickness.

辞書(じしょ)を買(か)ったのに、一度(いちど)も開(ひら)いていない。
I bought a dictionary and haven't opened it once.

Thickness and weight are two points on scales, so they can be out of proportion - わりに. Buying a dictionary and never opening it is not a matter of proportion at all; it is a plain contradiction, with the speaker's chagrin attached, which is のに.

～わりに · ～わりに (proportion) vs わりと (adverb)

Rule of thumb - if there is something to compare against, it is the proportion わりに and it needs の or a clause in front. If there is nothing to compare against, it is the adverb, and it means "fairly."

この問題(もんだい)はわりと簡単(かんたん)でした。
That question was fairly straightforward.

この問題(もんだい)は見(み)た目(め)のわりに簡単(かんたん)でした。
That question was easier than it looked.

The first tells you only that it was not hard. The second measures the difficulty against the intimidating look of it. In speech わりと is the commoner form of the adverb; わりに on its own can be either word, and the presence or absence of a comparison decides.

～わりに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 年(とし)わりに若(わか)く見(み)える → ✅ 年(とし)のわりに若(わか)く見(み)える
    わり is a noun, so a noun in front of it links with の - exactly like 年(とし)のせいで or 年(とし)の割合(わりあい).

  • ❌ 静(しず)かわりに人(ひと)が多(おお)い → ✅ 静(しず)かなわりに人(ひと)が多(おお)い
    な-adjectives link with な. い-adjectives, by contrast, attach in their plain form with nothing added.

  • ❌ よく寝(ね)たわりに、元気(げんき)になった → ✅ よく寝(ね)ただけあって、元気(げんき)になった
    Sleeping well and feeling well are in proportion, so there is no mismatch for わりに to report.

  • ❌ 値段(ねだん)わりとおいしい → ✅ 値段(ねだん)のわりにおいしい ／ わりとおいしい
    Two different words have been run together. Either compare against the price, with の, or use the bare adverb and compare against nothing.

～わりに · Practice 1

1. 兄(あに)は体(からだ)が大(おお)きい____、力(ちから)はあまりない。

兄(あに)は体(からだ)が大(おお)きいわりに、力(ちから)はあまりない。

Size and strength are two scales that ought to move together, and the sentence reports that they do not. い-adjectives attach in their plain form, so nothing goes between 大(おお)きい and わりに.

～わりに · Practice 2

2. Choose: この旅館(りょかん)は（値段(ねだん)のわりに・値段(ねだん)にしては）料理(りょうり)が立派(りっぱ)だ。

この旅館(りょかん)は値段(ねだん)のわりに料理(りょうり)が立派(りっぱ)だ。

"Price" here is a general scale rather than a definite figure, and にしては needs something definite to measure against. Pin the figure down - この値段(ねだん)にしては or 一万円(いちまんえん)にしては - and にしては becomes available again.

～わりに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "For a small company, they pay surprisingly well."

小(ちい)さい会社(かいしゃ)のわりに、給料(きゅうりょう)がいい。

A noun phrase links with の. You could also say 会社(かいしゃ)の規模(きぼ)のわりに, and 意外(いがい)に before いい would carry the "surprisingly" more explicitly.

～わりに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この町(まち)は有名(ゆうめい)わりに、観光客(かんこうきゃく)が少(すく)ない。

✅ この町(まち)は有名(ゆうめい)なわりに、観光客(かんこうきゃく)が少(すく)ない。

有名(ゆうめい) is a な-adjective, and わりに behaves like a noun, so the link is な. Compare the noun pattern, which links with の: 有名(ゆうめい)さのわりに would be the noun version of the same idea.

～わりに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 時間(じかん)をかけたわりに、いい結果(けっか)が出(で)なかった。 (b) 時間(じかん)をかけたのに、いい結果(けっか)が出(で)なかった。

(a) is an assessment of the ratio: a lot of time went in, not much came out, and the speaker is stating the imbalance more than complaining about it. It would sit comfortably in a written review of a project.

(b) is a complaint. のに says the time invested should have guaranteed a result, and the speaker feels let down - frustration first, arithmetic second. Both are correct Japanese, and the choice tells the listener how much you mind.
