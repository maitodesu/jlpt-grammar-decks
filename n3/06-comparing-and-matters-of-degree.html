～につれて

Meaning · as one thing gradually progresses, another gradually changes with it

～につれて ties two slow changes together and says they move as a pair. Neither side is a single event: something is in the process of increasing, advancing, ageing, warming, and something else shifts along the way. English usually says this with "as", and Japanese wants both halves to be movement along a scale.

～につれて · Formation

The clause before につれて is always non-past, however clearly the sentence is set in the past. Tense is carried by the final verb only.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (change of state) V(dictionary) + につれて 増(ふ)える → 増(ふ)えるにつれて
Verb, formal writing V(dictionary) + につれ 進(すす)む → 進(すす)むにつれ
Noun naming a process Noun + につれて 成長(せいちょう) → 成長(せいちょう)につれて
Noun with no change in it Noun + が + verb + につれて 時間(じかん) → 時間(じかん)がたつにつれて

～につれて · Formation (cont.)

Note - only nouns that already contain the idea of change work directly: 成長(せいちょう), 変化(へんか), 発展(はってん), 回復(かいふく). A plain noun needs a verb first - ❌時間(じかん)につれて → ✅時間(じかん)がたつにつれて.

～につれて · Examples

時間(じかん)がたつにつれて、風(かぜ)が強(つよ)くなった。
As time went on, the wind picked up.

山(やま)を登(のぼ)るにつれて、空気(くうき)が冷(つめ)たくなった。
As we climbed the mountain, the air turned colder.

日(ひ)が短(みじか)くなるにつれて、朝(あさ)起(お)きるのがつらくなる。
As the days get shorter, getting up in the morning gets harder.

試合(しあい)が進(すす)むにつれて、スタンドの声(こえ)が大(おお)きくなっていった。
As the match went on, the crowd got louder and louder.

子(こ)どもの成長(せいちょう)につれて、靴(くつ)のサイズが毎年(まいとし)変(か)わる。
As a child grows, their shoe size changes every year.

～につれて · Examples (cont.)

練習(れんしゅう)を重(かさ)ねるにつれて、指(ゆび)が自然(しぜん)に動(うご)くようになってきた。
As I put in more practice, my fingers started moving on their own.

工事(こうじ)が進(すす)むにつれて、駅前(えきまえ)の景色(けしき)はすっかり変(か)わってしまった。
As the construction went ahead, the area in front of the station changed completely.

人口(じんこう)が減(へ)るにつれ、村(むら)の行事(ぎょうじ)は一(ひと)つずつなくなっていった。
As the population fell, the village's annual events disappeared one by one.

～につれて · Nuance & register

～につれて is neutral in politeness - it sits inside the sentence, so the formality of the page is set by the ending (変(か)わる / 変(か)わります), not by the pattern. What it does carry is a mildly written flavour. It is at home in explanations, reports, narration and the sort of thing a newsreader says; in relaxed conversation people often reach for ～と、だんだん… instead: 夜(よる)になると、だんだん寒(さむ)くなる.

Clipping it to につれ raises the register another notch. That form belongs to written Japanese - newspapers, official notices, essays - and sounds stiff spoken aloud.

～につれて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two habits go with the pattern. The second clause is normally a description of change, so it very often ends in ～くなる, ～ていく or ～てくる. And because the sentence is reporting an observed process, the second clause cannot be a request, a command, an invitation or a statement of intention. "As it gets colder, please close the window" is not a につれて sentence; it is a たら or と sentence.

Finally, the speaker is standing outside watching. ～につれて describes; it does not persuade. If you want the emotional weight of "the more, the more", the correlative ～ば～ほど is the pattern with the pressure in it.

～につれて · ～につれて vs ～に従(したが)って

Rule of thumb - in the parallel-change sense the two are close to interchangeable. に従って additionally has a life につれて does not have at all: following rules, instructions and arrows.

Where both work, につれて describes a change that simply happens, while に従って leans a little towards one thing being led by the other, and is the stiffer of the two:

北(きた)へ進(すす)むにつれて、雪(ゆき)が深(ふか)くなった。
The further north we went, the deeper the snow got.

But only に従って can take an instruction as its object:

係員(かかりいん)の指示(しじ)に従(したが)って外(そと)に出(で)てください。
Please go outside following the attendant's instructions.

～につれて · ～につれて vs ～に従(したが)って (cont.)

❌指示(しじ)につれて is simply not Japanese. If you can replace the English "as" with "in accordance with", you need に従って.

～につれて · ～につれて vs ～とともに

Rule of thumb - につれて needs both sides to be gradual. とともに only needs them to be simultaneous, so it also covers sudden events and plain accompaniment.

春(はる)が近(ちか)づくにつれて、山(やま)の色(いろ)が変(か)わってきた。
As spring drew near, the colour of the mountains began to change.

Swap in とともに there and nothing breaks. But とともに also does this, and につれて cannot:

ベルが鳴(な)るとともに、生徒(せいと)たちが走(はし)り出(だ)した。
The moment the bell rang, the students broke into a run.

The bell is instantaneous - there is no scale to travel along - so ❌鳴(な)るにつれて is wrong. とともに is also the one that means "together with (a person)": 家族(かぞく)とともに暮(く)らす.

～につれて · ～につれて vs ～ば～ほど

Rule of thumb - につれて reports one actual process unfolding. ～ば～ほど states a general proportion, true whenever you test it, and it can be hypothetical.

練習(れんしゅう)するにつれて、発音(はつおん)がよくなってきた。
As I practised, my pronunciation improved.

That is a report on your own past few months. The correlative version makes a claim about the world instead:

発音(はつおん)は練習(れんしゅう)すればするほどよくなる。
The more you practise, the better your pronunciation gets.

The second one can be said by someone who has never practised in their life. It also allows advice and predictions after it, which につれて does not.

～につれて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 電気(でんき)を消(け)すにつれて、部屋(へや)が暗(くら)くなった。 → ✅ 電気(でんき)を消(け)すと、部屋(へや)が暗(くら)くなった。
    Flipping a switch is instantaneous. につれて needs a change that takes time and passes through middle stages.

  • ❌ 時間(じかん)がたったにつれて → ✅ 時間(じかん)がたつにつれて
    The clause before につれて is always non-past. The past tense goes on the end of the sentence.

  • ❌ 暑(あつ)くなるにつれて、水(みず)をたくさん飲(の)んでください。 → ✅ 暑(あつ)くなると、水(みず)をたくさん飲(の)んでください。
    A request cannot follow につれて. The second clause has to describe a change, not tell the listener to do something.

  • ❌ 時間(じかん)につれて、痛(いた)みが消(き)えた。 → ✅ 時間(じかん)がたつにつれて、痛(いた)みが消(き)えていった。
    時間(じかん) is not itself a change. Add the verb that supplies one.

～につれて · Practice 1

1. 年(とし)を____につれて、昔(むかし)の友(とも)だちに会(あ)いたくなる。

年(とし)を取(と)るにつれて、昔(むかし)の友(とも)だちに会(あ)いたくなる。

The verb before につれて stays in the dictionary form, and 年(とし)を取(と)る is the standard collocation for ageing, which is exactly the kind of slow, stage-by-stage change this pattern wants.

～につれて · Practice 2

2. Choose: 地図(ちず)（につれて・に従(したが)って）歩(ある)いたら、海(うみ)に出(で)た。

地図(ちず)に従(したが)って歩(ある)いたら、海(うみ)に出(で)た。

A map is something you obey, not a process that unfolds. につれて only links two gradual changes, so it cannot take a set of directions as its object.

～につれて · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "As the meeting went on, everyone got tired."

会議(かいぎ)が進(すす)むにつれて、みんな疲(つか)れてきた。

Both halves move by degrees, which is what licenses につれて, and the ending ～てきた is the natural way to show the tiredness accumulating rather than arriving all at once.

～につれて · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 駅(えき)に着(つ)くにつれて、雨(あめ)が降(ふ)り出(だ)した。

✅ 駅(えき)に近(ちか)づくにつれて、雨(あめ)が強(つよ)くなった。

着(つ)く happens in an instant, and so does 降(ふ)り出(だ)す - neither side offers a scale. Replacing them with 近(ちか)づく and 強(つよ)くなる gives two gradual changes to link.

～につれて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 人口(じんこう)が増(ふ)えるにつれて、ごみの量(りょう)も増(ふ)えた。 (b) 人口(じんこう)が増(ふ)えれば増(ふ)えるほど、ごみの量(りょう)も増(ふ)える。

(a) is a report about a particular place over a particular stretch of time: the population went up, and the rubbish went up alongside it. It is history.

(b) is a general law with no dates attached - for any town, more people means more rubbish. It would fit in an argument or a warning, and unlike (a) it can be said about a population increase that has not happened yet.

～に従って

Meaning · (1) in accordance with ～, following ～; (2) as ～ progresses, ～ changes with it

～に従(したが)って is the て-form of the verb 従(したが)う, "to follow, to obey", and both of its meanings come straight out of that. Follow a set of instructions and you get sense (1). Let one change follow another and you get sense (2), where it is close to ～につれて. Written as a single sentence-opening word, したがって is a third thing entirely: "therefore".

～に従って · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun: rule, order, model Noun + に従(したが)って 説明書(せつめいしょ) → 説明書(せつめいしょ)に従(したが)って
Noun naming a process Noun + に従(したが)って 時代(じだい)の変化(へんか) → 時代(じだい)の変化(へんか)に従(したが)って
Verb (change of state) V(dictionary) + に従(したが)って 進(すす)む → 進(すす)むに従(したが)って
Formal writing ～に従(したが)い 規則(きそく) → 規則(きそく)に従(したが)い
Modifying a noun ～に従(したが)った + Noun 指示(しじ) → 指示(しじ)に従(したが)った行動(こうどう)

～に従って · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
As an ordinary verb Noun + に従(したが)う ルールに従(したが)う

Note - 従(したが)う is an う-verb (五段(ごだん) / godan / Group 1), so its て-form is 従(したが)って with a small つ. ❌従(したが)て and ❌従(したが)いて are both wrong. As with ～につれて, the clause before it stays non-past.

～に従って · Examples

矢印(やじるし)に従(したが)って進(すす)んでください。
Please go on, following the arrows.

ルールに従(したが)ってゲームをしましょう。
Let's play the game by the rules.

医者(いしゃ)の注意(ちゅうい)に従(したが)って、塩分(えんぶん)を減(へ)らしています。
I'm cutting down on salt as the doctor told me to.

年(とし)を取(と)るに従(したが)って、夜(よ)更(ふ)かしがつらくなってきた。
As I get older, staying up late has started to take its toll.

技術(ぎじゅつ)が進(すす)むに従(したが)って、昔(むかし)の道具(どうぐ)は見(み)かけなくなった。
As technology moved on, the old tools stopped being a common sight.

～に従って · Examples (cont.)

地元(じもと)の習慣(しゅうかん)に従(したが)って、靴(くつ)を脱(ぬ)いで部屋(へや)に上(あ)がった。
Following the local custom, I took off my shoes and stepped up into the room.

法律(ほうりつ)の改正(かいせい)に従(したが)い、来月(らいげつ)から手続(てつづ)きが変(か)わります。
In accordance with the revision of the law, the procedure changes from next month.

マニュアルに従(したが)って操作(そうさ)したはずなのに、機械(きかい)はまったく動(うご)かなかった。
I'm sure I operated it according to the manual, but the machine wouldn't move at all.

～に従って · Nuance & register

This is a formal expression in both of its senses. In sense (1) it carries the weight of the verb it comes from: obeying, complying, submitting. Told to a friend that you cooked レシピに従(したが)って, it sounds oddly solemn - conversation prefers レシピどおりに or レシピを見(み)ながら. Where it belongs is signage, safety announcements, contracts, court reporting and workplace instructions.

Trimming to に従(したが)い is the written-only variant, one register above the て-form and common in notices: 規則(きそく)に従(したが)い処理(しょり)いたします.

Note who is doing the following. に従(したが)って puts the subject in the position of complying with something with authority behind it - a rule, an order, a doctor, a custom. Using it about a mere suggestion overstates the case, and using it about a person can sound like obedience rather than agreement: 部長(ぶちょう)に従(したが)って is "doing as the manager directs", not "along with the manager".

～に従って · Nuance & register (cont.)

In sense (2), the parallel-change use, it is drier and more analytical than ～につれて, and turns up mostly in writing that is explaining a trend. It also has a faint implication that the second change is driven by the first rather than merely accompanying it.

Note - sentence-initially, したがって means "therefore". It is one of the most formal connectives in the language, at home in academic papers and legal writing: 台風(たいふう)が接近(せっきん)している。したがって、明日(あす)の試合(しあい)は中止(ちゅうし)とする。 In speech you would say だから or ですから.

～に従って · ～に従(したが)って vs ～につれて

Rule of thumb - for two changes moving together, both work and に従って is the stiffer choice. For following instructions, only に従って is possible.

南(みなみ)へ下(くだ)るに従(したが)って、畑(はたけ)が広(ひろ)くなっていく。
As you go further south, the fields get wider.

Replace に従って with につれて there and almost nothing changes; the sentence just sounds a shade less like a geography textbook. Now the half につれて cannot reach:

先生(せんせい)のアドバイスに従(したが)って、書(か)き直(なお)してみました。
I rewrote it following my teacher's advice.

There is no gradual change here at all - just compliance - so ❌アドバイスにつれて is impossible. Test it in English: if "as" can become "in accordance with", it is に従って.

～に従って · ～に従(したが)って vs ～とおりに・～どおりに

Rule of thumb - どおりに means the result matches the model exactly. に従って means you took the instruction as your authority. Everyday speech prefers どおりに.

説明書(せつめいしょ)のとおりに組(く)み立(た)てたら、ねじが一(ひと)つ余(あま)った。
I assembled it exactly as the instructions said, and had one screw left over.

会社(かいしゃ)の方針(ほうしん)に従(したが)って、報告(ほうこく)書(しょ)の形式(けいしき)を変(か)えました。
We changed the format of the report in line with company policy.

A policy is an authority you obey; a diagram is a model you copy. Note also the shape of the word: after a noun with の it is とおりに (思(おも)ったとおりに), but glued straight onto a noun it becomes どおりに - 予定(よてい)どおりに, 時間(じかん)どおりに.

に従(したが)って inside a sentence vs したがって at the head of one

Rule of thumb - if the word is attached to a noun or verb, it means "following". If it is standing alone at the start of a sentence with a comma after it, it means "therefore".

前例(ぜんれい)に従(したが)って判断(はんだん)しました。
We made the decision following precedent.

前例(ぜんれい)がない。したがって、慎重(しんちょう)に判断(はんだん)する必要(ひつよう)がある。
There is no precedent. Therefore, the decision must be made carefully.

Same characters, unrelated jobs. The connective is normally written in kana in modern Japanese, which helps, but not reliably - and hearing it aloud, only the position in the sentence tells you which one it is.

～に従って · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 説明書(せつめいしょ)に従(したが)て → ✅ 説明書(せつめいしょ)に従(したが)って
    従(したが)う is an う-verb, so its て-form doubles the consonant: 従(したが)って.

  • ❌ 指示(しじ)につれて動(うご)いてください。 → ✅ 指示(しじ)に従(したが)って動(うご)いてください。
    Instructions are obeyed, not gradually undergone. につれて has no "in accordance with" meaning at all.

  • ❌ 年(とし)を取(と)ったに従(したが)って → ✅ 年(とし)を取(と)るに従(したが)って
    In the parallel-change sense the verb before it stays in the dictionary form, whatever the tense of the sentence.

  • ❌ 台風(たいふう)が来(き)ました。従(したが)って中止(ちゅうし)です。（in casual speech） → ✅ 台風(たいふう)が来(き)ました。だから中止(ちゅうし)です。
    Not ungrammatical, but したがって in a chat sounds like reading out a legal notice. Keep it for writing.

～に従って · Practice 1

1. 係員(かかりいん)の指示(しじ)____従(したが)って、ゆっくり外(そと)へ出(で)てください。

係員(かかりいん)の指示(しじ)に従(したが)って…

従(したが)う takes its object with に, never を - you comply to something in Japanese. The same に shows up in 規則(きそく)に従(したが)う and 命令(めいれい)に従(したが)う.

～に従って · Practice 2

2. Choose: 川(かわ)を下(くだ)る（に従(したが)って・のとおりに）、水(みず)の色(いろ)が茶色(ちゃいろ)くなった。

川(かわ)を下(くだ)るに従(したが)って、水(みず)の色(いろ)が茶色(ちゃいろ)くなった。

This is the parallel-change sense: two things shifting together along the length of the river. とおりに would need a model to copy, and a river is not giving anybody instructions.

～に従って · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Following the doctor's advice, I stopped drinking coffee at night."

医者(いしゃ)のアドバイスに従(したが)って、夜(よる)はコーヒーを飲(の)むのをやめました。

Advice from a doctor counts as an authority you comply with, so に従(したが)って fits. In casual conversation 医者(いしゃ)に言(い)われたとおりに would be the lighter way to say the same thing.

～に従って · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 時代(じだい)が変(か)わったに従(したが)い、働(はたら)き方(かた)も変(か)わってきた。

✅ 時代(じだい)が変(か)わるに従(したが)い、働(はたら)き方(かた)も変(か)わってきた。

The verb in front has to be non-past. The past sense is already carried by 変(か)わってきた at the end, and doubling it up is what makes the original ungrammatical.

～に従って · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 先輩(せんぱい)のやり方(かた)に従(したが)って作(つく)りました。 (b) 先輩(せんぱい)のやり方(かた)のとおりに作(つく)りました。

(a) frames the senior colleague's method as something you were expected to comply with. It suits a workplace where that method is the rule, and it quietly says you did not deviate from it.

(b) is about the product matching the model: you copied the method, step for step. It says nothing about authority, so it is the neutral, more conversational choice - and it is what you would say if you simply thought their way was good.

～ば～ほど

Meaning · the more ～, the more ～ - two quantities that rise and fall together

～ば～ほど links a cause and a result and says they are proportional: turn the first one up and the second one goes up with it. The same word appears twice, once in the ば form and once in the plain form, which is what makes the pattern instantly recognisable. Unlike ～につれて, it is not a report on something that happened - it is a claim about how things work.

～ば～ほど · Formation

The first copy of the word goes into the conditional ば form; the second copy goes into the form that can sit in front of ほど, which is the plain form for verbs and い-adjectives and the な form for な-adjectives.

～ば～ほど · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
う-verb ば-form + V(dictionary) + ほど 読(よ)む → 読(よ)めば読(よ)むほど
る-verb ば-form + V(dictionary) + ほど 考(かんが)える → 考(かんが)えれば考(かんが)えるほど
する すれば + する + ほど 練習(れんしゅう)する → 練習(れんしゅう)すれば練習(れんしゅう)するほど
い-adjective ～ければ + ～い + ほど 安(やす)い → 安(やす)ければ安(やす)いほど
な-adjective ～なら + ～な + ほど 静(しず)か → 静(しず)かなら静(しず)かなほど
な-adjective (formal) ～であれば + あるほど 便利(べんり) → 便利(べんり)であればあるほど
Noun ～であれば + あるほど プロ → プロであればあるほど
Negative ～なければ + ～ないほど 知(し)らない → 知(し)らなければ知(し)らないほど

～ば～ほど · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Shortened ～ほど（the ば half dropped） 早(はや)い → 早(はや)いほどいい

Note - う-verb / る-verb are the labels this book uses for what other books call 五段(ごだん) / godan / Group 1 and 一段(いちだん) / ichidan / Group 2. For な-adjectives, both 静(しず)かなら静(しず)かなほど and 静(しず)かであればあるほど are correct; the second is the written, formal one, and it is the only option after a noun.

～ば～ほど · Examples

この本(ほん)は読(よ)めば読(よ)むほどおもしろくなる。
The more you read this book, the more interesting it gets.

野菜(やさい)は新(あたら)しければ新(あたら)しいほどおいしい。
The fresher the vegetables, the better they taste.

考(かんが)えれば考(かんが)えるほど、答(こた)えが分(わ)からなくなった。
The more I thought about it, the less I understood the answer.

説明(せつめい)は簡単(かんたん)なら簡単(かんたん)なほどいいです。
The simpler the explanation, the better.

部屋(へや)は広(ひろ)ければ広(ひろ)いほど冬(ふゆ)は寒(さむ)い。
The bigger the room, the colder it is in winter.

～ば～ほど · Examples (cont.)

道具(どうぐ)は単純(たんじゅん)であればあるほど壊(こわ)れにくい。
The simpler a tool is, the less likely it is to break.

締(し)め切(き)りが近(ちか)づけば近(ちか)づくほど、なぜか部屋(へや)の掃除(そうじ)がしたくなる。
The closer the deadline gets, the more I somehow want to clean my room.

人(ひと)の前(まえ)で話(はな)すのは、準備(じゅんび)をすればするほど落(お)ち着(つ)いて話(はな)せるようになりますよ。
When it comes to speaking in public, the more you prepare, the more calmly you'll be able to talk.

～ば～ほど · Nuance & register

The pattern itself is register-neutral. Politeness lives on the final verb, so 安(やす)ければ安(やす)いほどいい and 安(やす)ければ安(やす)いほどいいです are the same grammar at different levels, and both are perfectly ordinary in conversation.

Because it states a proportion rather than an event, it is the natural shape for advice, opinions, slogans and rules of thumb - 早(はや)ければ早(はや)いほどいい is something you will hear said about applications, hospital visits and train tickets alike. It is equally at home in a report describing a correlation.

～ば～ほど · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two useful habits. First, the result half is very often an evaluation: いい, 楽(らく)だ, 大変(たいへん)だ, 安心(あんしん)する. Second, the result is frequently a deterioration despite the "more … more" English - 考(かんが)えれば考(かんが)えるほど 分(わ)からなくなる is one of the most common sentences built on this pattern, and English speakers reaching for "the more … the less" should notice that Japanese puts the "less" in the verb, not in the pattern.

One restriction worth remembering: the two halves must be the same word. This is not a general "if … then" - it is one scale echoing itself. If the cause and the result are different things, you want a plain ば, と or たら sentence.

～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs ～につれて

Rule of thumb - につれて narrates a change that actually took place. ば～ほど states a rule that holds every time, including in situations that have not happened.

夏(なつ)が近(ちか)づくにつれて、店(みせ)の前(まえ)に花(はな)が増(ふ)えてきた。
As summer approached, more and more flowers appeared in front of the shop.

That is one shop, one summer. The correlative version is a general truth and can be said in the middle of winter:

気温(きおん)が上(あ)がれば上(あ)がるほど、アイスがよく売(う)れる。
The higher the temperature, the better ice cream sells.

～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs ～につれて (cont.)

Practical consequence: only ば～ほど combines comfortably with advice, guesses and conditions - 早(はや)く申(もう)し込(こ)めば申(もう)し込(こ)む ほど安(やす)くなりますよ. Try that with につれて and it sounds like you are describing prices you have already watched fall.

～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs the shortened ～ほど

Rule of thumb - the ば half can be dropped when the word is an adjective and the result is a simple evaluation. Dropping it is shorter and slightly more written; keeping it adds emphasis.

部屋(へや)は明(あか)るいほどいい。
The brighter the room, the better.

部屋(へや)は明(あか)るければ明(あか)るいほどいい。
The brighter the room, the better it is - really, as bright as you can get it.

The short version reads like a specification; the full version sounds like someone insisting. With verbs the short form is much rarer, and ❌練習(れんしゅう)するほど上手(じょうず)になる can be understood but sounds clipped - say the verb twice.

～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs どんどん・ますます

Rule of thumb - ば～ほど states a relationship between two scales. どんどん and ますます are adverbs that simply say a single thing keeps increasing, with no second quantity attached.

待(ま)っている人(ひと)がどんどん増(ふ)えてきた。
The number of people waiting kept growing.

Nothing is being proportioned there - it is one line on a graph going up. Compare:

人気(にんき)が出(で)れば出(で)るほど、予約(よやく)が取(と)りにくくなる。
The more popular it gets, the harder it becomes to get a reservation.

Two scales, tied together. The two often appear in the same sentence, and that is fine: 練習(れんしゅう)すればするほどどんどん上手(じょうず)になる.

～ば～ほど · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 読(よ)むば読(よ)むほど → ✅ 読(よ)めば読(よ)むほど
    The first copy of the verb has to be the ば form. Only the second one stays in the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 高(たか)いければ高(たか)いほど → ✅ 高(たか)ければ高(たか)いほど
    An い-adjective drops its final い before ければ. The い survives only on the second copy, in front of ほど.

  • ❌ 食(た)べれば太(ふと)るほど → ✅ 食(た)べれば食(た)べるほど太(ふと)る
    The word before ば and the word before ほど must be the same one. The consequence goes at the end of the sentence.

  • ❌ 便利(べんり)なら便利(べんり)ならほど → ✅ 便利(べんり)なら便利(べんり)なほど
    What sits in front of ほど is the noun-modifying form, so a な-adjective needs its な. The formal alternative is 便利(べんり)であればあるほど.

～ば～ほど · Practice 1

1. この問題(もんだい)は急(いそ)____急(いそ)ぐほど間違(まちが)えやすい。

急(いそ)げば急(いそ)ぐほど間違(まちが)えやすい。

急(いそ)ぐ is an う-verb, so the ば form replaces the final ぐ with げば. The second copy is left in the dictionary form because ほど attaches to a noun-modifying form.

～ば～ほど · Practice 2

2. Choose: 子(こ)どもは大(おお)きくなる（につれて・ばなるほど）、親(おや)と話(はな)さなくなる。

子(こ)どもは大(おお)きくなるにつれて、親(おや)と話(はな)さなくなる。

The slot in the sentence already contains a full dictionary-form verb, so only につれて can attach there. To use the correlative you would have to rebuild the whole thing as 大(おお)きくなればなるほど, with the verb appearing twice.

～ば～ほど · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The more expensive the wine, the more carefully everyone drinks it."

ワインは高(たか)ければ高(たか)いほど、みんな大切(たいせつ)に飲(の)む。

高(たか)い supplies both halves - ければ first, plain い second - and the consequence, everyone's carefulness, comes last. Note that Japanese needs no comparative form on either adjective.

～ば～ほど · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この仕事(しごと)は慣(な)れるば慣(な)れるほど楽(らく)になります。

慣(な)れる is a る-verb, so its ば form is 慣(な)れれば - you cannot simply glue ば onto the dictionary form. The second copy is correct as it stands.

～ば～ほど · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 資料(しりょう)は多(おお)いほどいい。 (b) 資料(しりょう)は多(おお)ければ多(おお)いほど読(よ)むのが大変(たいへん)になる。

(a) is the clipped, specification-like form: more material is better, full stop. It is the kind of line you would write in a note or say when handing out instructions.

(b) spells the scale out twice, which gives it more force, and its result half runs the other way - the same increase that (a) welcomes is the thing making the work harder. Both are proportional statements; only the evaluation differs.

～ほど

Meaning · to the extent that ～; so much that ～ - and, with numbers, "about"

ほど measures. Its main job at N3 is to state how much by describing what that amount is enough to cause: you were tired to the point of not being able to stand, the room was quiet to the point that it was frightening. Attached to a number it does something simpler and softer - it turns the number into an approximation, "about".

～ほど · Formation

The measuring clause comes before ほど, and the thing being measured comes after. That is the reverse of the English order, and it is the single most important fact on this page.

～ほど · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (plain) V(plain) + ほど 歩(ある)けない → 歩(ある)けないほど疲(つか)れた
い-adjective ～い + ほど 恥(は)ずかしい → 恥(は)ずかしいほど赤(あか)くなった
な-adjective ～な + ほど 不思議(ふしぎ) → 不思議(ふしぎ)なほどよく眠(ねむ)れた
Noun used as a yardstick Noun + ほど 山(やま) → 山(やま)ほどある
Number / quantity Number + ほど（≒ぐらい） 三日(みっか) → 三日(みっか)ほど休(やす)む
Modifying a noun ～ほど + の + Noun 涙(なみだ)が出(で)るほどの話(はなし)

～ほど · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - English says "so X that Y"; Japanese says "Y ほど X". Build the sentence by writing the vivid consequence first, then ほど, then the plain statement it is decorating.

～ほど · Examples

駅(えき)まで十分(じゅっぷん)ほどかかります。
It takes about ten minutes to the station.

今日(きょう)は宿題(しゅくだい)が山(やま)ほどある。
I've got a mountain of homework today.

声(こえ)が出(で)ないほど叫(さけ)んでしまった。
I yelled so much I lost my voice.

信(しん)じられないほど静(しず)かな朝(あさ)だった。
It was an unbelievably quiet morning.

合格(ごうかく)の知(し)らせを聞(き)いて、泣(な)きたくなるほどうれしかった。
When I heard I'd passed, I was so happy I could have cried.

～ほど · Examples (cont.)

今日(きょう)は立(た)っていられないほど風(かぜ)が強(つよ)い。
The wind today is strong enough to knock you off your feet.

部長(ぶちょう)の話(はなし)は、時計(とけい)を見(み)たくなるほど長(なが)かった。
The manager's speech was long enough to have you checking your watch.

来週(らいしゅう)は一週間(いっしゅうかん)ほど出張(しゅっちょう)ですので、打(う)ち合(あ)わせは再来週(さらいしゅう)にしていただけませんか。
I'll be away on business for about a week, so could we make the meeting the week after next?

～ほど · Nuance & register

The degree use is neutral and works in any register, though it is a shade more written and a shade more emphatic than ぐらい. With numbers it is the more formal of the pair: a shop assistant says 三日(みっか)ほどおまちください, a friend says 三日(みっか)ぐらい待(ま)って.

Because the pattern works by naming a consequence, it invites exaggeration, and spoken Japanese takes the invitation. 死(し)ぬほど眠(ねむ)い, 嫌(いや)になるほど聞(き)いた and 山(やま)ほどある are set phrases you will hear constantly. They are casual and strong: 死(し)ぬほど is fine among friends and out of place in a meeting, where 信(しん)じられないほど or plain とても is safer.

The consequence clause does not have to be real. Nobody who says 泣(な)きたくなるほどうれしい is claiming to have cried; the clause is a measuring stick, not a report. This is why it so often appears in the negative potential - 歩(ある)けないほど, 見(み)ていられないほど - describing what the degree prevented.

～ほど · Nuance & register (cont.)

One caution: ほど measures a large degree. Japanese has a separate job of marking a small or minimal one - "at least this much", "even just a coffee" - and that belongs to ぐらい. Reaching for ほど there produces a sentence native speakers will not follow.

～ほど · ～ほど vs ～くらい・ぐらい

Rule of thumb - for a high degree the two overlap, with ほど the stronger and more formal. For a low or minimum degree only ぐらい works.

Overlapping:

足(あし)が痛(いた)くなるほど歩(ある)き回(まわ)った。
I walked around until my feet hurt.

Swap in ぐらい and you get the same meaning in a lighter, chattier voice. Now the place they part company:

コーヒーぐらいおごりますよ。
Let me at least buy you a coffee.

～ほど · ～ほど vs ～くらい・ぐらい (cont.)

Here ぐらい says "this much and no more, it's a trivial amount", and ❌コーヒーほど is impossible. The same goes for 挨拶(あいさつ)ぐらいしなさい - "the least you could do is say hello."

～ほど · ～ほど vs ～すぎて・～て（result）

Rule of thumb - same facts, opposite order. ほど puts the consequence first and the plain statement last; a て or すぎて clause puts the cause first and the consequence last.

昨日(きのう)は声(こえ)も出(で)ないほど疲(つか)れていた。
Yesterday I was so tired I couldn't even speak.

昨日(きのう)は疲(つか)れすぎて、声(こえ)も出(で)なかった。
Yesterday I was too tired, and couldn't even speak.

The difference is where the emphasis lands. The ほど version is a statement about how tired you were, with the silence used as a ruler. The すぎて version is a narrative: tiredness happened, and then this followed. Learners translating "so … that" word by word reliably produce the second when they wanted the first.

～ほど · ～ほど for degree vs ～ほど～ない for comparison

Rule of thumb - look at the main predicate. If it is negative and the thing before ほど is a rival being measured against, you are reading a comparison, not a degree.

昨日(きのう)は眠(ねむ)れないほど暑(あつ)かった。
Yesterday was so hot I couldn't sleep.

今日(きょう)は昨日(きのう)ほど暑(あつ)くない。
Today isn't as hot as yesterday.

In the first, 眠(ねむ)れない is inside the measuring clause and the sentence itself is positive. In the second, the negative is on the main adjective and 昨日(きのう) is the standard being beaten. When both readings are formally available the negative predicate decides it, which is why 山(やま)ほど高(たか)くない means "not as high as a mountain", not "mountainously un-high".

～ほど · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 疲(つか)れたほど歩(ある)けなかった。 → ✅ 歩(ある)けないほど疲(つか)れた。
    The clause before ほど is the measuring stick, so the consequence goes there. English word order pushes learners into writing this one backwards.

  • ❌ お茶(ちゃ)ほど飲(の)んでいきませんか。 → ✅ お茶(ちゃ)ぐらい飲(の)んでいきませんか。
    "At least a cup of tea" is a minimum, and ほど cannot mark a minimum. That is ぐらい territory.

  • ❌ 涙(なみだ)が出(で)るほどな話(はなし) → ✅ 涙(なみだ)が出(で)るほどの話(はなし)
    ほど behaves like a noun, so it links to a following noun with の.

  • ❌ 不思議(ふしぎ)いほど静(しず)かだ。 → ✅ 不思議(ふしぎ)なほど静(しず)かだ。
    A な-adjective keeps its な in front of ほど, exactly as it would in front of any other noun.

1. 息(いき)が____ほど走(はし)って、なんとか電車(でんしゃ)に間(ま)に合(あ)った。

息(いき)ができないほど走(はし)って…

The slot needs a consequence extreme enough to measure the running by, and the negative potential - literally "to the point of not being able to breathe" - is the most idiomatic filler for it.

～ほど · Practice 2

The speaker is complaining that they did not get even the smallest response, and a minimum like that is marked with ぐらい. ほど only measures large degrees, so it cannot carry "the least you could do".

～ほど · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The room was so cold that I couldn't take my coat off."

部屋(へや)はコートが脱(ぬ)げないほど寒(さむ)かった。

Japanese states the consequence first and the plain fact last, so "couldn't take my coat off" goes in front of ほど and 寒(さむ)かった ends the sentence. Writing it in English order would produce the ungrammatical 寒(さむ)かったほど….

～ほど · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 忙(いそが)しいほど昼(ひる)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べる時間(じかん)もなかった。

✅ 昼(ひる)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べる時間(じかん)もないほど忙(いそが)しかった。

The two halves were the wrong way round. Skipping lunch is the evidence, so it belongs in front of ほど, and the busyness is what the sentence is actually asserting, so it goes at the end.

～ほど · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 一時間(いちじかん)ほど待(ま)ちました。 (b) 一時間(いちじかん)も待(ま)ちました。

(a) is a neutral approximation: roughly an hour, no complaint attached. The ほど softens the number, and it is the version you would use in a polite report or when telling a colleague what happened.

(b) uses も to mark the hour as more than expected - "I waited a whole hour." The amount is now the point of the sentence, and the speaker is registering impatience or surprise.

～ほど～ない

Meaning · not as ～ as ～ - the standard side takes ほど, and it is the bigger one

This is how Japanese says "A isn't as X as B". The frame is A は B ほど X ない, and the trap is which noun goes where: ほど marks B, the one that is more X. English puts the winner last ("Osaka isn't as crowded as Tokyo"); Japanese puts it in the middle, and the sentence must end in a negative.

～ほど～ない · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (the standard) Noun + ほど + ～ない 兄(あに) → 兄(あに)ほど背(せ)が高(たか)くない
Verb (plain) V(plain) + ほど + ～ない 話(はな)す → 話(はな)すほど簡単(かんたん)ではない
An expectation V(た) + ほど + ～ない 思(おも)った → 思(おも)ったほど高(たか)くない
い-adjective predicate ～くない 寒(さむ)い → 去年(きょねん)ほど寒(さむ)くない
な-adjective predicate ～ではない 静(しず)か → 夜(よる)ほど静(しず)かではない
Noun predicate ～ではない 問題(もんだい) → 前回(ぜんかい)ほどの問題(もんだい)ではない
"nothing is as ～ as" Nほど + ～ + Nはない これ → これほどうれしいことはない

～ほど～ない · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - whatever ranks higher takes ほど. Say the English out loud: "not as X as B" - B is the one that gets the particle, and everything after it must be negative.

～ほど～ない · Examples

今年(ことし)の冬(ふゆ)は去年(きょねん)ほど寒(さむ)くない。
This winter isn't as cold as last year's.

私(わたし)は姉(あね)ほど料理(りょうり)が上手(じょうず)じゃない。
I'm not as good a cook as my older sister.

テストは思(おも)ったほど難(むずか)しくなかった。
The test wasn't as hard as I'd expected.

この辺(へん)は駅前(えきまえ)ほどにぎやかではないが、買(か)い物(もの)には困(こま)らない。
This area isn't as lively as the area by the station, but you can get everything you need.

口(くち)で言(い)うほど簡単(かんたん)ではありませんでした。
It wasn't as easy as it sounds.

～ほど～ない · Examples (cont.)

彼(かれ)ほど字(じ)のきれいな人(ひと)を見(み)たことがない。
I've never met anyone whose handwriting is as beautiful as his.

人(ひと)に教(おし)えることほど勉強(べんきょう)になることはない。
There's nothing that teaches you as much as teaching someone else.

新(あたら)しい職場(しょくば)は前(まえ)の会社(かいしゃ)ほど残業(ざんぎょう)が多(おお)くないので、夕(ゆう)ご飯(はん)を家(いえ)で食(た)べられるようになりました。
The new workplace doesn't have as much overtime as my old company, so I've been able to start eating dinner at home.

～ほど～ない · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. The pattern is as ordinary in a chat as in a report, and the politeness is set by the ending - 寒(さむ)くない / 寒(さむ)くないです / 寒(さむ)くありません.

What it does carry is a quiet concession. Saying 去年(きょねん)ほど寒(さむ)くない grants that last year set a high bar and places this year below it, without denying that it is cold. That makes it a polite way to decline a comparison you are being pushed into: 私(わたし)は先輩(せんぱい)ほどできません is modest, where できません alone would just be flat.

くらい / ぐらい can replace ほど here in casual speech, but the two are not equally safe. ぐらい also has its own "at least, merely" meaning, so 田中(たなか)さんぐらい話(はな)せない can be heard two ways. ほど is unambiguous, and it is what you want in writing.

～ほど～ない · Nuance & register (cont.)

The pattern's second life is as a superlative in disguise: N ほど … は ない, "there is nothing as … as N". It sounds more emphatic and more personal than the plain いちばん superlative, and it is the frame of a great many proverbs and strong opinions - 健康(けんこう)ほど大切(たいせつ)なものはない.

～ほど～ない · AはBほど～ない vs AよりBのほうが～

Rule of thumb - ほど attaches to the greater item and needs a negative. より attaches to the lesser item and needs a positive. Get the two mixed up and you say the opposite of what you meant.

The same fact, from both ends:

バスは電車(でんしゃ)ほど速(はや)くない。
The bus isn't as fast as the train.

バスより電車(でんしゃ)のほうが速(はや)い。
The train is faster than the bus.

～ほど～ない · AはBほど～ない vs AよりBのほうが～ (cont.)

Notice that 電車(でんしゃ), the faster one, carries ほど in the first sentence and follows より in the second. Learners who translate word by word tend to produce ❌電車(でんしゃ)はバスほど速(はや)くない, which claims that trains are the slow ones.

There is also a form restriction. Japanese does not like より in front of a negative predicate: ❌私(わたし)は兄(あに)より背(せ)が高(たか)くない is rejected by most textbooks and sounds wrong to most speakers. If your English has "not as … as" in it, use ほど.

～ほど～ない · ～ほど～ない vs あまり／そんなに～ない

Rule of thumb - ほど～ない needs a named standard. あまり～ない and そんなに～ない measure against a vague expectation instead.

この店(みせ)はあまり高(たか)くない。
This place isn't very expensive.

この店(みせ)は駅前(えきまえ)の店(みせ)ほど高(たか)くない。
This place isn't as expensive as the one by the station.

The first sets an absolute, if fuzzy, level; the second says nothing at all about whether the place is cheap - only that another shop is worse. This is why 思(おも)ったほど is so common: it names your own expectation as the standard, which is the most useful yardstick a speaker has.

～ほど～ない · Nほど～はない vs the いちばん superlative

Rule of thumb - いちばん ranks inside a stated group and is neutral. N ほど～はない claims nothing anywhere beats N, and it is an opinion with feeling behind it.

夏(なつ)の山(やま)で飲(の)む水(みず)ほどおいしいものはない。
There's nothing as good as water drunk on a mountain in summer.

飲(の)み物(もの)の中(なか)で水(みず)がいちばんおいしいです。
Of all drinks, water tastes the best.

The second is a survey result; the first is a declaration. Note the shape of the ほど version: the noun being praised comes first, and the sentence ends with a negated ない plus a general noun such as もの, こと, 人(ひと) or 所(ところ).

～ほど～ない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 富士山(ふじさん)は高尾山(たかおさん)ほど高(たか)くない。 → ✅ 高尾山(たかおさん)は富士山(ふじさん)ほど高(たか)くない。
    ほど goes on the taller mountain. Written the first way, the sentence says Fuji is the smaller one.

  • ❌ 私(わたし)は兄(あに)より背(せ)が高(たか)くない。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は兄(あに)ほど背(せ)が高(たか)くない。
    より does not sit comfortably in front of a negative. "Not as … as" is a ほど sentence.

  • ❌ 今日(きょう)は昨日(きのう)ほど暑(あつ)いです。 → ✅ 今日(きょう)は昨日(きのう)ほど暑(あつ)くないです。
    This ほど only exists inside a negative sentence. With a positive predicate the sentence has no meaning.

～ほど～ない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 健康(けんこう)ほど大切(たいせつ)なものがある。 → ✅ 健康(けんこう)ほど大切(たいせつ)なものはない。
    The superlative frame ends in はない. Its whole force comes from denying that anything else measures up.

～ほど～ない · Practice 1

1. 弟(おとうと)は兄(あに)____背(せ)が高(たか)くない。

弟(おとうと)は兄(あに)ほど背(せ)が高(たか)くない。

The older brother is the taller of the two, so he is the standard and takes ほど. The sentence then has to close in the negative, which is what turns it into "not as tall as".

～ほど～ない · Practice 2

2. Choose: 北海道(ほっかいどう)の冬(ふゆ)は東京(とうきょう)（ほど・より）寒(さむ)くない。 - is this sentence true to what you mean if you think Hokkaido is colder?

Neither version says what you mean. With ほど, the sentence reads "Hokkaido's winter isn't as cold as Tokyo's", which is the wrong way round; より cannot take a negative predicate at all. To say Hokkaido is colder, flip it: 東京(とうきょう)の冬(ふゆ)は北海道(ほっかいどう)ほど寒(さむ)くない.

～ほど～ない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "My new apartment isn't as quiet as the old one."

新(あたら)しいアパートは前(まえ)のアパートほど静(しず)かではない。

The old flat is the quieter one, so it is the standard and takes ほど. Because 静(しず)か is a な-adjective, the negative is ではない (casually じゃない), not 静(しず)かくない.

～ほど～ない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この映画(えいが)ほどおもしろい映画(えいが)があります。

✅ この映画(えいが)ほどおもしろい映画(えいが)はありません。

The Nほど…はない frame is a superlative built out of a denial, so it cannot end positively. As written, the original says something like "there is a film as interesting as this one", which is the opposite of the intended praise.

～ほど～ない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この仕事(しごと)は思(おも)ったほど大変(たいへん)じゃない。 (b) この仕事(しごと)はあまり大変(たいへん)じゃない。

(a) measures the job against your own earlier expectation. It admits you were braced for something worse, and it therefore says the job is demanding - just less so than you feared.

(b) has no standard in it at all. It simply rates the job low on an absolute scale: not much trouble. Someone who had never thought about the job before could say (b), but only someone with a prior expectation can say (a).

～に比べて

Meaning · compared with ～; against the standard of ～

～に比(くら)べて sets a backdrop. It names the thing you are measuring against, then leaves the rest of the sentence free to say whatever you like about the other side - bigger, cheaper, quieter, twenty per cent down. Unlike より, which has to sit next to a comparative predicate, に比(くら)べて hands you a whole clause to work in.

～に比べて · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + に比(くら)べて 去年(きょねん) → 去年(きょねん)に比(くら)べて
Noun (mutual comparison) Noun + と比(くら)べて 兄(あに) → 兄(あに)と比(くら)べて
Clause V(plain) + の + に比(くら)べて 歩(ある)く → 歩(ある)くのに比(くら)べて
Noun clause ～のに比(くら)べて 安(やす)い → 安(やす)いのに比(くら)べて
Formal writing ～に比(くら)べ 昨年(さくねん) → 昨年(さくねん)に比(くら)べ
Softer, spoken ～に比(くら)べると／～に比(くら)べたら 都会(とかい) → 都会(とかい)に比(くら)べると
As an ordinary verb A と B を比(くら)べる 値段(ねだん)と質(しつ)を比(くら)べる

～に比べて · Formation (cont.)

Note - the particle is に or と, never を. を belongs to the plain verb 比(くら)べる and marks the pair being compared: 二(ふた)つのプランを比(くら)べる.

～に比べて · Examples

去年(きょねん)に比(くら)べて、今年(ことし)は雨(あめ)が少(すく)ない。
Compared with last year, there's been little rain this year.

兄(あに)に比(くら)べて、弟(おとうと)はよくしゃべる。
Compared with his older brother, the younger one is quite the talker.

前(まえ)のモデルに比(くら)べて、音(おと)がずいぶん静(しず)かになりました。
Compared with the previous model, it's got a lot quieter.

都会(とかい)に比(くら)べると、この町(まち)は時間(じかん)の流(なが)れがゆっくりしている。
Compared with the city, time moves slowly in this town.

電車(でんしゃ)で行(い)くのに比(くら)べて、車(くるま)は時間(じかん)が読(よ)めない。
Compared with going by train, you can't predict how long the car will take.

～に比べて · Examples (cont.)

十年前(じゅうねんまえ)に比(くら)べ、市内(しない)の人口(じんこう)は二割(にわり)減(へ)っている。
Compared with ten years ago, the population of the city is down twenty per cent.

去年(きょねん)の夏(なつ)に比(くら)べて、電気(でんき)代(だい)が倍(ばい)になって驚(おどろ)いた。
Compared with last summer, the electricity bill has doubled - I was shocked.

同(おな)じ時期(じき)に入(はい)った人(ひと)たちに比(くら)べて、自分(じぶん)だけ仕事(しごと)が遅(おそ)い気(き)がして落(お)ち込(こ)んでいます。
Compared with the others who started at the same time, I feel like I'm the only slow one, and it's getting me down.

～に比べて · Nuance & register

Neutral, with a slight lean towards the explanatory: news bulletins, sales reports, weather summaries and any sentence with a percentage in it. It is perfectly normal in conversation too, especially in the に比(くら)べると and に比(くら)べたら shapes, which soften it and sound more like personal impression than analysis.

Clipping to に比(くら)べ is written-only, one notch up in formality, and extremely common in newspapers: 前年(ぜんねん)に比(くら)べ三(さん)パーセント増(ふ)えた.

に and と are close to interchangeable, with a tendency: に frames one thing as the fixed reference point you are working from, while と puts the two side by side as equals. 去年(きょねん)に比(くら)べて takes last year as the baseline; 兄(あに)と比(くら)べて sounds more like holding two people up against each other, and it is the one that can carry an edge of "why are you comparing us?"

～に比べて · Nuance & register (cont.)

One phrase worth having ready: ～に比(くら)べれば means "compared with that, this is nothing" - 去年(きょねん)の忙(いそが)しさに比(くら)べれば、 今(いま)は楽(らく)なものだ. It is a standard way of putting a complaint in perspective, your own or someone else's.

～に比べて · ～に比(くら)べて vs ～より

Rule of thumb - より is glued to a comparative statement and needs a gradable predicate. に比べて sets up a frame and then lets you say anything at all.

Where a single adjective is doing the work, both are fine and より is lighter:

地下鉄(ちかてつ)はバスより速(はや)い。
The subway is faster than the bus.

But に比べて can head a whole clause, and even a clause with no comparative in it:

バスに比(くら)べて、地下鉄(ちかてつ)は時間(じかん)が読(よ)めるので安心(あんしん)だ。
Compared with the bus, the subway runs to time, so it's less stressful.

～に比べて · ～に比(くら)べて vs ～より (cont.)

❌バスより安心(あんしん)だ strains, because 安心(あんしん)だ is not being measured on a scale - the sentence is describing a situation, not ranking two numbers. A second practical difference: に比べて normally opens the sentence, which makes it the natural choice when the comparison is the topic of a paragraph.

～に比べて · ～に比(くら)べて vs ～に対(たい)して

Rule of thumb - に比べて puts two things on one scale. に対して sets them in opposition, "whereas", with no shared measure required.

去年(きょねん)に比(くら)べて、新入生(しんにゅうせい)の数(かず)が増(ふ)えた。
Compared with last year, the number of new students has gone up.

Numbers, one axis. Now two facts that share no axis at all:

姉(あね)が外(そと)で遊(あそ)ぶのが好(す)きなのに対(たい)して、妹(いもうと)は一日中(いちにちじゅう)本(ほん)を読(よ)んでいる。
Where my older sister loves being outdoors, my younger sister reads all day.

～に比べて · ～に比(くら)べて vs ～に対(たい)して (cont.)

Nothing is more or less than anything here; the two halves are simply different. Substituting に比べて would suggest reading and playing outside are two settings of one dial, which is not what the speaker means.

～に比べて · ～に比(くら)べて vs ～と違(ちが)って

Rule of thumb - に比べて is a difference of degree. と違って is a difference of kind: A does this, B does not, and there is no "more" about it.

東京(とうきょう)の店(みせ)に比(くら)べて、こちらは値段(ねだん)が安(やす)い。
Compared with the shops in Tokyo, things here are cheap.

東京(とうきょう)の店(みせ)と違(ちが)って、この店(みせ)は日曜(にちよう)が休(やす)みだ。
Unlike the shops in Tokyo, this one closes on Sundays.

Closing on Sunday is not a lesser amount of anything, so it takes と違(ちが)って. A quick test: if you could add "by twenty per cent" to the English, you want に比(くら)べて.

～に比べて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 去年(きょねん)を比(くら)べて、今年(ことし)は暑(あつ)い。 → ✅ 去年(きょねん)に比(くら)べて、今年(ことし)は暑(あつ)い。
    The standard is marked with に or と. を would make last year the object being examined, which needs a second object to pair it with.

  • ❌ 歩(ある)くに比(くら)べて自転車(じてんしゃ)は楽(らく)だ。 → ✅ 歩(ある)くのに比(くら)べて自転車(じてんしゃ)は楽(らく)だ。
    A verb has to be nominalised with の before に比(くら)べて can attach to it.

  • ❌ 電車(でんしゃ)に比(くら)べて、バスより安(やす)い。 → ✅ 電車(でんしゃ)に比(くら)べて、バスは安(やす)い。
    One comparison, one marker. Once に比(くら)べて has named the standard, より is redundant and the sentence reads as if it had two of them.

～に比べて · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 姉(あね)に比(くら)べて、妹(いもうと)はめがねをかけていない。 → ✅ 姉(あね)と違(ちが)って、妹(いもうと)はめがねをかけていない。
    Wearing glasses is not a matter of degree, so there is nothing to compare. That is a と違(ちが)って sentence.

～に比べて · Practice 1

1. 先月(せんげつ)____比(くら)べて、今月(こんげつ)は残業(ざんぎょう)が少(すく)ない。

先月(せんげつ)に比(くら)べて、今月(こんげつ)は残業(ざんぎょう)が少(すく)ない。

Last month is the fixed reference point, and a reference point takes に. と is also grammatical here but would present the two months as rivals rather than as a baseline and a result.

2. Choose: 姉(あね)は文系(ぶんけい)なの（に比(くら)べて・に対(たい)して）、弟(おとうと)は理系(りけい)だ。

Arts and sciences are two different categories, not two points on one scale, so the sentence is contrasting rather than measuring. に比(くら)べて would imply one of them has more of something than the other.

The comparison heads the sentence, exactly as "compared with" does in English, and the rest is a plain statement - no より and no comparative form needed anywhere else in the sentence.

～に比べて · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 新(あたら)しいパソコンは古(ふる)いのを比(くら)べて動(うご)きが速(はや)い。

The standard takes に, not を. With を the sentence would need the shape 古(ふる)いのと新(あたら)しいのを比(くら)べる, "compare the old one and the new one", which is a different sentence altogether.

～に比べて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 去年(きょねん)に比(くら)べて、今年(ことし)は忙(いそが)しい。 (b) 去年(きょねん)に比(くら)べれば、今年(ことし)は楽(らく)だ。

(a) is a plain measurement: this year scores higher on busyness than last year did. It could open a report and carries no particular feeling.

(b) uses the conditional form, which adds a "when you put it that way" tone. It concedes that this year is hard but insists last year was worse, and it is the standard phrasing for talking yourself, or someone else, out of a complaint.

～一方だ

Meaning · keeps going in one direction; more and more ～, with no sign of turning back

～一方(いっぽう)だ reports a trend. Something is moving along a scale - rising, falling, worsening, spreading - and the speaker's point is that it has been moving that way steadily and shows no sign of stopping. It is the sentence-final shape of the newspaper headline "costs continue to climb".

～一方だ · Formation

Only verbs that contain a direction of change can take it. What goes in front is always the dictionary form; tense and politeness are carried by the だ at the end.

～一方だ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb of change V(dictionary) + 一方(いっぽう)だ 増(ふ)える → 増(ふ)える一方(いっぽう)だ
Polite ～一方(いっぽう)です 減(へ)る → 減(へ)る一方(いっぽう)です
Past ～一方(いっぽう)だった 下(さ)がる → 下(さ)がる一方(いっぽう)だった
い-adjective (via なる) ～くなる + 一方(いっぽう)だ 忙(いそが)しい → 忙(いそが)しくなる一方(いっぽう)だ
な-adjective / Noun (via なる) ～になる + 一方(いっぽう)だ 不便(ふべん) → 不便(ふべん)になる一方(いっぽう)だ
Continuing the sentence ～一方(いっぽう)で、… 増(ふ)える一方(いっぽう)で、手(て)が足(た)りない

～一方だ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Modifying a noun ～一方(いっぽう)の + Noun 増(ふ)える一方(いっぽう)の仕事(しごと)

Rule of thumb - if the verb cannot be pictured as an arrow on a graph, it cannot take 一方(いっぽう)だ. Adjectives and nouns get in only by first becoming a change verb with なる.

～一方だ · Examples

物価(ぶっか)は上(あ)がる一方(いっぽう)だ。
Prices just keep going up.

この村(むら)の人口(じんこう)は減(へ)る一方(いっぽう)です。
The population of this village keeps falling.

待(ま)っているだけでは、状況(じょうきょう)は悪(わる)くなる一方(いっぽう)だ。
If we just wait, the situation will only get worse.

スマートフォンのおかげで、生活(せいかつ)は便利(べんり)になる一方(いっぽう)だ。
Thanks to smartphones, life keeps getting more convenient.

台風(たいふう)が近(ちか)づき、雨(あめ)は強(つよ)くなる一方(いっぽう)だった。
As the typhoon drew closer, the rain just kept getting heavier.

～一方だ · Examples (cont.)

一人(ひとり)暮(ぐ)らしを始(はじ)めてから、コンビニに行(い)く回数(かいすう)が増(ふ)える一方(いっぽう)だ。
Since I started living alone, the number of trips I make to the convenience store has done nothing but go up.

医療(いりょう)費(ひ)は増(ふ)える一方(いっぽう)で、若(わか)い世代(せだい)の負担(ふたん)が重(おも)くなっている。
Medical costs keep on rising, and the burden on the younger generation is growing heavier.

会議(かいぎ)を何度(なんど)開(ひら)いても結論(けつろん)が出(で)なくて、メンバーの不満(ふまん)は大(おお)きくなる一方(いっぽう)でした。
However many meetings we held, no conclusion came out of them, and the members' frustration only grew.

～一方だ · Nuance & register

This is a written-leaning pattern. It belongs to news copy, reports, essays and the kind of speech that summarises a situation; in relaxed conversation the same idea comes out as どんどん増(ふ)えてる or だんだん悪(わる)くなってる. Saying 宿題(しゅくだい)が増(ふ)える一方(いっぽう)だ to a friend is not wrong, but it has a faintly editorial ring - which can be the joke.

The tone is observational. The speaker is standing back and reporting a curve, not reacting to it, and that is why the pattern sits so easily next to numbers and statistics. It is often used for unwelcome trends, simply because unwelcome trends are what people write about, but there is nothing negative in the grammar itself: 便利(べんり)になる一方(いっぽう)だ and 技術(ぎじゅつ)は進(すす)む一方(いっぽう)だ are perfectly ordinary.

～一方だ · Nuance & register (cont.)

One structural point. The subject of 一方(いっぽう)だ is normally a situation or a quantity rather than a person choosing to do something. A trend is not a decision, so 毎日(まいにち)走(はし)る一方(いっぽう)だ does not work as "I keep running every day" - what can rise is 走(はし)る距離(きょり), not the act itself.

～一方だ · ～一方(いっぽう)だ vs ～ばかりだ

Rule of thumb - both mean "only goes one way". 一方(いっぽう)だ is neutral and can describe a welcome trend; ばかりだ is almost always about something getting worse, and it sounds more emotional.

輸出(ゆしゅつ)は伸(の)びる一方(いっぽう)だ。
Exports keep growing.

返事(へんじ)を待(ま)つ間(あいだ)、不安(ふあん)は大(おお)きくなるばかりだった。
While I waited for a reply, my anxiety did nothing but grow.

Swapping them is not ungrammatical but does change the colour. 輸出(ゆしゅつ)は伸(の)びるばかりだ makes growth sound like a runaway problem, and 不安(ふあん)は大(おお)きくなる一方(いっぽう) だった makes your own anxiety sound like a line on a chart. Choose by whether you are reporting or complaining.

～一方だ · ～一方(いっぽう)だ vs ～ていく・～てくる

Rule of thumb - ～ていく and ～てくる simply track a change over time. 一方(いっぽう)だ adds the claim that it has gone only that way and is still going.

子(こ)どもの数(かず)が少(すく)なくなっていく。
The number of children is decreasing.

子(こ)どもの数(かず)は少(すく)なくなる一方(いっぽう)だ。
The number of children just keeps decreasing.

The first is a neutral description that leaves room for the line to level off; the second insists it has not and will not. ～ていく is also the everyday spoken form and works with any verb at all, while 一方(いっぽう)だ demands a verb of change and a register to match.

～一方(いっぽう)だ vs ～一方(いっぽう)（で） - "on the other hand"

Rule of thumb - at the end of a sentence, 一方(いっぽう)だ is a trend. Between two clauses that describe different things, 一方(いっぽう)（で） means "meanwhile, on the other hand".

田舎(いなか)の土地(とち)は安(やす)くなる一方(いっぽう)だ。
Land in the countryside just keeps getting cheaper.

都心(としん)の家賃(やちん)は上(あ)がっている。一方(いっぽう)、地方(ちほう)では空(あ)き家(や)が増(ふ)えている。
Rents in the city centre are rising. Meanwhile, empty houses are increasing in rural areas.

～一方(いっぽう)だ vs ～一方(いっぽう)（で） - "on the other hand" (cont.)

The two are told apart by position and by what surrounds them. A sentence-final 一方(いっぽう)だ after a dictionary-form verb of change is the trend reading; a clause-initial 一方(いっぽう), or ～一方(いっぽう)で joining two clauses about different subjects, is the contrast reading. The tricky middle case - 増(ふ)える一方(いっぽう)で、人手(ひとで)が足(た)りない - is the trend pattern in its て-form, and you can tell because both halves are about the same rising thing.

～一方だ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 人口(じんこう)が少(すく)ない一方(いっぽう)だ。 → ✅ 人口(じんこう)が減(へ)る一方(いっぽう)だ。
    An adjective describes a state, and a state is not a trend. You need a verb with movement in it, or an adjective converted with なる.

  • ❌ 生活(せいかつ)が苦(くる)しい一方(いっぽう)です。 → ✅ 生活(せいかつ)が苦(くる)しくなる一方(いっぽう)です。
    Same problem in polite clothing. ～くなる is the bridge from an い-adjective to a change verb.

  • ❌ 去年(きょねん)から売上(うりあげ)が増(ふ)えた一方(いっぽう)だ。 → ✅ 去年(きょねん)から売上(うりあげ)が増(ふ)える一方(いっぽう)だ。
    The verb stays in the dictionary form. If you need the past, put it on the end: 増(ふ)える一方(いっぽう)だった.

～一方だ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 私(わたし)は毎日(まいにち)走(はし)る一方(いっぽう)だ。 → ✅ 走(はし)る距離(きょり)が伸(の)びる一方(いっぽう)だ。
    A habit you have chosen is not a trend that is happening to you. The subject has to be something that can rise or fall on its own.

～一方だ · Practice 1

1. 地球(ちきゅう)の気温(きおん)は上(あ)がる____で、各国(かっこく)が対策(たいさく)を急(いそ)いでいる。

The blank needs the trend pattern in its て-form, which links the rising temperature to the response it is provoking. Both halves are about the same rise, which is what distinguishes it from the "on the other hand" 一方(いっぽう)で.

～一方だ · Practice 2

2. Choose: 部屋(へや)が（汚(きたな)い・汚(きたな)くなる）一方(いっぽう)だ。

部屋(へや)が汚(きたな)くなる一方(いっぽう)だ。

汚(きたな)い is a state, and this pattern needs a direction of travel. Turning the adjective into 汚(きたな)くなる supplies the movement that 一方(いっぽう)だ then declares to be one-way.

～一方だ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The number of foreign visitors keeps increasing."

外国(がいこく)からの観光客(かんこうきゃく)は増(ふ)える一方(いっぽう)だ。

増(ふ)える is already a change verb, so it goes straight into the dictionary form in front of 一方(いっぽう)だ. The English "keeps" needs no separate word - the pattern carries it.

～一方だ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この町(まち)の店(みせ)は閉(し)まった一方(いっぽう)だ。

✅ この町(まち)の店(みせ)は減(へ)る一方(いっぽう)だ。

Two things were wrong. The verb must be non-past, and 閉(し)まる is a one-off event rather than a movement along a scale. 減(へ)る supplies a countable quantity that can keep falling.

～一方だ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 野菜(やさい)の値段(ねだん)が上(あ)がっています。 (b) 野菜(やさい)の値段(ねだん)は上(あ)がる一方(いっぽう)です。

(a) reports the current state of prices: they are up, and they may go on rising or may not. It is the kind of thing you would say in a shop.

(b) makes a claim about the shape of the whole trend - the price has moved in one direction only, over some stretch of time, and there is no turn in sight. It sounds like commentary rather than observation, and it invites a "something must be done" to follow.

～ばかりだ

Meaning · things only go one way, and it is the wrong way - "it just keeps getting ～"

Put a verb of change in front of ばかりだ and you get a one-directional slide with a complaint attached. The pain only gets sharper, the misunderstanding only grows, the field only gets more overgrown. Where 一方(いっぽう)だ reports a trend from a distance, ばかりだ is the voice of someone watching it happen and not liking it.

～ばかりだ · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb of change V(dictionary) + ばかりだ 悪(わる)くなる → 悪(わる)くなるばかりだ
Polite ～ばかりです 増(ふ)える → 増(ふ)えるばかりです
Past ～ばかりだった 深(ふか)くなる → 深(ふか)くなるばかりだった
Continuing the sentence ～ばかりで、… 荒(あ)れる → 荒(あ)れるばかりで、人(ひと)が来(こ)ない
い-adjective (via なる) ～くなる + ばかりだ ひどい → ひどくなるばかりだ
な-adjective / Noun (via なる) ～になる + ばかりだ 不安(ふあん) → 不安(ふあん)になるばかりだ
"all that is left" あとは + V(dictionary) + ばかりだ 待(ま)つ → あとは待(ま)つばかりだ

～ばかりだ · Formation (cont.)

Note - the verb in front stays in the dictionary form. Swap it for the た-form and you have the completely different N4 pattern ～たばかりだ, "have just done": 着(つ)いたばかりだ means "I've only just arrived".

～ばかりだ · Examples

痛(いた)みは強(つよ)くなるばかりだ。
The pain just keeps getting worse.

何(なに)も言(い)わなければ、誤解(ごかい)は大(おお)きくなるばかりです。
If you say nothing, the misunderstanding will only grow.

話(はな)し合(あ)っても、二人(ふたり)の溝(みぞ)は深(ふか)くなるばかりだった。
However much they talked, the gap between them only got deeper.

薬(くすり)を飲(の)んでも、せきはひどくなるばかりだった。
Even with the medicine, my cough did nothing but get worse.

畑(はたけ)は荒(あ)れるばかりで、手入(てい)れをする人(ひと)がいない。
The fields just get more overgrown, and there's no one to look after them.

～ばかりだ · Examples (cont.)

準備(じゅんび)は終(お)わりました。あとは本番(ほんばん)を待(ま)つばかりです。
The preparations are done. All that's left now is to wait for the real thing.

人(ひと)が減(へ)った商店街(しょうてんがい)では、シャッターの閉(し)まった店(みせ)が増(ふ)えるばかりだ。
In a shopping street that has lost its people, the number of shuttered shops does nothing but grow.

荷物(にもつ)は全部(ぜんぶ)車(くるま)に積(つ)んだので、あとは明日(あした)の朝(あさ)出発(しゅっぱつ)するばかりです。
The luggage is all in the car, so all that remains is to set off tomorrow morning.

～ばかりだ · Nuance & register

ばかりだ in this sense is more literary than 一方(いっぽう)だ. It is at home in narration, in a written complaint, in a doctor's notes and in the sentence a novel uses to end a paragraph; in ordinary chat people say どんどんひどくなってる. Using it aloud gives what you say a slightly grave, resigned colour - which is often exactly the point.

It carries an evaluation, and the evaluation is negative. That is the clearest difference from 一方(いっぽう)だ, which is happy to report good news. Native speakers find 元気(げんき)になるばかりだ odd for a recovering patient, because the pattern is reaching for the wrong emotional register; the trend version of that sentence is 元気(げんき)になる一方(いっぽう)だ.

～ばかりだ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two form notes. The casual contraction ばっかり belongs to the "nothing but" ばかり you met at N4 - マンガばっかり読(よ)んでる - and is rarely heard in this trend use, where the tone is too serious for it. And the あとは～ばかりだ frame, "everything is done except this", is a set expression with a faintly formal, tidy feel; the everyday equivalent is あとは～だけだ.

～ばかりだ · ～ばかりだ vs ～一方(いっぽう)だ

Rule of thumb - same one-way movement, different speaker. 一方(いっぽう)だ reports; ばかりだ complains. Only 一方(いっぽう)だ can be used about a change you welcome.

技術(ぎじゅつ)は進(すす)む一方(いっぽう)だ。
Technology keeps advancing.

待(ま)たされて、いらいらが増(ふ)えるばかりだった。
Being kept waiting, I only got more and more irritated.

You could put ばかりだ on the first sentence, but it would turn progress into a threat. Going the other way, いらいらが増(ふ)える一方(いっぽう)だった sounds like a report on someone else's mood. Register differs too: 一方(いっぽう)だ is the newspaper, ばかりだ is the diary.

変化(へんか)の動詞(どうし) vs 動作(どうさ)の動詞(どうし) in front of ばかりだ

Rule of thumb - the verb decides the meaning. A verb of change gives "keeps getting worse". A verb of action gives "does nothing but ～".

状況(じょうきょう)は悪(わる)くなるばかりだ。
The situation only gets worse.

彼(かれ)は文句(もんく)を言(い)うばかりで、何(なに)も手伝(てつだ)ってくれない。
All he does is complain - he doesn't help with anything.

悪(わる)くなる moves along a scale, so the first is a trend. 言(い)う is something a person chooses to do, so the second is about exclusivity: complaining and nothing else. With an ongoing action the more common shape is ～てばかりいる - 文句(もんく)を言(い)ってばかりいる - and the two are close, with ばかりで leaning towards "…and therefore nothing else gets done".

～ばかりだ · V(dictionary)ばかりだ vs V(た)ばかりだ

Rule of thumb - dictionary form before ばかり = a change that keeps going. た-form before ばかり = something that finished a moment ago.

外(そと)は暗(くら)くなるばかりだ。
It's only getting darker outside.

昼(ひる)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べたばかりで、まだおなかがいっぱいだ。
I've only just eaten lunch, so I'm still full.

One string, two grammars, and the only visible difference is the form of the verb. Read the verb ending before you decide which one you are looking at - this is one of the most reliable ways to misread an N3 sentence.

～ばかりだ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 寒(さむ)いばかりだ。（meaning "it keeps getting colder"） → ✅ 寒(さむ)くなるばかりだ。
    The pattern needs a verb with movement in it. An い-adjective must first become ～くなる.

  • ❌ 病気(びょうき)は良(よ)くなるばかりだ。 → ✅ 病気(びょうき)は良(よ)くなる一方(いっぽう)だ。
    Not a form error but a tone error: ばかりだ frames the change as unwelcome, so using it about a recovery sounds contradictory.

  • ❌ あとは出発(しゅっぱつ)したばかりだ。 → ✅ あとは出発(しゅっぱつ)するばかりだ。
    In the "all that's left" frame the verb is still in the future, so it takes the dictionary form. With the た-form the sentence flips to "we've only just left".

  • ❌ 不満(ふまん)が大(おお)きくなったばかりだった。 → ✅ 不満(ふまん)が大(おお)きくなるばかりだった。
    Tense lives on だ at the end. The verb in front of ばかり stays in the dictionary form however far in the past the events are.

～ばかりだ · Practice 1

1. 問題(もんだい)を放(ほう)っておくと、状況(じょうきょう)は悪(わる)く____ばかりだ。

ばかりだ in this sense attaches to a dictionary-form verb of change, and なる is what turns the い-adjective 悪(わる)い into one. 悪(わる)いばかりだ has no movement in it and cannot express a trend.

2. Choose: 輸出(ゆしゅつ)は伸(の)びる（ばかりだ・一方(いっぽう)だ）。

Growing exports are good news, and ばかりだ colours its trend as unwelcome. The neutral, report-like 一方(いっぽう)だ is what a newspaper would print here.

～ばかりだ · Practice 4

As written, the sentence says the water had only just risen. For the trend reading the verb must be 増(ふ)える, with the past tense carried by だった at the end.

～ばかりだ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 弟(おとうと)はゲームをするばかりで、勉強(べんきょう)しない。 (b) 弟(おとうと)の成績(せいせき)は下(さ)がるばかりだ。

(a) is about exclusivity. する is an action he chooses, so ばかり says that action fills the whole picture and crowds everything else out.

(b) is about direction. 下(さ)がる is a movement along a scale, so ばかり says the marks have gone one way only, with the speaker's disapproval attached. Same word, and the verb type is what tells them apart.
