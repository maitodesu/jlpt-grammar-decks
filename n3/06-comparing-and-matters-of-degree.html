～につれて

Meaning · as one thing gradually progresses, another gradually changes with it

～につれて ties two slow changes together and says they move as a pair. Neither side is a single event: something is in the process of increasing, advancing, ageing, warming, and something else shifts along the way. English usually says this with "as", and Japanese wants both halves to be movement along a scale.

～につれて · Formation

The clause before につれて is always non-past, however clearly the sentence is set in the past. Tense is carried by the final verb only.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (change of state) V(dictionary) + につれて 増ふえる → 増ふえるにつれて
Verb, formal writing V(dictionary) + につれ 進すすむ → 進すすむにつれ
Noun naming a process Noun + につれて 成長せいちょう → 成長せいちょうにつれて
Noun with no change in it Noun + が + verb + につれて 時間じかん → 時間じかんがたつにつれて

～につれて · Formation (cont.)

Note - only nouns that already contain the idea of change work directly: 成長せいちょう, 変化へんか, 発展はってん, 回復かいふく. A plain noun needs a verb first - ❌時間じかんにつれて → ✅時間じかんがたつにつれて.

～につれて · Examples

時間じかんがたつにつれて、風かぜが強つよくなった。
As time went on, the wind picked up.

山やまを登のぼるにつれて、空気くうきが冷つめたくなった。
As we climbed the mountain, the air turned colder.

日ひが短みじかくなるにつれて、朝あさ起おきるのがつらくなる。
As the days get shorter, getting up in the morning gets harder.

試合しあいが進すすむにつれて、スタンドの声こえが大おおきくなっていった。
As the match went on, the crowd got louder and louder.

～につれて · Examples (cont.)

子こどもの成長せいちょうにつれて、靴くつのサイズが毎年まいとし変かわる。
As a child grows, their shoe size changes every year.

練習れんしゅうを重かさねるにつれて、指ゆびが自然しぜんに動うごくようになってきた。
As I put in more practice, my fingers started moving on their own.

工事こうじが進すすむにつれて、駅前えきまえの景色けしきはすっかり変かわってしまった。
As the construction went ahead, the area in front of the station changed completely.

人口じんこうが減へるにつれ、村むらの行事ぎょうじは一ひとつずつなくなっていった。
As the population fell, the village's annual events disappeared one by one.

～につれて · Nuance & register

～につれて is neutral in politeness - it sits inside the sentence, so the formality of the page is set by the ending (変かわる / 変かわります), not by the pattern. What it does carry is a mildly written flavour. It is at home in explanations, reports, narration and the sort of thing a newsreader says; in relaxed conversation people often reach for ～と、だんだん… instead: 夜よるになると、だんだん寒さむくなる.

Clipping it to につれ raises the register another notch. That form belongs to written Japanese - newspapers, official notices, essays - and sounds stiff spoken aloud.

～につれて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two habits go with the pattern. The second clause is normally a description of change, so it very often ends in ～くなる, ～ていく or ～てくる. And because the sentence is reporting an observed process, the second clause cannot be a request, a command, an invitation or a statement of intention. "As it gets colder, please close the window" is not a につれて sentence; it is a たら or と sentence.

Finally, the speaker is standing outside watching. ～につれて describes; it does not persuade. If you want the emotional weight of "the more, the more", the correlative ～ば～ほど is the pattern with the pressure in it.

～につれて · ～につれて vs ～に従って

Rule of thumb - in the parallel-change sense the two are close to interchangeable. に従って additionally has a life につれて does not have at all: following rules, instructions and arrows.

Where both work, につれて describes a change that simply happens, while に従って leans a little towards one thing being led by the other, and is the stiffer of the two:

北きたへ進すすむにつれて、雪ゆきが深ふかくなった。
The further north we went, the deeper the snow got.

But only に従って can take an instruction as its object:

係員かかりいんの指示しじに従したがって外そとに出でてください。
Please go outside following the attendant's instructions.

～につれて · ～につれて vs ～に従って (cont.)

❌指示しじにつれて is simply not Japanese. If you can replace the English "as" with "in accordance with", you need に従って.

～につれて · ～につれて vs ～とともに

Rule of thumb - につれて needs both sides to be gradual. とともに only needs them to be simultaneous, so it also covers sudden events and plain accompaniment.

春はるが近ちかづくにつれて、山やまの色いろが変かわってきた。
As spring drew near, the colour of the mountains began to change.

Swap in とともに there and nothing breaks. But とともに also does this, and につれて cannot:

ベルが鳴なるとともに、生徒せいとたちが走はしり出だした。
The moment the bell rang, the students broke into a run.

The bell is instantaneous - there is no scale to travel along - so ❌鳴なるにつれて is wrong. とともに is also the one that means "together with (a person)": 家族かぞくとともに暮くらす.

～につれて · ～につれて vs ～ば～ほど

Rule of thumb - につれて reports one actual process unfolding. ～ば～ほど states a general proportion, true whenever you test it, and it can be hypothetical.

練習れんしゅうするにつれて、発音はつおんがよくなってきた。
As I practised, my pronunciation improved.

That is a report on your own past few months. The correlative version makes a claim about the world instead:

発音はつおんは練習れんしゅうすればするほどよくなる。
The more you practise, the better your pronunciation gets.

～につれて · ～につれて vs ～ば～ほど (cont.)

The second one can be said by someone who has never practised in their life. It also allows advice and predictions after it, which につれて does not.

～につれて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 電気でんきを消けすにつれて、部屋へやが暗くらくなった。 → ✅ 電気でんきを消けすと、部屋へやが暗くらくなった。
    Flipping a switch is instantaneous. につれて needs a change that takes time and passes through middle stages.

  • ❌ 時間じかんがたったにつれて → ✅ 時間じかんがたつにつれて
    The clause before につれて is always non-past. The past tense goes on the end of the sentence.

  • ❌ 暑あつくなるにつれて、水みずをたくさん飲のんでください。 → ✅ 暑あつくなると、水みずをたくさん飲のんでください。
    A request cannot follow につれて. The second clause has to describe a change, not tell the listener to do something.

～につれて · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 時間じかんにつれて、痛いたみが消きえた。 → ✅ 時間じかんがたつにつれて、痛いたみが消きえていった。
    時間じかん is not itself a change. Add the verb that supplies one.

～につれて · Practice 1

1. 年としを____につれて、昔むかしの友ともだちに会あいたくなる。

年としを**取とる**につれて、昔むかしの友ともだちに会あいたくなる。

The verb before につれて stays in the dictionary form, and 年としを取とる is the standard collocation for ageing, which is exactly the kind of slow, stage-by-stage change this pattern wants.

～につれて · Practice 2

2. Choose: 地図ちず（につれて・に従したがって）歩あるいたら、海うみに出でた。

地図ちずに従したがって歩あるいたら、海うみに出でた。

A map is something you obey, not a process that unfolds. につれて only links two gradual changes, so it cannot take a set of directions as its object.

～につれて · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "As the meeting went on, everyone got tired."

会議かいぎが進すすむにつれて、みんな疲つかれてきた。

Both halves move by degrees, which is what licenses につれて, and the ending ～てきた is the natural way to show the tiredness accumulating rather than arriving all at once.

～につれて · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 駅えきに着つくにつれて、雨あめが降ふり出だした。

✅ 駅えきに近ちかづくにつれて、雨あめが強つよくなった。

着つく happens in an instant, and so does 降ふり出だす - neither side offers a scale. Replacing them with 近ちかづく and 強つよくなる gives two gradual changes to link.

～につれて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 人口じんこうが増ふえるにつれて、ごみの量りょうも増ふえた。 (b) 人口じんこうが増ふえれば増ふえるほど、ごみの量りょうも増ふえる。

(a) is a report about a particular place over a particular stretch of time: the population went up, and the rubbish went up alongside it. It is history.

(b) is a general law with no dates attached - for any town, more people means more rubbish. It would fit in an argument or a warning, and unlike (a) it can be said about a population increase that has not happened yet.

～に従って

Meaning · (1) in accordance with ～, following ～; (2) as ～ progresses, ～ changes with it

～に従したがって is the て-form of the verb 従したがう, "to follow, to obey", and both of its meanings come straight out of that. Follow a set of instructions and you get sense (1). Let one change follow another and you get sense (2), where it is close to ～につれて. Written as a single sentence-opening word, したがって is a third thing entirely: "therefore".

～に従って · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun: rule, order, model Noun + に従したがって 説明書せつめいしょ → 説明書せつめいしょに従したがって
Noun naming a process Noun + に従したがって 時代じだいの変化へんか → 時代じだいの変化へんかに従したがって
Verb (change of state) V(dictionary) + に従したがって 進すすむ → 進すすむに従したがって
Formal writing ～に従したがい 規則きそく → 規則きそくに従したがい
Modifying a noun ～に従したがった + Noun 指示しじ → 指示しじに従したがった行動こうどう
As an ordinary verb Noun + に従したがう ルールに従したがう

～に従って · Formation (cont.)

Note - 従したがう is an う-verb (五段ごだん / godan / Group 1), so its て-form is 従したがって with a small つ. ❌従したがて and ❌従したがいて are both wrong. As with ～につれて, the clause before it stays non-past.

～に従って · Examples

矢印やじるしに従したがって進すすんでください。
Please go on, following the arrows.

ルールに従したがってゲームをしましょう。
Let's play the game by the rules.

医者いしゃの注意ちゅういに従したがって、塩分えんぶんを減へらしています。
I'm cutting down on salt as the doctor told me to.

年としを取とるに従したがって、夜よ更ふかしがつらくなってきた。
As I get older, staying up late has started to take its toll.

～に従って · Examples (cont.)

技術ぎじゅつが進すすむに従したがって、昔むかしの道具どうぐは見みかけなくなった。
As technology moved on, the old tools stopped being a common sight.

地元じもとの習慣しゅうかんに従したがって、靴くつを脱ぬいで部屋へやに上あがった。
Following the local custom, I took off my shoes and stepped up into the room.

法律ほうりつの改正かいせいに従したがい、来月らいげつから手続てつづきが変かわります。
In accordance with the revision of the law, the procedure changes from next month.

マニュアルに従したがって操作そうさしたはずなのに、機械きかいはまったく動うごかなかった。
I'm sure I operated it according to the manual, but the machine wouldn't move at all.

～に従って · Nuance & register

This is a formal expression in both of its senses. In sense (1) it carries the weight of the verb it comes from: obeying, complying, submitting. Told to a friend that you cooked レシピに従したがって, it sounds oddly solemn - conversation prefers レシピどおりに or レシピを見みながら. Where it belongs is signage, safety announcements, contracts, court reporting and workplace instructions.

Trimming to に従したがい is the written-only variant, one register above the て-form and common in notices: 規則きそくに従したがい処理しょりいたします.

～に従って · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note who is doing the following. に従したがって puts the subject in the position of complying with something with authority behind it - a rule, an order, a doctor, a custom. Using it about a mere suggestion overstates the case, and using it about a person can sound like obedience rather than agreement: 部長ぶちょうに従したがって is "doing as the manager directs", not "along with the manager".

In sense (2), the parallel-change use, it is drier and more analytical than ～につれて, and turns up mostly in writing that is explaining a trend. It also has a faint implication that the second change is driven by the first rather than merely accompanying it.

～に従って · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - sentence-initially, したがって means "therefore". It is one of the most formal connectives in the language, at home in academic papers and legal writing: 台風たいふうが接近せっきんしている。したがって、明日あすの試合しあいは中止ちゅうしとする。 In speech you would say だから or ですから.

～に従って · ～に従って vs ～につれて

Rule of thumb - for two changes moving together, both work and に従って is the stiffer choice. For following instructions, only に従って is possible.

南みなみへ下くだるに従したがって、畑はたけが広ひろくなっていく。
As you go further south, the fields get wider.

Replace に従って with につれて there and almost nothing changes; the sentence just sounds a shade less like a geography textbook. Now the half につれて cannot reach:

先生せんせいのアドバイスに従したがって、書かき直なおしてみました。
I rewrote it following my teacher's advice.

～に従って · ～に従って vs ～につれて (cont.)

There is no gradual change here at all - just compliance - so ❌アドバイスにつれて is impossible. Test it in English: if "as" can become "in accordance with", it is に従って.

～に従って · ～に従って vs ～とおりに・～どおりに

Rule of thumb - どおりに means the result matches the model exactly. に従って means you took the instruction as your authority. Everyday speech prefers どおりに.

説明書せつめいしょのとおりに組くみ立たてたら、ねじが一ひとつ余あまった。
I assembled it exactly as the instructions said, and had one screw left over.

会社かいしゃの方針ほうしんに従したがって、報告ほうこく書しょの形式けいしきを変かえました。
We changed the format of the report in line with company policy.

～に従って · ～に従って vs ～とおりに・～どおりに (cont.)

A policy is an authority you obey; a diagram is a model you copy. Note also the shape of the word: after a noun with の it is とおりに (思おもったとおりに), but glued straight onto a noun it becomes どおりに - 予定よていどおりに, 時間じかんどおりに.

に従って inside a sentence vs したがって at the head of one

Rule of thumb - if the word is attached to a noun or verb, it means "following". If it is standing alone at the start of a sentence with a comma after it, it means "therefore".

前例ぜんれいに従したがって判断はんだんしました。
We made the decision following precedent.

前例ぜんれいがない。したがって、慎重しんちょうに判断はんだんする必要ひつようがある。
There is no precedent. Therefore, the decision must be made carefully.

Same characters, unrelated jobs. The connective is normally written in kana in modern Japanese, which helps, but not reliably - and hearing it aloud, only the position in the sentence tells you which one it is.

～に従って · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 説明書せつめいしょに従したがて → ✅ 説明書せつめいしょに従したがって
    従したがう is an う-verb, so its て-form doubles the consonant: 従したがって.

  • ❌ 指示しじにつれて動うごいてください。 → ✅ 指示しじに従したがって動うごいてください。
    Instructions are obeyed, not gradually undergone. につれて has no "in accordance with" meaning at all.

  • ❌ 年としを取とったに従したがって → ✅ 年としを取とるに従したがって
    In the parallel-change sense the verb before it stays in the dictionary form, whatever the tense of the sentence.

～に従って · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 台風たいふうが来きました。従したがって中止ちゅうしです。（in casual speech） → ✅ 台風たいふうが来きました。だから中止ちゅうしです。
    Not ungrammatical, but したがって in a chat sounds like reading out a legal notice. Keep it for writing.

～に従って · Practice 1

1. 係員かかりいんの指示しじ____従したがって、ゆっくり外そとへ出でてください。

係員かかりいんの指示しじに従したがって…

従したがう takes its object with に, never を - you comply to something in Japanese. The same に shows up in 規則きそくに従したがう and 命令めいれいに従したがう.

～に従って · Practice 2

2. Choose: 川かわを下くだる（に従したがって・のとおりに）、水みずの色いろが茶色ちゃいろくなった。

川かわを下くだるに従したがって、水みずの色いろが茶色ちゃいろくなった。

This is the parallel-change sense: two things shifting together along the length of the river. とおりに would need a model to copy, and a river is not giving anybody instructions.

～に従って · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Following the doctor's advice, I stopped drinking coffee at night."

医者いしゃのアドバイスに従したがって、夜よるはコーヒーを飲のむのをやめました。

Advice from a doctor counts as an authority you comply with, so に従したがって fits. In casual conversation 医者いしゃに言いわれたとおりに would be the lighter way to say the same thing.

～に従って · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 時代じだいが変かわったに従したがい、働はたらき方かたも変かわってきた。

✅ 時代じだいが変かわるに従したがい、働はたらき方かたも変かわってきた。

The verb in front has to be non-past. The past sense is already carried by 変かわってきた at the end, and doubling it up is what makes the original ungrammatical.

～に従って · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 先輩せんぱいのやり方かたに従したがって作つくりました。 (b) 先輩せんぱいのやり方かたのとおりに作つくりました。

(a) frames the senior colleague's method as something you were expected to comply with. It suits a workplace where that method is the rule, and it quietly says you did not deviate from it.

(b) is about the product matching the model: you copied the method, step for step. It says nothing about authority, so it is the neutral, more conversational choice - and it is what you would say if you simply thought their way was good.

～ば～ほど

Meaning · the more ～, the more ～ - two quantities that rise and fall together

～ば～ほど links a cause and a result and says they are proportional: turn the first one up and the second one goes up with it. The same word appears twice, once in the ば form and once in the plain form, which is what makes the pattern instantly recognisable. Unlike ～につれて, it is not a report on something that happened - it is a claim about how things work.

～ば～ほど · Formation

The first copy of the word goes into the conditional ば form; the second copy goes into the form that can sit in front of ほど, which is the plain form for verbs and い-adjectives and the な form for な-adjectives.

～ば～ほど · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
う-verb ば-form + V(dictionary) + ほど 読よむ → 読よめば読よむほど
る-verb ば-form + V(dictionary) + ほど 考かんがえる → 考かんがえれば考かんがえるほど
する すれば + する + ほど 練習れんしゅうする → 練習れんしゅうすれば練習れんしゅうするほど
い-adjective ～ければ + ～い + ほど 安やすい → 安やすければ安やすいほど
な-adjective ～なら + ～な + ほど 静しずか → 静しずかなら静しずかなほど
な-adjective (formal) ～であれば + あるほど 便利べんり → 便利べんりであればあるほど
Noun ～であれば + あるほど プロ → プロであればあるほど
Negative ～なければ + ～ないほど 知しらない → 知しらなければ知しらないほど
Shortened ～ほど（the ば half dropped） 早はやい → 早はやいほどいい

～ば～ほど · Formation (cont.)

Note - う-verb / る-verb are the labels this book uses for what other books call 五段ごだん / godan / Group 1 and 一段いちだん / ichidan / Group 2. For な-adjectives, both 静しずかなら静しずかなほど and 静しずかであればあるほど are correct; the second is the written, formal one, and it is the only option after a noun.

～ば～ほど · Examples

この本ほんは読よめば読よむほどおもしろくなる。
The more you read this book, the more interesting it gets.

野菜やさいは新あたらしければ新あたらしいほどおいしい。
The fresher the vegetables, the better they taste.

考かんがえれば考かんがえるほど、答こたえが分わからなくなった。
The more I thought about it, the less I understood the answer.

説明せつめいは簡単かんたんなら簡単かんたんなほどいいです。
The simpler the explanation, the better.

～ば～ほど · Examples (cont.)

部屋へやは広ひろければ広ひろいほど冬ふゆは寒さむい。
The bigger the room, the colder it is in winter.

道具どうぐは単純たんじゅんであればあるほど壊こわれにくい。
The simpler a tool is, the less likely it is to break.

締しめ切きりが近ちかづけば近ちかづくほど、なぜか部屋へやの掃除そうじがしたくなる。
The closer the deadline gets, the more I somehow want to clean my room.

人ひとの前まえで話はなすのは、準備じゅんびをすればするほど落おち着ついて話はなせるようになりますよ。
When it comes to speaking in public, the more you prepare, the more calmly you'll be able to talk.

～ば～ほど · Nuance & register

The pattern itself is register-neutral. Politeness lives on the final verb, so 安やすければ安やすいほどいい and 安やすければ安やすいほどいいです are the same grammar at different levels, and both are perfectly ordinary in conversation.

Because it states a proportion rather than an event, it is the natural shape for advice, opinions, slogans and rules of thumb - 早はやければ早はやいほどいい is something you will hear said about applications, hospital visits and train tickets alike. It is equally at home in a report describing a correlation.

～ば～ほど · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two useful habits. First, the result half is very often an evaluation: いい, 楽らくだ, 大変たいへんだ, 安心あんしんする. Second, the result is frequently a deterioration despite the "more … more" English - 考かんがえれば考かんがえるほど 分わからなくなる is one of the most common sentences built on this pattern, and English speakers reaching for "the more … the less" should notice that Japanese puts the "less" in the verb, not in the pattern.

One restriction worth remembering: the two halves must be the same word. This is not a general "if … then" - it is one scale echoing itself. If the cause and the result are different things, you want a plain ば, と or たら sentence.

～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs ～につれて

Rule of thumb - につれて narrates a change that actually took place. ば～ほど states a rule that holds every time, including in situations that have not happened.

夏なつが近ちかづくにつれて、店みせの前まえに花はなが増ふえてきた。
As summer approached, more and more flowers appeared in front of the shop.

That is one shop, one summer. The correlative version is a general truth and can be said in the middle of winter:

気温きおんが上あがれば上あがるほど、アイスがよく売うれる。
The higher the temperature, the better ice cream sells.

～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs ～につれて (cont.)

Practical consequence: only ば～ほど combines comfortably with advice, guesses and conditions - 早はやく申もうし込こめば申もうし込こむ ほど安やすくなりますよ. Try that with につれて and it sounds like you are describing prices you have already watched fall.

～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs the shortened ～ほど

Rule of thumb - the ば half can be dropped when the word is an adjective and the result is a simple evaluation. Dropping it is shorter and slightly more written; keeping it adds emphasis.

部屋へやは明あかるいほどいい。
The brighter the room, the better.

部屋へやは明あかるければ明あかるいほどいい。
The brighter the room, the better it is - really, as bright as you can get it.

～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs the shortened ～ほど (cont.)

The short version reads like a specification; the full version sounds like someone insisting. With verbs the short form is much rarer, and ❌練習れんしゅうするほど上手じょうずになる can be understood but sounds clipped - say the verb twice.

～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs どんどん・ますます

Rule of thumb - ば～ほど states a relationship between two scales. どんどん and ますます are adverbs that simply say a single thing keeps increasing, with no second quantity attached.

待まっている人ひとがどんどん増ふえてきた。
The number of people waiting kept growing.

Nothing is being proportioned there - it is one line on a graph going up. Compare:

人気にんきが出でれば出でるほど、予約よやくが取とりにくくなる。
The more popular it gets, the harder it becomes to get a reservation.

Two scales, tied together. The two often appear in the same sentence, and that is fine: 練習れんしゅうすればするほどどんどん上手じょうずになる.

～ば～ほど · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 読よむば読よむほど → ✅ 読よめば読よむほど
    The first copy of the verb has to be the ば form. Only the second one stays in the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 高たかいければ高たかいほど → ✅ 高たかければ高たかいほど
    An い-adjective drops its final い before ければ. The い survives only on the second copy, in front of ほど.

  • ❌ 食たべれば太ふとるほど → ✅ 食たべれば食たべるほど太ふとる
    The word before ば and the word before ほど must be the same one. The consequence goes at the end of the sentence.

  • ❌ 便利べんりなら便利べんりならほど → ✅ 便利べんりなら便利べんりなほど
    What sits in front of ほど is the noun-modifying form, so a な-adjective needs its な. The formal alternative is 便利べんりであればあるほど.

～ば～ほど · Practice 1

1. この問題もんだいは急いそ____急いそぐほど間違まちがえやすい。

急いそげば急いそぐほど間違まちがえやすい。

急いそぐ is an う-verb, so the ば form replaces the final ぐ with げば. The second copy is left in the dictionary form because ほど attaches to a noun-modifying form.

～ば～ほど · Practice 2

2. Choose: 子こどもは大おおきくなる（につれて・ばなるほど）、親おやと話はなさなくなる。

The slot in the sentence already contains a full dictionary-form verb, so only につれて can attach there. To use the correlative you would have to rebuild the whole thing as 大おおきくなればなるほど, with the verb appearing twice.

～ば～ほど · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The more expensive the wine, the more carefully everyone drinks it."

ワインは高たかければ高たかいほど、みんな大切たいせつに飲のむ。

高たかい supplies both halves - ければ first, plain い second - and the consequence, everyone's carefulness, comes last. Note that Japanese needs no comparative form on either adjective.

～ば～ほど · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この仕事しごとは慣なれるば慣なれるほど楽らくになります。

✅ この仕事しごとは慣なれれば慣なれるほど楽らくになります。

慣なれる is a る-verb, so its ば form is 慣なれれば - you cannot simply glue ば onto the dictionary form. The second copy is correct as it stands.

～ば～ほど · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 資料しりょうは多おおいほどいい。 (b) 資料しりょうは多おおければ多おおいほど読よむのが大変たいへんになる。

(a) is the clipped, specification-like form: more material is better, full stop. It is the kind of line you would write in a note or say when handing out instructions.

(b) spells the scale out twice, which gives it more force, and its result half runs the other way - the same increase that (a) welcomes is the thing making the work harder. Both are proportional statements; only the evaluation differs.

～ほど

Meaning · to the extent that ～; so much that ～ - and, with numbers, "about"

ほど measures. Its main job at N3 is to state how much by describing what that amount is enough to cause: you were tired to the point of not being able to stand, the room was quiet to the point that it was frightening. Attached to a number it does something simpler and softer - it turns the number into an approximation, "about".

～ほど · Formation

The measuring clause comes before ほど, and the thing being measured comes after. That is the reverse of the English order, and it is the single most important fact on this page.

～ほど · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (plain) V(plain) + ほど 歩あるけない → 歩あるけないほど疲つかれた
い-adjective ～い + ほど 恥はずかしい → 恥はずかしいほど赤あかくなった
な-adjective ～な + ほど 不思議ふしぎ → 不思議ふしぎなほどよく眠ねむれた
Noun used as a yardstick Noun + ほど 山やま → 山やまほどある

～ほど · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - English says "so X that Y"; Japanese says "Y ほど X". Build the sentence by writing the vivid consequence first, then ほど, then the plain statement it is decorating.

～ほど · Examples

駅えきまで十分じゅっぷんほどかかります。
It takes about ten minutes to the station.

今日きょうは宿題しゅくだいが山やまほどある。
I've got a mountain of homework today.

声こえが出でないほど叫さけんでしまった。
I yelled so much I lost my voice.

信しんじられないほど静しずかな朝あさだった。
It was an unbelievably quiet morning.

～ほど · Examples (cont.)

合格ごうかくの知しらせを聞きいて、泣なきたくなるほどうれしかった。
When I heard I'd passed, I was so happy I could have cried.

今日きょうは立たっていられないほど風かぜが強つよい。
The wind today is strong enough to knock you off your feet.

部長ぶちょうの話はなしは、時計とけいを見みたくなるほど長ながかった。
The manager's speech was long enough to have you checking your watch.

来週らいしゅうは一週間いっしゅうかんほど出張しゅっちょうですので、打うち合あわせは再来週さらいしゅうにしていただけませんか。
I'll be away on business for about a week, so could we make the meeting the week after next?

～ほど · Nuance & register

The degree use is neutral and works in any register, though it is a shade more written and a shade more emphatic than ぐらい. With numbers it is the more formal of the pair: a shop assistant says 三日みっかほどおまちください, a friend says 三日みっかぐらい待まって.

Because the pattern works by naming a consequence, it invites exaggeration, and spoken Japanese takes the invitation. 死しぬほど眠ねむい, 嫌いやになるほど聞きいた and 山やまほどある are set phrases you will hear constantly. They are casual and strong: 死しぬほど is fine among friends and out of place in a meeting, where 信しんじられないほど or plain とても is safer.

～ほど · Nuance & register (cont.)

The consequence clause does not have to be real. Nobody who says 泣なきたくなるほどうれしい is claiming to have cried; the clause is a measuring stick, not a report. This is why it so often appears in the negative potential - 歩あるけないほど, 見みていられないほど - describing what the degree prevented.

One caution: ほど measures a large degree. Japanese has a separate job of marking a small or minimal one - "at least this much", "even just a coffee" - and that belongs to ぐらい. Reaching for ほど there produces a sentence native speakers will not follow.

～ほど · ～ほど vs ～くらい・ぐらい

Rule of thumb - for a high degree the two overlap, with ほど the stronger and more formal. For a low or minimum degree only ぐらい works.

Overlapping:

足あしが痛いたくなるほど歩あるき回まわった。
I walked around until my feet hurt.

Swap in ぐらい and you get the same meaning in a lighter, chattier voice. Now the place they part company:

コーヒーぐらいおごりますよ。
Let me at least buy you a coffee.

～ほど · ～ほど vs ～くらい・ぐらい (cont.)

Here ぐらい says "this much and no more, it's a trivial amount", and ❌コーヒーほど is impossible. The same goes for 挨拶あいさつぐらいしなさい - "the least you could do is say hello."

～ほど · ～ほど vs ～すぎて・～て（result）

Rule of thumb - same facts, opposite order. ほど puts the consequence first and the plain statement last; a て or すぎて clause puts the cause first and the consequence last.

昨日きのうは声こえも出でないほど疲つかれていた。
Yesterday I was so tired I couldn't even speak.

昨日きのうは疲つかれすぎて、声こえも出でなかった。
Yesterday I was too tired, and couldn't even speak.

The difference is where the emphasis lands. The ほど version is a statement about how tired you were, with the silence used as a ruler. The すぎて version is a narrative: tiredness happened, and then this followed. Learners translating "so … that" word by word reliably produce the second when they wanted the first.

～ほど · ～ほど for degree vs ～ほど～ない for comparison

Rule of thumb - look at the main predicate. If it is negative and the thing before ほど is a rival being measured against, you are reading a comparison, not a degree.

昨日きのうは眠ねむれないほど暑あつかった。
Yesterday was so hot I couldn't sleep.

今日きょうは昨日きのうほど暑あつくない。
Today isn't as hot as yesterday.

～ほど · ～ほど for degree vs ～ほど～ない for comparison (cont.)

In the first, 眠ねむれない is inside the measuring clause and the sentence itself is positive. In the second, the negative is on the main adjective and 昨日きのう is the standard being beaten. When both readings are formally available the negative predicate decides it, which is why 山やまほど高たかくない means "not as high as a mountain", not "mountainously un-high".

～ほど · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 疲つかれたほど歩あるけなかった。 → ✅ 歩あるけないほど疲つかれた。
    The clause before ほど is the measuring stick, so the consequence goes there. English word order pushes learners into writing this one backwards.

  • ❌ お茶ちゃほど飲のんでいきませんか。 → ✅ お茶ちゃぐらい飲のんでいきませんか。
    "At least a cup of tea" is a minimum, and ほど cannot mark a minimum. That is ぐらい territory.

  • ❌ 涙なみだが出でるほどな話はなし → ✅ 涙なみだが出でるほどの話はなし
    ほど behaves like a noun, so it links to a following noun with の.

～ほど · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 不思議ふしぎいほど静しずかだ。 → ✅ 不思議ふしぎなほど静しずかだ。
    A な-adjective keeps its な in front of ほど, exactly as it would in front of any other noun.

～ほど · Practice 1

1. 息いきが____ほど走はしって、なんとか電車でんしゃに間まに合あった。

息いきができないほど走はしって…

The slot needs a consequence extreme enough to measure the running by, and the negative potential - literally "to the point of not being able to breathe" - is the most idiomatic filler for it.

～ほど · Practice 2

2. Choose: 返事へんじ（ほど・ぐらい）してくれてもいいのに。

返事へんじぐらいしてくれてもいいのに。

The speaker is complaining that they did not get even the smallest response, and a minimum like that is marked with ぐらい. ほど only measures large degrees, so it cannot carry "the least you could do".

～ほど · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The room was so cold that I couldn't take my coat off."

部屋へやはコートが脱ぬげないほど寒さむかった。

Japanese states the consequence first and the plain fact last, so "couldn't take my coat off" goes in front of ほど and 寒さむかった ends the sentence. Writing it in English order would produce the ungrammatical 寒さむかったほど….

～ほど · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 忙いそがしいほど昼ひるご飯はんを食たべる時間じかんもなかった。

✅ 昼ひるご飯はんを食たべる時間じかんもないほど忙いそがしかった。

The two halves were the wrong way round. Skipping lunch is the evidence, so it belongs in front of ほど, and the busyness is what the sentence is actually asserting, so it goes at the end.

～ほど · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 一時間いちじかんほど待まちました。 (b) 一時間いちじかんも待まちました。

(a) is a neutral approximation: roughly an hour, no complaint attached. The ほど softens the number, and it is the version you would use in a polite report or when telling a colleague what happened.

(b) uses も to mark the hour as more than expected - "I waited a whole hour." The amount is now the point of the sentence, and the speaker is registering impatience or surprise.

～ほど～ない

Meaning · not as ～ as ～ - the standard side takes ほど, and it is the bigger one

This is how Japanese says "A isn't as X as B". The frame is A は B ほど X ない, and the trap is which noun goes where: ほど marks B, the one that is more X. English puts the winner last ("Osaka isn't as crowded as Tokyo"); Japanese puts it in the middle, and the sentence must end in a negative.

～ほど～ない · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (the standard) Noun + ほど + ～ない 兄あに → 兄あにほど背せが高たかくない
Verb (plain) V(plain) + ほど + ～ない 話はなす → 話はなすほど簡単かんたんではない
An expectation V(た) + ほど + ～ない 思おもった → 思おもったほど高たかくない
い-adjective predicate ～くない 寒さむい → 去年きょねんほど寒さむくない
な-adjective predicate ～ではない 静しずか → 夜よるほど静しずかではない
Noun predicate ～ではない 問題もんだい → 前回ぜんかいほどの問題もんだいではない
"nothing is as ～ as" Nほど + ～ + Nはない これ → これほどうれしいことはない
Colloquial variant ほど → くらい 去年きょねんくらい寒さむくない

～ほど～ない · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - whatever ranks higher takes ほど. Say the English out loud: "not as X as B" - B is the one that gets the particle, and everything after it must be negative.

～ほど～ない · Examples

今年ことしの冬ふゆは去年きょねんほど寒さむくない。
This winter isn't as cold as last year's.

私わたしは姉あねほど料理りょうりが上手じょうずじゃない。
I'm not as good a cook as my older sister.

テストは思おもったほど難むずかしくなかった。
The test wasn't as hard as I'd expected.

この辺へんは駅前えきまえほどにぎやかではないが、買かい物ものには困こまらない。
This area isn't as lively as the area by the station, but you can get everything you need.

～ほど～ない · Examples (cont.)

口くちで言いうほど簡単かんたんではありませんでした。
It wasn't as easy as it sounds.

彼かれほど字じのきれいな人ひとを見みたことがない。
I've never met anyone whose handwriting is as beautiful as his.

人ひとに教おしえることほど勉強べんきょうになることはない。
There's nothing that teaches you as much as teaching someone else.

新あたらしい職場しょくばは前まえの会社かいしゃほど残業ざんぎょうが多おおくないので、夕ゆうご飯はんを家いえで食たべられるようになりました。
The new workplace doesn't have as much overtime as my old company, so I've been able to start eating dinner at home.

～ほど～ない · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. The pattern is as ordinary in a chat as in a report, and the politeness is set by the ending - 寒さむくない / 寒さむくないです / 寒さむくありません.

What it does carry is a quiet concession. Saying 去年きょねんほど寒さむくない grants that last year set a high bar and places this year below it, without denying that it is cold. That makes it a polite way to decline a comparison you are being pushed into: 私わたしは先輩せんぱいほどできません is modest, where できません alone would just be flat.

～ほど～ない · Nuance & register (cont.)

くらい / ぐらい can replace ほど here in casual speech, but the two are not equally safe. ぐらい also has its own "at least, merely" meaning, so 田中たなかさんぐらい話はなせない can be heard two ways. ほど is unambiguous, and it is what you want in writing.

The pattern's second life is as a superlative in disguise: N ほど … は ない, "there is nothing as … as N". It sounds more emphatic and more personal than the plain いちばん superlative, and it is the frame of a great many proverbs and strong opinions - 健康けんこうほど大切たいせつなものはない.

～ほど～ない · AはBほど～ない vs AよりBのほうが～

Rule of thumb - ほど attaches to the greater item and needs a negative. より attaches to the lesser item and needs a positive. Get the two mixed up and you say the opposite of what you meant.

The same fact, from both ends:

バスは電車でんしゃほど速はやくない。
The bus isn't as fast as the train.

バスより電車でんしゃのほうが速はやい。
The train is faster than the bus.

～ほど～ない · AはBほど～ない vs AよりBのほうが～ (cont.)

Notice that 電車でんしゃ, the faster one, carries ほど in the first sentence and follows より in the second. Learners who translate word by word tend to produce ❌電車でんしゃはバスほど速はやくない, which claims that trains are the slow ones.

There is also a form restriction. Japanese does not like より in front of a negative predicate: ❌私わたしは兄あにより背せが高たかくない is rejected by most textbooks and sounds wrong to most speakers. If your English has "not as … as" in it, use ほど.

～ほど～ない · ～ほど～ない vs あまり／そんなに～ない

Rule of thumb - ほど～ない needs a named standard. あまり～ない and そんなに～ない measure against a vague expectation instead.

この店みせはあまり高たかくない。
This place isn't very expensive.

この店みせは駅前えきまえの店みせほど高たかくない。
This place isn't as expensive as the one by the station.

～ほど～ない · ～ほど～ない vs あまり／そんなに～ない (cont.)

The first sets an absolute, if fuzzy, level; the second says nothing at all about whether the place is cheap - only that another shop is worse. This is why 思おもったほど is so common: it names your own expectation as the standard, which is the most useful yardstick a speaker has.

～ほど～ない · Nほど～はない vs the いちばん superlative

Rule of thumb - いちばん ranks inside a stated group and is neutral. N ほど～はない claims nothing anywhere beats N, and it is an opinion with feeling behind it.

夏なつの山やまで飲のむ水みずほどおいしいものはない。
There's nothing as good as water drunk on a mountain in summer.

飲のみ物ものの中なかで水みずがいちばんおいしいです。
Of all drinks, water tastes the best.

～ほど～ない · Nほど～はない vs the いちばん superlative (cont.)

The second is a survey result; the first is a declaration. Note the shape of the ほど version: the noun being praised comes first, and the sentence ends with a negated ない plus a general noun such as もの, こと, 人ひと or 所ところ.

～ほど～ない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 富士山ふじさんは高尾山たかおさんほど高たかくない。 → ✅ 高尾山たかおさんは富士山ふじさんほど高たかくない。
    ほど goes on the taller mountain. Written the first way, the sentence says Fuji is the smaller one.

  • ❌ 私わたしは兄あにより背せが高たかくない。 → ✅ 私わたしは兄あにほど背せが高たかくない。
    より does not sit comfortably in front of a negative. "Not as … as" is a ほど sentence.

  • ❌ 今日きょうは昨日きのうほど暑あついです。 → ✅ 今日きょうは昨日きのうほど暑あつくないです。
    This ほど only exists inside a negative sentence. With a positive predicate the sentence has no meaning.

  • ❌ 健康けんこうほど大切たいせつなものがある。 → ✅ 健康けんこうほど大切たいせつなものはない。
    The superlative frame ends in はない. Its whole force comes from denying that anything else measures up.

～ほど～ない · Practice 1

1. 弟おとうとは兄あに____背せが高たかくない。

弟おとうとは兄あにほど背せが高たかくない。

The older brother is the taller of the two, so he is the standard and takes ほど. The sentence then has to close in the negative, which is what turns it into "not as tall as".

～ほど～ない · Practice 2

2. Choose: 北海道ほっかいどうの冬ふゆは東京とうきょう（ほど・より）寒さむくない。 - is this sentence true to what you mean if you think Hokkaido is colder?

Neither version says what you mean. With ほど, the sentence reads "Hokkaido's winter isn't as cold as Tokyo's", which is the wrong way round; より cannot take a negative predicate at all. To say Hokkaido is colder, flip it: 東京とうきょうの冬ふゆは北海道ほっかいどうほど寒さむくない.

～ほど～ない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "My new apartment isn't as quiet as the old one."

新あたらしいアパートは前まえのアパートほど静しずかではない。

The old flat is the quieter one, so it is the standard and takes ほど. Because 静しずか is a な-adjective, the negative is ではない (casually じゃない), not 静しずかくない.

～ほど～ない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この映画えいがほどおもしろい映画えいががあります。

✅ この映画えいがほどおもしろい映画えいがはありません。

The Nほど…はない frame is a superlative built out of a denial, so it cannot end positively. As written, the original says something like "there is a film as interesting as this one", which is the opposite of the intended praise.

～ほど～ない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この仕事しごとは思おもったほど大変たいへんじゃない。 (b) この仕事しごとはあまり大変たいへんじゃない。

(a) measures the job against your own earlier expectation. It admits you were braced for something worse, and it therefore says the job is demanding - just less so than you feared.

(b) has no standard in it at all. It simply rates the job low on an absolute scale: not much trouble. Someone who had never thought about the job before could say (b), but only someone with a prior expectation can say (a).

～に比べて

Meaning · compared with ～; against the standard of ～

～に比くらべて sets a backdrop. It names the thing you are measuring against, then leaves the rest of the sentence free to say whatever you like about the other side - bigger, cheaper, quieter, twenty per cent down. Unlike より, which has to sit next to a comparative predicate, に比くらべて hands you a whole clause to work in.

～に比べて · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + に比くらべて 去年きょねん → 去年きょねんに比くらべて
Noun (mutual comparison) Noun + と比くらべて 兄あに → 兄あにと比くらべて

～に比べて · Formation (cont.)

Note - the particle is に or と, never を. を belongs to the plain verb 比くらべる and marks the pair being compared: 二ふたつのプランを比くらべる.

～に比べて · Examples

去年きょねんに比くらべて、今年ことしは雨あめが少すくない。
Compared with last year, there's been little rain this year.

兄あにに比くらべて、弟おとうとはよくしゃべる。
Compared with his older brother, the younger one is quite the talker.

前まえのモデルに比くらべて、音おとがずいぶん静しずかになりました。
Compared with the previous model, it's got a lot quieter.

都会とかいに比くらべると、この町まちは時間じかんの流ながれがゆっくりしている。
Compared with the city, time moves slowly in this town.

～に比べて · Examples (cont.)

電車でんしゃで行いくのに比くらべて、車くるまは時間じかんが読よめない。
Compared with going by train, you can't predict how long the car will take.

十年前じゅうねんまえに比くらべ、市内しないの人口じんこうは二割にわり減へっている。
Compared with ten years ago, the population of the city is down twenty per cent.

去年きょねんの夏なつに比くらべて、電気でんき代だいが倍ばいになって驚おどろいた。
Compared with last summer, the electricity bill has doubled - I was shocked.

同おなじ時期じきに入はいった人ひとたちに比くらべて、自分じぶんだけ仕事しごとが遅おそい気きがして落おち込こんでいます。
Compared with the others who started at the same time, I feel like I'm the only slow one, and it's getting me down.

～に比べて · Nuance & register

Neutral, with a slight lean towards the explanatory: news bulletins, sales reports, weather summaries and any sentence with a percentage in it. It is perfectly normal in conversation too, especially in the に比くらべると and に比くらべたら shapes, which soften it and sound more like personal impression than analysis.

Clipping to に比くらべ is written-only, one notch up in formality, and extremely common in newspapers: 前年ぜんねんに比くらべ三さんパーセント増ふえた.

～に比べて · Nuance & register (cont.)

に and と are close to interchangeable, with a tendency: に frames one thing as the fixed reference point you are working from, while と puts the two side by side as equals. 去年きょねんに比くらべて takes last year as the baseline; 兄あにと比くらべて sounds more like holding two people up against each other, and it is the one that can carry an edge of "why are you comparing us?"

One phrase worth having ready: ～に比くらべれば means "compared with that, this is nothing" - 去年きょねんの忙いそがしさに比くらべれば、 今いまは楽らくなものだ. It is a standard way of putting a complaint in perspective, your own or someone else's.

～に比べて · ～に比べて vs ～より

Rule of thumb - より is glued to a comparative statement and needs a gradable predicate. に比べて sets up a frame and then lets you say anything at all.

Where a single adjective is doing the work, both are fine and より is lighter:

地下鉄ちかてつはバスより速はやい。
The subway is faster than the bus.

But に比べて can head a whole clause, and even a clause with no comparative in it:

バスに比くらべて、地下鉄ちかてつは時間じかんが読よめるので安心あんしんだ。
Compared with the bus, the subway runs to time, so it's less stressful.

～に比べて · ～に比べて vs ～より (cont.)

❌バスより安心あんしんだ strains, because 安心あんしんだ is not being measured on a scale - the sentence is describing a situation, not ranking two numbers. A second practical difference: に比べて normally opens the sentence, which makes it the natural choice when the comparison is the topic of a paragraph.

～に比べて · ～に比べて vs ～に対して

Rule of thumb - に比べて puts two things on one scale. に対して sets them in opposition, "whereas", with no shared measure required.

去年きょねんに比くらべて、新入生しんにゅうせいの数かずが増ふえた。
Compared with last year, the number of new students has gone up.

Numbers, one axis. Now two facts that share no axis at all:

姉あねが外そとで遊あそぶのが好すきなのに対たいして、妹いもうとは一日中いちにちじゅう本ほんを読よんでいる。
Where my older sister loves being outdoors, my younger sister reads all day.

～に比べて · ～に比べて vs ～に対して (cont.)

Nothing is more or less than anything here; the two halves are simply different. Substituting に比べて would suggest reading and playing outside are two settings of one dial, which is not what the speaker means.

～に比べて · ～に比べて vs ～と違って

Rule of thumb - に比べて is a difference of degree. と違って is a difference of kind: A does this, B does not, and there is no "more" about it.

東京とうきょうの店みせに比くらべて、こちらは値段ねだんが安やすい。
Compared with the shops in Tokyo, things here are cheap.

東京とうきょうの店みせと違ちがって、この店みせは日曜にちようが休やすみだ。
Unlike the shops in Tokyo, this one closes on Sundays.

Closing on Sunday is not a lesser amount of anything, so it takes と違ちがって. A quick test: if you could add "by twenty per cent" to the English, you want に比くらべて.

～に比べて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 去年きょねんを比くらべて、今年ことしは暑あつい。 → ✅ 去年きょねんに比くらべて、今年ことしは暑あつい。
    The standard is marked with に or と. を would make last year the object being examined, which needs a second object to pair it with.

  • ❌ 歩あるくに比くらべて自転車じてんしゃは楽らくだ。 → ✅ 歩あるくのに比くらべて自転車じてんしゃは楽らくだ。
    A verb has to be nominalised with の before に比くらべて can attach to it.

  • ❌ 電車でんしゃに比くらべて、バスより安やすい。 → ✅ 電車でんしゃに比くらべて、バスは安やすい。
    One comparison, one marker. Once に比くらべて has named the standard, より is redundant and the sentence reads as if it had two of them.

～に比べて · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 姉あねに比くらべて、妹いもうとはめがねをかけていない。 → ✅ 姉あねと違ちがって、妹いもうとはめがねをかけていない。
    Wearing glasses is not a matter of degree, so there is nothing to compare. That is a と違ちがって sentence.

Last month is the fixed reference point, and a reference point takes に. と is also grammatical here but would present the two months as rivals rather than as a baseline and a result.

Arts and sciences are two different categories, not two points on one scale, so the sentence is contrasting rather than measuring. に比くらべて would imply one of them has more of something than the other.

～に比べて · Practice 4

～に比べて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 去年きょねんに比くらべて、今年ことしは忙いそがしい。 (b) 去年きょねんに比くらべれば、今年ことしは楽らくだ。

(a) is a plain measurement: this year scores higher on busyness than last year did. It could open a report and carries no particular feeling.

(b) uses the conditional form, which adds a "when you put it that way" tone. It concedes that this year is hard but insists last year was worse, and it is the standard phrasing for talking yourself, or someone else, out of a complaint.

～一方だ

Meaning · keeps going in one direction; more and more ～, with no sign of turning back

～一方いっぽうだ reports a trend. Something is moving along a scale - rising, falling, worsening, spreading - and the speaker's point is that it has been moving that way steadily and shows no sign of stopping. It is the sentence-final shape of the newspaper headline "costs continue to climb".

～一方だ · Formation

Only verbs that contain a direction of change can take it. What goes in front is always the dictionary form; tense and politeness are carried by the だ at the end.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb of change V(dictionary) + 一方いっぽうだ 増ふえる → 増ふえる一方いっぽうだ
Polite ～一方いっぽうです 減へる → 減へる一方いっぽうです
Past ～一方いっぽうだった 下さがる → 下さがる一方いっぽうだった
い-adjective (via なる) ～くなる + 一方いっぽうだ 忙いそがしい → 忙いそがしくなる一方いっぽうだ
な-adjective / Noun (via なる) ～になる + 一方いっぽうだ 不便ふべん → 不便ふべんになる一方いっぽうだ
Continuing the sentence ～一方いっぽうで、… 増ふえる一方いっぽうで、手てが足たりない
Modifying a noun ～一方いっぽうの + Noun 増ふえる一方いっぽうの仕事しごと

～一方だ · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - if the verb cannot be pictured as an arrow on a graph, it cannot take 一方いっぽうだ. Adjectives and nouns get in only by first becoming a change verb with なる.

～一方だ · Examples

物価ぶっかは上あがる**一方いっぽうだ**。
Prices just keep going up.

この村むらの人口じんこうは減へる**一方いっぽうです**。
The population of this village keeps falling.

待まっているだけでは、状況じょうきょうは悪わるくなる**一方いっぽうだ**。
If we just wait, the situation will only get worse.

スマートフォンのおかげで、生活せいかつは便利べんりになる**一方いっぽうだ**。
Thanks to smartphones, life keeps getting more convenient.

～一方だ · Examples (cont.)

台風たいふうが近ちかづき、雨あめは強つよくなる**一方いっぽうだった**。
As the typhoon drew closer, the rain just kept getting heavier.

一人ひとり暮ぐらしを始はじめてから、コンビニに行いく回数かいすうが増ふえる**一方いっぽうだ**。
Since I started living alone, the number of trips I make to the convenience store has done nothing but go up.

医療いりょう費ひは増ふえる**一方いっぽうで**、若わかい世代せだいの負担ふたんが重おもくなっている。
Medical costs keep on rising, and the burden on the younger generation is growing heavier.

～一方だ · Examples (cont.)

会議かいぎを何度なんど開ひらいても結論けつろんが出でなくて、メンバーの不満ふまんは大おおきくなる**一方いっぽうでした**。
However many meetings we held, no conclusion came out of them, and the members' frustration only grew.

～一方だ · Nuance & register

This is a written-leaning pattern. It belongs to news copy, reports, essays and the kind of speech that summarises a situation; in relaxed conversation the same idea comes out as どんどん増ふえてる or だんだん悪わるくなってる. Saying 宿題しゅくだいが増ふえる一方いっぽうだ to a friend is not wrong, but it has a faintly editorial ring - which can be the joke.

The tone is observational. The speaker is standing back and reporting a curve, not reacting to it, and that is why the pattern sits so easily next to numbers and statistics. It is often used for unwelcome trends, simply because unwelcome trends are what people write about, but there is nothing negative in the grammar itself: 便利べんりになる一方いっぽうだ and 技術ぎじゅつは進すすむ一方いっぽうだ are perfectly ordinary.

～一方だ · Nuance & register (cont.)

One structural point. The subject of 一方いっぽうだ is normally a situation or a quantity rather than a person choosing to do something. A trend is not a decision, so 毎日まいにち走はしる一方いっぽうだ does not work as "I keep running every day" - what can rise is 走はしる距離きょり, not the act itself.

～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～ばかりだ

Rule of thumb - both mean "only goes one way". 一方いっぽうだ is neutral and can describe a welcome trend; ばかりだ is almost always about something getting worse, and it sounds more emotional.

輸出ゆしゅつは伸のびる**一方いっぽうだ**。
Exports keep growing.

返事へんじを待まつ間あいだ、不安ふあんは大おおきくなるばかりだった。
While I waited for a reply, my anxiety did nothing but grow.

～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～ばかりだ (cont.)

Swapping them is not ungrammatical but does change the colour. 輸出ゆしゅつは伸のびるばかりだ makes growth sound like a runaway problem, and 不安ふあんは大おおきくなる一方いっぽう だった makes your own anxiety sound like a line on a chart. Choose by whether you are reporting or complaining.

～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～ていく・～てくる

Rule of thumb - ～ていく and ～てくる simply track a change over time. 一方いっぽうだ adds the claim that it has gone only that way and is still going.

子こどもの数かずが少すくなくなっていく。
The number of children is decreasing.

子こどもの数かずは少すくなくなる**一方いっぽうだ**。
The number of children just keeps decreasing.

～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～ていく・～てくる (cont.)

The first is a neutral description that leaves room for the line to level off; the second insists it has not and will not. ～ていく is also the everyday spoken form and works with any verb at all, while 一方いっぽうだ demands a verb of change and a register to match.

～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～一方（で） - "on the other hand"

Rule of thumb - at the end of a sentence, 一方いっぽうだ is a trend. Between two clauses that describe different things, 一方いっぽう（で） means "meanwhile, on the other hand".

田舎いなかの土地とちは安やすくなる**一方いっぽうだ**。
Land in the countryside just keeps getting cheaper.

都心としんの家賃やちんは上あがっている。一方いっぽう、地方ちほうでは空あき家やが増ふえている。
Rents in the city centre are rising. Meanwhile, empty houses are increasing in rural areas.

～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～一方（で） - "on the other hand" (cont.)

The two are told apart by position and by what surrounds them. A sentence-final 一方いっぽうだ after a dictionary-form verb of change is the trend reading; a clause-initial 一方いっぽう, or ～一方いっぽうで joining two clauses about different subjects, is the contrast reading. The tricky middle case - 増ふえる一方いっぽうで、人手ひとでが足たりない - is the trend pattern in its て-form, and you can tell because both halves are about the same rising thing.

～一方だ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 人口じんこうが**少すくない**一方いっぽうだ。 → ✅ 人口じんこうが**減へる**一方いっぽうだ。
    An adjective describes a state, and a state is not a trend. You need a verb with movement in it, or an adjective converted with なる.

  • ❌ 生活せいかつが**苦くるしい**一方いっぽうです。 → ✅ 生活せいかつが**苦くるしくなる**一方いっぽうです。
    Same problem in polite clothing. ～くなる is the bridge from an い-adjective to a change verb.

  • ❌ 去年きょねんから売上うりあげが**増ふえた**一方いっぽうだ。 → ✅ 去年きょねんから売上うりあげが**増ふえる**一方いっぽうだ。
    The verb stays in the dictionary form. If you need the past, put it on the end: 増ふえる一方いっぽうだった.

  • ❌ 私わたしは**毎日まいにち走はしる**一方いっぽうだ。 → ✅ 走はしる**距離きょりが伸のびる**一方いっぽうだ。
    A habit you have chosen is not a trend that is happening to you. The subject has to be something that can rise or fall on its own.

～一方だ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 野菜やさいの値段ねだんが上あがっています。 (b) 野菜やさいの値段ねだんは上あがる**一方いっぽうです**。

(b) makes a claim about the shape of the whole trend - the price has moved in one direction only, over some stretch of time, and there is no turn in sight. It sounds like commentary rather than observation, and it invites a "something must be done" to follow.

～ばかりだ

Meaning · things only go one way, and it is the wrong way - "it just keeps getting ～"

Put a verb of change in front of ばかりだ and you get a one-directional slide with a complaint attached. The pain only gets sharper, the misunderstanding only grows, the field only gets more overgrown. Where 一方いっぽうだ reports a trend from a distance, ばかりだ is the voice of someone watching it happen and not liking it.

～ばかりだ · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Verb of change V(dictionary) + ばかりだ 悪わるくなる → 悪わるくなるばかりだ
Polite ～ばかりです 増ふえる → 増ふえるばかりです
Past ～ばかりだった 深ふかくなる → 深ふかくなるばかりだった
Continuing the sentence ～ばかりで、… 荒あれる → 荒あれるばかりで、人ひとが来こない
い-adjective (via なる) ～くなる + ばかりだ ひどい → ひどくなるばかりだ
な-adjective / Noun (via なる) ～になる + ばかりだ 不安ふあん → 不安ふあんになるばかりだ
"all that is left" あとは + V(dictionary) + ばかりだ 待まつ → あとは待まつばかりだ

～ばかりだ · Formation (cont.)

Note - the verb in front stays in the dictionary form. Swap it for the た-form and you have the completely different N4 pattern ～たばかりだ, "have just done": 着ついたばかりだ means "I've only just arrived".

～ばかりだ · Examples

痛いたみは強つよくなるばかりだ。
The pain just keeps getting worse.

何なにも言いわなければ、誤解ごかいは大おおきくなるばかりです。
If you say nothing, the misunderstanding will only grow.

話はなし合あっても、二人ふたりの溝みぞは深ふかくなるばかりだった。
However much they talked, the gap between them only got deeper.

薬くすりを飲のんでも、せきはひどくなるばかりだった。
Even with the medicine, my cough did nothing but get worse.

～ばかりだ · Examples (cont.)

畑はたけは荒あれるばかりで、手入ていれをする人ひとがいない。
The fields just get more overgrown, and there's no one to look after them.

準備じゅんびは終おわりました。あとは本番ほんばんを待まつばかりです。
The preparations are done. All that's left now is to wait for the real thing.

人ひとが減へった商店街しょうてんがいでは、シャッターの閉しまった店みせが増ふえるばかりだ。
In a shopping street that has lost its people, the number of shuttered shops does nothing but grow.

荷物にもつは全部ぜんぶ車くるまに積つんだので、あとは明日あしたの朝あさ出発しゅっぱつするばかりです。
The luggage is all in the car, so all that remains is to set off tomorrow morning.

～ばかりだ · Nuance & register

ばかりだ in this sense is more literary than 一方いっぽうだ. It is at home in narration, in a written complaint, in a doctor's notes and in the sentence a novel uses to end a paragraph; in ordinary chat people say どんどんひどくなってる. Using it aloud gives what you say a slightly grave, resigned colour - which is often exactly the point.

It carries an evaluation, and the evaluation is negative. That is the clearest difference from 一方いっぽうだ, which is happy to report good news. Native speakers find 元気げんきになるばかりだ odd for a recovering patient, because the pattern is reaching for the wrong emotional register; the trend version of that sentence is 元気げんきになる一方いっぽうだ.

～ばかりだ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two form notes. The casual contraction ばっかり belongs to the "nothing but" ばかり you met at N4 - マンガばっかり読よんでる - and is rarely heard in this trend use, where the tone is too serious for it. And the あとは～ばかりだ frame, "everything is done except this", is a set expression with a faintly formal, tidy feel; the everyday equivalent is あとは～だけだ.

～ばかりだ · ～ばかりだ vs ～一方だ

Rule of thumb - same one-way movement, different speaker. 一方いっぽうだ reports; ばかりだ complains. Only 一方いっぽうだ can be used about a change you welcome.

技術ぎじゅつは進すすむ**一方いっぽうだ**。
Technology keeps advancing.

待またされて、いらいらが増ふえるばかりだった。
Being kept waiting, I only got more and more irritated.

～ばかりだ · ～ばかりだ vs ～一方だ (cont.)

You could put ばかりだ on the first sentence, but it would turn progress into a threat. Going the other way, いらいらが増ふえる一方いっぽうだった sounds like a report on someone else's mood. Register differs too: 一方いっぽうだ is the newspaper, ばかりだ is the diary.

～ばかりだ · 変化の動詞 vs 動作の動詞 in front of ばかりだ

Rule of thumb - the verb decides the meaning. A verb of change gives "keeps getting worse". A verb of action gives "does nothing but ～".

状況じょうきょうは悪わるくなるばかりだ。
The situation only gets worse.

彼かれは文句もんくを言いうばかりで、何なにも手伝てつだってくれない。
All he does is complain - he doesn't help with anything.

～ばかりだ · 変化の動詞 vs 動作の動詞 in front of ばかりだ (cont.)

悪わるくなる moves along a scale, so the first is a trend. 言いう is something a person chooses to do, so the second is about exclusivity: complaining and nothing else. With an ongoing action the more common shape is ～てばかりいる - 文句もんくを言いってばかりいる - and the two are close, with ばかりで leaning towards "…and therefore nothing else gets done".

～ばかりだ · V(dictionary)ばかりだ vs V(た)ばかりだ

Rule of thumb - dictionary form before ばかり = a change that keeps going. た-form before ばかり = something that finished a moment ago.

外そとは暗くらくなるばかりだ。
It's only getting darker outside.

昼ひるご飯はんを食たべたばかりで、まだおなかがいっぱいだ。
I've only just eaten lunch, so I'm still full.

One string, two grammars, and the only visible difference is the form of the verb. Read the verb ending before you decide which one you are looking at - this is one of the most reliable ways to misread an N3 sentence.

～ばかりだ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 寒さむいばかりだ。（meaning "it keeps getting colder"） → ✅ 寒さむくなるばかりだ。
    The pattern needs a verb with movement in it. An い-adjective must first become ～くなる.

  • ❌ 病気びょうきは**良よくなるばかりだ**。 → ✅ 病気びょうきは**良よくなる一方いっぽうだ**。
    Not a form error but a tone error: ばかりだ frames the change as unwelcome, so using it about a recovery sounds contradictory.

  • ❌ あとは出発しゅっぱつしたばかりだ。 → ✅ あとは出発しゅっぱつするばかりだ。
    In the "all that's left" frame the verb is still in the future, so it takes the dictionary form. With the た-form the sentence flips to "we've only just left".

～ばかりだ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 不満ふまんが**大おおきくなったばかりだった**。 → ✅ 不満ふまんが**大おおきくなるばかりだった**。
    Tense lives on だ at the end. The verb in front of ばかり stays in the dictionary form however far in the past the events are.

～ばかりだ · Practice 1

1. 問題もんだいを放ほうっておくと、状況じょうきょうは悪わるく____ばかりだ。

状況じょうきょうは悪わるくなるばかりだ。

ばかりだ in this sense attaches to a dictionary-form verb of change, and なる is what turns the い-adjective 悪わるい into one. 悪わるいばかりだ has no movement in it and cannot express a trend.

～ばかりだ · Practice 2

2. Choose: 輸出ゆしゅつは伸のびる（ばかりだ・一方いっぽうだ）。

輸出ゆしゅつは伸のびる**一方いっぽうだ**。

Growing exports are good news, and ばかりだ colours its trend as unwelcome. The neutral, report-like 一方いっぽうだ is what a newspaper would print here.

～ばかりだ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Everything's ready - all that's left is to press the button."

準備じゅんびはできました。あとはボタンを押おすばかりです。

The あとは～ばかりだ frame says every other step is finished and one action remains. The remaining action is still to come, so the verb stays in the dictionary form.

～ばかりだ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 雨あめがやまなくて、水みずが増ふえたばかりだった。

✅ 雨あめがやまなくて、水みずが増ふえるばかりだった。

As written, the sentence says the water had only just risen. For the trend reading the verb must be 増ふえる, with the past tense carried by だった at the end.

～ばかりだ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 弟おとうとはゲームをするばかりで、勉強べんきょうしない。 (b) 弟おとうとの成績せいせきは下さがるばかりだ。

(a) is about exclusivity. する is an action he chooses, so ばかり says that action fills the whole picture and crowds everything else out.

(b) is about direction. 下さがる is a movement along a scale, so ばかり says the marks have gone one way only, with the speaker's disapproval attached. Same word, and the verb type is what tells them apart.
