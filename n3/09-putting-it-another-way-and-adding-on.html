という - called; named

Meaning · attaches a name to the thing it names - "a Y called X"

山本やまもとさんという人ひと is "a person called Yamamoto"; 「そら」という店みせ is "a shop called Sora". The name goes first, the ordinary noun goes last, and という - literally "which one says" - is the joint between them. The same joint fastens a whole clause to a noun: 中止ちゅうしになったという話はなし, "word that it has been called off."

という - called; named · Formation

という sits between two nouns, or between a plain-form clause and a noun. Nothing in front of it changes shape, and a clause ending in a noun or a な-adjective keeps its だ.

という - called; named · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the name comes first. ❌人ひとという山本やまもとさん reverses the two and says something nonsensical. Whatever という points forward to is the general word; whatever sits behind it is the label.

Note - という is for names the listener may not know. Once a name is common ground it drops out: you say 京都きょうとに行いきました, not 京都きょうとという町まちに行いきました, unless you are genuinely introducing the place to someone who has never heard of it.

という - called; named · Examples

田中たなかさんという方かたから電話でんわがありました。
There was a call from someone called Tanaka.

「そら」という店みせを知しっていますか。
Do you know a shop called Sora?

それは何なんという花はなですか。
What is that flower called?

この近ちかくに「みどり」という喫茶店きっさてんがあります。
There's a coffee shop called Midori near here.

という - called; named · Examples (cont.)

来週らいしゅうから工事こうじが始はじまるという話はなしを聞ききました。
I've heard talk that the building work starts next week.

社長しゃちょうが辞やめるという噂うわさが社内しゃないに広ひろがっています。
A rumour that the president is stepping down is going round the company.

息子むすこの学校がっこうから、来月らいげつ運動会うんどうかいを行おこなうというお知しらせが届とどきました。
A notice came from my son's school saying that sports day will be held next month.

「星ほしの降ふる夜よる」という小説しょうせつが映画えいがになると聞きいて、久ひさしぶりに本屋ほんやに行いきました。
I heard that a novel called "The Night the Stars Fell" is being made into a film, so I went to a bookshop for the first time in ages.

という - called; named · Nuance & register

という is register-neutral and enormously common. It is not decoration: leaving it out where it is needed produces a noun phrase that will not parse, and putting it in where it is not needed makes you sound as though you doubt the listener has heard of Kyoto.

In speech it shrinks. という becomes っていう, and then simply って: 田中たなかさんって人ひと, 辞やめるって話はなし. You will also see it written ってゆう in casual messages, which reflects how the reduced form is actually pronounced. All of these are conversational only - in a report or an email to a client, write という in full. Note too that the bare って is doing several jobs at once in speech (quotation, topic-marking, hearsay), so context rather than form tells you which one you are hearing.

という - called; named · Nuance & register (cont.)

With a person's name, the phrase carries a small social signal: it presents the person as unknown to the speaker as well as to the listener. 田中たなかさんという方かたがいらっしゃいました is what a receptionist says about a stranger. Said about a colleague you see daily, it would sound as though you were disowning them.

For your own name the pattern is not used. Introducing yourself, you say 田中たなかと申もうします - the という frame describes a name from the outside, and you are on the inside of yours.

という - called; named · Nuance & register (cont.)

One shape to keep separate. When the same noun appears on both sides of という - 家いえという家いえ, 店みせという店みせ - it is not naming anything. That is a different pattern, treated at N1, meaning "every single one of them". The test is mechanical: two different nouns means "called"; the same noun twice means "every".

という - called; named · X という Y vs X の Y

Rule of thumb - という says the two nouns are the same thing, one naming the other. の says they are different things in some relationship.

田中たなかさんという医者いしゃに見みてもらいました。
I was seen by a doctor called Tanaka.

田中たなかさんの医者いしゃに見みてもらいました。
I was seen by Tanaka's doctor.

という - called; named · X という Y vs X の Y (cont.)

In the first, Tanaka is the doctor. In the second, Tanaka is somebody else and the doctor belongs to him. The same split explains 「坊ぼっちゃん」という本ほん (a book whose title is Botchan) against 先生せんせいの本ほん (a book belonging to, or written by, the teacher).

Japanese does have an appositive の - 友ともだちの木村きむらさん, "my friend Kimura" - but notice the order there: the general noun comes first and the name second. With という the name always leads. If you find yourself wanting の between a name and the word it names, in that order, you want という.

Clause + という + noun vs a plain noun-modifying clause

Rule of thumb - if the noun is a container for words or information - 話はなし, 噂うわさ, 知しらせ, 情報じょうほう, 意見いけん, 点てん - the clause needs という. If the clause simply describes the noun, attach it directly.

林はやしさんが入院にゅういんしたという知しらせが届とどいた。
Word came that Hayashi had gone into hospital.

林はやしさんが入院にゅういんした病院びょういんは駅えきの前まえです。
The hospital Hayashi went into is in front of the station.

Clause + という + noun vs a plain noun-modifying clause (cont.)

The first noun holds the content of the clause - the news is that he was admitted - and という is the join. The second noun is a place the clause simply describes, so the clause attaches with nothing in between. Slipping という in there is not ungrammatical, but it changes the sentence: 入院にゅういんしたという病院びょういん is "the hospital he is said to have gone into", with the information marked as second-hand. That reportive flavour is a side effect of the quotative, and it is worth knowing about before you add a という you did not need.

という - called; named · Naming という vs the repeated-noun という

Rule of thumb - count the nouns. Two different nouns is the naming pattern on this page. The same noun on both sides is the N1 pattern meaning "every single one".

台風たいふうのあと、港みなとに「あおば」という船ふねだけが残のこっていた。
After the typhoon, the only boat left in the harbour was one called Aoba.

台風たいふうのあと、港みなとの**船ふねという船ふね**が傷きずついていた。
After the typhoon, every last boat in the harbour was damaged.

Same three characters, two unrelated grammars, and no real ambiguity once you have seen the repetition. The repeated form is vivid and slightly exaggerated, and it needs a set big enough to sweep across; the naming form is neutral and works with one item.

という - called; named · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 人ひとという山本やまもとさんから電話でんわがありました。 → ✅ 山本やまもとさんという人ひとから電話でんわがありました。
    The name goes in front, the general noun behind. Reversing them is the single commonest error with this pattern, and it comes straight from the English word order "a person called Yamamoto".

  • ❌ 給料きゅうりょうが高たかいだという話はなしを聞きいた。 → ✅ 給料きゅうりょうが高たかいという話はなしを聞きいた。
    い-adjectives never take だ. The だ in the Formation table belongs to clauses that end in a noun or a な-adjective.

  • ❌ それは何なんという意味いみですか。 → ✅ それはどういう意味いみですか。
    Asking what something means is どういう意味いみ, not 何なんという. Keep 何なんという for asking a name: 何なんという店みせですか.

という - called; named · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌（in a written report）「中村なかむら」って人ひとが来きました。 → ✅ 中村なかむらさんという方かたが来こられました。
    って is the spoken reduction of という and looks careless on paper. The full form is the one that belongs in writing.

The name is being attached to the general noun 方かた, which is what という is for. The phrasing also signals that the visitor is a stranger to everyone in the room - exactly what a receptionist means.

という - called; named · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference: (a) 通とおりの店みせという店みせが閉しまっていた。 (b) 通とおりの「みどり」という店みせが閉しまっていた。

(a) repeats the same noun on both sides, so it is not naming anything. It means every single shop on the street was shut, and it carries a note of surprise or dismay at the scale of it. That repeated-noun pattern is treated at N1.

(b) has two different nouns: a name and the general word it labels. It reports one shop, identified by the name Midori. If you are unsure which pattern you are looking at, check whether the words on either side of という are the same.

～というのは - means; is

Meaning · picks a word up and announces that its definition is coming - "X means…", "X is…"

言いう is doing its literal work here: というのは is "the one that gets called X". Everything in front of it is a label; everything after it is what the label stands for. This is the frame you use when you know the listener does not know the word - and it is how dictionaries, glossaries and patient colleagues all sound.

～というのは - means; is · Formation

というのは attaches to whatever you are holding up as a term: usually a noun, sometimes a quoted phrase, sometimes a whole clause when the thing being defined is an action.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + というのは 敷金しききん → 敷金しききんというのは
Quoted word or phrase 「…」+ というのは 「遠慮えんりょ」 → 「遠慮えんりょ」というのは
Clause - defining an action plain form + というのは 人ひとに頼たよる → 人ひとに頼たよるというのは
Usual closing …ことだ／…という意味いみだ …預あずけるお金かねのことです
Casual contraction っていうのは／ってのは 敷金しききんってのは

Note - the noun in front takes no だ: ❌敷金しききんだというのは. And the sentence normally lands on …ことです or …という意味いみです, because what you are describing is a word, not an object.

～というのは - means; is · Examples

「大家おおや」というのは、アパートの持もち主ぬしのことです。
"Ōya" means the owner of the building you rent from.

「寝坊ねぼう」というのは、朝あさ起おきられないことです。
"Nebō" means failing to get up in the morning.

敷金しききんというのは、部屋へやを借かりるときに大家おおやさんに預あずけるお金かねです。
A shikikin is the money you leave with the landlord when you rent a room.

自由じゆうというのは、何なにをしてもいいという意味いみではない。
Freedom does not mean that anything goes.

～というのは - means; is · Examples (cont.)

「花見はなみ」というのは、桜さくらの下したで飲のんだり食たべたりする春はるの行事ぎょうじです。
Hanami is the spring custom of eating and drinking under the cherry trees.

うちの会社かいしゃで「直行ちょっこう」というのは、家いえから直接ちょくせつ取引先とりひきさきへ行いくことを指さします。
At our company, "chokkō" refers to going straight from home to a client.

日本にほんの会社かいしゃで「検討けんとうします」というのは、やんわり断ことわる言いい方かただと考かんがえたほうがいいでしょう。
At a Japanese company, "we'll look into it" is best understood as a gentle refusal.

人ひとを育そだてるというのは、自分じぶんも一緒いっしょに変かわっていくことなのだと、最近さいきん思おもうようになりました。
I've come to feel lately that raising someone means changing alongside them.

～というのは - means; is · Nuance & register

The pattern itself is register-neutral, but the act is not: you are explaining a word, so you are momentarily the one who knows. Used on a word the listener obviously knows, it sounds like a lecture. Used on a word you have just introduced yourself, it is simply helpful, and it is exactly what a Japanese speaker will produce if you ask 「敷金しききんって何なんですか」.

In speech the whole thing collapses. っていうのは is the everyday form, ってのは is looser still and leans male in casual conversation, and plain って - the N4 workhorse - does the same job with less ceremony. In writing you go the other way: とは is the compact form that opens dictionary headwords and legal definitions.

～というのは - means; is · Nuance & register (cont.)

One structural habit worth copying: the definition normally ends on …ことです, …という意味いみです or …ということです. Because the subject of the sentence is a word rather than a thing, closing on a bare noun can feel like you stopped halfway. 大家おおやというのはアパートの持もち主ぬしのことです is the polished version of a sentence many learners end at 持もち主ぬしです.

～というのは - means; is · というのは vs とは

Rule of thumb - same job, different medium. というのは is what you say; とは is what you write. とは is shorter, drier, and expects a full definition after it.

「時効じこう」とは、一定いっていの時間じかんが過すぎて権利けんりがなくなることをいう。
"Jikō" denotes the lapsing of a right after a fixed period of time.

「時効じこう」というのは、時間じかんが経たつともう訴うったえられなくなる、ということですね。
So "jikō" means that after enough time passes you can't be sued any more, right?

～というのは - means; is · というのは vs とは (cont.)

The first belongs in a textbook; the second belongs in a conversation about one. とは also turns up in speech with a raised eyebrow - プロとは思おもえない, "you'd never call that professional" - but that is a different とは and does not define anything.

～というのは - means; is · というのは vs ということは

Rule of thumb - というのは explains a word. ということは draws a conclusion from a fact. If English wants "which means…", you need ということは.

「定休日ていきゅうび」というのは、毎週まいしゅう決きまっている休やすみの日ひのことです。
"Teikyūbi" means the fixed day each week when a shop is closed.

明日あしたは定休日ていきゅうびだ。ということは、買かい物ものは今日きょう済すませないといけない。
Tomorrow's their closing day. Which means the shopping has to be done today.

The giveaway is what sits in front. A bare label takes というのは; a finished statement of fact takes ということは.

～というのは - means; is · というのは vs のは

Rule of thumb - のは takes an act and comments on it. というのは takes the idea or the name of that act and explains it. If you could put quotation marks round the front half, you want というのは.

朝あさ早はやく起おきるのは苦手にがてです。
I'm no good at getting up early.

「朝活あさかつ」というのは、出勤しゅっきん前まえの時間じかんを勉強べんきょうや運動うんどうに使つかうことです。
"Asakatsu" means using the time before work for study or exercise.

The first is about your actual mornings. The second never touches anyone's morning - it is about a term. Swapping them produces sentences that are grammatical and beside the point.

～というのは - means; is · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 敷金しききんだというのは、預あずけるお金かねのことです。 → ✅ 敷金しききんというのは、預あずけるお金かねのことです。
    No だ on the noun being defined. The だ version is the quoting という ("saying that it's a deposit"), which is not what you mean.

  • ❌ 「花見はなみ」というのは、とても楽たのしいです。 → ✅ 「花見はなみ」は、とても楽たのしいです。
    というのは promises a definition, not an opinion. If you are saying how you feel about the thing itself, plain は is what you want.

  • ❌ 私わたしというのは田中たなかです。 → ✅ 私わたしは田中たなかです。
    You are not a vocabulary item. というのは frames its noun as a term to be explained, so using it on yourself sounds like you are defining the word "I".

～というのは - means; is · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 残業ざんぎょうということは、決きめられた時間じかんの後あとに働はたらくことです。 → ✅ 残業ざんぎょうというのは、…
    A single word takes というのは. ということは needs a whole fact in front of it and produces an inference, not a definition.

～というのは - means; is · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 一人暮ひとりぐらしをするのは大変たいへんです。 (b) 一人暮ひとりぐらしというのは、思おもっているより大変たいへんなものです。

(a) comments on the act itself, and most naturally on your own or a specific person's situation: living alone is hard work.

というのは - because

Meaning · opens the sentence that gives the reason for what you have just said - "the reason being that…"

You make a statement. You can hear the listener's silent "why?". というのは starts a new sentence and answers it. Unlike から or ので, which live inside the sentence they explain, this one stands at the head of its own - which is why it can carry a long, unhurried reason that would sag if you tried to bolt it on.

というのは - because · Formation

This というのは is a sentence-opening connective. It follows a full stop, takes a comma after it, and the clause it introduces almost always closes on からだ, ためだ or のだ.

Attaches to Form Example
After a finished sentence …。というのは、…からだ。 行いけない。というのは、熱ねつがあるからだ。
Polite style …。というのは、…からです。 …熱ねつがあるからです。
With のだ instead of から …。というのは、…のです。 …急きゅうに熱ねつが出でたのです。
Near-identical variant というのも、…からだ というのも、熱ねつがあるからだ
Casual speech っていうのは、…んだ っていうのは、熱ねつがあるんだ
Formal writing なぜなら（ば）、…からである -

というのは - because · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - if you delete the reason clause, what is left must still be a complete, sensible sentence. というのは is an afterthought by design; it cannot be the grammatical backbone of anything.

というのは - because · Examples

今日きょうは早はやく帰かえります。というのは、母ははが来くるからです。
I'm going home early today - my mother is coming, you see.

その仕事しごとは断ことわった。というのは、時間じかんが足たりなかったからだ。
I turned that job down. The reason is that there wasn't enough time.

来週らいしゅうの集あつまりには行いけません。というのは、その日ひは出張しゅっちょうが入はいっているからです。
I can't make next week's get-together - I've got a business trip that day.

この辞書じしょをずっと使つかっています。というのは、例文れいぶんが多おおくて分わかりやすいからです。
I've been using this dictionary for years, because it has plenty of example sentences and is easy to follow.

というのは - because · Examples (cont.)

彼かれの話はなしはあまり信しんじないほうがいい。というのは、前まえにも同おなじことで人ひとを困こまらせているからだ。
You'd do better not to take what he says at face value; he's landed people in trouble with the same story before.

今年ことしの夏なつは旅行りょこうをやめることにした。というのは、去年きょねん買かった車くるまのローンがまだ残のこっているからだ。
We've decided against a holiday this summer - the loan on the car we bought last year still isn't paid off.

申もうし訳わけありませんが、明日あしたの会議かいぎは欠席けっせきさせてください。というのは、工場こうじょうのほうで急きゅうに問題もんだいが起おきてしまったのです。
I'm sorry, but please let me miss tomorrow's meeting - a problem has suddenly come up at the plant.

というのは - because · Examples (cont.)

私わたしはこの町まちを離はなれられない。というのは、ここには祖父そふが残のこしてくれた畑はたけがあるからだ。
I can't leave this town. The reason is that my grandfather's fields are here.

というのは - because · Nuance & register

This is a considered connective. Splitting the reason off into its own sentence buys you a beat of pause, so it suits explanations you have thought about: declining an invitation, justifying a decision, walking a reader through an argument. It is at home in essays, reports, formal e-mail and prepared speech, and it appears in careful conversation too - but in fast, casual talk people reach for だって, から or a bare のだ instead.

というのも is the same connective with も and is, if anything, slightly more common in writing. Treat them as interchangeable at this level; も adds a faint sense of "and there is this on top of it", which is why it often follows a reason already given.

というのは - because · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two small register facts. First, the polite version is fully polite - the ending carries the politeness (…からです), while というのは itself is fixed and never becomes といいますのは in ordinary speech. Second, dropping the からだ at the end and simply stating the reason flat sounds abrupt in writing, though it is normal in speech: 「行いけない。というのは、熱ねつがあってね。」

というのは - because · というのは vs なぜなら

Rule of thumb - same slot, different formality. なぜなら is written, structured and a little insistent; というのは is the version you can say out loud without sounding like a textbook.

この方法ほうほうはすすめられない。なぜなら、コストが高たかすぎるからである。
This approach cannot be recommended, because the cost is too high.

この方法ほうほうはあまりすすめません。というのは、コストが高たかすぎるからです。
I wouldn't really recommend this approach - the cost is too high.

なぜなら all but demands からだ／からである at the end; leaving it off feels unfinished. というのは is looser about it. Note too that なぜなら announces a reason before you have heard any of it, which is what makes it feel like an argument being built.

というのは - because · というのは vs だって

Rule of thumb - だって is the same move at the bottom of the register ladder, and it carries a defensive tone: you are answering a criticism, not filling in background.

全部ぜんぶ食たべちゃった。だって、おいしかったんだもん。
I ate the lot. Well - it was good!

全部ぜんぶ食たべてしまいました。というのは、残のこすと失礼しつれいになると思おもったからです。
I finished everything, because I thought leaving food would be rude.

だって is casual and reads as childish or petulant from an adult in a formal setting. というのは is neutral. If you are explaining yourself to a superior, the choice is not stylistic.

The "because" というのは vs the "means" というのは

Rule of thumb - look at what sits in front. A finished sentence and a full stop → "because". A bare noun or quoted word → "means". The endings confirm it: …からだ versus …ことだ.

傘かさを持もっていったほうがいい。というのは、午後ごごから雨あめになるからだ。
You'd better take an umbrella - it's going to rain from the afternoon.

「夕立ゆうだち」というのは、夏なつの午後ごごに急きゅうに降ふる雨あめのことだ。
"Yūdachi" means the rain that falls suddenly on a summer afternoon.

Nothing about the phrase itself distinguishes them; position and closing do all the work. In speech, a clear pause before というのは also marks the "because" one.

というのは - because · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 熱ねつがあるからというのは、行いけません。 → ✅ 行いけません。というのは、熱ねつがあるからです。
    The order is fixed: statement first, then というのは, then the reason. Putting the reason in front leaves the connective with nothing to attach back to.

  • ❌ 行いけません。というのは、熱ねつがあります。 → ✅ …熱ねつがあるからです。
    In writing, the reason clause needs からだ／からです, ためだ or のです to close it. A bare statement leaves the sentence sounding cut off.

というのは - because · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 雨あめが降ふっているというのは、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしです。 → ✅ 雨あめが降ふっているので、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしです。
    Here the reason comes first and the result second - that is ので/から territory. というのは can only look backwards.

  • ❌ 遅おくれてすみません。というのは、電車でんしゃが止とまったからです。（to a customer, in person）→ ✅ 遅おくれて申もうし訳わけありません。電車でんしゃが止とまってしまいまして…
    Not ungrammatical, but announcing "the reason is" while apologising sounds like you are building a defence. Apologies usually run the reason in with て and trail off.

～というのは - the thing about X

Meaning · lifts a noun up and holds it there while you say what it is really like

This is というのは used not to define a word but to single a topic out. は would just say "as for X"; というのは says "X - now, the thing about X is…", and it commits you to a verdict. The noun in front can be a whole class （親おや, 時間じかん, 人生じんせい） or one particular thing you want to pick out of the room, and the sentence that follows is almost always an opinion, a generalisation, or something you have discovered the hard way.

The shape is identical to the defining というのは. What tells them apart is what comes after: a definition, or a judgement.

～というのは - the thing about X · Nuance & register

This use has a reflective feel. It sets the noun down, steps back, and pronounces - which is why it turns up in essays, in advice, in the mouths of older speakers, and in anyone's voice at the end of a long day. Said about something trivial it sounds pompous: このボールペンというのは安やすい is a strange sentence, because nobody needs a verdict on a pen.

In conversation the contraction って does the same work and does it constantly: 「親おやってありがたいよね」, 「会議かいぎって長ながいよね」. That form is casual but not rude, and it is the single most common way this idea gets expressed out loud. っていうのは sits in between - spoken, but with a beat of deliberation.

～というのは - the thing about X · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because the pattern promises a generalisation, it pairs naturally with ものだ, ものではない, らしい and のだろう, and it likes adverbs of the "as a rule" family - なかなか, どうしても, つい, やはり. If your sentence has none of that flavour and you only want to say what one thing is like right now, plain は is the better particle.

～というのは - the thing about X · というのは vs は

Rule of thumb - は reports on this thing. というのは raises the noun to "X, as a kind of thing" and warns the listener that a verdict is coming.

父ちちは厳きびしい人ひとでした。
My father was a strict man.

父親ちちおやというのは、子こどもの前まえでは弱よわいところを見みせないものだ。
Fathers, as a rule, don't let their children see them weak.

The first is a fact about one man. The second is a claim about fathers in general, and the speaker is prepared to defend it. Note that you cannot swap them freely: the second sentence with plain は would sound like a report on some specific father, and the first with というのは would sound like you were about to define the word "father".

～というのは - the thing about X · というのは vs って

Rule of thumb - identical meaning, different register. って is the spoken default; というのは is what you write, and what you say when you want the weight.

引ひっ越こしって、思おもったよりお金かねがかかるね。
Moving house costs more than you'd think, doesn't it.

引ひっ越こしというのは、思おもっている以上いじょうにお金かねも時間じかんもかかるものです。
Moving house takes far more money and time than people imagine.

The second would sound stiff between friends and exactly right in an article or a piece of advice to a junior colleague. The first would look sloppy in writing. Nothing else separates them.

～というのは - the thing about X · というのは vs というものは

Rule of thumb - というものは is the same gesture with more weight behind it. It sounds like the opening of a proverb, and it is nearly always followed by a sweeping claim.

失敗しっぱいというのは、誰だれにでもあることです。
Failure is something that happens to anybody.

時ときというものは、待まってはくれない。
Time, in the end, waits for no one.

Use というものは sparingly. In ordinary conversation it can sound theatrical - the linguistic equivalent of standing up before you speak - whereas というのは passes unnoticed.

～というのは - the thing about X · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この部屋へやというのは広ひろいです。 → ✅ この部屋へやは広ひろいです。
    A plain description of one thing takes は. というのは promises either a definition or a verdict, and "this room is big" is neither.

  • ❌ 親おやだというのは、ありがたいものだ。 → ✅ 親おやというのは、ありがたいものだ。
    No だ between the noun and という. With だ it becomes a quotation - "the fact that it's a parent" - which is not the topic-raising pattern.

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんというのは、昨日きのう会社かいしゃを休やすみました。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんは、昨日きのう会社かいしゃを休やすみました。
    A one-off event about a named person is straightforward news, so it takes は. というのは would suggest you were about to characterise Tanaka as a type.

  • ❌ あなたというのは、いつも親切しんせつですね。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんはいつも親切しんせつですね。

～というと - speaking of; when you say…

Meaning · takes a word that has just been said and uses it as a springboard - either for what it brings to mind, or for a "wait, which one do you mean?"

というと is と, the conditional: "if we say X, then…". That is why it has two faces. Point it forward and you get automatic association - 京都きょうとというと、お寺てら. Point it back at the person who spoke and you get a request for clarification - 田中たなかさんというと、営業えいぎょうの? Both start from the same place: a word has landed, and you are working out what follows from it.

The phrase attaches to whatever word you are picking up. It is a connective, so a comma usually follows.

～というと - speaking of; when you say… · Nuance & register

というと is reactive. Something has to be on the table already - a word the other person used, a place name that has just come up, a topic you yourself introduced one sentence earlier. That is what separates it from simply starting to talk about something.

Register is neutral. といいますと is the polite form and is standard in customer-facing speech: 「来週らいしゅうといいますと、何日なんにちごろでしょうか。」 In casual speech it becomes っていうと or, with the vowel stretched, ってゆうと, which is very common and mildly sloppy in writing.

～というと - speaking of; when you say… · Nuance & register (cont.)

The clarification use deserves separate practice, because it is the politest way to say "I'm not sure I follow." A bare というと？ puts the burden back on the speaker without accusing them of being unclear, and in a meeting it is far softer than 「どういう意味いみですか」. The full form …というと、…ということですか is the careful version.

One thing というと does not do is give advice. It draws out what a word implies; it does not tell the listener what to do about it. That job belongs to なら.

～というと - speaking of; when you say… · というと vs といえば

Rule of thumb - heavily overlapping. As a tendency: といえば freely raises a topic by association, including one nobody asked about; というと leans on a word that has just been said, and is the one that turns into a question.

温泉おんせんといえば、来月らいげつ草津くさつに行いくんです。
Speaking of hot springs - I'm off to Kusatsu next month.

温泉おんせんというと、やはり冬ふゆに行いきたくなりますね。
Hot springs - you do want to go in winter, don't you.

The first changes the subject to the speaker's own news; that is といえば territory, and そういえば ("that reminds me") is its most common form. The second stays on the general association. In the association use they are close to interchangeable, and nobody will misunderstand you either way.

～というと - speaking of; when you say… · というと vs といったら

Rule of thumb - といったら is the emotional one. It sets the noun up so you can exclaim about it: "when it comes to X - honestly!"

京都きょうとの夏なつの暑あつさといったら、外そとを歩あるく気きにもなれない。
The heat of a Kyoto summer - you don't even feel like stepping outside.

京都きょうとの夏なつというと、祇園祭ぎおんまつりを思おもい出だす人ひとが多おおいでしょう。
Summer in Kyoto brings the Gion Festival to mind for most people.

といったら almost always ends in something extreme - an exclamation, a negative you cannot fix, a comparison nobody could beat. Used for a flat, neutral association it sounds like you started an exclamation and forgot to finish it.

～というと - speaking of; when you say… · というと vs なら

Rule of thumb - both pick up what the other person said. というと asks what it means or what it evokes; なら accepts it as a premise and gives advice.

「京都きょうとに行いこうと思おもっています。」「京都きょうとなら、秋あきがおすすめですよ。」
"I'm thinking of going to Kyoto." "If it's Kyoto, I'd recommend autumn."

「京都きょうとに行いこうと思おもっています。」「京都きょうとというと、お寺てらを回まわるんですか。」
"I'm thinking of going to Kyoto." "Kyoto - so you'll be doing the temples?"

Swap them and both replies go wrong: なら cannot ask what a word meant, and というと cannot recommend a season.

    For "when spring comes", plain と is the conditional you want. というと needs a word or a statement in front of it, not an event.

  • ❌ 京都きょうとだというと、お寺てらですね。 → ✅ 京都きょうとというと、お寺てらですね。
    Nouns attach directly. The だ version quotes a proposition - "if we say that it is Kyoto" - which is not what you mean.

  • ❌ 「時間じかんがなくて困こまっています。」「というと、手伝てつだいましょうか。」 → ✅ 「それなら、手伝てつだいましょうか。」

(b) is an exclamation. といったら sets the noun up so the speaker can pile on how extreme it was, and the sentence duly ends in something you could not exaggerate further. It is speech, it is personal, and it expects a reaction.

～というより - rather than

Meaning · rejects one description as not quite right and puts a better one in its place - "it's not so much X as Y"

というより compares two ways of describing the same thing, not two things. The first label is usually the one already in the air - the word the other person used, or the polite word you started to reach for - and the second is what you actually mean. The first is never called wrong, only inexact, which is why the pattern is so useful for saying blunt things gently.

～というより - rather than · Formation

Everything attaches in its plain form, and nouns and な-adjectives normally drop だ.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + というより 趣味しゅみ → 趣味しゅみというより
な-adjective な-adj + というより（no な） 静しずか → 静しずかというより
い-adjective plain form + というより 安やすい → 安やすいというより
Verb plain form + というより 走はしる → 走はしっているというより

Casual っていうより 趣味しゅみっていうより

～というより - rather than · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the better label goes second. English puts it first ("more a hobby than a job"), Japanese puts it last （仕事しごとというより趣味しゅみだ）. Reversing the order reverses the meaning, and this is the single commonest error with the pattern.

～というより - rather than · Examples

これは食事しょくじというよりおやつだね。
This is more of a snack than a meal.

今日きょうは涼すずしいというより、もう寒さむいくらいだ。
Today isn't so much cool as downright cold.

彼かれは静しずかというより、人ひとと話はなすのが苦手にがてなんだと思おもう。
He's not quiet so much as bad at talking to people, I think.

あの人ひとの話はなしは、説明せつめいというより言いい訳わけに聞きこえました。
What he said sounded less like an explanation than an excuse.

～というより - rather than · Examples (cont.)

向むこうから来くる人ひとは、走はしっているというより急いそいで歩あるいているように見みえた。
The person coming towards us looked less like they were running than walking in a hurry.

この値段ねだんは安やすいというより、安やすすぎて逆ぎゃくに心配しんぱいになる。
This price isn't cheap so much as suspiciously cheap.

彼女かのじょが毎朝まいあさ走はしっているのは、やせたいというより、一日いちにちを気持きもちよく始はじめたいからだそうです。
She runs every morning not so much to lose weight as to get the day off to a good start.

今回こんかいの変更へんこうは改善かいぜんというより、前まえのやり方かたに戻もどしただけだという声こえも社内しゃないにはあります。
Some inside the company say this change is less an improvement than simply a return to the old way.

～というより - rather than · Nuance & register

Register-neutral in itself, but the move it makes is a politeness device. Naming the gentler word first and correcting it downwards lets you land a hard judgement without having stated it flatly: まずいというより、味あじが薄うすい is far kinder than starting with 「まずい」. The same works upwards - 上手じょうずというより、もうプロだ - where the pattern reads as genuine admiration rather than flattery, because you appear to be searching for the accurate word.

～というより - rather than · Nuance & register (cont.)

むしろ is its natural companion and adds "if anything": 嫌きらいというより、むしろ好すきなほうです. も on the end (というよりも) simply adds weight. In casual speech the whole thing contracts to っていうより, and in fast conversation you will hear it trailing off entirely - 「怒おこってるっていうより…ね」 - where the listener is expected to supply the better word.

Because the pattern is essentially a self-correction, it also works as a hedge on your own speech. Starting a sentence over with というより mid-turn signals that you are refining your point, not contradicting yourself, and it buys you a moment to find the word you want.

～というより - rather than · というより vs より

Rule of thumb - より compares two things on one scale. というより compares two labels for one thing. If you can insert 「のほうが」 after the second item, you want plain より.

去年きょねんより今年ことしのほうが雨あめが多おおい。
There's more rain this year than last.

今年ことしは雨あめが多おおいというより、ずっと降ふり続つづいている感かんじだ。
It's not that there's a lot of rain this year - it feels like it simply hasn't stopped.

In the first sentence there are two years and one quantity of rain. In the second there is one year and two ways of putting it. That is the whole distinction, and the grammar follows from it: より takes a noun, というより takes a full description.

～というより - rather than · というより vs ではなく

Rule of thumb - ではなく says the first label is wrong. というより says it is understandable but inexact.

これは誤解ごかいではなく、はっきりとした間違まちがいです。
This isn't a misunderstanding - it's an outright error.

これは誤解ごかいというより、説明せつめいの仕方しかたが悪わるかったのだと思おもいます。
I'd call this less a misunderstanding than a failure to explain properly.

ではなく corrects; というより negotiates. In a disagreement the second is much easier to say to someone senior, because it concedes that their word was a reasonable first attempt.

～というより - rather than · というより vs どころか

Rule of thumb - どころか overshoots on purpose: not X - in fact something far beyond X, often in the opposite direction. というより stays calm and simply re-labels.

彼かれは泳およげないどころか、水みずに顔かおをつけることもできない。
Far from being unable to swim, he can't even put his face in the water.

彼かれは泳およげないというより、水みずが怖こわいのだろう。
It's not that he can't swim - it's more that he's afraid of the water.

どころか is emphatic and often a little dramatic; というより is analytical. Choosing the wrong one is not ungrammatical, but it misreports how strongly you feel.

～というより - rather than · Common mistakes

  • ❌ これはおやつというより食事しょくじだ。（meaning "it's more a snack than a meal"） → ✅ これは食事しょくじというよりおやつだ。
    The accurate label goes second. Word order alone decides which description you are endorsing, so a reversed pair says the opposite of what you meant.

  • ❌ 静しずかなというより → ✅ 静しずかというより
    な-adjectives lose their な here, and they do not take だ either. Only the bare stem goes in front of という.

  • ❌ 兄あにというより弟おとうとのほうが背せが高たかい。 → ✅ 兄あにより弟おとうとのほうが背せが高たかい。
    Two different people on one scale is a plain より comparison. というより would mean you were choosing between calling the same person an older or a younger brother.

～というより - rather than · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 嫌きらいというより。 → ✅ 嫌きらいというより、苦手にがてなんです。
    The pattern needs both halves. Stopping after the rejected label leaves the listener with the word you have just told them not to use. (In casual speech people do trail off like this - but only when the better word is obvious from context.)

～というより - rather than · Practice 1

1. 彼かれの部屋へやは汚きたない____、物ものが多おおすぎて片付かたづけられないだけだ。

汚きたないというより、…

The sentence sets aside "dirty" as an unfair word and offers a fairer account of the same room. Note the order: the label being rejected comes first, the accurate one second.

～というより - rather than · Practice 2

2. Choose: この問題もんだいは難むずかしい（より・というより）、説明せつめいが分わかりにくいのだと思おもいます。

難むずかしいというより、説明せつめいが分わかりにくいのだと思おもいます。

There is only one problem here and two competing descriptions of it, so you need というより. Plain より would require two items being ranked against each other.

～というより - rather than · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "He isn't kind so much as he simply can't say no."

彼かれは優やさしいというより、断ことわれないだけなんです。

The gentle label goes first and is set aside; the sharper, more accurate one follows. The だけ at the end does the work of English "simply".

～というより - rather than · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: あの店みせは喫茶店きっさてんというより静しずかなというより図書館としょかんのようだ。

✅ あの店みせは喫茶店きっさてんというより図書館としょかんのようだ。

Two problems: な-adjectives take no な before という, and a single sentence carries one というより. The re-labelling happens once - first label, then the better one.

～というより - rather than · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 今回こんかいの案あんは新あたらしいというより、昔むかしのやり方かたに近ちかい。 (b) 今回こんかいの案あんは新あたらしいではなく、昔むかしのやり方かただ。

(a) allows that someone could reasonably have called the plan new, then quietly moves the description somewhere more accurate. It is the version you can say in a meeting without embarrassing the person who wrote the plan.

(b) - leaving aside that it should be 新あたらしいのではなく with an い-adjective - flatly denies the first description. It is clearer and colder, and it is what you use when the point of the sentence is that someone was wrong.

～とおり - in the way; the same as

Meaning · exactly as some model specifies - as told, as taught, as planned, as expected

The noun behind it is 道みち's cousin 通とおり, "a route", and the metaphor still shows: something has laid down a path - an instruction, a schedule, a prediction - and the action follows it without stepping off. What とおり insists on is exactness. If there is room for interpretation, this is the wrong word.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Formation

Two things decide the shape: whether a verb or a noun comes first, and - for nouns - whether you use の. The どおり form with rendaku is not optional.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (plain non-past) V(る) + とおり（に） 言いう → 言いうとおりに
Verb (plain past) V(た) + とおり（に） 習ならう → 習ならったとおりに
Noun, with の Noun + の + とおり（に） 説明せつめい → 説明せつめいのとおりに
Noun, bare Noun + どおり（に） - rendaku 予定よてい → 予定よていどおりに

Rule of thumb - の + とおり, or bare noun + どおり. Never の + どおり, and never a bare noun + とおり. 予定よていのとおり and 予定よていどおり are both correct; ❌予定よていのどおり and ❌予定よていとおり are not.

Note - the に is dropped when the phrase is followed by だ／です or stands as a comment: 思おもったとおりだ, そのとおりです. Keep the に when it modifies a verb: 思おもったとおりに進すすんだ. Written in kanji the bare-noun form is 予定通よていどおり - same reading, and the rendaku is still there.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Examples

先生せんせいが言いったとおりにやってみます。
I'll try doing it exactly as the teacher said.

電車でんしゃは時間じかんどおりに着つきました。
The train arrived right on schedule.

説明書せつめいしょのとおりに組くみ立たてたのに、なぜか動うごきません。
I put it together exactly as the manual said, and for some reason it doesn't work.

思おもったとおり、あの店みせは今日きょうも混こんでいた。
Just as I thought, that place was packed again today.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Examples (cont.)

「準備じゅんびは早はやめにということですね。」「そのとおりです。」
"So you're saying we should prepare early." "Exactly."

工事こうじは予定よていどおり進すすんでいますので、来月らいげつには終おわる見みこみです。
The work is going to plan, so we expect it to finish next month.

ご覧らんのとおり、会場かいじょうはもう満席まんせきですので、立たち見みになってしまいます。
As you can see, the hall is already full, so you'd have to stand.

母ははに教おそわったとおりに作つくったつもりでしたが、味あじはまったく別物べつものになってしまいました。
I thought I'd made it just the way my mother taught me, but it came out tasting like something else entirely.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral and equally at home in a manual, a news report and a kitchen. What varies is the fixed phrases built on it, and several of them are worth learning as units.

そのとおりです is strong agreement - "precisely so" - and is common in business Japanese, where a bare 「はい」 can sound thin. Its polite version, おっしゃるとおりです, agrees with a superior and is one of the most useful phrases in an office; おっしゃるとおりですが… is how you begin to disagree politely. ご覧らんのとおり ("as you can see") belongs to presentations, and 文字もじどおり means "literally".

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Nuance & register (cont.)

The bare-noun どおり forms behave almost like single words - 予定よていどおり, 時間じかんどおり, 計画けいかくどおり, 期待きたいどおり, 注文ちゅうもんどおり - and they carry a faint official tone, the sound of things going as they were supposed to. The verb forms with とおりに are more personal: someone told you, taught you or predicted, and you matched it.

One tense point that trips people up: both 言いうとおりに and 言いったとおりに are correct, and they are not quite the same. The non-past form is habitual or still ongoing - "as he says", the way he always tells you. The past form points at one particular occasion - "as he said (that time)".

～とおり - in the way; the same as · とおりに vs ように

Rule of thumb - とおりに means "matching the model exactly". ように means "in a manner resembling", and it also does purpose ("so that"), which とおりに cannot do at all.

見本みほんのとおりに書かいてください。
Please write it exactly like the sample.

子こどもにも読よめるように、大おおきく書かいてください。
Please write it large, so that children can read it too.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · とおりに vs ように (cont.)

The second sentence has no model to copy - it states a goal. Note also that ように can take a resemblance with no instruction behind it （鳥とりのように飛とぶ）, where とおりに would be nonsense: a bird has issued no instructions.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · とおりに vs まま

Rule of thumb - とおりに is about following something. まま is about not changing something. One copies a model; the other leaves a state alone.

習ならったとおりに包丁ほうちょうを使つかえば、指ゆびを切きることはありません。
If you use the knife the way you were taught, you won't cut your finger.

窓まどを開あけたまま出でかけてしまいました。
I went out and left the window open.

The two look similar after a past-tense verb, which is where the confusion starts. Ask what the previous action is doing: supplying a pattern to imitate (とおりに), or supplying a condition that persists (まま)?

～とおり - in the way; the same as · とおりに vs にしたがって

Rule of thumb - にしたがって is the formal "in accordance with", used for rules, orders and procedures. It also has a second life meaning "as X progresses, Y", which とおりに never has.

係員かかりいんの指示しじにしたがって移動いどうしてください。
Please move in accordance with the staff's instructions.

係員かかりいんが言いったとおりに階段かいだんを下おりました。
We went down the stairs exactly as the attendant told us.

にしたがって sounds like a public announcement; とおりに sounds like a person recounting what they did. For the "as X increases" meaning - 年としをとるにしたがって - only したがって works.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 予定よていのどおり進すすんでいます。 → ✅ 予定よていどおり／予定よていのとおり進すすんでいます。
    Pick one: の plus とおり, or a bare noun plus どおり. The two cannot be combined, and this is the single most common error with the pattern.

  • ❌ 地図ちずとおりに歩あるきました。 → ✅ 地図ちずどおりに歩あるきました。
    A noun attaching directly triggers rendaku. とおり only stays unvoiced after の or after a verb.

  • ❌ 先生せんせいが言いったのとおりに → ✅ 先生せんせいが言いったとおりに
    No の after a verb. The の in 説明せつめいのとおり belongs to the noun, not to the pattern.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 鳥とりのとおりに空そらを飛とんでみたい。 → ✅ 鳥とりのように空そらを飛とんでみたい。
    とおりに needs an instruction, plan or prediction to follow. For plain resemblance you want ように.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Practice 1

1. バスは時間じかん____来きませんでしたが、会議かいぎには間まに合あいました。

バスは時間じかんどおりに来きませんでしたが、…

A bare noun in front means the rendaku form どおり. Writing 時間じかんのとおりに is grammatical but sounds oddly heavy; 時間じかんどおり is the set phrase everyone uses.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Practice 2

2. Choose: 子こどもが喜よろこぶ（とおりに・ように）部屋へやを飾かざりました。

子こどもが喜よろこぶように部屋へやを飾かざりました。

This is a purpose - decorating so that the children will be pleased - and purpose is ように only. とおりに would require the children to have specified how the room was to be decorated.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "It turned out exactly as she predicted."

彼女かのじょが言いったとおりになりました。

A prediction is a model that reality then matched, which is exactly what とおり is for. The past-tense verb points at the one occasion when she said it. 彼女かのじょの予想よそうどおりになりました is an equally natural noun version.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 係かかりの人ひとが説明せつめいしたのとおりに、書類しょるいを出だしました。

Remove the の. Verbs attach straight to とおり; only nouns take の, and a noun version here would be 説明せつめいのとおりに or 説明せつめいどおりに.

～とおり - in the way; the same as · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部長ぶちょうが言いうとおりにやっています。 (b) 部長ぶちょうが言いったとおりにやりました。

(a) uses the non-past, so the model is standing and general: this is how the manager always wants it done, and you are still doing it that way. It describes a working practice.

(b) uses the past, which pins the instruction to one occasion - he said something, and you did precisely that. It is the sentence you produce when the result has gone wrong and you would like it on record whose instructions you were following.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand

Meaning · sets two true sides of the same situation next to each other, without cancelling either

一方いっぽう is "one side". The pattern says: here is one side, and here is the other, and both are the case. That is what separates it from でも and しかし, which put a stronger fact on top of a weaker one. 一方いっぽうで weighs; it does not overrule. It is also how you say that one person or one thing is doing two things at once.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · Formation

The clause in front is plain form. Only nouns and な-adjectives need help joining.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + 一方いっぽう（で） 働はたらく → 働はたらく一方いっぽうで
い-adjective plain form + 一方いっぽうで 安やすい → 安やすい一方いっぽうで
な-adjective ～な／～である + 一方いっぽうで 便利べんり → 便利べんりな一方いっぽうで
Noun Noun + である + 一方いっぽうで 教師きょうし → 教師きょうしである一方いっぽうで
Past tense V(た) + 一方いっぽうで 増ふえた → 増ふえた一方いっぽうで
Starting a new sentence 一方いっぽう、／その一方いっぽうで、 一方いっぽう、地方ちほうでは…

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - do not confuse ～する一方いっぽうで with ～する一方いっぽうだ. The first joins two sides; the second means "keeps getting more so, and only that". 物価ぶっかが上あがる一方いっぽうだ means prices just keep climbing, and it needs no second clause at all.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · Examples

兄あには働はたらく**一方いっぽうで**、大学だいがくにも通かよっています。
My brother works and goes to university at the same time.

この町まちは静しずかでいい**一方いっぽうで**、買かい物ものが不便ふべんです。
This town is nicely quiet, but on the other side of that, shopping is inconvenient.

スマホは便利べんりな**一方いっぽうで**、時間じかんをうばうものでもある。
Smartphones are convenient, and by the same token they eat your time.

都会とかいの人口じんこうは増ふえ続つづけている。一方いっぽう、地方ちほうでは人ひとが減へっている。
City populations keep growing. In the regions, meanwhile, people are leaving.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · Examples (cont.)

父ちちは厳きびしい**一方いっぽうで**、困こまったときには必かならず助たすけてくれました。
My father was strict, and at the same time he never failed to help when I was in trouble.

この仕事しごとは給料きゅうりょうがいい**一方いっぽうで**、休やすみがほとんど取とれないそうです。
The pay in that job is good, but on the other hand you can hardly take any time off.

新あたらしい制度せいどは若わかい社員しゃいんに喜よろこばれている**一方いっぽうで**、長ながく勤つとめてきた人ひとたちからは不満ふまんの声こえも出でています。
The new system has gone down well with younger staff, while long-serving employees have voiced complaints.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · Examples (cont.)

祖母そぼは畑はたけの仕事しごとを続つづける**一方いっぽうで**、週しゅうに一度いちどは町まちの教室きょうしつで習字しゅうじを習ならっています。
My grandmother keeps working her field, and on top of that goes into town once a week for a calligraphy class.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · Nuance & register

This is a written pattern first. It belongs to newspaper articles, reports, essays and prepared presentations, and it is the standard way to present a balanced picture: an advantage and its cost, a national trend and its regional exception, a policy and the grumbling it caused. In casual conversation people join the same two ideas with けど, し or 「でも」, and reaching for 一方いっぽうで over a drink sounds like you are reading from notes.

The tone is even-handed, and this is what learners most often miss. Because the construction gives both clauses equal footing, the writer comes across as fair rather than critical, which is why it appears so often in business writing where a flat complaint would be impolitic. If you want the second clause to win, use が or しかし.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · Nuance & register (cont.)

The two clauses do not have to be opposites. A perfectly ordinary use is simple simultaneity - one subject doing two things （働はたらく一方いっぽうで大学だいがくにも通かよう） - where English reaches for "while also". When the clauses are opposites, the で is often dropped in more formal writing: 増ふえる一方いっぽう、質しつは落おちている. Sentence-initial 一方いっぽう、 and その一方いっぽうで、 do the same job across a full stop and are extremely common in news writing.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · 一方で vs 反面

Rule of thumb - 反面はんめん needs the two sides to be opposite in evaluation: a merit and its matching drawback, in the same subject. 一方いっぽうで is wider - the two clauses may simply be parallel, and may even have different subjects.

この素材そざいは軽かるい**反面はんめん**、傷きずがつきやすい。
This material is light, but by the same token it scratches easily.

姉あねは銀行ぎんこうで働はたらく**一方いっぽうで**、週末しゅうまつはボランティアもしています。
My sister works at a bank, and on top of that volunteers at weekends.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · 一方で vs 反面 (cont.)

Put 反面はんめん in the second sentence and it goes wrong, because volunteering is not the downside of banking. Anywhere 反面はんめん works, 一方いっぽうで also works; the reverse is not true.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · 一方で vs が・しかし

Rule of thumb - が and しかし make the second clause the point. 一方いっぽうで leaves both clauses standing. If you are building to a conclusion, you want the adversative; if you are describing a situation, you want 一方いっぽうで.

値段ねだんは安やすいが、品質ひんしつが心配しんぱいだ。
It's cheap, but I'm worried about the quality.

値段ねだんが安やすい**一方いっぽうで**、品質ひんしつにはばらつきがあるようだ。
While the price is low, the quality seems to vary.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · 一方で vs が・しかし (cont.)

The first is heading towards a decision not to buy. The second is a report, and the reader is left to weigh it. In a recommendation or a complaint, が is what you want; in an analysis, it can sound like you have prejudged.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · ～一方で vs ～一方だ

Rule of thumb - with で it needs a second clause and means "on the other side of that". With だ it ends the sentence and means "keeps going that way, more and more".

外国人がいこくじんの観光客かんこうきゃくは増ふえる**一方いっぽうだ**。
The number of foreign tourists just keeps rising.

観光客かんこうきゃくが増ふえる**一方いっぽうで**、地元じもとの生活せいかつは不便ふべんになっている。
As visitor numbers rise, life for locals is getting less convenient.

～一方いっぽうだ takes only a non-past verb of change - 増ふえる, 減へる, 悪わるくなる - and always carries a sense that the trend is one-way and usually unwelcome.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この方法ほうほうは簡単かんたんだ一方いっぽうで、時間じかんがかかる。 → ✅ この方法ほうほうは簡単かんたんな一方いっぽうで、時間じかんがかかる。
    な-adjectives take な (or the formal である), never plain だ.

  • ❌ 彼かれは医者いしゃ一方いっぽうで、小説しょうせつも書かいている。 → ✅ 彼かれは医者いしゃである一方いっぽうで、小説しょうせつも書かいている。
    A noun cannot attach bare. It needs である - and note that this makes the sentence distinctly written in tone.

  • ❌ 雨あめが降ふった一方いっぽうで、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしになった。 → ✅ 雨あめが降ふったので、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしになった。
    Cause and effect is not two sides of anything. 一方いっぽうで joins clauses that are independently true, not a reason and its result.

～一方で - on one hand; on the other hand · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 仕事しごとが増ふえる一方いっぽうで。 → ✅ 仕事しごとが増ふえる一方いっぽうだ。
    If you mean "the work just keeps piling up" and have no second clause, you want 一方いっぽうだ. With で the sentence is left hanging.

～だけでなく - not only… but also

Meaning · the first item is true, and it is not the whole story - something further comes with it

だけ draws a boundary; でなく says the boundary is in the wrong place. Together they set up a second item that the listener was not counting on. This is the everyday, neutral member of the "not only… but also" family - the one you can use in a meeting, an essay or a conversation without anyone noticing the choice.

～だけでなく - not only… but also · Formation

The clause or noun in front takes its plain form. The second half almost always carries も, and leaving it out is the commonest thing that makes these sentences sound unfinished.

The new app isn't just easy to operate - it handles foreign languages too, so you can use it while travelling.

～だけでなく - not only… but also · Examples (cont.)

I hear teams are coming to this tournament from the neighbouring prefecture as well as from schools in the city.

～だけでなく - not only… but also · Nuance & register

This is the neutral default of its family. It is equally natural in ます-form speech, in plain-form conversation, in a report and in a school essay, and nothing about it marks you as formal or casual. The casual contraction だけじゃなく - often だけじゃなくて - is what you actually hear in relaxed talk, and it is the only register move the pattern makes. At the other end, のみならず is the written formal equivalent and belongs in documents rather than speech.

～だけでなく - not only… but also · Nuance & register (cont.)

The も in the second half is doing real work: it marks the second item as an addition to the first, which is what stops the sentence sounding like a correction. Compare 安やすいだけでなく、味あじもいい with 安やすいだけでなく、味あじがいい - the second is not wrong, but it reads as if the taste were a replacement for the cheapness rather than a bonus on top. When the second item goes further than you might expect, まで or さえ replaces も and adds a note of surprise.

One use worth naming: だけでなく is a tactful way to widen a demand. Saying 今回こんかいだけでなく、次回じかいからもお願ねがいします adds a request without repeating it, because the first half is already agreed.

～だけでなく - not only… but also · だけでなく vs ばかりでなく

Rule of thumb - the same structure, one shade apart in register. だけでなく is neutral and the more frequent in speech; ばかりでなく leans written and puts a little more weight on the second item.

この辞書じしょは説明せつめいが詳くわしいだけでなく、例文れいぶんも多おおい。
This dictionary isn't just detailed in its explanations - it has plenty of examples too.

この問題もんだいは一ひとつの会社かいしゃばかりでなく、業界ぎょうかい全体ぜんたいにかかわっている。
This issue concerns not just one company but the industry as a whole.

You could swap them in either sentence and be understood. The difference is the room they sit in: the first belongs to a conversation about a dictionary, the second to a newspaper.

～だけでなく - not only… but also · だけでなく vs うえに

Rule of thumb - だけでなく corrects an assumption: "you thought it was just X - it's Y as well." 上うえに simply piles on: "X, and on top of that Y." Only だけでなく implies somebody was underestimating.

この部屋へやは狭せまい**上うえに**、日ひも当あたらない。
This room is cramped, and on top of that it gets no sun.

この部屋へやは狭せまいだけでなく、日ひも当あたらないんです。
It isn't just that the room is small - it gets no sun either.

～だけでなく - not only… but also · だけでなく vs うえに (cont.)

The first is a straight list of grievances. The second answers someone who thought the size was the only problem. Note also that 上うえに is almost always used within one topic and one evaluation direction, while だけでなく can span two separate subjects.

～だけでなく - not only… but also · だけでなく vs はもちろん

Rule of thumb - はもちろん presents the first item as too obvious to argue about and moves straight to the second. だけでなく makes no such claim; it treats both items as news.

この温泉おんせんは地元じもとの人ひとはもちろん、外国がいこくからの客きゃくにも人気にんきがあります。
This hot spring is popular with locals, needless to say, and with visitors from abroad too.

この温泉おんせんは地元じもとの人ひとだけでなく、外国がいこくからの客きゃくにも人気にんきがあります。
This hot spring is popular not only with locals but with visitors from abroad.

Nearly interchangeable. The first flatters the reader slightly - of course you knew about the locals - and is common in advertising for that reason. The second is plainer and safer when the first item may genuinely be new information.

～だけでなく - not only… but also · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 英語えいごだけでなく、フランス語ごが話はなせます。 → ✅ 英語えいごだけでなく、フランス語ごも話はなせます。
    The second half needs も (or まで, さえ). Without it, the addition reads as a substitution and the sentence feels cut short.

  • ❌ この店みせは便利べんりだけでなく、安やすいです。 → ✅ この店みせは便利べんりなだけでなく、安やすくもあります。
    な-adjectives keep their な in front of だけ. The だ form never appears here.

  • ❌ 漢字かんじをだけでなく、文法ぶんぽうも勉強べんきょうした。 → ✅ 漢字かんじだけでなく、文法ぶんぽうも勉強べんきょうした。
    が and を drop before だけ. に, で and と stay: 友ともだちにだけでなく.

～だけでなく - not only… but also · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この店みせは安やすいだけでなく、まずいです。 → ✅ この店みせは安やすいですが、まずいです。
    The two halves have to run in the same direction. A good point followed by a bad one is a contrast, and needs が or けれど.

～ばかりでなく - not only… but also; as well as

Meaning · one item is not the limit of it - and what follows often goes further than the listener expected

Structurally this is the twin of だけでなく: first item, then a second one added with も, まで or さえ. What ばかり brings is the attitude it always brings. It says the first item was being treated as the whole picture, and it very often introduces a second item that is heavier, worse or more surprising than the first. It also leans towards the written page.

The system is said to benefit not just employees but employers substantially as well.

祖母そぼの家いえでは、正月しょうがつになると親戚しんせきばかりでなく、近所きんじょの人ひとまで集あつまってきてにぎやかになります。
At my grandmother's house, New Year brings not just the relatives but the neighbours too, and the place fills up.

～ばかりでなく - not only… but also; as well as · Nuance & register

ばかりでなく is the written-leaning member of the family. It is at home in newspapers, reports, formal letters and public announcements, and it is perfectly usable in careful speech - but in relaxed conversation most speakers say だけでなく or だけじゃなくて. Nothing about it is stiff enough to sound wrong out loud; it simply sounds considered.

The attitude carried over from plain ばかり is worth noticing. The first item is presented as something that was being taken for the whole, and the second often lands harder - which is why the pattern is so common in accounts of things going wrong: 遅おくれたばかりでなく、連絡れんらくもなかった. When the second item is genuinely startling, まで or さえ replaces も and the sentence tips into indignation or admiration.

～ばかりでなく - not only… but also; as well as · Nuance & register (cont.)

ばかりか is the same idea intensified, and it is common in writing. Two practical differences: it is more emphatic, and it cannot be followed by a request, suggestion or command - a restriction ばかりでなく does not share. That makes ばかりでなく the safer choice whenever your sentence is heading towards 「～てください」 or 「～べきだ」.

ばかりでなく vs だけでなく

Rule of thumb - same grammar, different room. だけでなく is neutral and dominates speech; ばかりでなく is a shade more formal and puts more weight on what follows.

この大学だいがくは授業料じゅぎょうりょうが高たかいだけでなく、寮りょうの費用ひようもかかります。
This university isn't just expensive in tuition - there are dormitory costs as well.

環境かんきょうの問題もんだいは一ひとつの国くにばかりでなく、世界せかい全体ぜんたいで考かんがえるべきだ。
Environmental problems must be considered not by one country alone but by the world as a whole.

Put ばかりでなく in a chat about dormitory fees and it sounds like you are quoting a brochure. Put だけでなく in the second sentence and nothing breaks - it simply reads a little lighter.

ばかりでなく vs ばかりか

Rule of thumb - ばかりか is stronger and reports only. ばかりでなく is milder and can lead into a request or a recommendation.

彼かれは道みちを教おしえてくれたばかりか、駅えきまで送おくってくれた。
He not only told me the way - he walked me to the station.

子こどもばかりでなく、大人おとなも一緒いっしょに参加さんかしてください。
Please join in together - adults as well as children.

Reverse them and the second sentence goes wrong: ばかりか cannot carry an invitation. The first sentence would survive the swap, just with less punch.

ばかりでなく vs plain ばかり

Rule of thumb - plain ばかり says "nothing but X, and that's too much". ばかりでなく says "not only X - Y as well". Same word, opposite work: one narrows, the other widens.

弟おとうとは漫画まんがばかり読よんでいる。
My brother reads nothing but comics.

弟おとうとは漫画まんがばかりでなく、小説しょうせつも読よむようになった。
My brother has started reading novels as well as comics.

The giveaway is でなく, which negates the boundary. Learners who have met ばかり first often read the second sentence as a complaint; it is the opposite - a small piece of good news.

～ばかりでなく - not only… but also; as well as · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この部屋へやはきれいばかりでなく、静しずかだ。 → ✅ この部屋へやはきれいなばかりでなく、静しずかでもある。
    な-adjectives keep な in front of ばかり, and the second half needs something (も, でもある) marking it as an addition.

  • ❌ 時間じかんをばかりでなく、お金かねもかかる。 → ✅ 時間じかんばかりでなく、お金かねもかかる。
    を and が disappear before ばかり. に, で, から and と survive.

  • ❌ 水みずばかりか、食料しょくりょうも持もっていってください。 → ✅ 水みずばかりでなく、食料しょくりょうも持もっていってください。
    ばかりか can only report a state of affairs. Once the sentence ends in a request or an instruction, you need ばかりでなく.

～ばかりでなく - not only… but also; as well as · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 彼かれはゲームばかりでなくしている。 → ✅ 彼かれはゲームばかりしている。
    If you mean "he does nothing but play games", that is plain ばかり. ばかりでなく requires a second item to follow.

The first season is presented as not being the whole story, and the second is added with も. だけでなく would fit equally well and sound slightly more conversational.

～ばかりでなく - not only… but also; as well as · Practice 2

2. Choose: 説明せつめいを聞きく（ばかりか・ばかりでなく）、自分じぶんでも試ためしてみてください。

説明せつめいを聞きくばかりでなく、自分じぶんでも試ためしてみてください。

The sentence ends in a request, and ばかりか cannot be followed by one. It reports what happened; it does not tell anyone what to do.

～ばかりでなく - not only… but also; as well as · Practice 3

Both halves are misfortunes running the same way, and the second is worse than the first - which is why まで rather than も is natural here. ばかりか would also work, with more indignation.

～ばかりでなく - not only… but also; as well as · Practice 4

～ばかりでなく - not only… but also; as well as · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 息子むすこは野菜やさいばかり食たべている。 (b) 息子むすこは野菜やさいばかりでなく、魚さかなも食たべるようになった。

(a) draws a boundary and complains about it: vegetables and nothing else. Plain ばかり nearly always carries the sense that there is too much of one thing.

(b) breaks that boundary open. The でなく negates the "only", so the sentence reports an expansion - fish has joined the list - and the tone flips from complaint to mild satisfaction.

～か何か - or something

Meaning · names one example and deliberately leaves the door open - "X, or something like it"

か何なにか is か ("or") plus 何なにか ("something"), and it means exactly what the parts say: this, or whatever else would do. You use it when you do not know （風邪かぜか何なにかだろう）, when it does not matter （お茶ちゃか何なにか買かってくる）, or when being precise would sound demanding. It has a small family - か誰だれか for people and かどこか for places - built exactly the same way.

～か何か - or something · Formation

The whole phrase behaves as a single noun, so any particle attaches after it.

Rule of thumb - the question word has to match the category. Things take 何なにか, people take 誰だれか, places take どこか. ❌田中たなかさんか何なにか is not vague - it is rude, because it files a person under "things".

～か何か - or something · Examples

コーヒーか何なにか飲のみませんか。
Would you like a coffee or something?

かばんの中なかにペンか何なにか入はいっていませんか。
Is there a pen or something in your bag?

熱ねつがあるみたいだから、風邪かぜか何なにかだと思おもう。
I seem to have a temperature, so I think it's a cold or something.

お礼れいにお菓子かしか何なにか送おくろうと思おもっています。
I'm thinking of sending sweets or something as a thank-you.

～か何か - or something · Examples (cont.)

この辺へんにコンビニか何なにかありませんか。
Is there a convenience store or anything around here?

さっきから外そとで音おとがするけど、風かぜか何なにかかな。
There's been a noise outside for a while - the wind or something, maybe.

急きゅうに連絡れんらくが取とれなくなったので、事故じこか何なにかにあったのではないかと心配しんぱいしています。
I suddenly can't reach him, and I'm worried he's had an accident or something.

一人ひとりで行いくのが不安ふあんなら、山田やまださんか誰だれかに一緒いっしょに来きてもらったらどうですか。
If you're nervous about going alone, why not ask Yamada or somebody to come with you?

～か何か - or something · Nuance & register

The pattern is neutral in politeness and extremely common in speech. What it adds is vagueness on purpose, and vagueness is a social tool. Asking ペンか何なにか貸かしてもらえますか is softer than asking for a pen, because you have told the listener that anything will do and left them a way to help without going to trouble. The same applies to gifts, invitations and suggestions: お茶ちゃか何なにかどうですか commits nobody to anything.

The other main use is a guess you cannot verify - 工事こうじか何なにかでしょう, 病気びょうきか何なにかだったらしい. Here the phrase protects you from asserting something you do not know, which is why it appears so often with でしょう, かな, らしい and みたい.

～か何か - or something · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two register limits. In formal writing, など does this job instead: 身分証明書みぶんしょうめいしょなどをご用意よういください, never か何なにか. And pointing it at a person is risky. The correct member of the family for people is か誰だれか, and even that should be used for someone not present - saying it about the person in front of you sounds like they are interchangeable.

～か何か - or something · ～か何か vs でも

Rule of thumb - でも is for offers and suggestions: "how about X or something?" か何なにか is for reference: naming a thing loosely because the exact identity is unknown or unimportant.

お茶ちゃでも飲のみに行いきませんか。
Shall we go for a cup of tea or something?

のどが渇かわいたので、お茶ちゃか何なにか買かってきます。
I'm thirsty, so I'll go and buy some tea or something.

～か何か - or something · ～か何か vs でも (cont.)

The first is an invitation, and でも is what makes it casual enough to refuse. The second is a plan of your own, where there is nothing to be polite about - and でも there would sound as though you were inviting the tea. As a rough guide: if the sentence ends in 「～ませんか」 or 「～ましょう」, use でも.

～か何か - or something · ～か何か vs とか

Rule of thumb - とか gives two or more examples of a set. か何なにか gives one, and leaves the rest blank.

果物くだものとかお菓子かしとかを持もっていこう。
Let's take fruit or sweets or something like that.

果物くだものか何なにかを持もっていこう。
Let's take fruit or something.

とか sketches the shape of the category by listing; か何なにか points at one member and shrugs. Both are casual, and both are avoided in formal writing.

～か何か - or something · ～か何か vs 何か

Rule of thumb - 何なにか alone means "something" with no anchor. か何なにか needs a noun in front of it to be an alternative to.

冷蔵庫れいぞうこに**何なにか**残のこっていますか。
Is there anything left in the fridge?

冷蔵庫れいぞうこに卵たまごか何なにか残のこっていますか。
Are there eggs or something left in the fridge?

The first asks about the contents in general. The second has a specific thing in mind and is willing to accept a substitute - which, incidentally, makes it the more useful sentence when you are about to cook.

～か何か - or something · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 何なにかコーヒー飲のむ? → ✅ コーヒーか何なにか飲のむ?
    The example noun comes first and か何なにか follows it. Putting 何なにか in front produces "something coffee", which is not a phrase.

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんか何なにかに聞きいてみます。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんか誰だれかに聞きいてみます。
    People take か誰だれか. Using 何なにか for a person is not merely wrong; it is insulting.

  • ❌ 果物くだものや何なにか買かってきて。 → ✅ 果物くだものか何なにか買かってきて。
    The particle is か ("or"), not や. や lists things that are all actually present.

～か何か - or something · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 申込書もうしこみしょか何なにかをご提出ていしゅつください。 → ✅ 申込書もうしこみしょなどをご提出ていしゅつください。
    In formal or written instructions, など is the vague-example word. か何なにか sounds too offhand when you are telling someone what to submit.

～か何か - or something · Practice 1

1. かぜをひいたみたいだから、薬くすり____飲のんで早はやく寝ねます。

薬くすりか何なにか飲のんで早はやく寝ねます。

You are naming one obvious option and leaving room for whatever is actually in the cupboard. Note that を is comfortably dropped here, as it usually is in speech.

～か何か - or something · Practice 2

2. Choose: 時間じかんがあったら、映画えいが（か何なにか・でも）見みに行いきませんか。

The sentence is an invitation ending in 「～ませんか」, and でも is the particle that makes an invitation easy to decline. か何なにか would sound as though you were listing options rather than asking.

～か何か - or something · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Could you ask Suzuki or somebody where the meeting room is?"

A person takes か誰だれか, and the particle に attaches after the whole phrase, not inside it.

～か何か - or something · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 机つくえの上うえに何なにかはさみ置おいていませんでしたか。

The word order was reversed. The concrete example goes first and か何なにか trails it; 何なにか on its own cannot sit in front of another noun.

～か何か - or something · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 会議かいぎに何なにか持もっていったほうがいいですか。 (b) 会議かいぎに資料しりょうか何なにか持もっていったほうがいいですか。

(a) is an open question: is there anything at all I should bring? The asker has no idea what would be appropriate.

(b) shows that the asker has already guessed - handouts, probably - and is checking while leaving room to be corrected. It sounds better prepared, and it gives the listener an easier question to answer, which is why it is the more natural thing to say to a colleague.

なんか / なんて - things like; something

Meaning · throws a word out lightly - as an example, as something beneath discussion, or as something you can hardly believe

Both come from 何なに. なんか is the worn-down form of 何なにか and of など; なんて is the worn-down form of などと and of という. What survives in both is a dismissive shrug - the speaker is holding the word at arm's length. Aim that shrug at yourself and it becomes modesty; aim it at your listener and it becomes an insult. Aim it at a fact and it becomes disbelief.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Noun - downplaying it Noun + なんか マンガ → マンガなんか
Noun - stronger, colder Noun + なんて お金かね → お金かねなんて
Noun + が／を particle is dropped ❌私わたしをなんか → ✅私わたしなんか
Noun + に／で／と particle is kept 駅えきで → 駅えきでなんか待まちたくない
Clause - marking it as astonishing plain form + なんて 来くる → 来くるなんて思おもわなかった
Nothing - adverbial "somehow" なんか + predicate なんか変へんだ
Exclamation なんて + adjective／noun + んだろう なんて寒さむい日ひなんだろう
Naming, in place of という Noun + なんて + Noun 田中たなかなんて人ひと
Formal written equivalents など／などと 値段ねだんなど

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Formation (cont.)

Note - the "somehow" なんか （なんか変へんだ）is a different animal from the particle なんか （マンガなんか）. The first stands in front of the predicate with no noun attached; the second always follows a noun. Both are casual.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Examples

なんか今日きょうは疲つかれたなあ。
I'm somehow worn out today.

私わたしなんかにはとてもできません。
Someone like me could never do it.

お金かねなんていらないよ。
I don't want your money.

彼かれが来くるなんて思おもわなかった。
I never imagined he'd come.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Examples (cont.)

マンガなんか読よんでいないで、少すこしは手伝てつだってよ。
Stop sitting there with your comics and give me a hand.

なんてきれいな夕焼ゆうやけなんだろう。
What a beautiful sunset.

試験しけんの前まえの日ひに朝あさまでゲームをするなんて、落おちても文句もんくは言いえないよ。
Playing games till dawn the night before an exam - you can hardly complain if you fail.

部長ぶちょうに直接ちょくせつ言いうなんて、よくそんな勇気ゆうきがありましたね。
Telling the manager to his face - I'm impressed you had the nerve.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Examples (cont.)

「あの店みせのラーメン、どうだった?」「うーん、なんか思おもっていたのと違ちがったかな。」
"How was the ramen at that place?" "Mm - somehow not what I was expecting."

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Nuance & register

Both words are casual and spoken. In writing they become など and などと, and a report that says 値段ねだんなんか reads as though it slipped out. Neither is rude in itself; what matters is where the shrug lands.

Pointed at yourself, なんか is the standard humble move: 私わたしなんかでよければ, 私わたしなんかまだまだです. This is polite, common, and expected when you are accepting praise you do not want to accept out loud. Pointed at your listener, the same word is an attack - お前まえなんて is the language of an argument, and learners should treat あなたなんて as unusable. Pointed at a thing, it downgrades it, which is fine for your own possessions and risky for someone else's.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Nuance & register (cont.)

なんて is the stronger of the two, and it has one job なんか cannot do: marking a whole clause as astonishing. 一人ひとりで登のぼったなんてすごい works; 一人ひとりで登のぼったなんかすごい does not. That use is not always negative - surprise cuts both ways, and admiration is as common as disapproval. なんて also appears as an exclamation before an adjective （なんて寒さむい日ひだろう）, where it is the spoken cousin of literary なんと.

Finally, the filler なんか - "like", "sort of" - is everywhere in casual speech, especially among younger speakers. One or two soften a sentence; a string of them reads as vague, and it is one of the first habits teachers try to break in presentations.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · なんか vs など

Rule of thumb - identical grammar, different register. など is the written and formal form; なんか is what it sounds like in relaxed speech.

回答かいとうでは、値段ねだんや品質ひんしつなどに対たいする不満ふまんの声こえが多おおく見みられた。
In the responses, complaints about price and quality among other things were prominent.

あの店みせは値段ねだんなんか気きにしない人ひとが行いく店みせだよ。
That shop is for people who don't worry about little things like price.

Note that など is neutral in attitude - it simply means "and the like" - while なんか usually keeps the downgrading tone even when it is only listing.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · なんか vs なんて

Rule of thumb - なんか follows a noun and downplays it. なんて is stronger, and it is the only one that can take a whole clause and mark it as unbelievable.

テレビなんか見みていないで、早はやく寝ねなさい。
Never mind the television - get to bed.

こんな時間じかんまでテレビを見みているなんて、明日あした起おきられないよ。
Watching television at this hour - you'll never get up tomorrow.

After a noun the two overlap and なんて is simply harsher: テレビなんて見みない rejects television more firmly than テレビなんか見みない. After a clause, only なんて is possible.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · なんか・なんて vs とか

Rule of thumb - とか lists examples neutrally. なんか and なんて name one thing and comment on it. If you have no attitude to express, とか is the safer word.

週末しゅうまつは洗濯せんたくとか掃除そうじとかで終おわってしまう。
Weekends get eaten up by laundry, cleaning and so on.

掃除そうじなんか明日あしたでいいよ。
The cleaning can wait until tomorrow.

The first sentence is a flat report of how the weekend goes. The second dismisses cleaning as not worth doing now - and if you said it to the person who has just started cleaning, it would sting.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんなんて、いつも親切しんせつですね。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんは、いつも親切しんせつですね。
    Aimed at another person, なんて and なんか belittle. A compliment built on them reads as sarcasm.

  • ❌ 彼かれが合格ごうかくしたなんか、信しんじられない。 → ✅ 彼かれが合格ごうかくしたなんて、信しんじられない。
    A clause marked as astonishing takes なんて. なんか only attaches to nouns (or stands alone meaning "somehow").

  • ❌ 私わたしをなんか誘さそわないでください。 → ✅ 私わたしなんか誘さそわないでください。
    が and を drop in front of なんか. に, で and と stay.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ アンケートの結果けっかでは、時間じかんなんかが問題もんだいだという意見いけんが多おおかった。 → ✅ …時間じかんなどが問題もんだいだという意見いけんが多おおかった。
    In a written report the neutral など is the right word. なんか drags in a dismissive tone the writer did not intend.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Practice 1

1. こんな大雨おおあめの中なかを歩あるいて帰かえる____、風邪かぜをひくよ。

…歩あるいて帰かえるなんて、風邪かぜをひくよ。

A whole clause is being held up as unwise, and only なんて can mark a clause that way. なんか here would need a noun in front of it.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Practice 2

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Practice 3

Turned on yourself, なんか is the standard humble gesture, and it pairs naturally with とても…ない. The に stays because only が and を drop before なんか.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 部長ぶちょうなんて、いつも丁寧ていねいに教おしえてくださいます。

なんて on a superior belittles them, which collides head-on with the honorific くださいます. Praise takes plain は.

なんか / なんて - things like; something · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) お礼れいなんかいりませんよ。 (b) お礼れいなんていりませんよ。

Both wave away an offer of thanks, and in most conversations either would pass unnoticed.

(a) with なんか is the softer of the two: a light shrug, "there's no need for that sort of thing." (b) with なんて pushes harder - it rejects the whole idea of being thanked, and depending on your tone it can sound either warmly emphatic or faintly annoyed. When you simply mean "please don't worry about it", なんか is the safer choice.

～っけ - what was… again?

Meaning · asks for something you once knew and have mislaid - "what was it again?", "did I…?"

っけ is a sentence-final particle for retrieval, not enquiry. Using it says two things at once: I do not have this information right now, and I ought to. That admission is why it sounds friendly rather than demanding - you are not asking the listener to inform you, you are asking them to jog your memory. It also explains where it cannot go: you cannot っけ something you were never told.

～っけ - what was… again? · Formation

っけ wants a だ or a past tense in front of it. That is the whole difficulty, and it is where nearly every mistake comes from.

～っけ - what was… again? · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + だっけ／だったっけ 明日あした → 明日あしただっけ
な-adjective ～だっけ／～だったっけ 大丈夫だいじょうぶ → 大丈夫だいじょうぶだったっけ
い-adjective ～かったっけ／～いんだっけ 高たかい → 高たかかったっけ
Verb, past V(た) + っけ 言いった → 言いったっけ
Verb, non-past V(る) + んだっけ 来くる → 来くるんだっけ

Rule of thumb - a plain non-past verb does not take っけ directly. Insert んだ: ❌何時なんじに来くるっけ → ✅何時なんじに来くるんだっけ. Past-tense verbs need nothing: 言いったっけ is fine on its own.

～っけ - what was… again? · Examples

会議かいぎは何時なんじからだっけ。
What time was the meeting again?

Hm - where did I put my umbrella?

これ、もう話はなしたっけ?
Have I told you this already?

明日あしたって休やすみだったっけ。
Tomorrow's a day off, isn't it - or was I imagining that?

～っけ - what was… again? · Examples (cont.)

すみません、受付うけつけは2階かいでしたっけ。
Sorry - reception was on the second floor, wasn't it?

山田やまださんって、去年きょねん結婚けっこんしたんだっけ?
Yamada got married last year, didn't he?

田中たなかさんにこの書類しょるいを渡わたすように言いわれたんですが、締しめ切きりは今日きょうでしたっけ。
I was told to pass these papers to Tanaka - the deadline was today, wasn't it?

子こどものころ、この川かわでよく遊あそんだっけ。
We used to play in this river when we were children, didn't we.

～っけ - what was… again? · Nuance & register

っけ is casual. In plain form it belongs among friends, family and colleagues you are comfortable with, and it carries a slight thinking-out-loud quality - it often arrives with 「あれ」, 「えーと」 or a tilt of the head. It is used by speakers of every age and gender and is not marked either way.

～っけ - what was… again? · Nuance & register (cont.)

The polite forms でしたっけ and ましたっけ are the useful ones for work, and they do something clever: they ask for information while implying you were already given it. 「会議かいぎは3時じからでしたっけ」 is softer than 「何時なんじからですか」 because it credits the listener with having told you. That said, the particle stays casual underneath the polite ending, so it is for colleagues, not for a first meeting with a client - and asking a senior person their name with お名前なまえは何なんでしたっけ is memorably bad, because it advertises that you were told and forgot.

～っけ - what was… again? · Nuance & register (cont.)

There is a second, quieter use: with a past-tense verb and no question intended, っけ becomes reminiscence. よく二人ふたりで歩あるいたっけ is not asking anything; it is "we used to walk here, didn't we", said half to yourself. Falling intonation marks it. This use is warm and slightly wistful and turns up constantly in song lyrics and in older speakers' reminiscing.

～っけ - what was… again? · っけ vs か

Rule of thumb - か asks for information. っけ asks for information back. Use っけ only when you had it once.

会議かいぎは何時なんじからですか。
What time does the meeting start?

会議かいぎは何時なんじからでしたっけ。
What time was the meeting again?

The first is a plain question and works with a stranger, at an information desk, about anything at all. The second only makes sense if the meeting time has already been announced - otherwise the listener is left wondering when they told you.

～っけ - what was… again? · っけ vs かな

Rule of thumb - かな wonders about something unsettled; っけ reaches for something already settled but forgotten.

明日あしたは晴はれるかな。
I wonder if it'll be fine tomorrow.

明日あしたは休やすみだったっけ。
Tomorrow was a day off - was it?

～っけ - what was… again? · っけ vs かな (cont.)

Tomorrow's weather is not a fact anyone possesses yet, so っけ is impossible there. Tomorrow's holiday is a fact on a calendar, and you have simply lost it. The two do combine - どこに置おいたっけかな - when you are searching your own memory aloud.

～っけ - what was… again? · っけ vs ね

Rule of thumb - ね checks something you believe; っけ admits you have lost it. ね invites agreement, っけ asks to be told.

明日あしたは10時じからですね。
It's from ten tomorrow, isn't it.

明日あしたは10時じからでしたっけ。
Was it ten tomorrow?

The ね version is close to a confirmation of something you are fairly sure of, and the listener need only say 「はい」. The っけ version concedes that you might be wrong, which is more modest - and, if you have already asked once, more honest.

～っけ - what was… again? · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日あしたは休やすみっけ? → ✅ 明日あしたは休やすみだっけ?
    A noun needs だ (or だった) before っけ. The bare noun form does not exist in standard speech.

  • ❌ 何時なんじに出でるっけ? → ✅ 何時なんじに出でるんだっけ?
    A plain non-past verb takes んだ first. With the past tense you can attach っけ directly: 何時なんじに出でたっけ.

  • ❌ この店みせ、高たかいっけ? → ✅ この店みせ、高たかかったっけ?／高たかいんだっけ?
    い-adjectives behave like verbs here: past tense, or んだ inserted.

～っけ - what was… again? · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ （初対面しょたいめんの人ひとに）お名前なまえは何なんでしたっけ。 → ✅ お名前なまえをうかがってもよろしいでしょうか。
    っけ claims you were told before. Used on someone you have just met it is merely confusing; used on someone who did tell you, it announces that you forgot.

～っけ - what was… again? · Practice 1

1. あれ、会議室かいぎしつの鍵かぎ、誰だれに返かえした____。

誰だれに返かえしたっけ。

The verb is already in the past tense, so っけ attaches directly. The sentence is addressed as much to yourself as to anyone - the classic use.

～っけ - what was… again? · Practice 2

2. Choose: 来週らいしゅうの試験しけん、範囲はんいはどこまで（ですか・でしたっけ）。

範囲はんいはどこまででしたっけ。

If the exam range has already been announced and you have forgotten it, でしたっけ is the natural and slightly apologetic choice. 「ですか」 would be right only if you had never been told.

～っけ - what was… again? · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Did I already send you the file?"

そのファイル、もう送おくりましたっけ。

A past-tense polite verb takes っけ directly. This is exactly the situation the particle exists for: you did something recently and cannot now recall whether you did.

～っけ - what was… again? · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 山田やまださんの誕生日たんじょうびは来月らいげつっけ?

✅ 山田やまださんの誕生日たんじょうびは来月らいげつだっけ?

来月らいげつ is a noun, so it needs だ before っけ. 来月らいげつだったっけ is equally natural and sounds a shade less certain.

～っけ - what was… again? · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 去年きょねんも同おなじ店みせで飲のみましたね。 (b) 去年きょねんも同おなじ店みせで飲のんだっけ。

(a) states something you remember clearly and invites the listener to share the memory. It is polite, and the listener is expected to agree.

(b) is hazier and more casual. Depending on the intonation it is either a genuine question - was it this place last year? - or, with a falling tone, a piece of fond reminiscence said half to yourself. Either way it admits that the memory has gone soft, which is something ね never does.
