～によると / ～によれば

Meaning · according to ～ - names where a piece of information came from, when the information is not your own

によると attaches to a source: a person, a broadcast, a survey, a rumour, a map. It says "this is not mine - here is who or what it came from." Because of that, it is only half of the pattern. The other half is the ending, which has to agree that the information is second-hand: そうだ, ということだ, とのことだ, らしい, or, in newspaper style, という.

～によると / ～によれば · Formation

によると follows a noun. It never attaches to a verb or an adjective directly - a clause has to be turned into a noun-like source first.

～によると / ～によれば · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (source) Noun + によると 天気てんき予報よほう → 天気てんき予報よほうによると
Noun (source), written / formal Noun + によれば 調査ちょうさ → 調査ちょうさによれば
Noun (source), announcements and broadcasting Noun + によりますと 気象庁きしょうちょう → 気象庁きしょうちょうによりますと
Clause repackaged as a source Clause + ところによると 聞きく → 聞きいたところによると
A person's account, casual Person + の話はなしでは 姉あね → 姉あねの話はなしでは
Ending the frame requires … + そうだ / ということだ / とのことだ / らしい / という 降ふる → 降ふるそうです

～によると / ～によれば · Formation (cont.)

Note - the source has to be outside the speaker. ❌私わたしによると is not Japanese; for your own view, say 私わたしの考かんがえでは or 私わたしに言いわせれば. The one exception people do say is 私わたしの記憶きおくによると - "as far as I remember" - where the source is your memory rather than you.

～によると / ～によれば · Examples

天気てんき予報よほうによると、午後ごごから風かぜが強つよくなるそうです。
According to the forecast, the wind picks up from the afternoon.

姉あねによると、あの店みせは水曜日すいようびが休やすみだそうだ。
My sister says that shop is closed on Wednesdays.

この地図ちずによると、橋はしはもう少すこし先さきにあるはずだ。
Going by this map, the bridge should be a little further on.

うわさによれば、来年らいねんから制服せいふくが変かわるらしい。
Rumour has it the uniforms are changing from next year.

～によると / ～によれば · Examples (cont.)

駅えきの放送ほうそうによると、事故じこの影響えいきょうで電車でんしゃが20分ぷんほど遅おくれているとのことです。
According to the station announcement, the trains are running about twenty minutes late because of an accident.

昨夜ゆうべ届とどいたメールによりますと、面接めんせつの場所ばしょが変かわったということです。
According to the email that came last night, the interview venue has changed.

市しが行おこなった調査ちょうさによれば、この町まちに住すむ外国人がいこくじんは10年ねんで倍ばいに増ふえたという。
According to a survey carried out by the city, the number of foreign residents in this town has doubled in ten years.

～によると / ～によれば · Examples (cont.)

近所きんじょの人ひとから聞きいた話はなしによると、向むかいの家いえは来月らいげつ引ひっ越こすそうですよ。
From what I heard from the neighbours, the family across the road are moving out next month.

～によると / ～によれば · Nuance & register

The three shapes differ only in formality, and the ordering is easy to hold on to. によると is the everyday one and is at home in conversation, email and writing alike. によれば - literally the ば-conditional of the same verb よる - leans written and formal; newspapers, reports and academic prose use it constantly, and it sounds a touch stiff in chat. によりますと is the polite-verb version and belongs to announcements: news readers, station staff and company briefings use it, and it would sound theatrical over dinner.

Below all three sits the casual option, 話はなしでは: 山田やまださんの話はなしでは、今週こんしゅうは来こられないって。 That is what you actually say to a friend.

～によると / ～によれば · Nuance & register (cont.)

The ending is not decoration. Because によると announces that the information is somebody else's, closing with と思おもいます pulls the sentence in two directions at once - the frame disclaims ownership and the ending claims it. Keep the ending on the hearsay side. The one systematic exception is that a source you are reading off rather than being told - a map, a timetable, a set of figures - can take an inference ending instead: この表ひょうによると、去年きょねんより安やすいですね.

One limit worth stating. A source is something that carries a statement: a person, a document, a broadcast, a survey. Physical evidence you interpret yourself is not a source. For the sound of rain on the window or the look of the sky, you want この音おとからすると or plain ようだ, not によると.

～によると / ～によれば · によると vs によれば vs によりますと

Rule of thumb - same meaning, three registers: によると neutral, によれば written and formal, によりますと for announcements. If in doubt in speech, use によると.

となりのクラスの子こによると、テストは来週らいしゅうに延のびたらしいよ。
A kid in the next class says the test has been pushed back to next week.

政府せいふの発表はっぴょうによれば、制度せいどの見直みなおしは来年度らいねんど以降いこうになるという。
According to the government announcement, the review of the system will not happen until next fiscal year or later.

ただいま入はいった情報じょうほうによりますと、高速こうそく道路どうろの通行止つうこうどめは解除かいじょされたもようです。
According to information just in, the motorway closure has been lifted.

～によると / ～によれば · によると vs によって

Rule of thumb - both come from the verb よる, and they do unrelated jobs. によると frames where information came from; によって marks a means, an agent, a cause, or a thing that varies.

専門家せんもんかによると、この建物たてものは地震じしんに強つよいそうだ。
According to the experts, this building stands up well to earthquakes.

この建物たてものは有名ゆうめいな建築家けんちくかによって設計せっけいされた。
This building was designed by a famous architect.

～によると / ～によれば · によると vs によって (cont.)

The second sentence is a passive with an agent; swapping in によると there would mean "according to the architect", which is not what it says. The other によって you will meet is the "it varies" one - 人ひとによって考かんがえ方かたが違ちがう - and again the two are not interchangeable in either direction.

～によると / ～によれば · によると vs ～と言っていた

Rule of thumb - によると frames the information and lets the ending mark it as hearsay. ～と言いっていた quotes the person and is closer to their actual words.

部長ぶちょうによると、会議かいぎは30分ぷん早はやまったとのことです。
According to the manager, the meeting has been moved half an hour earlier.

部長ぶちょうが「会議かいぎを30分ぷん早はやめよう」と言いっていました。
The manager said, "let's move the meeting half an hour earlier."

～によると / ～によれば · によると vs ～と言っていた (cont.)

The quoting version keeps the manager's own wording, including his volitional form, so it reports an utterance. The によると version reports a fact he is the source of, which is what you want when you pass information on to a third person. In casual speech the quoting version contracts to って: 部長ぶちょう、会議かいぎ早はやめるって言いってたよ。

～によると / ～によれば · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私わたしによると、この映画えいがは面白おもしろいです。 → ✅ 私わたしの考かんがえでは、この映画えいがは面白おもしろいです。
    によると points away from the speaker. You cannot cite yourself as a source of news you are inventing on the spot.

  • ❌ 天気てんき予報よほうによると、明日あしたは雨あめが降ふると思おもいます。 → ✅ …雨あめが降ふるそうです。
    The frame says "not my information"; と思おもいます says "my judgment". Finish with そうです, ということです, とのことです or らしいです.

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんによると聞ききました。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんから聞ききました。
    によると frames a whole reported sentence. It is not a particle for marking who you heard something from - that is から.

～によると / ～によれば · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この音おとによると、外そとは雨あめのようだ。 → ✅ この音おとからすると、外そとは雨あめのようだ。
    A sound is evidence you are interpreting, not a source that told you something.

～によると / ～によれば · Practice 1

1. ニュース____、台風たいふうは夜中よなかに関東かんとうに近ちかづくそうです。

ニュースによると、台風たいふうは夜中よなかに関東かんとうに近ちかづくそうです。

The news is the source of information, and the sentence already ends in そうです, so the frame that agrees with it is によると (or the more formal によれば).

～によると / ～によれば · Practice 2

2. Choose: この論文ろんぶん（によると・によって）、睡眠すいみん時間じかんは記憶きおくに大おおきく影響えいきょうするという。

この論文ろんぶんによると、睡眠すいみん時間じかんは記憶きおくに大おおきく影響えいきょうするという。

The paper is where the claim comes from, so it is a source: によると. によって would make the paper the agent of something, which fits a passive like 論文ろんぶんによって明あきらかにされた, not a reported claim.

～によると / ～によれば · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "According to my grandfather, there used to be a river here."

祖父そふによると、昔むかしここに川かわがあったそうです。

The person is the source, so によると (casually, 祖父そふの話はなしでは), and because you are passing on what he told you, the sentence closes with そうです rather than a bare でした.

～によると / ～によれば · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 山田やまださんによると、新あたらしい係長かかりちょうはとても厳きびしいだと思おもいます。

✅ 山田やまださんによると、新あたらしい係長かかりちょうはとても厳きびしいそうです。

Two problems. 厳きびしいだ is not a possible form - い-adjectives never take だ. And the ending has to keep the information on Yamada's side of the fence, so そうです replaces と思おもいます.

～によると / ～によれば · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 中村なかむらさんによると、工事こうじは来週らいしゅう終おわるそうです。 (b) 中村なかむらさんは「工事こうじは来週らいしゅう終おわります」と言いっていました。

(a) presents Nakamura as the source of a piece of information you are now handing on as fact-you-heard. The listener's attention goes to the content - the work finishes next week - and Nakamura is the guarantee behind it.

(b) reports the act of speaking, close to his actual words. It is what you say when the point is that he said it - useful if you want to leave room for "well, that is what he told me" and step back from the claim yourself.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ

Meaning · I hear that ～; word is that ～ - relays information you received from somewhere else, with the wording kept at arm's length

Both endings hand a piece of information on. ということだ is the general relay: whatever you were told, read or heard, repackaged as a report. とのことだ is the message-carrier's version - it fits a phone call, an email or a note you are passing to someone else, and it is the standard shape in business Japanese.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Formation

Both attach to a plain-form clause. Nothing changes shape except the noun and な-adjective slots.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + ということだ 戻もどる → 戻もどるということだ
Verb (past) た-form + ということだ 届とどいた → 届とどいたということです
い-adjective plain form + ということだ 忙いそがしい → 忙いそがしいということだ
な-adjective ～だ + ということだ 不便ふべんだ → 不便ふべんだということだ
Noun Noun + だ + ということだ 中止ちゅうし → 中止ちゅうしだということです
Noun, with とのことだ Noun + とのことだ 中止ちゅうし → 中止ちゅうしとのことです
Polite past … + ということでした / とのことでした 遅おくれる → 遅おくれるとのことでした
Casual speech … + ってことだ 無理むりだ → 無理むりだってことだ

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Formation (cont.)

Note - before とのことだ, the だ after a noun is normally left out: 休やすみとのことです rather than 休やすみだとのことです. Before ということだ the だ is usually there. This is a tendency in how the two are written, not a hard rule of grammar.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Examples

社長しゃちょうは来週らいしゅう戻もどるということです。
I'm told the president is back next week.

受付うけつけは5時じまでとのことです。
Reception is open until five, apparently.

先生せんせいのお話はなしでは、試験しけんの範囲はんいは変かわらないということだ。
According to the teacher, the scope of the exam isn't changing.

林はやしさんから連絡れんらくがあって、渋滞じゅうたいで少すこし遅おくれるとのことでした。
Hayashi got in touch - he says he'll be a little late because of the traffic.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Examples (cont.)

歓迎会かんげいかいは来月らいげつに延期えんきになったということなので、みんなに伝つたえておきます。
The welcome party has been put off until next month, I gather, so I'll let everyone know.

お客様きゃくさまは明日あしたの午前中ごぜんちゅうにもう一度いちど電話でんわをくださるとのことです。
The customer says she'll ring again tomorrow morning.

担当者たんとうしゃに確認かくにんしたところ、書類しょるいは金曜日きんようびまでに出だせば間まに合あうとのことでした。
I checked with the person in charge, and she said we'll be in time if we submit the papers by Friday.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Examples (cont.)

工事こうじの音おとがうるさいという苦情くじょうが何件なんけんか来きているということですので、作業さぎょうの時間じかんを見直みなおしましょう。
Word is there have been several complaints about the noise from the building work, so let's reconsider the working hours.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Nuance & register

Both are more formal than the everyday hearsay そうだ. ということだ is the ending a news bulletin closes on and a report uses to attribute a fact; in speech it sounds careful and slightly detached, which is exactly why it is useful when you do not want to be held responsible for the content. とのことだ is narrower and more human: somebody told you, and you are passing it along. In an office you will hear 部長ぶちょうは本日ほんじつ直帰ちょっきとのことです many times a week.

In plain form, ということだ and とのことだ both read as written or announcement Japanese. Casual speech contracts the first to ってことだ - 「あの店みせ、 今日きょう休やすみだってことだよ」 - and often drops the frame altogether in favour of a bare って: 「休やすみだって」.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Nuance & register (cont.)

ということだ has a second job that shares the same form: drawing a conclusion, "which means", "so what it amounts to is". It usually arrives with つまり or 要ようするに and restates something already said rather than reporting news.

返事へんじがないということは、興味きょうみがないということだ。
No reply means they aren't interested.

Context tells the two apart without much effort: a named source, or a fresh fact, points to hearsay; a premise sitting right in front of it points to the conclusion reading.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · ということだ vs そうだ

Rule of thumb - same information, different formality - and one grammatical difference: hearsay そうだ does not conjugate, while ということだ does.

Hearsay そうだ has exactly one shape. There is no ❌そうでした, no ❌そうだった and no ❌そうではない in the hearsay sense; those forms all belong to the looks-like そうだ. So when you need a past or a negative on the reporting frame itself, ということだ is the pattern that has one.

昨日きのう問とい合あわせたときは、在庫ざいこがまだあるということでした。
When I enquired yesterday, they said there was still stock.

在庫ざいこがまだあるそうです。
Apparently there's still stock.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · ということだ vs そうだ (cont.)

The second is what you say to a colleague in passing. The first pins the report to a moment in time, and only ということだ can do it.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · とのことだ vs ということだ

Rule of thumb - とのことだ relays a message from an identifiable person, usually one you received directly. ということだ covers that too, but also general report, rumour and conclusion.

山やまの上うえの旅館りょかんは来年らいねん閉しめるらしい、ということだ。
Word going round is that the inn up the mountain is closing next year.

予約よやくの変更へんこうはメールでお願ねがいしたいとのことです。
They'd like changes to bookings to be made by email, they said.

Swapping them is rarely ungrammatical, but とのことだ on an anonymous rumour sounds oddly official, and ということだ on a message you took two minutes ago sounds needlessly distant.

ということだ ("I hear") vs ということだ ("which means")

Rule of thumb - if it introduces new information from a source, it is hearsay. If it restates something already on the table, it is a conclusion.

新あたらしい支店してんは駅前えきまえにできるということです。
I hear the new branch is going up in front of the station.

駅前えきまえの土地とちを買かったのなら、そこに支店してんを作つくるということですね。
If they've bought the land by the station, that means they're going to build a branch there.

The second one has its premise attached with なら, and it adds no facts - it just spells out what follows. That is the conclusion use, and it is the one that turns up in ということは at the head of a sentence.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日あしたは雨あめだそうでした。 → ✅ 明日あしたは雨あめだということでした。
    Hearsay そうだ has no past form. When the reporting itself happened in the past, use ということでした or とのことでした.

  • ❌ 部屋へやが狭せまいだということです。 → ✅ 部屋へやが狭せまいということです。
    い-adjectives never take だ. Only nouns and な-adjectives do, and only before ということだ.

  • ❌ 来週らいしゅう休やすむそうということです。 → ✅ 来週らいしゅう休やすむということです。
    One hearsay marker per sentence. Stacking そうだ and ということだ reports the same second-hand status twice.

  • ❌ 私わたしはこの案あんに賛成さんせいだということです。 → ✅ 私わたしはこの案あんに賛成さんせいです。
    The frame exists to put distance between you and the content. Your own position needs no distance.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Practice 1

1. 受付うけつけから電話でんわがあって、お客様きゃくさまがロビーで待まっている____。

…待まっているとのことです（or ということです）。

A specific person rang you with a specific message, so とのことです is the natural relay. ということです is also correct, just a shade more impersonal.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Practice 2

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Practice 4

そう and ということ are both hearsay markers, and one is enough. Keep the だ after the な-adjective and drop the そう.

～ということだ / ～とのことだ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 会議かいぎは中止ちゅうしになったそうです。 (b) 会議かいぎは中止ちゅうしになったとのことです。

(a) is everyday hearsay: you heard it somewhere and you are mentioning it. It fits conversation with a colleague on the stairs.

(b) reads as a message being passed on in an official capacity - somebody informed you, and you are relaying the notification. It is what you would write in an email to the team, and it quietly implies that the information is reliable and came through a proper channel.

～に違いない

Meaning · it must be ～; I'm sure ～ - read as ちがいない, the speaker's near-certainty about something they have worked out rather than been told

に違ちがいない is the top of the guessing range. You have not been told, and you cannot check; but the evidence in front of you leaves you in no real doubt. The conviction is the speaker's own, which is why the pattern belongs to inference and not to fact - you never use it about something you simply know.

～に違いない · Formation

Attaches to a plain form, with the usual noun and な-adjective trimming: the だ disappears.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + に違ちがいない 落おとした → 落おとしたに違ちがいない
Verb (negative) ない-form + に違ちがいない 知しらない → 知しらないに違ちがいない
い-adjective plain form + に違ちがいない 高たかい → 高たかいに違ちがいない
な-adjective drop だ + に違ちがいない 不安ふあんだ → 不安ふあんに違ちがいない
Noun Noun + に違ちがいない 本物ほんもの → 本物ほんものに違ちがいない
Polite … + に違ちがいありません 疲つかれている → 疲つかれているに違ちがいありません
Very formal / legal writing … + に相違そういない 事実じじつ → 事実じじつに相違そういない

～に違いない · Examples

この字じを書かいたのは姉あねに違ちがいない。
My sister must be the one who wrote this.

電気でんきが消きえているから、もう寝ねたに違ちがいない。
The lights are off, so they must have gone to bed.

こんなに人ひとが並ならんでいるのだから、この店みせはおいしいに違ちがいない。
With a queue like that, the food here must be good.

返事へんじがまだ来こない。きっと忙いそがしいに違ちがいありません。
Still no reply. They must be snowed under.

～に違いない · Examples (cont.)

窓まどが割われているところを見みると、誰だれかが入はいったに違ちがいない。
Judging by the broken window, someone must have got in.

机つくえに置おいたはずの封筒ふうとうがない。誰だれかが片付かたづけてしまったに違ちがいない。
The envelope I'm sure I left on the desk is gone. Somebody must have tidied it away.

あれだけ練習れんしゅうを重かさねてきたのだから、本人ほんにんもこの結果けっかには納得なっとくしていないに違ちがいない。
After all the practice he put in, he can't possibly be satisfied with this result himself.

彼女かのじょが何なにも言いわずに帰かえったのは、よほど嫌いやなことがあったに違ちがいありません。
For her to leave without a word, something must have upset her badly.

～に違いない · Nuance & register

Everything from かもしれない up to a flat assertion is a ladder, and it pays to have the whole thing in view rather than learning each rung separately.

Pattern English What it rests on
かもしれない might, could one possibility among several
だろう / でしょう probably a general guess, no working shown
はずだ should be, is supposed to be a fact or rule you can point to
に違ちがいない must be, I'm certain evidence plus the speaker's conviction
に決きまっている obviously, of course conviction alone, stated bluntly

～に違いない · Nuance & register (cont.)

に違ちがいない is a strong, slightly dramatic ending. It is completely at home in writing - novels lean on it for a character's inner deduction, and essays use it to press a point - and in speech it sounds emphatic and a little declamatory. It is not rude; it just takes up space. In relaxed conversation people more often reach for きっと with a plain ending, or for に決きまってる, or simply はずだ.

に違ちがいありません is the polite form and is unremarkable in a careful spoken report. に相違そういない is the same pattern in legal and formal-document register and is worth recognising rather than using.

～に違いない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two things it will not do. It does not stack with other hedges - ❌に違ちがいないでしょう and ❌に違ちがいないかもしれない cancel the certainty they just claimed - and it does not apply to your own plans and feelings, which are not things you deduce. Say 明日あした行いきます, not ❌明日あした行いくに違ちがいない.

～に違いない · に違いない vs はずだ

Rule of thumb - はずだ reasons from something you can point to and survives being wrong; に違ちがいない states your conviction and does not.

はずだ has an escape hatch built in. It says "given what I know, this follows", which is why はずだったのに - "it was supposed to be, and yet" - is such a common sentence. に違ちがいない has no such form; you cannot walk back a certainty you just declared.

会議かいぎは3時じに始はじまるはずです。
The meeting should start at three.

～に違いない · に違いない vs はずだ (cont.)

あの様子ようすでは、会議かいぎはまだ終おわっていないに違ちがいない。
Going by the look of things, the meeting can't have finished yet.

The first reads off a schedule; you did not deduce anything, so に違ちがいない there would sound oddly overwrought. The second has no schedule to read - only evidence and the speaker's reading of it, which is exactly に違ちがいない territory.

～に違いない · に違いない vs かもしれない・だろう

Rule of thumb - same guess, three strengths: かもしれない leaves the other possibilities open, だろう picks one without much working, に違ちがいない closes the question.

あの音おとは雷かみなりかもしれない。
That noise might be thunder.

あの音おとは雷かみなりだろう。
That noise is probably thunder.

あの音おとは雷かみなりに違ちがいない。
That noise must be thunder.

～に違いない · に違いない vs かもしれない・だろう (cont.)

Notice what changes with the evidence. The first is fine when you barely heard it; the third needs the sky to have flashed a second earlier. Using the top rung on thin evidence is the commonest way learners sound strange with this pattern - it is not wrong, it is simply overclaiming.

～に違いない · に違いない vs に決まっている

Rule of thumb - に違ちがいない is a considered conclusion; に決きまっている is a blunt "obviously", often impatient, and much more casual.

今いまから走はしっても間まに合あわないに決きまっているよ。
Of course we won't make it, even if we run.

この時間じかんに出でたのだから、渋滞じゅうたいに巻まき込こまれたに違ちがいない。
Leaving at that hour, they must have got caught in traffic.

に決きまっている treats the conclusion as too obvious to argue with, which can come across as dismissive of the person you are talking to. に違ちがいない shows its working and stays civil.

～に違いない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この辺へんは静しずかだに違ちがいない。 → ✅ この辺へんは静しずかに違ちがいない。
    The だ of a な-adjective or a noun drops before に違ちがいない, exactly as it does before らしい.

  • ❌ 私わたしは来週らいしゅう大阪おおさかに行いくに違ちがいない。 → ✅ 私わたしは来週らいしゅう大阪おおさかに行いきます。
    Your own plans are not something you deduce. The pattern is for conclusions about the world outside your head.

  • ❌ 受付うけつけは9時じからだに違ちがいないので、少すこし待まちましょう。 → ✅ 受付うけつけは9時じからのはずなので、少すこし待まちましょう。
    Opening times come from a notice, not from deduction. Facts you can look up take はずだ.

～に違いない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 彼かれは知しっていたに違ちがいないでしょう。 → ✅ 彼かれは知しっていたに違ちがいありません。
    でしょう weakens what に違ちがいない has just declared. Use the polite form instead of piling a hedge on top.

～に違いない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 山田やまださんはもう着ついているはずです。 (b) 山田やまださんはもう着ついているに違ちがいありません。

(a) is expectation with its reasoning available: he left at nine, the trip takes an hour, so by now he should be there. If it turns out he is not, you say 着ついているはずなのに… and nobody is embarrassed.

(b) is the speaker's conviction, usually prompted by fresh evidence - his coat is on the chair, his laptop is open. It is stronger, more personal, and harder to retreat from; you have said the question is settled.

～そうもない

Meaning · there's no sign of it happening; very unlikely to ～ - the negative of the "looks like it will" そうだ, and considerably stronger than a plain "probably not"

～そうもない says that you have looked at the situation and can see no prospect at all of the thing happening. It is not a calculation and not a rumour: it is what the evidence in front of you looks like. Because the も is a "not even" particle, the pattern lands harder than ～ないだろう - the rain is not merely unlikely to stop, it shows no sign whatsoever of stopping.

～そうもない · Formation

This is a verb pattern. It attaches to the ます-stem, exactly like the "looks like" そうだ it negates.

～そうもない · Examples

雨あめは当分とうぶん止やみそうもない。
There's no sign of the rain stopping any time soon.

この仕事しごとは今日中きょうじゅうに終おわりそうにない。
This job isn't going to get finished today by the look of it.

席せきが空あきそうもないから、別べつの店みせにしよう。
No sign of a table coming free - let's try somewhere else.

どんなに考かんがえても、いい案あんが出でてきそうにもない。
However hard I think about it, no good idea looks likely to come out.

～そうもない · Examples (cont.)

渋滞じゅうたいがひどくて、約束やくそくの時間じかんには着つけそうもありません。
The traffic is dreadful - I don't think I can possibly get there for the time we agreed.

熱ねつは下さがりましたが、咳せきのほうはしばらく治なおりそうにありません。
The fever has come down, but the cough shows no sign of clearing for a while.

隣となりの部屋へやの話はなしし合あいは、まだ当分とうぶん終おわりそうにないですね。
The discussion in the next room doesn't look like ending for some time yet, does it.

部品ぶひんがまだ届とどいていないので、来週らいしゅうの発売はつばいには間まに合あいそうもありません。
The parts still haven't arrived, so there's no realistic chance of making next week's launch.

～そうもない · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. You will hear it from a site foreman, a schoolchild and a news reporter alike, and the polite forms そうもありません / そうにありません are ordinary business Japanese for delivering bad news gently - the fault lies with the situation, not with you.

The three shapes are near-identical in meaning. そうにない is the plainest, そうもない adds the "not even" flavour of も, and そうにもない piles both on and is the most emphatic. Treat this as a tendency rather than a rule: in most sentences the three are freely interchangeable, and speakers vary.

～そうもない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two things follow from そうもない being the negative of looks-like そうだ. First, it is about observable prospects, so it wants a subject whose behaviour you can watch: rain, a queue, a deadline, a person's progress. It sits badly on abstract impossibility, which belongs to わけがない. Second, it does not mean "I hear that … not". The hearsay そうだ negates inside the clause instead: 来こないそうだ - "I hear they aren't coming."

The pattern loves potential verbs, and that combination is the everyday way to decline gracefully: 今週こんしゅうは行いけそうもありません softens "I can't come" into "it doesn't look as though I'll manage it."

～そうもない · ～そうもない vs ～なさそうだ

Rule of thumb - そうもない negates the appearance of it happening; なさそうだ negates the verb and then reports how that looks. The first says "no sign of it"; the second says "looks like not".

バスは来きそうもない。
There's no sign of a bus coming at all.

この時間じかんだと、バスは来こなさそうだね。
At this hour it looks like no bus is coming.

～そうもない · ～そうもない vs ～なさそうだ (cont.)

Both are natural, and the difference is one of weight. The first is what you say after twenty minutes at the stop, with impatience; the second is a calmer reading of the timetable and the empty road. With adjectives you have no choice - only the なさそう route exists, which is why ❌高たかそうもない should be 高たかくなさそうだ.

～そうもない · ～そうもない vs ～ないだろう

Rule of thumb - そうもない is grounded in what you can see right now. ないだろう is a guess that needs no evidence at all.

明日あしたは雪ゆきは降ふらないだろう。
It probably won't snow tomorrow.

空そらが明あかるくなってきた。もう降ふりそうもない。
The sky's brightening. It doesn't look like it's going to come down any more.

Because そうもない reports signs, it usually talks about the near future or a situation already under way. A guess about next year takes ないだろう; a guess about the next twenty minutes, with the sky in front of you, takes そうもない.

～そうもない · ～そうもない vs わけがない・はずがない

Rule of thumb - そうもない says there is no sign of it. わけがない and はずがない say it is impossible. Unlikely is not the same as ruled out.

今いまから始はじめても、今日中きょうじゅうには終おわりそうもない。
Even if we start now, it doesn't look like finishing today.

一人ひとりでこの量りょうを運はこべるわけがない。
There's no way one person can shift this much.

The first leaves the door open - a miracle, extra hands, a shorter route. The second slams it. Learners reach for わけがない when they only mean the first, and it comes out sounding much more forceful than they intended.

～そうもない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 雨あめが降ふるそうもない。 → ✅ 雨あめが降ふりそうもない。
    It attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form. The dictionary form plus そうだ is the hearsay pattern, which is a different thing entirely.

  • ❌ この問題もんだいは簡単かんたんそうもない。 → ✅ この問題もんだいは簡単かんたんではなさそうだ。
    Adjectives do not take そうもない. Negate the adjective first, then add そう.

  • ❌ 今日中きょうじゅうに終おわらなそうもない。 → ✅ 今日中きょうじゅうに終おわりそうもない。
    The negative is already inside そうもない. Negating the verb as well leaves you saying it looks like it will finish.

～そうもない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 田中たなかさんは来くるそうもない（meaning "I hear he isn't coming"）。 → ✅ 田中たなかさんは来こないそうだ。
    For reported information, put the negative in the clause and keep plain そうだ.

～そうもない · Practice 1

1. 風かぜがどんどん強つよくなっている。この様子ようすでは船ふねは出で____。

The wind is visible evidence, and the speaker reads it as leaving no prospect of the boat sailing. 出でる is a る-verb, so its ます-stem is 出で.

～そうもない · Practice 2

2. Choose: この辞書じしょは古ふるいけれど、（高たかそうもない・高たかくなさそうだ）。

高たかい is an adjective, and そうもない only attaches to verb stems. The adjective route is to negate first - 高たかくない - and then add そう, which inserts さ: 高たかくなさそう.

～そうもない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'm afraid I won't be able to make it to Saturday's meeting."

The potential form plus そうもありません is the standard polite way to decline: it presents the refusal as a reading of the circumstances rather than as a flat "no".

～そうもない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 部長ぶちょうは今日きょう来くるそうもないと聞ききました。

✅ 部長ぶちょうは今日きょう来こないそうです。

Two things went wrong. そうもない needs the ます-stem （来きそうもない）, and more importantly the sentence is reporting what you were told, which is the hearsay そうだ with the negative inside the clause.

～そうもない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれが試合しあいに勝かてそうもない。 (b) 彼かれが試合しあいに勝かてるわけがない。

(a) is a reading of what you can see - the score, his condition, the opponent. It says the prospects look bad, and it leaves room for a surprise.

(b) declares the thing impossible on principle, and it carries a note of contempt or exasperation that (a) does not have. Said in front of him, (a) is a discouraged observation and (b) is an insult.

～ような気がする

Meaning · I have a feeling that ～ - a hunch, offered without the evidence that would make it a claim

Read as ような き がする. The pattern reports an impression that has formed in you without your being able to show your working. It is the softest thing in the whole certainty range: weaker than と思おもう, weaker than ようだ, and much weaker than はずだ. Japanese uses it constantly, both to hedge a real opinion and to describe a genuine sensation you cannot account for.

～ような気がする · Formation

ような is the noun-modifying form of ようだ, and 気き is the noun it modifies. So the clause attaches exactly the way it does before any noun.

Attaches to Form Example

～ような気がする · Examples

I feel like I've met them somewhere before.

この部屋へや、少すこし寒さむいような気きがします。
This room feels a bit cold to me.

鍵かぎをかけ忘わすれたような気きがして、家いえまで戻もどった。
I had a feeling I'd forgotten to lock up, so I went all the way back home.

彼かれの説明せつめいは、どこか本当ほんとうではないような気きがする。
Something about his explanation feels untrue to me.

～ような気がする · Examples (cont.)

最近さいきん、一日いちにちが過すぎるのが速はやくなったような気きがしませんか。
Don't you feel like the days go by faster lately?

まだ連絡れんらくはないけれど、今回こんかいはうまくいくような気きがする。
I haven't heard anything yet, but I've got a feeling it'll work out this time.

初はじめて来きた町まちのはずなのに、前まえにもこの坂さかを歩あるいたような気きがした。
It was supposed to be my first time in the town, and yet I felt as though I'd walked up that hill before.

説明せつめいは分わかりやすかったのですが、一番いちばん大事だいじなところを見落みおとしているような気きがします。
The explanation was clear enough, but I have the feeling we're overlooking the most important part.

～ような気がする · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and one of the highest-frequency hedges in ordinary conversation. 気きがする in plain form is casual, 気きがします fits any polite situation, and nothing about it is stiff or literary. In writing, the close relative ～ように思おもう does the same job with a more composed tone.

Two flavours share the one form, and context separates them. The first is a real sensation: something smells faintly of smoke, a room feels colder, you half heard your name. The second is politeness - you have an opinion, and you present it as a mere feeling so that disagreeing with you costs nothing. In a meeting, この案あんは少すこし急いそぎすぎているような気きがします is a carefully wrapped objection, and everyone hears it as one.

～ような気がする · Nuance & register (cont.)

The variant ような気きもする is worth having. Replacing が with も turns it into "part of me feels", and it is how you sit on the fence out loud: 安やすいような気きもするし、高たかいような気きもする.

Do not confuse the phrase with its neighbours. 気きになる means something is on your mind or bothering you; 気きに入いる means you like it. Only 気きがする means "I have the impression", and the particle is が, never を.

～ような気がする · ような気がする vs と思う

Rule of thumb - と思おもう is an opinion you could defend if pressed. ような気きがする is an impression you could not.

この料理りょうりには塩しおが足たりないと思おもいます。
I think this dish needs more salt.

この料理りょうりには塩しおが足たりないような気きがします。
This dish feels like it could do with more salt.

～ような気がする · ような気がする vs と思う (cont.)

The first invites a reply - agreement, or "no, it's fine as it is". The second barely puts the claim on the table, and that is often the point. Note the practical consequence: if a superior asks for your judgment, answering only with ような気きがします can read as evasive rather than modest.

～ような気がする · ような気がする vs ようだ・みたいだ

Rule of thumb - ようだ is a conclusion drawn from evidence; ような気きがする is a feeling that arrives without it.

玄関げんかんに靴くつがある。誰だれか来きているようだ。
There are shoes in the entrance. It seems someone's here.

家いえに誰だれかいるような気きがする。
I have a feeling there's someone in the house.

～ような気がする · ような気がする vs ようだ・みたいだ (cont.)

The first points at the shoes; anybody could check the reasoning. The second points at nothing at all, which is exactly why it is the sentence that belongs in a ghost story. Because they sit next to each other, the two combine happily: いるような気きがしただけかもしれない.

～ような気がする · ような気がする vs 気がする

Rule of thumb - the bare 気きがする is a shade more direct; adding よう puts one more layer of "sort of" between you and the claim.

前まえにも同おなじ話はなしを聞きいた**気きがする**。
I think I've heard this story before.

前まえにも同おなじ話はなしを聞きいたような気きがする。
I have some vague feeling I've heard this story before.

Both are natural, and in casual speech the short form is if anything the commoner of the two. Choose the long one when you want the extra padding - an awkward opinion, a memory you distrust, a criticism you would rather not sharpen.

～ような気がする · Common mistakes

  • ❌ そのやり方かたは危あぶない**気きになります**。 → ✅ そのやり方かたは危あぶない**気きがします**。
    気きになる means "it bothers me / I keep wondering about it". For "I have the impression", the verb is 気きがする.

  • ❌ 忘わすれ物ものをしたような**気きをします**。 → ✅ 忘わすれ物ものをしたような**気きがします**。
    The particle is が. Nothing is being done to the feeling; the feeling simply occurs.

  • ❌ この部屋へやは静しずかだような気きがする。 → ✅ この部屋へやは静しずかな気きがする。
    Before a noun, な-adjectives take な, never だ - and with this pattern the short form is the one people actually say.

  • ❌ 私わたしは来年らいねん30歳さいになるような気きがします。 → ✅ 私わたしは来年らいねん30歳さいになります。
    A fact you know about yourself is not a hunch. Hedging it sounds as though you are unsure how old you are.

気き is a noun, so the clause in front of it has to be in noun-modifying shape: past plain form plus ような. Dropping よう altogether is also fine and slightly more direct.

～ような気がする · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この計画けいかくには無理むりがあると思おもいます。 (b) この計画けいかくには無理むりがあるような気きがします。

～ふりをする

Meaning · to pretend to ～; to act as if - deliberately putting on an appearance you know to be false

The noun ふり (sometimes written 振ふり) is the show you put on, and する is doing it. Two things are built into the pattern: the appearance is produced on purpose, and the person producing it knows it is not true. That is what separates it from every "it seems" pattern in the book - those report how things look, while this one reports somebody staging how things look.

～ふりをする · Formation

ふり is a noun, so whatever comes in front of it takes the shape a noun modifier takes.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (present) plain form + ふりをする 泣なく → 泣なくふりをする
Verb (past state) た-form + ふりをする 寝ねた → 寝ねたふりをする
Verb (negative) ない-form + ふりをする 知しらない → 知しらないふりをする
Verb (progressive) ている + ふりをする 聞きいている → 聞きいているふりをする
い-adjective plain form + ふりをする 痛いたい → 痛いたいふりをする
な-adjective ～な + ふりをする 平気へいきな → 平気へいきなふりをする
Noun ～の + ふりをする 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいのふりをする
Continuing the sentence ふりをして + verb 気きづかない → 気きづかないふりをして通とおる

～ふりをする · Formation (cont.)

Note - the tense inside matters. 寝ねたふり is the normal way to say "pretending to be asleep", because what you are faking is the state of having fallen asleep. 寝ねるふり would be miming the act of dropping off - possible, but rarely what people mean.

～ふりをする · Examples

弟おとうとは寝ねたふりをしている。
My little brother is pretending to be asleep.

知しらないふりをしないでください。
Please don't pretend you don't know.

道みちで先生せんせいに会あったが、気きづかないふりをして通とおり過すぎた。
I ran into my teacher in the street, but I walked past pretending not to notice.

彼女かのじょは平気へいきなふりをしていたが、手てが震ふるえていた。
She was acting as though nothing was wrong, but her hands were shaking.

～ふりをする · Examples (cont.)

子こどもたちは警察官けいさつかんのふりをして遊あそんでいる。
The children are playing at being police officers.

会議かいぎ中ちゅうはどうしても眠ねむくて、話はなしを聞きいているふりをするだけでやっとだった。
I was so sleepy in the meeting that it was all I could do to look as if I was listening.

上司じょうしの冗談じょうだんに、みんな面白おもしろいふりをして笑わらっていた。
Everyone laughed at the boss's joke, pretending to find it funny.

困こまっている人ひとを見みかけても見みて見みぬふりをするような大人おとなにはなりたくない。
I don't want to grow into the sort of adult who looks the other way when someone is in trouble.

～ふりをする · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and very common in ordinary speech. In writing you will meet both ふり and 振ふり; kana is the safer default in a sentence, since the kanji is also used for unrelated words.

The pattern carries a faint moral charge, because pretending is a small deception. How heavy that charge is depends entirely on the content: 寝ねたふり to avoid a chore is comic, while 知しらないふり about something you were told is an accusation. Said about yourself it is often self-deprecating; said about someone else it is rarely a compliment.

～ふりをする · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two shortenings are worth recognising. In casual speech を drops - 聞きこえないふりした - and the fixed expression 知しらんぷり ("playing dumb", "blanking someone") turns up constantly in conversation with no する in sight: 「あいつ、知しらんぷりしてたよ」.

The idiom 見みて見みぬふりをする - literally "see and do the not- seeing show" - means turning a blind eye, and it is worth learning whole; the ぬ is an old negative you will otherwise not meet at this level.

One limitation. Because the pattern needs an actor, an inanimate subject cannot use it. Clouds do not pretend to be a face; for that you want ようだ or みたいだ.

～ふりをする · ふりをする vs まねをする

Rule of thumb - ふりをする is pretending to be something you are not. まねをする is copying somebody's behaviour, with no claim about who you are.

息子むすこは父親ちちおやのまねをして、新聞しんぶんを広ひろげて読よんでいる。
My son copies his father, spreading out the newspaper and reading it.

息子むすこは大人おとなのふりをして、映画館えいがかんに入はいろうとした。
My son tried to get into the cinema pretending to be an adult.

Copying can be admiring, playful or educational - まねをする is how you learn a craft. Pretending is aimed at somebody's belief: the point of the second sentence is that the ticket seller was meant to be fooled.

～ふりをする · ふりをする vs ように見える

Rule of thumb - ふりをする describes what the actor is doing; ように見みえる describes what the observer sees. Only the first claims intent.

彼かれは元気げんきなふりをしている。
He's putting on a brave face.

彼かれは元気げんきそうに**見みえる**。
He looks well.

The first says you have seen through him; the second says nothing about whether the appearance is genuine. Getting these backwards is how learners accidentally accuse people of lying, so keep the intent inside ふり firmly in mind.

～ふりをする · ふりをする vs ～つもりだ

Rule of thumb - with ふり, you know it is false and want others to believe it. With the "acting as if" つもり, you have convinced yourself.

祖父そふはまだ若わかいつもりで、重おもい荷物にもつを運はこぼうとする。
My grandfather still thinks of himself as young and tries to carry heavy things.

祖父そふは若わかいふりをして、階段かいだんを駆かけ上あがってみせた。
My grandfather put on a show of youth and ran up the stairs.

The つもり version is about his self-image; nobody is being deceived except possibly himself. The ふり version has an audience. Note that つもり here is not the "intend to" つもり you learned at N5 - same word, and context does the sorting.

～ふりをする · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 医者いしゃふりをする。 → ✅ 医者いしゃのふりをする。
    ふり is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs の, exactly as in 医者いしゃの息子むすこ.

  • ❌ 元気げんきだふりをする。 → ✅ 元気げんきなふりをする。
    な-adjectives take な before a noun. The だ form never modifies anything.

  • ❌ 忙いそがしいふりがする。 → ✅ 忙いそがしいふりをする。
    The particle is を - you do a pretence. 気きがする has が because a feeling happens to you; a performance does not.

  • ❌ 授業じゅぎょう中ちゅうに寝ねるふりをした。（meaning "I pretended to be asleep"） → ✅ 授業じゅぎょう中ちゅうに寝ねたふりをした。
    What you fake is the state of being asleep, and that state is the た-form.

～ふりをする · Practice 1

1. 質問しつもんされたくなかったので、下したを向むいて聞きこえない____。

聞きこえないふりをした。

The negative plain form attaches directly, and the whole point is that the speaker could hear perfectly well - a deliberate performance, which is what ふり marks.

～ふりをする · Practice 2

2. Choose: 妹いもうとは歌手かしゅの（ふりをして・まねをして）、鏡かがみの前まえで歌うたっている。

妹いもうとは歌手かしゅのまねをして、鏡かがみの前まえで歌うたっている。

She is copying a singer's manner for fun, with nobody to deceive. ふりをして would suggest she is trying to pass herself off as a professional.

～ふりをする · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "He pretended not to have heard the question and changed the subject."

彼かれは質問しつもんが聞きこえなかったふりをして、話はなしを変かえた。

Use the past negative inside, because what he is faking is the state of not having caught it, and continue the sentence with ふりをして.

～ふりをする · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼かれは病気びょうきだふりをして、会社かいしゃを休やすんだ。

✅ 彼かれは病気びょうきのふりをして、会社かいしゃを休やすんだ。

病気びょうき is a noun, so it links to ふり with の. Neither だ nor a bare noun will do.

～ふりをする · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 母ははは**大丈夫だいじょうぶなふりをしていた**。 (b) 母ははは**大丈夫だいじょうぶそうだった**。

(a) says your mother was staging it and you saw through the performance. The sentence quietly tells us she was not all right, and that she had a reason to hide it.

(b) reports only how she struck you: she seemed fine. It makes no claim about what was going on underneath, and it could easily be followed by "…but I was wrong".

～わけだ

Meaning · so that's why; it follows that; that means - presents the sentence as the conclusion that falls out of something already known

わけ is the noun 訳わけ, "reason, logic, the way a thing works out". わけだ therefore never stands alone: it always looks back at a premise - something just said, just discovered, or plainly visible - and says "given that, this is how it adds up." Three flavours share the one form: no wonder (a realisation), it follows that (a calculation), and in other words (a restatement).

～わけだ · Formation

わけ is a noun, so the clause in front of it is in noun-modifying shape.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + わけだ 知しっている → 知しっているわけだ
Verb (negative) ない-form + わけだ 通つうじない → 通つうじないわけだ
い-adjective plain form + わけだ 寒さむい → 寒さむいわけだ
な-adjective ～な + わけだ 不便ふべんな → 不便ふべんなわけだ
Noun ～な / ～という + わけだ 留学生りゅうがくせい → 留学生りゅうがくせいなわけだ
Polite … + わけです 高たかい → 高たかいわけです
Restating what was just said ～という + わけだ 来こられない → 来こられないというわけだ
Connecting two sentences というわけで - → というわけで、中止ちゅうしします

～わけだ · Formation (cont.)

Note - the realisation use is almost always announced in advance by どうりで ("no wonder") or だから. Hearing どうりで, a Japanese listener is already expecting わけだ at the end of the sentence.

～わけだ · Examples

窓まどが開あいている。どうりで寒さむいわけだ。
The window's open. No wonder it's cold.

北海道ほっかいどうのご出身しゅっしんですか。雪ゆきに慣なれているわけですね。
You're from Hokkaido? That explains why snow doesn't bother you.

彼かれは10年ねん大阪おおさかに住すんでいたのだから、関西弁かんさいべんが話はなせるわけだ。
He lived in Osaka for ten years, so of course he can speak Kansai dialect.

バスが一時間いちじかんに一本いっぽんしかないのだから、不便ふべんなわけだ。
There's only one bus an hour - no wonder the place is inconvenient.

～わけだ · Examples (cont.)

毎日まいにち3時間じかん練習れんしゅうすれば、一週間いっしゅうかんで21時間じかんになるわけです。
If you practise three hours a day, that works out at twenty-one hours a week.

つまり、今回こんかいの旅行りょこうには参加さんかできないというわけですね。
So what you're saying is that you can't join this trip.

去年きょねんの夏なつに工場こうじょうが閉しまったのだから、この町まちの人口じんこうが減へっているわけだ。
The factory shut last summer, so it stands to reason the town's population is falling.

毎朝まいあさ5時じに起おきて走はしっているそうです。あんなに速はやく走はしれるわけですね。
Apparently he's up at five every morning to run. No wonder he's that fast.

～わけだ · Nuance & register

わけだ is register-neutral in itself; わけです fits any polite setting and turns up constantly in teaching, briefings and explanations, because its whole job is showing how a conclusion follows. In casual speech it contracts to わけ with the だ dropped - 「あ、それで静しずかなわけか」 - and わけか is the shape a realisation takes when you are talking to yourself.

The realisation use is warm and companionable: you have just understood something, and saying so out loud gives the other person credit for explaining it. The restating use - つまり…というわけですね - is the standard way to confirm you have followed a complicated explanation, and in business it is a genuinely useful move. Handle it with a little care, though. Restating something the listener already knows perfectly well can sound as if you are explaining them to themselves, and delivered flatly it comes across as condescending. That is a tendency, not a rule; tone and context decide.

～わけだ · Nuance & register (cont.)

というわけで deserves separate mention. As a sentence opener it means "and so", wrapping up a line of reasoning and moving to what follows from it: というわけで、今日きょうはここまでにします. It is extremely common in spoken explanation and in blog-style writing.

One thing わけだ cannot do: announce your own decision. A decision is not something that follows from evidence, so 行いくことにしました or 行いくつもりです is what you want there.

～わけだ · わけだ vs のだ・んです

Rule of thumb - んです supplies the background to a situation. わけだ draws the conclusion from a background already established.

昨日きのうは休やすみました。熱ねつがあったんです。
I was off yesterday. I had a temperature.

熱ねつがあったのなら、休やすんだわけだ。
If you had a temperature, then naturally you took the day off.

The two point in opposite directions. んです runs from the situation back to its explanation; わけだ runs from the explanation forward to the situation. That is why わけだ is so often preceded by だから, どうりで or a のだから clause - the premise has to already be on the table.

～わけだ · わけだ vs はずだ

Rule of thumb - はずだ is an expectation about something you have not confirmed. わけだ accounts for something you already know to be so.

田中たなかさんは中国語ちゅうごくごが話はなせるはずです。
Tanaka should be able to speak Chinese.

上海シャンハイで育そだったのだから、中国語ちゅうごくごが話はなせるわけです。
He grew up in Shanghai, so of course he speaks Chinese.

In the first, you have not heard him speak it; you are predicting. In the second you have just heard him, and the sentence explains why. This is why はずだったのに exists and there is no natural equivalent for わけだ - you cannot be disappointed by a conclusion about a fact you have already observed.

～わけだ · わけだ vs はずだ (cont.)

The overlap that confuses people is that both can translate as "so he must speak Chinese". Ask which way the sentence is facing: forward to an unverified prediction (はずだ), or backward to explain a known fact (わけだ).

～わけだ · わけだ vs ということだ

Rule of thumb - in the "that means" sense the two overlap; わけだ stresses that the conclusion follows logically, ということだ simply restates the content.

全員ぜんいんが反対はんたいしたのなら、この案あんは通とおらないわけだ。
If everyone opposed it, then the proposal isn't going through.

全員ぜんいんが反対はんたいした。この案あんは通とおらないということだ。
Everyone opposed it. That means the proposal isn't going through.

The difference is thin here, and in many sentences either will pass. Where they part company is that ということだ also carries the hearsay meaning - "I hear that" - and わけだ never does. If a source is named, only ということだ is possible.

～わけだ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ あの人ひとは医者いしゃだわけだ。 → ✅ あの人ひとは医者いしゃなわけだ。（or 医者いしゃというわけだ）
    A noun links to わけ with な or という, never with だ.

  • ❌ 明日あしたは雨あめが降ふるわけだ。（said out of nowhere） → ✅ 明日あしたは雨あめが降ふるでしょう。
    わけだ needs a premise behind it. With nothing to conclude from, the sentence leaves the listener waiting for the missing first half.

  • ❌ 私わたしは来年らいねん留学りゅうがくするわけです。 → ✅ 私わたしは来年らいねん留学りゅうがくするつもりです。
    Your own plans are decisions, not conclusions. わけだ would suggest your studying abroad was somehow deduced from circumstances.

～わけだ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ どうりで道みちが込こんでいるはずだ。 → ✅ どうりで道みちが込こんでいるわけだ。
    どうりで introduces an explanation for something you are looking at right now, and that pairs with わけだ. はずだ would turn a fact into a prediction.

～わけだ · Practice 1

1. 時計とけいが10分じゅっぷん遅おくれていたのか。それで電車でんしゃに乗のり遅おくれた____。

それで電車でんしゃに乗のり遅おくれたわけだ。

The clock is the premise, and missing the train is the fact being accounted for. それで plus わけだ is the classic realisation shape - "ah, so that's why".

～わけだ · Practice 2

2. Choose: 山田やまださんは昨日きのう出張しゅっちょうに行いったから、今日きょうは会社かいしゃにいない（はずだ・わけだ）。

今日きょうは会社かいしゃにいないはずだ。

You have not checked his desk; you are predicting from what you know. That is はずだ. わけだ would fit only if you had already found the desk empty and were explaining why.

～わけだ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The heating was off all night - no wonder the room is freezing."

一晩中ひとばんじゅう暖房だんぼうが切きれていたのか。どうりで部屋へやが寒さむいわけだ。

"No wonder" is どうりで, and the conclusion it introduces takes わけだ. The い-adjective attaches with no change at all.

～わけだ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼女かのじょは元気げんきだわけだから、心配しんぱいしなくていい。

✅ 彼女かのじょは元気げんきなわけだから、心配しんぱいしなくていい。

な-adjectives take な before わけ. The だ of 元気げんきだ cannot modify a noun, and わけ is a noun.

～わけだ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれは毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうしているんです。 (b) 彼かれは毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうしているわけです。

(a) offers his daily practice as the explanation for something else - why he is good, why he is never free, why he looks tired. It supplies background.

(b) presents the daily practice as what follows from something already said: you have just been told he trains with a club six evenings a week, and this sentence adds it up. Said with no premise in front of it, (b) sounds unfinished.

～わけがない

Meaning · there's no way ～; it's impossible that ～ - denies that any path of reasoning could lead to the thing being true

Keep the literal sense in view: わけ is the 訳わけ, the reason or logic by which something could come about, and がない says there is none. So わけがない does not say "unlikely" or "I doubt it". It says the door is shut. This is the strongest denial in the わけ family, and it is the one learners over-use.

What is negated: the possibility itself. That single fact separates it from わけではない, which leaves the possibility standing and denies only an inference.

～わけがない · Formation

わけ is a noun, so the clause in front takes noun-modifying shape.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + わけがない 勝かてる → 勝かてるわけがない
Verb (negative) ない-form + わけがない 気きづかない → 気きづかないわけがない
い-adjective plain form + わけがない 安やすい → 安やすいわけがない
な-adjective ～な + わけがない 簡単かんたんな → 簡単かんたんなわけがない
Noun ～な / ～という + わけがない 本物ほんもの → 本物ほんものなわけがない
Slightly softer / more written … + わけはない 忘わすれる → 忘わすれるわけはない
Polite … + わけがありません 届とどく → 届とどくわけがありません
Casual speech … + わけない できる → できるわけないよ

～わけがない · Examples

子こどもにこの漢字かんじが読よめるわけがない。
There's no way a child can read this kanji.

あの人ひとがうそをつくわけがありません。
He would never lie - it's simply not possible.

練習れんしゅうもしないで、試合しあいに勝かてるわけがないよ。
You don't practise, so of course you can't win the match.

こんなに甘あまいのに、カロリーが低ひくいわけがない。
As sweet as this is, there is no chance it's low in calories.

～わけがない · Examples (cont.)

一度いちど会あっただけの人ひとの名前なまえを覚おぼえているわけがないじゃないか。
Come on, nobody remembers the name of someone they met once.

説明書せつめいしょも読よまずに組くみ立たてて、うまくいくわけがないでしょう。
Putting it together without even reading the instructions - that was never going to work.

毎日まいにち夜中よなかまで残業ざんぎょうが続つづいているのだから、体からだに影響えいきょうが出でないわけがない。
With overtime running until the middle of the night every day, it cannot possibly be leaving him unaffected.

～わけがない · Examples (cont.)

あれほど楽たのしみにしていたのだから、中止ちゅうしと聞きいて娘むすめががっかりしないわけがありません。
She'd been looking forward to it so much that there is no way our daughter isn't crushed to hear it's cancelled.

～わけがない · Nuance & register

This is a forceful, emotionally coloured pattern. It does not weigh evidence the way はずがない does; it announces that the speaker regards the idea as out of the question. That makes it excellent for arguing, for reassuring - 忘わすれるわけがないよ - and for sarcasm, and risky when the thing you are ruling out is what the person in front of you just said. Contradicting a superior with そんなわけがありません is blunt to the point of rudeness; softer options are そうとは思おもえません or はずがありません.

～わけがない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Casual speech drops が: できるわけない, 知しってるわけないじゃん. That short form is very common among friends and carries a shrug with it. Going the other way, わけはない - with は instead of が - reads a shade calmer and more written, because the は gives it a whiff of contrast rather than flat denial.

Two neighbours to keep separate. わけにはいかない looks similar and means something else entirely - "I can't, for reasons of duty or circumstance" - and わけではない is the partial denial treated on its own page. Confusing わけがない with わけではない is the single most common error in this corner of the grammar, so the first contrast below is worth reading twice.

～わけがない · わけがない vs わけではない

Rule of thumb - わけがない denies the thing itself: it cannot be true. わけではない denies an inference: the thing may well be partly true, but it does not follow the way you assumed.

大好だいすきな先輩せんぱいに会あえるのに、嬉うれしくないわけがない。
I get to see the senior I adore - there's no way I'm not delighted.

会あえて嬉うれしいが、緊張きんちょうしていないわけではない。
I'm glad to see her, though that isn't to say I'm not nervous.

Try the substitution test. Replace わけではない with "it's not that…" and the sentence should still work; replace わけがない with "there's no way…" and that should work. If both readings feel possible, look at what the speaker is doing: are they rejecting a claim outright, or trimming an over-reading?

～わけがない · わけがない vs わけではない (cont.)

彼かれが犯人はんにんであるわけがない。
There's no way he's the culprit.

彼かれを疑うたがっているわけではない。
It's not that I suspect him.

The first defends him absolutely. The second only corrects an impression you may have given - you are not accusing him, but you have not said he is innocent either. That gap between the two is exactly the gap between the patterns.

～わけがない · わけがない vs はずがない

Rule of thumb - はずがない argues from a known fact: given X, Y cannot follow. わけがない states the speaker's conviction that Y is impossible, with or without an argument.

鍵かぎは私わたしが持もっていたのだから、誰だれかが入はいれたはずがない。
I had the key on me, so nobody can have got in.

あんなに優やさしい人ひとが、そんなひどいことを言いうわけがない。
Someone that kind would never say something so cruel.

～わけがない · わけがない vs はずがない (cont.)

The first is a piece of reasoning you could write out step by step. The second is conviction about a person's character, and it would be odd to demand the speaker's evidence. In many sentences either pattern is acceptable and the difference is temperature: はずがない cool, わけがない warm. Note also that はずがない more comfortably admits it was wrong - はずがないと思おもっていたのに - while わけがない sounds strange doing so.

～わけがない · わけがない vs わけにはいかない

Rule of thumb - わけがない is about what is possible. わけにはいかない is about what is permissible: you could do it, but circumstances or obligation stop you.

明日あしたは大事だいじな試験しけんだから、休やすむわけにはいかない。
There's an important exam tomorrow, so I can't very well take the day off.

あの忙いそがしさで、ゆっくり休やすめるわけがない。
As busy as that, there's no chance of getting any real rest.

In the first the speaker is perfectly able to stay home; what rules it out is what is at stake. In the second nothing is stopping him except reality. Because the two forms sit one syllable apart, they get swapped constantly - check whether you mean "cannot happen" or "mustn't happen".

～わけがない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この仕事しごとが簡単かんたんだわけがない。 → ✅ この仕事しごとが簡単かんたんなわけがない。
    A な-adjective takes な before わけ. The same goes for nouns, which take な or という.

  • ❌ 嫌きらいなわけがないが、毎日まいにちは食たべたくない。 → ✅ 嫌きらいなわけではないが、毎日まいにちは食たべたくない。
    The speaker means "it's not that I dislike it" - a partial denial. わけがない would claim disliking it is impossible, which clashes with the second half.

  • ❌ 明日あしたは雨あめが降ふるわけがないから、傘かさはいらないだろう。 → ✅ 明日あしたは雨あめが降ふりそうもないから、傘かさはいらないだろう。
    Tomorrow's weather is uncertain, not impossible. わけがない is far too absolute for a forecast.

～わけがない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 車くるまで来きたので、お酒さけを飲のむわけがない。 → ✅ 車くるまで来きたので、お酒さけを飲のむわけにはいかない。
    You are able to drink; you must not. Impossibility and prohibition are different patterns.

～わけがない · Practice 1

1. 徹夜てつやで作つくった資料しりょうだ。間違まちがいが一ひとつもない____。

間違まちがいが一ひとつもないわけがない。

Something written overnight cannot plausibly be flawless, and the speaker rules the idea out rather than merely doubting it - the categorical denial わけがない.

～わけがない · Practice 2

2. Choose: 甘あまいものが（嫌きらいなわけがない・嫌きらいなわけではない）が、健康けんこうのために控ひかえている。

甘あまいものが**嫌きらいなわけではない**が、健康けんこうのために控ひかえている。

The second half admits the speaker avoids sweets, so the first half is correcting a likely misreading - "not that I dislike them". わけがない would declare disliking them impossible and leave the が clause hanging.

～わけがない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "He only started last month - there's no way he knows the whole system."

先月せんげつ入はいったばかりの人ひとが、システム全体ぜんたいを分わかっているわけがない。

The point is impossibility, not doubt, so わけがない (or, arguing more coolly from the same fact, はずがありません).

～わけがない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 大切たいせつな会議かいぎだから、遅おくれるわけがない。（intended: "I mustn't be late"）

✅ 大切たいせつな会議かいぎだから、遅おくれるわけにはいかない。

As written, the sentence claims that being late is impossible. What the speaker means is that it is not allowed to happen, and that is わけにはいかない.

～わけがない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれの気持きもちが分わからないわけがない。 (b) 彼かれの気持きもちが分わからないわけではない。

(a) insists that failing to understand him is impossible - you know him too well, or the situation is too obvious. It is emphatic, and often reproachful: "of course you understand how he feels."

(b) concedes the opposite way round. You do, to some extent, understand him; the sentence heads off the assumption that you are unsympathetic, and it almost always continues with が or けど into whatever the objection really is.

～わけではない

Meaning · it's not that ～; that doesn't mean ～ - cancels a conclusion somebody has drawn, without denying the facts behind it

わけではない is a partial denial. Something has been said, or is about to be assumed, and the natural inference from it is wrong - so you reach in and take away the inference, leaving everything else standing. English does the same job with "it's not that…", "that's not to say…" and "not necessarily".

What is negated: the inference, not the possibility. わけがない slams the door on the whole idea; わけではない only says the idea does not follow from what you have just heard. Learners swap the two constantly, and the results are much ruder than intended.

～わけではない · Formation

わけ is a noun, so the clause in front of it takes noun-modifying shape.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + わけではない 嫌きらっている → 嫌きらっているわけではない
Verb (negative) ない-form + わけではない 払はらえない → 払はらえないわけではない
い-adjective plain form + わけではない 安やすい → 安やすいわけではない
な-adjective ～な + わけではない 嫌きらいな → 嫌きらいなわけではない
Noun ～な / ～という + わけではない 専門家せんもんか → 専門家せんもんかというわけではない
Polite … + わけではありません 反対はんたいしている → 反対はんたいしているわけではありません
Casual speech … + わけじゃない 怒おこっている → 怒おこっているわけじゃない
Softened further … + というわけではない 嫌きらいだ → 嫌きらいというわけではない

～わけではない · Formation (cont.)

Note - the pattern is at its most natural with a partner phrase in front of it: 必かならずしも ("not necessarily"), 全まったく ("not entirely"), いつも, 全部ぜんぶ, or a からといって clause ("just because…"). Those words all set up the over-general idea that わけではない then trims back.

～わけではない · Examples

毎日まいにち走はしっているが、大会たいかいに出でるわけではない。
I run every day, but that doesn't mean I'm going to enter a race.

反対はんたいしているわけではありませんが、もう少すこし考かんがえたいです。
It's not that I'm against it - I'd just like a bit more time to think.

返事へんじが遅おそくなっただけで、怒おこっているわけじゃないよ。
I was just slow replying; it's not that I'm cross with you.

お金かねがないわけではないが、これは高たかすぎる。
It's not that I can't afford it - this is simply too expensive.

～わけではない · Examples (cont.)

日本語にほんごが全まったく話はなせないわけではないけれど、会議かいぎについていくのは難むずかしい。
It isn't that I can't speak Japanese at all, but keeping up in meetings is hard.

一人ひとりで暮くらしているからといって、寂さびしいわけではない。
Living alone doesn't automatically mean being lonely.

この方法ほうほうがいつでもうまくいくというわけではないので、状況じょうきょうを見みて判断はんだんしてください。
This approach doesn't always work, so use your judgment as the situation requires.

辞やめたいと言いったのは本当ほんとうですが、今いますぐ会社かいしゃを出でていくわけではありません。
It's true that I said I want to quit, but that doesn't mean I'm walking out tomorrow.

～わけではない · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and indispensable in polite conversation. Its social function is face: it lets you disagree with an interpretation while accepting the facts, so nobody has to be wrong. In a meeting, 予算よさんが足たりないというわけではありません concedes that money has been discussed and merely corrects what the listener made of it.

Casual speech contracts it to わけじゃない, which is one of the most frequent phrases in everyday Japanese - 「べつに嫌きらいなわけじゃないけど」. The べつに in front is almost a fixed companion, and adding けど at the end leaves room for whatever the real point turns out to be.

～わけではない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because the pattern corrects an inference, it needs the inference to exist. Said out of the blue, with nothing for it to push back against, it sounds like an answer to a question nobody asked. Give it a premise: a からといって clause, a previous remark, or a visible situation.

Two combinations are worth memorising whole. 必かならずしも～わけではない is the standard "not necessarily", and it is common in written argument. ～ないわけではない is a double negative meaning "it isn't that I can't" - grudging agreement, one step short of saying yes.

～わけではない · わけではない vs わけがない

Rule of thumb - わけではない denies the inference; わけがない denies the possibility. One trims, the other refuses.

漢字かんじが苦手にがてなわけではない。ただ書かく練習れんしゅうが足たりないだけだ。
It isn't that I'm bad at kanji - I just haven't practised writing them enough.

習ならってもいない漢字かんじが書かけるわけがない。
There's no way anyone can write kanji they've never been taught.

～わけではない · わけではない vs わけがない (cont.)

The first accepts that the speaker's kanji are weak and rejects only the explanation offered for it. The second rules the whole proposition out. Swap them and you get nonsense: ❌漢字かんじが苦手にがてなわけがない would insist that being bad at kanji is impossible.

～わけではない · わけではない vs ではない

Rule of thumb - plain ではない denies the fact. わけではない leaves the fact alone and denies what you would read into it.

この店みせのコーヒーはおいしくない。
The coffee at this place isn't good.

この店みせのコーヒーがおいしいわけではないが、静しずかなのでよく来くる。
It's not as if the coffee here is any good, but it's quiet, so I come often.

The first is a verdict on the coffee. The second is a verdict on your reasoning: you assumed the coffee was why the speaker keeps coming, and they are correcting that, not reviewing the drink. This is why わけではない so often has a が or けど clause attached - the real point is usually in the second half.

～わけではない · わけではない vs とは限らない

Rule of thumb - とは限かぎらない attacks a generalisation: it isn't always so. わけではない attacks an inference about the case in front of you.

高たかい店みせの料理りょうりがおいしいとは**限かぎらない**。
Expensive restaurants aren't always good.

値段ねだんで選えらんだわけではない。味あじが気きに入いったから選えらんだのだ。
I didn't choose it on price - I chose it because I liked the taste.

～わけではない · わけではない vs とは限らない (cont.)

The first would sit comfortably in an essay about restaurants in general. The second is about one decision, one speaker, one misreading being corrected. With 必かならずしも in front, the two come very close together, and in sentences about general rules they are often interchangeable.

～わけではない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 嫌きらいだわけではない。 → ✅ 嫌きらいなわけではない。（or 嫌きらいというわけではない）
    な-adjectives and nouns link to わけ with な or という, never だ.

  • ❌ この問題もんだいが解とけるわけではない。（intended: "there's no way I can solve this"） → ✅ この問題もんだいが解とけるわけがない。
    For flat impossibility you want わけがない. わけではない only says that being able to solve it does not follow from whatever was just said.

  • ❌ 私わたしは医者いしゃなわけではありません。（simply stating you are not a doctor） → ✅ 私わたしは医者いしゃではありません。
    A plain fact takes a plain negative. わけではない is for cancelling an inference, and using it here implies somebody had just concluded that you were one.

～わけではない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 寒さむくないわけではないから、上着うわぎはいらない。 → ✅ 寒さむくないわけではないから、上着うわぎを持もっていこう。
    ～ないわけではない is a double negative: "it isn't that it's not cold", i.e. it is somewhat cold. Make sure the second half agrees with the arithmetic.

～わけではない · Practice 1

1. ダイエット中ちゅうだと言いっても、何なにも食たべない____。

何なにも食たべないわけではない。

Saying you are on a diet invites the conclusion that you eat nothing, and the sentence heads that conclusion off while leaving the diet itself intact - the textbook use of わけではない.

2. Choose: 彼かれはまだ新人しんじんだから、一人ひとりで全部ぜんぶ任まかせられる（わけがない・わけではない）。

一人ひとりで全部ぜんぶ任まかせられるわけがない。

He is brand new, and the speaker is ruling the idea out entirely. わけではない would say only that being new does not mean he can be trusted with everything, which is a much milder claim than the からだ clause sets up.

～わけではない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Just because a book is famous doesn't mean it's interesting."

有名ゆうめいな本ほんだからといって、面白おもしろいわけではない。

からといって sets up the inference - fame implies quality - and わけではない removes it. The famousness of the book is not being denied at all.

～わけではない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: お茶ちゃが好すきだわけではないが、毎朝まいあさ飲のんでいる。

✅ お茶ちゃが好すきなわけではないが、毎朝まいあさ飲のんでいる。

好すきだ is a な-adjective, so it becomes 好すきな before the noun わけ. The sense is right: drinking it daily does not prove fondness.

～わけではない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼女かのじょのことを信しんじていないわけがない。 (b) 彼女かのじょのことを信しんじていないわけではない。

(a) declares that distrusting her is impossible - of course the speaker believes her, and the sentence is emphatic, almost indignant.

(b) is a careful concession: the speaker does trust her, up to a point, and is correcting the impression that they do not. It nearly always continues with が or けど into the reservation that prompted the sentence in the first place. The grammatical distance between the two is one particle; the social distance is enormous.

～とは限らない

Meaning · not necessarily ～; ～ isn't always the case - denies that a general rule holds every time

Read as とは かぎらない. The verb 限かぎる means "to be limited to", so とは限かぎらない literally says "things are not limited to that outcome". You use it when somebody - the listener, common sense, or the speaker's own earlier sentence - is treating something as a rule, and you want to point out the exceptions without denying the rule's usual force.

～とは限らない · Formation

It attaches to a whole plain-form clause, because what it denies is a whole proposition, not a word.

～とは限らない · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + とは限かぎらない 勝かつ → 勝かつとは限かぎらない
Verb (negative) ない-form + とは限かぎらない 来こない → 来こないとは限かぎらない
い-adjective plain form + とは限かぎらない 正ただしい → 正ただしいとは限かぎらない
な-adjective ～だ + とは限かぎらない 安全あんぜんだ → 安全あんぜんだとは限かぎらない
Noun Noun (+ だ) + とは限かぎらない 本当ほんとう → 本当ほんとうだとは限かぎらない
Polite … + とは限かぎりません 届とどく → 届とどくとは限かぎりません
With the usual partner adverb 必かならずしも + … + とは限かぎらない 安やすい → 必かならずしも安やすいとは限かぎらない
Double-negative variant ～ない + とも限かぎらない 起おこらない → 起おこらないとも限かぎらない

～とは限らない · Formation (cont.)

Note - 必かならずしも is not required, but it is the adverb the pattern expects, and it makes the "not always" reading unmistakable. In written argument the pair 必かならずしも～とは限かぎらない is close to a fixed phrase.

～とは限らない · Examples

高たかいものが長持ながもちするとは限かぎらない。
Expensive things don't always last longer.

日本人にほんじんなら誰だれでも漢字かんじが得意とくいだとは限かぎりません。
Not every Japanese person is good at kanji.

天気てんき予報よほうが必かならずしも当あたるとは限かぎらない。
The forecast isn't necessarily right.

早はやく並ならべば席せきが取とれるとは限かぎらないよ。
Queuing early doesn't guarantee you a seat, you know.

～とは限らない · Examples (cont.)

説明書せつめいしょに書かいてあることが全すべて正ただしいとは限かぎらない。
Not everything written in the manual is necessarily correct.

給料きゅうりょうの高たかい会社かいしゃが働はたらきやすいとは限かぎらない。
A company that pays well isn't necessarily a comfortable place to work.

経験けいけんが長ながいからといって、教おしえ方かたが上手じょうずだとは限かぎりません。
Long experience doesn't automatically make someone a good teacher.

この辺へんは静しずかですが、夜中よなかに工事こうじの音おとがしないとも限かぎりませんので、窓まどは閉しめておきましょう。
It's quiet around here, but you never know when there might be noise from the roadworks at night, so let's keep the windows shut.

～とは限らない · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, with a slight lean towards careful speech and writing. It is the standard way to introduce a caveat in an argument, so it fills newspaper columns, textbooks and advice of every kind, and it is equally at home in conversation when you want to warn someone off an assumption.

Socially it is gentle. Because it denies only universality, not the claim itself, it lets you push back without contradicting anyone flatly: "true as a rule, not true every time." 必かならずしもそうとは限かぎりません is a stock polite disagreement in a meeting, and it is much softer than そんなわけがありません.

～とは限らない · Nuance & register (cont.)

The variant ～ないとも限かぎらない deserves attention, because it is a double negative and learners routinely read it backwards. Literally "it is not limited to not happening", it means "it might well happen - you never know", and it almost always introduces a risk worth preparing for: 地震じしんが起おきないとも限かぎらない. If a sentence with とも限らない seems to be arguing for complacency, you have the polarity the wrong way round.

One boundary: the pattern needs something general to push against - a rule, a tendency, a "just because" clause, an everyone or an always. Applied to a plain one-off prediction it sounds oddly stiff, and かもしれません or とは思おもえません is what a speaker would actually use.

～とは限らない · とは限らない vs わけではない

Rule of thumb - とは限かぎらない denies that something is always so. わけではない denies that a conclusion follows in this case.

人気にんきのある店みせがいつも安やすいとは限かぎらない。
Popular shops aren't always cheap.

その店みせが安やすいわけではない。ただ近ちかいから通かよっているだけだ。
It's not that the shop is cheap - I go there simply because it's close.

～とは限らない · とは限らない vs わけではない (cont.)

The first is about shops in general and could open a paragraph of consumer advice. The second is about one shop and one listener's mistaken assumption. Where a sentence is about a general rule, the two patterns often both fit, and 必かならずしも can appear with either.

～とは限らない · とは限らない vs ～ないとも限らない

Rule of thumb - Xとは限かぎらない = "X is not always so". Xないとも限かぎらない = "it's not impossible that X happens" - a warning.

明日あした晴はれるとは限かぎらない。
It won't necessarily be fine tomorrow.

急きゅうに雨あめが降ふらないとも限かぎらないから、傘かさを持もっていきなさい。
It's not out of the question that it'll suddenly rain, so take an umbrella.

The first removes a guarantee. The second raises a possibility you had dismissed, and it points forward to a precaution. Note the different attitudes: とは限らない dampens optimism, とも限らない disturbs complacency.

～とは限らない · とは限らない vs かもしれない

Rule of thumb - かもしれない floats one possibility. とは限かぎらない corrects someone's generalisation.

明日あしたの試合しあいは中止ちゅうしになるかもしれない。
Tomorrow's match might be called off.

雨あめが降ふったら必かならず中止ちゅうしになるとは限かぎらない。
Rain doesn't automatically mean the match is off.

The first adds a new thought to the conversation. The second takes an existing belief - rain means cancellation - and puts a limit on it. If nobody in the conversation holds the belief, とは限らない has nothing to work on.

～とは限らない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この道みちが安全あんぜんなとは限かぎらない。 → ✅ この道みちが安全あんぜんだとは限かぎらない。
    What comes before と is a full sentence, not a noun modifier, so a な-adjective keeps its だ rather than turning into な.

  • ❌ 私わたしは明日あした行いくとは限かぎりません。 → ✅ 私わたしは明日あした行いくかどうか、まだ分わかりません。
    Your own undecided plans are not a general rule with exceptions. The pattern is for correcting what is taken to be always true.

～とは限らない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 彼かれが来こないとも限かぎらないから、準備じゅんびしなくてもいい。 → ✅ 彼かれが来こないとも限かぎらないから、準備じゅんびしておこう。
    The double negative means "he may well turn up after all". That is a reason to get ready, not a reason to relax.

  • ❌ 必かならずしも安やすいとは限かぎらないではない。 → ✅ 必かならずしも安やすいとは限かぎらない。
    限かぎらない is already the negative of 限かぎる. Adding a second negative on the end leaves the sentence saying the opposite of your point.

～とは限らない · Practice 5

～ないことはない

Meaning · it's not that ～ can't be done; it isn't impossible - a double negative that concedes a small, reluctant yes

Two negatives make a weak positive. 飲のめないことはない does not mean "I can drink"; it means "it isn't the case that I can't drink" - which in practice is a hedged, half-hearted "I can, up to a point". The pattern exists precisely so that you can agree without committing, and a が or けど clause spelling out the reservation almost always follows.

Softer, commoner in speech ～ない + こともない 間まに合あわない → 間まに合あわないこともない
Polite … + ないことはありません できない → できないことはありません
Usual continuation … + が / けど + reservation - → できないことはないが、時間じかんがかかる

～ないことはない · Examples

お酒さけは飲のめないことはない。
It's not that I can't drink.

漢字かんじは読よめないことはないが、時間じかんがかかる。
I can read kanji well enough, though it takes me a while.

一人ひとりで行いかないこともないけど、できれば誰だれかについてきてほしい。
I could go on my own, I suppose, but I'd rather someone came with me.

この服ふく、似合にあわないことはないけれど、色いろが少すこし派手はでかな。
It's not that these clothes don't suit me - the colour is a bit loud, that's all.

～ないことはない · Examples (cont.)

走はしれば終電しゅうでんに間まに合あわないこともない。
If we run, we might just make the last train.

「土曜日どようび、お願ねがいできますか。」「出でられないことはありませんが、午後ごごからになります。」
"Could I ask you to come in on Saturday?" "I can manage it, but not before the afternoon."

予算よさんを増ふやせば来月らいげつまでに完成かんせいできないこともありませんが、品質ひんしつが心配しんぱいです。
With a bigger budget it wouldn't be impossible to finish by next month, but I'd worry about the quality.

彼かれの言いい分ぶんも分わからないことはないが、あのやり方かたには賛成さんせいできない。
I can see his side of it, up to a point, but I can't agree with the way he went about it.

～ないことはない · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and heavily used in polite negotiation, because it is the shape of a qualified yes. When a Japanese colleague answers a request with できないことはありませんが…, the important information is in whatever comes after が; the first half has only agreed that the thing is theoretically possible. Hearing it as an enthusiastic acceptance is a classic misreading.

ないこともない - with も instead of は - is softer again and is the version you hear most in conversation. Both forms keep company with hedging words: 少すこし, なんとか, どうにか and a trailing けど all cluster around them.

～ないことはない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Tone matters. Said about your own abilities the pattern is modest, which is usually welcome. Said about someone else's work - 悪わるくないことはない - it can come out grudging or faintly damning, so it is worth being aware that you are praising with the handbrake on. Used constantly, it starts to sound evasive; a speaker who never gives a straight yes is noticed.

Finally, note that the pattern says nothing about willingness on its own. It reports that the door is not closed. Whether you actually intend to walk through it is the job of the sentence that follows.

～ないことはない · ～ないことはない vs ～ことはない

Rule of thumb - with the negative inside, it means "it's not impossible". Without it, ～ことはない means "there's no need to" - a completely different pattern that happens to look similar.

そんなに急いそぐことはないよ。時間じかんはまだある。
There's no need to rush. We've still got time.

急いそがないことはないが、走はしるほどではない。
We do need to get a move on, though not to the point of running.

The first tells someone to relax; the second grudgingly admits some hurry is called for. The only visible difference is the negative on the inner verb, so read these two slowly - mixing them up reverses your advice.

～ないことはない · ～ないことはない vs ～できる

Rule of thumb - the potential form states an ability plainly. The double negative concedes it with reservations attached.

明日あしたまでに直なおせます。
I can have it fixed by tomorrow.

明日あしたまでに直なおせないことはありませんが、ほかの仕事しごとが止とまります。
I could have it fixed by tomorrow, but everything else would have to stop.

The first is a promise; the second is an opening position in a negotiation. If you mean the first and say the second, you will be heard as reluctant - and if you mean the second and say the first, you have just agreed to something you did not intend.

～ないことはない · ～ないことはない vs ～ないわけではない

Rule of thumb - they overlap a great deal. The tendency: ないことはない concedes a possibility or ability; ないわけではない corrects an impression the listener has formed.

自分じぶんで作つくれないことはないが、買かったほうが早はやい。
I could make one myself, but buying one is quicker.

料理りょうりが嫌きらいなわけではない。ただ時間じかんがないだけだ。
It's not that I dislike cooking - I just haven't the time.

The first weighs whether the thing can be done; the second repairs a wrong reading of the speaker's feelings. In many sentences either would pass, and a native speaker would not always be able to say why they chose one. Where the sentence is about capability, ないことはない is the safer choice.

～ないことはない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 食たべるないことはない。 → ✅ 食たべないことはない。
    The inner clause has to be a proper ない-form. Attaching ない to the dictionary form is not a possible shape in Japanese.

  • ❌ この辺へんは静しずかないことはない。 → ✅ この辺へんは静しずかではないことはない。
    な-adjectives and nouns negate with ではない (casually じゃない) before the pattern.

  • ❌ 「一緒いっしょに行いきませんか。」「行いけないことはありません!」 → ✅ 「ぜひ行いきたいです。」
    As a reply to an invitation you are pleased to accept, the double negative sounds as if you are being dragged along. Save it for when you really do have a reservation.

～ないことはない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ そんなに心配しんぱいしないことはないよ。（intended: "there's no need to worry"） → ✅ そんなに心配しんぱいすることはないよ。
    "No need to" takes the dictionary form plus ことはない, with no inner negative.

～ないことはない · Practice 1

1. 「この辞書じしょ、貸かしてもらえますか。」「貸かせ____が、明日あした返かえしてくださいね。」

貸かせないことはありませんが、明日あした返かえしてくださいね。

A conditional yes: lending it is possible, and the condition follows after が. 貸かせないこともありません would be a shade softer still.

～ないことはない · Practice 2

2. Choose: 時間じかんはたっぷりあるから、そんなに急いそぐ（ことはない・ないことはない）。

そんなに急いそぐことはない。

With plenty of time in hand, the speaker is saying there is no need to hurry, and that is the dictionary form plus ことはない. The double negative would mean the opposite: that some hurry is required.

～ないことはない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I can't say I don't understand your feelings, but rules are rules."

お気持きもちが分わからないことはありませんが、規則きそくは規則きそくです。

The double negative concedes sympathy without endorsing the request - exactly the job this pattern does in polite refusals.

～ないことはない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この問題もんだいは難むずかしいことはないが、時間じかんがかかる。（intended: "it's not that it's difficult"）

✅ この問題もんだいは難むずかしくないことはないが、時間じかんがかかる。

Better still: この問題もんだいは**難むずかしくない**が、時間じかんがかかる。 As originally written, 難むずかしいことはない means "there is no need for it to be hard", which is not what the speaker had in mind. い-adjectives take the くない form inside the pattern.

～ないことはない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 日曜日にちようびも働はたらけます。 (b) 日曜日にちようびも働はたらけないことはありません。

(a) offers Sunday freely. Your manager will write it in the rota and think no more about it.

(b) says the same thing on paper and something quite different in the room: Sunday is technically possible, you would rather it did not happen, and there is a condition coming. A Japanese listener will wait for the が before answering.

～のでしょうか

Meaning · I wonder if ～; might I ask ～ - a question wrapped in two layers of softening

Take it apart and it explains itself. The の is the explanatory の you met in んです: it asks about the circumstances behind something rather than the bare fact. The でしょう is the tentative ending: it presents the question as a musing rather than a demand. Put together, のでしょうか is the politest ordinary way to ask about something, and it is what fills enquiry emails, service counters and the rhetorical questions in speeches.

～のでしょうか · Formation

The clause takes the same shape it takes before んです.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain form + のでしょうか 届とどく → 届とどくのでしょうか
Verb (past) た-form + のでしょうか 止とまった → 止とまったのでしょうか
い-adjective plain form + のでしょうか 正ただしい → 正ただしいのでしょうか
な-adjective ～な + のでしょうか 必要ひつような → 必要ひつようなのでしょうか
Noun ～な + のでしょうか 無料むりょう → 無料むりょうなのでしょうか
Spoken contraction … + んでしょうか 開あく → 開あくんでしょうか
Plain, written musing … + のだろうか 続つづく → 続つづくのだろうか
Casual, to yourself … + のかな / のかしら 来くる → 来くるのかな

～のでしょうか · Formation (cont.)

Note - with no の, plain でしょうか attaches straight to a noun or a ます-form and asks a simple tentative question: お名前なまえは何なんでしょうか. Adding の changes what you are asking about - see the second contrast below.

～のでしょうか · Examples

この席せき、空あいているのでしょうか。
Is this seat free, I wonder?

どうして今日きょうはこんなに人ひとが多おおいのでしょうか。
Why are there so many people here today?

明日あしたの説明会せつめいかいは何時なんじに終おわるのでしょうか。
What time does tomorrow's briefing finish, may I ask?

すみません、この書類しょるいはこちらの窓口まどぐちに出だせばいいのでしょうか。
Excuse me - is this the window I should hand these papers in at?

～のでしょうか · Examples (cont.)

彼かれはどうしてあんなに急きゅうに引ひっ越こしたのでしょうか。
Why on earth did he move away so suddenly?

お申込もうしこみは来週らいしゅうまで受うけ付つけていただけるのでしょうか。
Would you still be accepting applications up to next week?

なぜ人ひとは同おなじ失敗しっぱいを何度なんども繰くり返かえしてしまうのでしょうか。
Why is it that people repeat the same mistakes over and over?

先週せんしゅう注文ちゅうもんした品物しなものがまだ届とどかないのですが、いつごろ発送はっそうしていただけるのでしょうか。
The item I ordered last week still hasn't arrived - could I ask roughly when it will be dispatched?

～のでしょうか · Nuance & register

のでしょうか is polite and slightly formal. It fits enquiry emails, counters, customer calls, and any moment when you would rather not sound as though you were interrogating someone. んでしょうか is the same thing in spoken shape: natural in conversation, a little too casual for a written enquiry to a company you do not know. のだろうか is the plain form and reads as thinking aloud - it is very common in essays and in a narrator's inner voice, and rare as a real question to a person.

のかな is the casual, mostly self-directed version - 「間まに合あうのかな」 - and can be aimed gently at a friend. のかしら does the same job with a softer ending that leans feminine in most people's speech; that is a tendency, not a rule, and it also sounds a touch old-fashioned to younger speakers.

～のでしょうか · Nuance & register (cont.)

The rhetorical use is worth noticing. In a presentation or an essay, なぜ～のでしょうか asks a question nobody is expected to answer, and the speaker answers it themselves in the next sentence. It is one of the standard ways to open a paragraph in Japanese exposition.

Two cautions. First, the softness is real but it is not infinite politeness: for a request, you still need a request form - いただけませんか, お願ねがいできますでしょうか - because a question about circumstances is not an ask. Second, business Japanese is fond of stacking ますでしょうか (as in いただけますでしょうか). You will hear it constantly; some style guides call it redundant. It is safe to recognise it and to use the simpler いただけないでしょうか yourself.

～のでしょうか · のでしょうか vs ですか・ますか

Rule of thumb - ますか asks for a fact. のでしょうか asks about the situation behind the fact, and takes the edge off the asking.

この電車でんしゃは東京とうきょうに止とまりますか。
Does this train stop at Tokyo?

この電車でんしゃは東京とうきょうに止とまらないのでしょうか。
Am I right that this train doesn't stop at Tokyo?

～のでしょうか · のでしょうか vs ですか・ますか (cont.)

The first is a clean request for information and is perfectly polite. The second implies you have noticed something - the train shot past the platform, the board says something odd - and are asking about that. Note the practical effect: with a stranger, のでしょうか sounds a little more deferential, but a long string of them can come across as roundabout. For a simple factual question, ますか is not rude.

～のでしょうか · のでしょうか vs でしょうか

Rule of thumb - no の: you are asking which/whether. With の: you are asking what lies behind it. Why-questions almost always need the の.

会議かいぎは何時なんじからでしょうか。
What time is the meeting, please?

会議かいぎの時間じかんが変かわったのでしょうか。
Has the meeting time been changed, then?

～のでしょうか · のでしょうか vs でしょうか (cont.)

The first asks for a scheduled fact. The second asks about a change you suspect has happened and want explained. This is also why ❌どうして来こなかったでしょうか sounds broken while どうして来こなかったのでしょうか is fine: a "why" question is by definition about circumstances, and circumstances are what の marks.

～のでしょうか · のでしょうか vs のかな・のだろうか

Rule of thumb - same question, three registers: のでしょうか polite and addressed to someone, のかな casual and half to yourself, のだろうか plain and written.

この店みせ、まだやっているのかな。
Is this place still open, I wonder.

こちらのお店みせは何時なんじまで営業えいぎょうしているのでしょうか。
Until what time is your shop open, may I ask?

人間にんげんはなぜ物語ものがたりを必要ひつようとするのだろうか。
Why is it that human beings need stories?

～のでしょうか · のでしょうか vs のかな・のだろうか (cont.)

The first would be muttered to a friend outside a dark window; the second said to the person behind the counter; the third printed at the head of a chapter. Using the casual one at the counter is not offensive, only childish-sounding; using the polite one to yourself is merely odd.

～のでしょうか · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 受付うけつけは3階がいのでしょうか。 → ✅ 受付うけつけは3階がいなのでしょうか。（or 3階がいでしょうか）
    A noun needs な before のでしょうか, exactly as it needs な before んです.

  • ❌ どうして昨日きのう休やすんだでしょうか。 → ✅ どうして昨日きのう休やすんだのでしょうか。
    A why-question asks about circumstances, and that is precisely the job of の. Leaving it out makes the sentence sound unfinished.

  • ❌ この荷物にもつを運はこんでくださるのでしょうか。（intended as a request） → ✅ この荷物にもつを運はこんでいただけませんか。
    Softening a question does not turn it into a request. As written, you are asking whether the person happens to be about to carry it.

～のでしょうか · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 部長ぶちょう、この件けんはどうするんでしょうか。（in a formal email） → ✅ 部長ぶちょう、この件けんはどのように進すすめればよろしいでしょうか。
    んでしょうか is the spoken contraction and reads as casual on the page; in writing, use のでしょうか or a plainly deferential request form.

～のでしょうか · Practice 1

1. すみません、この荷物にもつはどこで受うけ取とればいい____。

どこで受うけ取とればいいのでしょうか。

You are asking about the arrangements rather than a bare fact, and you are asking a stranger, so both the の and the tentative でしょうか are doing work.

～のでしょうか · Practice 2

2. Choose: 試験しけんの結果けっかは来週らいしゅう（でしょうか・なのでしょうか）。

試験しけんの結果けっかは来週らいしゅうでしょうか。

A plain "is it next week?" needs no explanatory の. Adding it - 来週らいしゅうなのでしょうか - is grammatical too, but it implies you have heard something surprising and are checking the story behind it.

～のでしょうか · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I wonder why the shop closed down so suddenly."

あの店みせはどうしてあんなに急きゅうに閉しまってしまったのでしょうか。

The question is about the reason behind an event, which calls for の; でしょうか keeps it as a wondering rather than a demand. To yourself you would say 閉しまってしまったのかな.

～のでしょうか · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この部屋へやは静しずかだのでしょうか。

✅ この部屋へやは静しずかなのでしょうか。

静しずかだ is a な-adjective, and before の it takes な. The だ form cannot sit in front of a nominalising の.

～のでしょうか · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 明日あしたは来きますか。 (b) 明日あしたは来こられるのでしょうか。

(a) is a straightforward question about the listener's plans, and there is nothing impolite about it.

(b) steps back twice. The potential form asks whether coming is feasible rather than whether they intend to, and のでしょうか asks about the circumstances, leaving them room to say no without refusing anything. It is what you use when you suspect the answer is no and would rather not force it.
