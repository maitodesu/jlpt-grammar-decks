～っぽい

Meaning · has the quality of X; comes across as X-ish

～っぽい says that something gives off the character of the word it attaches to, without necessarily being it. Attached to a verb stem it names a personal disposition - the kind of person who keeps doing that thing. Both uses lean mildly critical: ～っぽい is what you reach for when a thing is X in a way you would rather it were not.

～っぽい · Formation

Whatever it attaches to, the result is an い-adjective and conjugates like one: 水みずっぽい → 水みずっぽくない → 水みずっぽかった → 水みずっぽくて. There is no だ after it.

～っぽい · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + っぽい 水みず → 水みずっぽい
Noun (person type) Noun + っぽい 子供こども → 子供こどもっぽい
い-adjective (stem, drop い) Stem + っぽい 安やすい → 安やすっぽい
Colour word (stem) Stem + っぽい 白しろい → 白しろっぽい
Verb (ます-stem) ます-stem + っぽい 忘わすれます → 忘わすれっぽい
Result before a noun っぽい + Noun 子供こどもっぽい話はなし
Result as an adverb っぽく + Verb 安やすっぽく見みえる
Casual speech only - plain form + っぽい Verb/Noun/Adj plain + っぽい 降ふる → 降ふるっぽい（"looks like it'll rain"）

～っぽい · Formation (cont.)

Note - this is not a suffix you can bolt onto any noun you like. The everyday stock is short and worth learning as vocabulary: 水みずっぽい・油あぶらっぽい・湿しめっぽい・ほこりっぽい・安やすっぽい・荒あらっぽい・子供こどもっぽい・色いろっぽい・白しろっぽい, and from verbs 忘わすれっぽい・飽あきっぽい・怒おこりっぽい・惚ほれっぽい. The last row of the table is a separate, very productive slang pattern that young speakers use for "seems like"; keep it out of writing and out of exams.

～っぽい · Examples

このスープはちょっと**水みずっぽい**。
This soup is a bit watery.

弟おとうとは**忘わすれっぽくて**、いつも鍵かぎをさがしている。
My little brother is forgetful and is always hunting for his keys.

そんな**子供こどもっぽい**ことを言いうのはやめなさい。
Stop saying such childish things.

父ちちは年としをとってから、前まえより**怒おこりっぽく**なった。
My father has got quicker-tempered as he's aged.

～っぽい · Examples (cont.)

霧きりの向むこうに**白しろっぽい**建物たてものがぼんやり見みえた。
A whitish building showed faintly through the fog.

今日きょうの揚あげ物ものは**油あぶらっぽくて**、半分はんぶんしか食たべられなかった。
Today's fried food was so greasy I could only get through half of it.

値段ねだんのわりに**安やすっぽく**見みえるので、お祝いわいには向むかないと思おもう。
For the price it looks cheap and nasty, so I don't think it works as a celebration gift.

姉あねは**飽あきっぽい**ので、今年ことし始はじめた習ならい事ごとはもう三みっつ目めだ。
My sister loses interest quickly - the class she started this year is already her third.

～っぽい · Nuance & register

The default flavour is mildly negative. 子供こどもっぽい is not "sweetly childlike", it is "immature"; 安やすっぽい is not "affordable", it is "cheap-looking". If you want the approving version of "properly X", that is a different suffix (らしい), and mixing them up is how learners accidentally insult people.

Two families escape the negativity. Colour words - 白しろっぽい, 黒くろっぽい, 赤あかっぽい - are purely descriptive "-ish", the polite way to hedge a colour you cannot name exactly. And 色いろっぽい ("alluring") is a compliment, though a fairly forward one.

～っぽい · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral-to-casual. ～っぽい in its ordinary sense appears in novels and newspapers without comment. The slang extension does not: attaching っぽい to a whole plain-form clause - 無理むりっぽい, 行いくっぽい, 終おわったっぽい - is conversational shorthand for みたい that spread through younger speech and still reads as loose. It is worth being able to understand; it is not worth writing in an email to a client.

Because the result is an い-adjective, everything an い-adjective does is available: 水みずっぽくないです, 油あぶらっぽかったら残のこしてください, 忘わすれっぽくなる. Learners who mentally file っぽい as "a noun ending" produce ❌子供こどもっぽいだ, which is the same error as ❌高たかいだ.

～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～らしい

Rule of thumb - らしい says the thing has the qualities it ought to have and approves; っぽい says it has the surface flavour of something it is not supposed to be, and disapproves.

The two attach to the same nouns and mean nearly opposite things about the speaker's attitude:

田中たなかさんは**男おとこらしい**人ひとだと、みんなが言いう。
Everyone says Tanaka is a manly sort of person.

話はなし方かたが**男おとこっぽい**とよく言いわれるが、気きにしていない。
People often tell me I talk in a blokey way, but it doesn't bother me.

～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～らしい (cont.)

The clearest test is to apply both to the same subject. A child described as 子供こどもらしい is behaving exactly as a child should - lively, honest, unaffected. An adult described as 子供こどもっぽい is behaving as a child would, and should not be.

大人おとななのに**子供こどもっぽい**ところが、彼かれの欠点けってんだ。
His weakness is that he's childish for a grown man.

Note that らしい has a second, unrelated job - hearsay, "apparently" - attached to a whole clause. っぽい never carries hearsay, only quality.

～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～みたい / ～のよう

Rule of thumb - みたい compares one thing to another thing; っぽい reports a quality the thing has in itself.

彼かれは先生せんせいみたいだ。
He's like a teacher. (he resembles one - maybe he isn't one)

話はなし方かたが先生せんせいっぽいね。
You've got a rather teacherly way of talking.

～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～みたい / ～のよう (cont.)

みたい makes a comparison you could spell out ("like a teacher does"). っぽい picks out an attribute and hangs it on the subject directly, which is why it works on materials and textures where a comparison would be strange: 水みずっぽいスープ is a soup that is thin, not a soup that resembles water. ❌水みずみたいなスープ would be an exaggeration about a completely different thing.

The one place they overlap is the slang use, where 雨あめっぽい and 雨あめみたい both mean "looks like rain" - and there みたい is the safe choice in any register above chatting with friends.

～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～やすい

Rule of thumb - verb-stem + っぽい describes a person's standing disposition; verb-stem + やすい describes how easy the action is, for anyone.

やすい was covered at N4 as "easy to do", and it does double duty as "prone to happen". That second sense is what collides with っぽい:

母ははは**忘わすれっぽい**ので、買かい物ものメモを渡わたしている。
My mother is forgetful, so I hand her a shopping list.

このパスワードは長ながすぎて**忘わすれやすい**。
This password is so long it's easy to forget.

～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～やすい (cont.)

The first is a claim about my mother. The second is a claim about the password - anybody would forget it. Swap them and both sentences go wrong: a password does not have a personality, and ❌母ははは忘わすれやすい sounds like my mother is an object that is easily mislaid.

Only a handful of verbs take っぽい at all （忘わすれる・飽あきる・怒おこる・惚ほれる）, and every one of them names a temperament. やすい attaches to almost any verb.

～っぽい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 忘わすれるっぽい（meaning "forgetful"） → ✅ 忘わすれっぽい
    With verbs, っぽい takes the ます-stem. 忘わすれるっぽい is not ungrammatical, but it is the slang "seems like they'll forget" - a different sentence.

  • ❌ この人ひとは子供こどもっぽいだ。 → ✅ この人ひとは子供こどもっぽい。
    The result is an い-adjective. No だ, and the polite form is 子供こどもっぽいです.

  • ❌ 白しろいっぽい服ふく → ✅ 白しろっぽい服ふく
    With an い-adjective, the final い drops before っぽい - the same trim you make before さ or すぎる.

～っぽい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ お父とうさんは男おとこっぽくて尊敬そんけいしています。 → ✅ お父とうさんは男おとこらしくて尊敬そんけいしています。
    Praise wants らしい. っぽい would read as "he's a bit of a lad", which is not what you meant to say about someone's father.

  • ❌ この部屋へやはほこりっぽいだらけだ。 → ✅ この部屋へやはほこりっぽい。
    Two different suffixes cannot stack. Pick one: っぽい for the stuffy feel of the air, だらけ for visible dust lying on everything.

～っぽい · Practice 1

1. このみそ汁しる、お湯ゆを入いれすぎて水みず____なってしまった。

水みずっぽくなってしまった。

っぽい builds an い-adjective, so before なる it takes the adverbial form っぽく - exactly like 安やすくなる. The sentence complains that the soup has gone thin and watery.

～っぽい · Practice 2

2. Choose: 妹いもうとは（飽あきっぽい・飽あきやすい）ので、どの楽器がっきも長ながく続つづかない。

妹いもうとは**飽あきっぽい**ので、どの楽器がっきも長ながく続つづかない。

The sentence is about my sister's temperament, and a standing personal disposition is っぽい territory. 飽あきやすい would describe something that is easily got bored of - a property of the instrument, not of the player.

～っぽい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The room smelled dusty, so I opened the window."

部屋へやがほこりっぽかったので、窓まどを開あけた。

ほこりっぽい is the standing expression for air that feels dusty and stale. The past tense is the ordinary い-adjective past, っぽかった. ほこりだらけ would be a different complaint - dust you can see lying on the furniture.

～っぽい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 新入生しんにゅうせいのスピーチは元気げんきで、とても学生がくせいっぽくてよかった。

✅ 新入生しんにゅうせいのスピーチは元気げんきで、とても学生がくせいらしくてよかった。

The speaker is praising the speech, and っぽい cannot carry praise - 学生がくせいっぽい would suggest the speech was amateurish. When the point is that something has the qualities it should have, use らしい.

～っぽい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれは大人おとなっぽい。 (b) 彼かれは大人おとならしい。

(a) says he gives off a grown-up air without actually being one - the natural reading is that he is young, and that his manner, clothes or voice are more adult than his age. It is often said of teenagers, and can be admiring or faintly uneasy.

(b) says he is an adult and behaves as an adult properly should: responsible, measured, the way the role is supposed to be filled. It is straightforwardly approving, and it presupposes that he really is one.

～ぎみ

Meaning · showing a slight touch of; a bit on the ～ side

～ぎみ (written 気味きみ when it stands alone, voiced to ぎみ as a suffix) reports that something is a little way toward a state, right now. Not fully there - just far enough that you can see it. It is the word for a cold you have not properly caught yet and a train that is running four minutes behind.

～ぎみ · Formation

The result is a noun. It predicates with だ/です, modifies a following noun with の, and takes な before ので and のに exactly as any noun does.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + ぎみ 風邪かぜ → 風邪かぜぎみ
Noun (する-verb noun) Noun + ぎみ 緊張きんちょう → 緊張きんちょうぎみ
Verb (ます-stem) ます-stem + ぎみ 太ふとります → 太ふとりぎみ
Predicate ～ぎみ + だ / です 疲つかれぎみです
Before a noun ～ぎみ + の + Noun 疲つかれぎみの顔かお
Before ので・のに ～ぎみ + な + ので 遅おくれぎみなので
As an adverb ～ぎみ + に + Verb 焦あせりぎみに話はなす

～ぎみ · Formation (cont.)

Note - the reading is ぎみ, with rendaku. The unvoiced 気味きみ is a separate word meaning "sensation", and it survives in 気味きみが悪わるい - "creepy, unsettling" - which has nothing to do with this suffix. Say きみわるい for that and ぎみ for this.

～ぎみ · Examples

今日きょうはちょっと風邪かぜぎみです。
I've got a bit of a cold today.

最近さいきん、少すこし太ふとりぎみだ。
I've put on a little weight lately.

会議かいぎは予定よていより遅おくれぎみで進すすんでいる。
The meeting is running slightly behind schedule.

疲つかれぎみの顔かおをしていたので、早はやく帰かえるように言いった。
He looked a bit worn out, so I told him to go home early.

～ぎみ · Examples (cont.)

緊張きんちょうぎみの声こえで、新人しんじんは自己紹介じこしょうかいを始はじめた。
In a slightly nervous voice, the new employee began her self-introduction.

このところ売うり上あげが下さがりぎみなので、来月らいげつから広告こうこくを増ふやします。
Sales have been trending a little down lately, so we'll step up the advertising from next month.

息子むすこは受験じゅけんが近ちかづいてから寝不足ねぶそくぎみで、朝あさなかなか起おきられない。
Since the entrance exams got close my son has been a bit short of sleep and struggles to get up.

部長ぶちょうは焦あせりぎみに話はなしていたが、結局けっきょく結論けつろんは出でなかった。
The manager spoke with a hint of impatience, but in the end nothing was decided.

～ぎみ · Nuance & register

～ぎみ almost always points away from normal in an unwelcome direction. Health that is slipping, a schedule that is slipping, a number that is slipping. You will not hear ❌元気げんきぎみ or ❌上手じょうずぎみ: there is no such thing as being slightly too well.

It is also about degree, not frequency. 風邪かぜぎみ does not mean "I often catch colds"; it means "right now, a small amount of cold". That is why it pairs so naturally with 少すこし and ちょっと, which reinforce rather than contradict it: 少すこし疲つかれぎみです.

～ぎみ · Nuance & register (cont.)

The register is neutral and it is entirely at home in business Japanese: 下さがりぎみ, 遅おくれぎみ and 伸のびぎみ are ordinary vocabulary in a sales report. In writing it is normally spelled with the kanji 気味きみ; the reading of the suffix is still ぎみ.

One social use worth knowing: ～ぎみ softens. Telling a colleague 疲つかれぎみですね is far gentler than 疲つかれていますね, because it presents the observation as slight and provisional rather than as a verdict on how they look. The same hedging instinct makes 風邪かぜぎみなので休やすみます a polite way to call in sick without claiming to be seriously ill.

～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ～がち

Rule of thumb - ぎみ is how much, がち is how often. ぎみ says one state is slightly present now; がち says an event keeps recurring.

今朝けさの電車でんしゃは遅おくれぎみだった。
This morning's train was running a bit late.

この路線ろせんの電車でんしゃは遅おくれがちだ。
Trains on this line tend to run late.

～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ～がち (cont.)

The first sentence is about one morning and a small number of minutes. The second is about a pattern across many mornings and says nothing about how late. Use the wrong one and you make a claim you did not intend: telling your boss 遅おくれがちです about a single delayed delivery accuses the whole operation of being unreliable.

They also behave differently in a sentence. ぎみ builds a noun and takes の before a following noun; がち builds a な-adjective and takes な: 疲つかれぎみの顔かお but 病気びょうきがちな子こども.

～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ～っぽい

Rule of thumb - ぎみ says a little of the real thing is present; っぽい says it feels like the thing, whether or not it is.

The pair that shows this best is a cold, because both are said constantly:

昨日きのうから風邪かぜぎみで、薬くすりを飲のんでいる。
I've had a touch of a cold since yesterday, so I'm taking medicine.

何なんとなく風邪かぜっぽいから、今日きょうは早はやく寝ねる。
I feel like I might be coming down with something, so I'm going to bed early.

～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ～っぽい (cont.)

ぎみ states a mild fact: there is a cold, in small quantity. っぽい states an impression: something about how I feel resembles a cold. Which is why 風邪かぜっぽい is what you say before you are sure, and 風邪かぜぎみ once you are.

Grammatically they part company completely. っぽい produces an い-adjective - 風邪かぜっぽかった, 風邪かぜっぽくない - while ぎみ produces a noun: 風邪かぜぎみだった, 風邪かぜぎみではない.

～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ちょっと・すこし

Rule of thumb - ちょっと turns any predicate down a notch. ぎみ only attaches to a fixed kind of word, and it carries a judgement that ちょっと does not.

For a plain matter of degree, an adverb is the natural choice, and ぎみ is simply not available:

このコーヒーはちょっと苦にがい。
This coffee is a little bitter.

❌苦にがいぎみ is impossible - ぎみ does not attach to い-adjectives at all. Nor does it attach to happy states: ❌うれしいぎみ, ❌楽たのしみぎみ.

Where both are possible, ぎみ adds the sense of a deviation from where things should be:

～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ちょっと・すこし (cont.)

体重たいじゅうが増ふえぎみなので、夜よるは食たべないようにしている。
My weight has been creeping up, so I'm trying not to eat at night.

体重たいじゅうが少すこし増ふえた is a neutral measurement. 増ふえぎみ is a measurement plus a frown. And the two combine happily: 少すこし増ふえぎみ.

～ぎみ · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 太ふとるぎみ → ✅ 太ふとりぎみ
    With verbs, ぎみ attaches to the ます-stem, never the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 風邪かぜぎみのです。 → ✅ 風邪かぜぎみです。
    の goes between ～ぎみ and a following noun （風邪かぜぎみの学生がくせい）, not before です.

  • ❌ 今日きょうは元気げんきぎみです。 → ✅ 今日きょうは元気げんきです。
    ぎみ marks a slide toward something unwanted. There is no polite way to be a little bit too healthy.

  • ❌ 彼かれは学校がっこうを休やすみぎみだ。（meaning "he's often absent"） → ✅ 彼かれは学校がっこうを休やすみがちだ。
    Repeated absences are frequency, and frequency is がち.

～ぎみ · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ かぜきみです。 → ✅ かぜぎみです。
    The suffix voices. きみ unvoiced belongs to 気味きみが悪わるい, an unrelated expression.

～ぎみ · Practice 1

1. 昨日きのうあまり寝ねていないので、今日きょうは疲つかれ____です。

疲つかれぎみです。

疲つかれる takes its ます-stem 疲つかれ before ぎみ, and the result is a noun, so です attaches directly. The sentence claims mild tiredness right now, not a habit of being tired.

～ぎみ · Practice 2

2. Choose: この時計とけいは少すこし（進すすみぎみ・進すすみがち）なので、時間じかんを信しんじすぎないほうがいい。

この時計とけいは少すこし**進すすみぎみ**なので…

The clock is permanently a small amount fast - that is degree, so ぎみ. がち would need repeated separate events, which does not fit a clock that simply runs slightly ahead. Note the な before ので, which is the noun pattern.

～ぎみ · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The numbers have been a little low this month, so let's meet on Friday."

今月こんげつは数字すうじが下さがりぎみなので、金曜日きんようびに集あつまりましょう。

下さがりぎみ is standard business phrasing for a mild downward trend, and it is softer than 下さがっています - which is exactly why people reach for it in meetings.

～ぎみ · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 最近さいきんの天気てんきは曇くもりぎみで、洗濯物せんたくものが乾かわかない日ひが多おおい。

✅ 最近さいきんの天気てんきは曇くもりがちで、洗濯物せんたくものが乾かわかない日ひが多おおい。

The clue is 日ひが多おおい - the sentence is counting days, so it is about how often, not how much. Cloudiness recurring across many days is 曇くもりがち, a fixed and very common combination.

～ぎみ · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼女かのじょは緊張きんちょうぎみだった。 (b) 彼女かのじょは緊張きんちょうしていた。

(a) reports a small, visible amount of nerves - her voice a little tight, her hands a little busy - and it hedges. The speaker is describing an impression from the outside and leaving room to be wrong.

(b) states plainly that she was nervous, with no ceiling on how much. It is the neutral description and could cover anything from mild to badly shaken. Between colleagues, (a) is the kinder thing to say out loud.

～がち

Meaning · has a habit of ～ing; keeps ending up that way

～がち says that something happens often, and shouldn't. It is not a measurement of degree and not a statement about one occasion - it is a complaint about a pattern, whether the pattern belongs to a person, a machine or the weather.

～がち · Formation

The result is a な-adjective: がちだ as a predicate, がちな before a noun, がちに before a verb.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (ます-stem) ます-stem + がち 忘わすれます → 忘わすれがち
Verb (ます-stem of a する-verb) Noun + し + がち 無理むりします → 無理むりしがち
Noun (short fixed list) Noun + がち 病気びょうき → 病気びょうきがち
Predicate ～がち + だ / です 遅おくれがちです
Before a noun ～がち + な + Noun 見落みおとしがちな点てん
Before a verb ～がち + に + Verb 遠慮えんりょがちに話はなす
Common set phrase ～がち + になる 休やすみがちになる

～がち · Formation (cont.)

Note - with verbs, がち is broadly productive. With nouns it is not: the usable list is essentially 病気びょうきがち, 曇くもりがち, 留守るすがち, 遠慮えんりょがち, 伏ふし目めがち, and the lexicalised ありがち ("all too common"). Inventing new noun combinations - ❌ 元気げんきがち, ❌雨あめがち - does not work.

～がち · Examples

冬ふゆは運動不足うんどうぶそくになりがちだ。
In winter you end up short of exercise.

弟おとうとは小ちいさいころ**病気びょうきがち**でした。
My little brother was ill a lot when he was small.

今日きょうは**曇くもりがち**で、洗あらったシーツがなかなか乾かわかない。
It's cloudy on and off today, and the sheets I washed just won't dry.

忙いそがしいと、朝あさごはんを**抜ぬきがち**になる。
When you're busy you get into the habit of skipping breakfast.

～がち · Examples (cont.)

彼かれは**遠慮えんりょがちに**手てを挙あげて、小ちいさな声こえで質問しつもんした。
He raised his hand hesitantly and asked his question in a small voice.

メールの返事へんじを後回あとまわしにすると、そのまま**忘わすれがち**だ。
If you put off replying to an email, you tend to forget about it altogether.

説明書せつめいしょの細こまかい注意ちゅういは**見落みおとしがちな**ところなので、声こえに出だして読よんでください。
The small print in the manual is the sort of thing people miss, so read it aloud.

一人ひとり暮ぐらしを始はじめてから、野菜やさいが足たりなくなりがちで、自分じぶんでも反省はんせいしている。
Since I started living alone I keep ending up short on vegetables, and I'm not proud of it.

～がち · Nuance & register

The judgement built into がち is the thing to remember. Every ordinary use points at something the speaker would rather did not keep happening: illness, absence, oversight, delay, cloud. Turn it on a good outcome and the sentence collapses - ❌成功せいこうしがち, ❌元気げんきになりがち - not because the grammar breaks but because the word carries a frown that has nowhere to land.

The frown can be aimed at yourself, and often is: 私わたしも 無理むりしがちなので気きをつけます is a standard, slightly rueful thing to say. It can also be aimed at people in general, which is where the pattern earns its keep in writing - ～と考かんがえがちだが, "we tend to think that, but", is a staple opening move in essays and newspaper columns.

～がち · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral. がち appears in conversation, in business email and in print without shifting tone. Two combinations have drifted into fixed vocabulary and no longer feel like grammar at all: 遠慮えんりょがち ("diffident, hanging back") and ありがち ("the sort of thing that happens all the time" - usually said of a predictable plot or a familiar mistake).

Finally, がち needs repetition. It cannot describe one event. Reporting a single late train as 遅おくれがちだった is wrong in the same way that saying "the train tends to be late" about one specific morning is wrong in English.

～がち · ～がち vs ～やすい

Rule of thumb - やすい is about how easily something can happen, and can be good or bad. がち is about how often it does happen, and is always bad.

N4 taught やすい as "easy to do". Its second sense, "liable to", is what overlaps here - and the two still split cleanly:

この皿さらは薄うすいので**割われやすい**。
These plates are thin, so they break easily.

息子むすこにまかせると、皿さらを**割わりがち**だ。
If I leave it to my son, he's forever breaking plates.

～がち · ～がち vs ～やすい (cont.)

The first is a property of the plates that would hold even if nobody ever broke one. The second counts actual breakages and blames somebody for them. This is also why やすい is happy in praise - 使つかいやすい, 分わかりやすい - and がち never is.

They also build different words: やすい makes an い-adjective （割われやすかった, 割われやすくない）, がち a な-adjective （割わりがちだった, 割わりがちな人ひと）.

～がち · ～がち vs ～ぎみ

Rule of thumb - がち counts occasions; ぎみ measures amount. If you could answer with "three times a week", it is がち. If you could answer with "just a little", it is ぎみ.

彼かれは去年きょねんから会社かいしゃを**休やすみがち**だ。
He's been off work a lot since last year.

今日きょうは少すこし**疲つかれぎみ**なので、残業ざんぎょうはやめておく。
I'm a bit worn out today, so I'll skip the overtime.

～がち · ～がち vs ～ぎみ (cont.)

Some verbs take both, and then the difference is unmistakable. 太ふとりぎみ means I am currently a couple of kilos heavier than I should be; 太ふとりがち means I am the kind of person who puts weight on again and again. Only one of those is a comment about a body shape, and only one is a comment about a life.

～がち · ～がち vs ことが多い

Rule of thumb - ことが多おおい reports frequency as a neutral fact. がち reports the same frequency and disapproves of it.

冬ふゆは山やまに雪ゆきが降ふることが多おおい。
It often snows in the mountains in winter.

That sentence is a piece of information. Nothing is wrong with the snow. Swap in がち and you have implied that the snow is a nuisance - fine if you are a driver, odd in a geography textbook.

～がち · ～がち vs ことが多い (cont.)

The neutral phrase is therefore the safe default for objective writing, and it takes a whole clause rather than a stem, so it works with verbs がち cannot reach: 雨あめが降ふることが多おおい is fine, while ❌雨あめがちだ is not Japanese.

～がち · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 遅おくれるがち → ✅ 遅おくれがち
    がち takes the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 病気びょうきがち**子こども** → ✅ 病気びょうきがちな子こども
    The result is a な-adjective, so it needs な before a noun.

  • ❌ 今日きょうの電車でんしゃは遅おくれがちだった。（about one morning） → ✅ 今日きょうの電車でんしゃは遅おくれていた。
    がち requires a repeated pattern. One late train is just a late train.

  • ❌ 最近さいきんよく眠ねむれて、元気げんきになりがちです。 → ✅ 最近さいきんよく眠ねむれて、元気げんきになってきました。
    がち only fits tendencies the speaker regrets. Nobody regrets getting healthier.

～がち · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 雨あめがちな天気てんき → ✅ 雨あめが降ふりがちな天気てんき
    Only a handful of nouns take がち directly. 曇くもり is on the list; 雨あめ is not, so go through the verb.

～だらけ - covered all over

Meaning · with X all over it; caked in X

～だらけ attaches to a noun and says that the stuff named is all over the surface of something, in an amount nobody wanted. Mud on a coat, dust on a shelf, scratches on a table. The word is a complaint before it is a description.

～だらけ - covered all over · Formation

だらけ attaches to a bare noun - no particle in front of it - and the result is itself a noun. It predicates with だ/です, modifies a following noun with の, and pairs constantly with なる.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun Noun + だらけ 泥どろ → 泥どろだらけ
Predicate ～だらけ + だ / です 机つくえがほこりだらけだ
Before a noun ～だらけ + の + Noun 泥どろだらけの靴くつ

Negation ～だらけ + では ない 傷きずだらけではない

Note - だらけ never attaches to a verb or an adjective, and never takes な before a noun. It is also not freely productive in the way it looks: the noun has to name something you would rather were not there. 泥どろ・砂すな・血ち・ほこり・傷きず・毛け・しわ are all natural; ❌花はなだらけ and ❌光ひかりだらけ are not, unless you are being pointedly sarcastic.

～だらけ - covered all over · Examples

子こどもの服ふくが**泥どろだらけ**になった。
The kids' clothes ended up covered in mud.

棚たなの上うえがほこりだらけだ。
The top of the shelf is thick with dust.

転ころんで、手てが**血ちだらけ**になってしまった。
I fell over and my hands ended up covered in blood.

海うみから上あがったら、体中からだじゅう砂すなだらけだった。
When I came out of the sea I was covered in sand from head to foot.

～だらけ - covered all over · Examples (cont.)

猫ねこを抱だき上あげたら、コートが**毛けだらけ**になった。
I picked the cat up and my coat ended up covered in fur.

工事こうじが終おわったばかりの部屋へやは、窓まどわくまで白しろい**粉こなだらけ**だった。
The room where the work had just finished was covered in white dust, right up to the window frames.

祖父そふの手ては**傷きずだらけ**で、長ながく畑はたけ仕事しごとをしてきたことがすぐに分わかった。
My grandfather's hands were covered in scars - you could tell at once he'd worked the fields for years.

～だらけ - covered all over · Examples (cont.)

雨あめの中なかを走はしってきたらしく、弟おとうとは靴くつも鞄かばんも**泥どろだらけ**のまま玄関げんかんに立たっていた。
He must have run through the rain - my brother was standing in the entryway with his shoes and bag still caked in mud.

～だらけ - covered all over · Nuance & register

だらけ is evaluative. Even when the fact reported is neutral, the word supplies the reaction: dismay, disgust, or a weary "look at the state of this". A parent says 泥どろだらけ about a child's trousers with the sigh already in it.

It is also hyperbolic. 傷きずだらけ does not mean every square centimetre is scarred; it means enough of them that the scars are what you notice. Nobody counts.

Register is ordinary spoken and written Japanese. It is not slangy, but it is blunt, so in a formal report or a polite complaint to a company you would soften it - 汚よごれがひどい状態じょうたいでした rather than 泥どろだらけでした. Between friends and inside a family it is completely standard.

～だらけ - covered all over · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note the shape of the sentence: what is covered is the subject, and what covers it goes in front of だらけ with nothing between. 机つくえがほこりだらけだ - the desk is the topic of the complaint, the dust is the incriminating substance. Learners often reverse this and produce ❌ほこりが机つくえだらけだ, which says the dust is covered in desks.

～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～まみれ

Rule of thumb - まみれ is for a liquid or a fine powder smeared over a surface. だらけ is for many separate things scattered all over, and it also reaches abstract nouns, which まみれ cannot.

Where the substance is genuinely a coating, both work, and まみれ is the more vivid, slightly more literary of the two:

工場こうじょうから出でてきた作業員さぎょういんは、顔かおまで**油あぶらまみれ**だった。
The worker who came out of the factory was covered in oil right up to his face.

台所だいどころの床ゆかが**油あぶらだらけ**で、滑すべりそうだった。
The kitchen floor was greasy all over and felt like it would be slippery.

～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～まみれ (cont.)

The line shows itself at the edges. Sweat forms a film, so 汗あせまみれ is normal and ❌汗あせだらけ is odd. Scars are countable marks, so 傷きずだらけ is normal and ❌傷きずまみれ is odd. Dust, mud and blood sit in the middle and take either - with 血ちまみれ sounding like a crime scene and 血ちだらけ sounding like a kitchen accident.

まみれ extends metaphorically to only a very small set of fixed expressions （借金しゃっきんまみれ, 罪つみにまみれる）, always with the image of being smeared. It does not take scattered abstract defects: ❌間違まちがいまみれ, ❌穴あなまみれ.

～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～っぽい

Rule of thumb - だらけ says the stuff is physically there, all over it. っぽい says the thing merely has the quality of the stuff.

The clearest pair is dust, because both are everyday complaints about a room:

使つかっていない部屋へやはほこりだらけで、まず掃除そうじから始はじめた。
The unused room was thick with dust, so we started with the cleaning.

窓まどを閉しめきっていたので、部屋へやがほこりっぽくて息苦いきぐるしい。
We'd kept the windows shut, so the room feels dusty and stuffy.

～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～っぽい (cont.)

The first is about what is lying on the surfaces; you could photograph it. The second is about how the air feels; you could not. Grammatically they are unrelated too - だらけ builds a noun, っぽい an い-adjective - so the endings never look alike: ほこりだらけだった against ほこりっぽかった.

～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～がついている

Rule of thumb - ～がついている reports that some of the stuff is on there. だらけ reports that it is everywhere on there. The difference is amount, and with it, tone.

シャツに醤油しょうゆがついているよ。
You've got soy sauce on your shirt.

試合しあいのあと、ユニフォームは**泥どろだらけ**だった。
After the match the uniform was caked in mud.

～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～がついている (cont.)

The first is a helpful heads-up about one spot. The second is a verdict on the whole garment. This is why ついている is the polite way to tell a stranger about a stain and だらけ is not: pointing at someone's jacket and saying 泥どろだらけですよ makes it sound as though they had rolled in a field.

～だらけ - covered all over · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 泥どろがだらけ → ✅ 泥どろだらけ
    Nothing goes between the noun and だらけ - no が, no で, no の.

  • ❌ 泥どろだらけな靴くつ → ✅ 泥どろだらけの靴くつ
    The result is a noun, not a な-adjective, so it links with の.

  • ❌ 部屋へやが汚よごれているだらけだ。 → ✅ 部屋へやがごみだらけだ。
    だらけ attaches only to nouns. A verb or an adjective in front of it is not possible.

  • ❌ 汗あせだらけになって走はしっていた → ✅ 汗あせまみれになって走はしっていた
    Sweat is a film over the skin, not a scatter of separate marks, so it takes まみれ. 汗あせびっしょり is the everyday alternative.

～だらけ - covered all over · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 庭にわが花はなだらけできれいだ。 → ✅ 庭にわが花はなでいっぱいできれいだ。
    だらけ cannot be used approvingly. Saying it about flowers turns a pretty garden into an infestation.

～だらけ - full of

This is the same suffix used of things you can count rather than a substance smeared over a surface: mistakes in an essay, holes in a jumper, tourists in a street, debts on a balance sheet. The tone is unchanged - だらけ never reports abundance neutrally. It reports abundance and objects to it.

My brother is up to his neck in debt and apparently can't even bring himself to talk to the family.

The old sweater had got so full of holes that I finally threw it out.

When we got to the mountain hut at the end of summer the inside of the windows was crawling with insects, and I couldn't face going in.

一年いちねん手てを入いれていない庭にわは**雑草ざっそうだらけ**で、どこから片かたづければいいのか分わからない。

～だらけ - full of · Nuance & register

This sense of だらけ is a verdict. Calling a document 間違まちがいだらけ is not the same as saying it contains errors; it is saying the document is not worth reading in its present state. Aimed at a colleague's work it is genuinely rude, and Japanese office language has softer replacements - 間違まちがいが多おおいようです, 少すこし直なおすところがあります. Aimed at your own work it is a normal, slightly theatrical form of apology.

～だらけ - full of · Nuance & register (cont.)

Like the physical sense it is hyperbolic: 穴あなだらけ needs three or four holes, not fifty. And it is saturating, not gradable, so it does not take degree adverbs. ❌とても間違まちがいだらけ is impossible for the same reason "very full of holes" is odd in English - the phrase already means "as many as matters".

One extension worth knowing because it looks like an exception: まみれ, which otherwise refuses abstract nouns, does take 借金しゃっきん - 借金しゃっきんまみれ is as common as 借金しゃっきんだらけ. The image there is of debt smeared over a person like grease, which is why that one combination works and ❌間違まちがいまみれ does not.

～だらけ - full of · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register: everyday spoken and written Japanese, blunt but not vulgar. Headlines and opinion columns use it freely; contracts and formal reports do not.

～だらけ - full of · ～だらけ vs ～ばかり

Rule of thumb - ばかり says nothing but X, to the exclusion of other things. だらけ says X everywhere you look, and adds a complaint.

弟おとうとは肉にくばかり食たべて、野菜やさいに手てをつけない。
My brother eats nothing but meat and won't touch the vegetables.

冷蔵庫れいぞうこの中なかが古ふるい食たべ物ものだらけで、捨すてるところから始はじめた。
The fridge was full of old food, so I started by throwing things out.

～だらけ - full of · ～だらけ vs ～ばかり (cont.)

ばかり sets up a contrast with what is absent, and it is neutral about whether that is good. You can say 本ほんばかり読よんでいる about a studious child admiringly. だらけ has no such flexibility, and it never comments on absence - only on how much of the offending thing is present.

They also fit differently into a sentence. ばかり follows verbs, particles and quantities as well as nouns（見みてばかりいる, 東京とうきょうにばかり）; だらけ follows a bare noun and nothing else.

～だらけ - full of · ～だらけ vs ～でいっぱい

Rule of thumb - でいっぱい reports a container being full, with no judgement, and works for good things. だらけ reports a mess.

会場かいじょうは開演かいえん前まえから人ひとでいっぱいだった。
The hall was packed with people well before the performance began.

駅前えきまえは人ひとだらけで、前まえに進すすめなかった。
The area in front of the station was jammed with people and I couldn't get through.

Both describe crowds; only the second is annoyed about it. That is why でいっぱい is the one that can carry positive nouns - 幸しあわせでいっぱい, 花はなでいっぱいの公園こうえん - while だらけ cannot go near them.

Note the particle: でいっぱい takes で before it, だらけ takes nothing.

～だらけ - full of · ～だらけ vs ～が多い

Rule of thumb - 多おおい is a gradable, neutral measurement. だらけ is an ungradable verdict. Anywhere you need to be polite or precise, use 多おおい.

この報告書ほうこくしょは数字すうじの間違まちがいが**少すこし多おおい**ようです。
There seem to be rather a lot of numerical errors in this report.

自分じぶんで読よみ返かえしたら、一いち枚目まいめから**間違まちがいだらけ**だった。
When I read it back myself, it was riddled with mistakes from page one.

～だらけ - full of · ～だらけ vs ～が多い (cont.)

The first sentence can be handed to the person who wrote the report. The second cannot - but it is exactly right for talking about your own draft. The grammatical consequence of the difference is that 多おおい takes degree words （少すこし多おおい, とても多おおい）and だらけ refuses them.

～だらけ - full of · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 間違まちがいがだらけの作文さくぶん → ✅ 間違まちがいだらけの作文さくぶん
    No particle before だらけ, and の after it when a noun follows.

  • ❌ この町まちは静しずかだらけだ。 → ✅ この町まちはとても静しずかだ。
    だらけ needs a countable or concrete noun. Adjectives and states cannot go in front of it.

  • ❌ 机つくえの上うえがとても本ほんだらけだ。 → ✅ 机つくえの上うえが本ほんだらけだ。
    The suffix already means "saturated", so it takes no degree adverb.

  • ❌ 新あたらしい公園こうえんは花はなだらけで、気持きもちがいい。 → ✅ 新あたらしい公園こうえんは花はなでいっぱいで、気持きもちがいい。
    Praise wants でいっぱい. だらけ would make the flowers sound like a problem the council should deal with.

～だらけ - full of · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 先生せんせいのレポートは間違まちがいだらけですね。 → ✅ 先生せんせいのレポート、少すこし直なおすところがあるようです。
    Grammatically fine, socially not. Save だらけ for your own work, or for things rather than people.

～だらけ - full of · Practice 1

1. 三年さんねん使つかっていない物置ものおきを開あけたら、中なかはくもの巣す____だった。

中なかはくもの巣すだらけだった。

Cobwebs are countable things scattered everywhere, and they are unwelcome - both conditions だらけ needs. It attaches straight to the noun and takes だった for the past, because the whole phrase is a noun.

～だらけ - full of · Practice 2

2. Choose: この案あんは（問題もんだいだらけ・問題もんだいでいっぱい）で、このままでは会議かいぎに出だせない。

この案あんは**問題もんだいだらけ**で、このままでは会議かいぎに出だせない。

でいっぱい describes a container being full and is neutral, so it fits badly with an abstract defect like 問題もんだい. だらけ supplies exactly the disapproval the second half of the sentence assumes.

～だらけ - full of · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The first draft was full of holes, so I rewrote it from scratch."

最初さいしょの原稿げんこうは穴あなだらけだったので、一いちから書かき直なおした。

穴あなだらけ works for a physical sweater and, as here, for gaps in an argument - one of the few abstract nouns that keeps the concrete image. Since the speaker is criticising their own work, the bluntness is fine.

～だらけ - full of · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 会場かいじょうは子こどもだらけがいて、とてもにぎやかだった。

✅ 会場かいじょうは子こどもだらけで、とてもにぎやかだった。

だらけ already contains "there are lots of them", so it cannot be followed by がいる. It behaves as a noun, so it joins the next clause with で. Note that with にぎやか the sentence reads as good-humoured exaggeration rather than a real complaint - that is about as positive as だらけ ever gets.

～だらけ - full of · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この辞書じしょは間違まちがいが多おおい。 (b) この辞書じしょは間違まちがいだらけだ。

(a) is a measured criticism: more errors than you would like, but the dictionary is still a dictionary. You could follow it with a figure, or with 少すこし, because 多おおい is a matter of degree.

(b) writes the thing off. It says errors are everywhere in it and, by implication, that it should not be trusted or bought. It admits no degree word and no polite softening - which is why you would say it to a friend and not to the publisher.

～まま

Meaning · in the same state, unchanged; while still ～

まま freezes a state. Something reached a condition, nobody changed it, and whatever happens next happens with that condition still in place. Shoes still on, light still burning, town still exactly as it was ten years ago.

～まま · Formation

まま is a formal noun. What comes in front of it must be in a form that can modify a noun, which is why verbs use the た-form and not the dictionary form.

～まま · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (past) た-form + まま 立たつ → 立たったまま
Verb (negative) ない-form + まま 食たべない → 食たべないまま
Noun Noun + の + まま 昔むかし → 昔むかしのまま
い-adjective Plain form + まま 熱あつい → 熱あついまま
な-adjective Stem + な + まま 静しずか → 静しずかなまま
Demonstrative この / その / あの + まま そのまま
As a predicate ～まま + だ / です 昔むかしのままです
Deliberately keeping it so ～まま + に + する / しておく そのままにしておく

～まま · Formation (cont.)

Note - the た-form here is not past tense. 立たったまま does not mean "having stood in the past"; it means the standing-up has happened and the resulting posture is still current. This is why only verbs that leave a visible result work well: 着きたまま, つけたまま, 開あけたまま. Verbs that leave nothing behind - ❌食たべたまま話はなす for "talk while eating" - produce nonsense.

～まま · Examples

靴くつをはいたまま上あがらないでください。
Please don't come up with your shoes on.

電気でんきをつけたまま寝ねてしまった。
I fell asleep with the light on.

この町まちは十年じゅうねん前まえのままだ。
This town is exactly as it was ten years ago.

何なにも言いわないまま、彼かれは部屋へやを出でて行いった。
He left the room without ever saying anything.

～まま · Examples (cont.)

スープは熱あついまま運はこんでください。
Please carry the soup while it's still hot.

窓まどを開あけたまま出でかけてしまい、一日中いちにちじゅう気きになっていた。
I went out having left the window open, and it bothered me all day.

借かりた本ほんを読よまないまま返かえすことになりそうだ。
It looks as though I'll end up returning the book I borrowed without having read it.

祖母そぼの部屋へやは亡なくなったときのままにしてあり、机つくえの上うえのめがねも動うごかしていない。
My grandmother's room has been left just as it was when she died - even the glasses on her desk haven't been moved.

～まま · Nuance & register

まま itself is completely neutral and register-free. It appears in children's speech, in polite requests, in legal notices（そのままお待まちください） and in literary description without changing colour.

What supplies the colour is the combination. When the frozen state is one that ought to have been undone, the sentence sounds careless: 靴くつをはいたまま, 電気でんきをつけたまま, 化粧けしょうをしたまま寝ねる. When the frozen state is one being deliberately preserved, it sounds respectful or careful: 昔むかしのまま, 熱あついまま. The suffix is only holding the state still; the judgement comes from what the state is.

～まま · Nuance & register (cont.)

Three connected forms are worth separating. Bare まま and ままで are largely interchangeable in the adverbial slot - 立たったまま食たべる and 立たったままで食たべる are both ordinary, with で a shade more explicit. ままに is different: it is literary, and it also heads a small family of fixed expressions about following someone else's lead - 言いわれるままに書類しょるいにサインした, "I signed the papers just as I was told to."

Finally, そのまま is so common that it has become a word in its own right: "as is, leave it, don't touch it". そのままでいいです ("it's fine like that") is one of the most useful short sentences in the language.

～まま · ～まま vs ～ながら

Rule of thumb - ながら joins two actions happening at once. まま holds one state in place while an action happens. If both halves are things you do, you want ながら.

音楽おんがくを聞ききながら宿題しゅくだいをしている。
He's doing his homework while listening to music.

電車でんしゃが込こんでいて、立たったまま本ほんを読よんだ。
The train was crowded, so I read standing up.

～まま · ～まま vs ～ながら (cont.)

Listening and doing homework are two ongoing activities, so ながら. Standing is not an activity you keep performing - you stand up once and then you are standing - so it takes まま. That is why ❌立たちながら読よむ sounds wrong to a native ear, and why ❌聞きいたまま宿題しゅくだいをする does too.

A second, purely grammatical difference: ながら needs the same subject for both halves, while まま does not care - 窓まどを開あけたまま雨あめが降ふり出だした is fine.

～まま · ～まま vs ～っぱなし

Rule of thumb - っぱなし is まま with blame attached, and it only attaches to verb stems. まま is neutral and attaches to nouns and adjectives too.

テレビをつけたまま、台所だいどころで洗あらい物ものをしていた。
I was doing the washing-up in the kitchen with the TV left on.

テレビをつけっぱなしにして出でかけるのはやめてほしい。
I wish you'd stop going out and leaving the TV on.

The first simply reports the arrangement; the TV may well have been on for a reason. The second is a complaint about carelessness, and っぱなし is what carries it.

～まま · ～まま vs ～っぱなし (cont.)

There is one thing っぱなし can do that まま cannot: describe an action going on without a break. 立たちっぱなしだった means "I was on my feet the whole time", which is about duration, not about a posture held during some other action. 立たったまま cannot mean that.

～まま · ～ないまま vs ～ないで

Rule of thumb - ないで says you did B instead of A. ないまま says the un-done state of A persisted - usually longer than it should have.

朝あさごはんを食たべないで家いえを出でた。
I left the house without eating breakfast.

朝あさから何なにも食たべないまま、夜よるになってしまった。
I got all the way to evening without having eaten anything since morning.

The first is about one moment: at the point of leaving, breakfast had not happened. The second stretches the emptiness across a whole day and invites you to notice how long it went on. Where a stretch of time is involved - an unread book, an unanswered letter, an unresolved argument - ないまま is the natural choice and carries a faint regret that ないで does not.

～まま · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 立たつまま待まつ → ✅ 立たったまま待まつ
    Verbs take the た-form. The dictionary form before まま is not possible.

  • ❌ 昔むかしままの建物たてもの → ✅ 昔むかしのままの建物たてもの
    A noun needs の to reach まま, exactly as it does to reach any other noun.

  • ❌ 静しずかままだった → ✅ 静しずかなままだった
    A な-adjective keeps its な.

  • ❌ ご飯はんを食たべたまま話はなさないでください。（meaning "don't talk while eating"） → ✅ ご飯はんを食たべながら話はなさないでください。
    Eating is an ongoing action, not a state left behind, so it takes ながら. The た-form version would mean "don't talk in the state of having eaten", which is not a thing.

～まま · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 窓まどを開あけたままする → ✅ 窓まどを開あけたままにする
    To say you are deliberately leaving something in a state, the pattern is ～ままにする or ～ままにしておく.

～まま · Practice 1

1. テレビをつけた____寝ねてしまって、朝あさまで音おとが出でていた。

テレビをつけたまま寝ねてしまって…

The switching-on happened once and its result - the TV being on - carried through the whole night, which is exactly what まま expresses. Note the た-form つけた, even though nothing about the sentence is past-tense in meaning.

～まま · Practice 2

2. Choose: 会議かいぎが長ながくて、後うしろの人ひとたちは（立たったまま・立たちながら）聞きいていた。

後うしろの人ひとたちは**立たったまま**聞きいていた。

Standing is a posture you enter once and then hold, not an activity you keep performing, so it is a state - まま territory. 立たちながら is not used; ながら wants two genuine ongoing actions, like listening and taking notes.

～まま · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Don't leave the door open - just leave it as it is and I'll close it."

ドアを開あけたままにしないで。そのままにしておいてくれれば、私わたしが閉しめます。

Two uses in one answer: 開あけたままにする for deliberately leaving something in a state, and そのままにしておく - the everyday "leave it be" - where そのまま stands in for the whole state.

～まま · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 冷蔵庫れいぞうこに入いれないまま、牛乳ぎゅうにゅうを飲のみながら一晩ひとばん置おいてしまった。

✅ 冷蔵庫れいぞうこに入いれないまま、牛乳ぎゅうにゅうを一晩ひとばん置おいてしまった。

飲のみながら has no business here - nobody is drinking while leaving the milk out, and ながら would need a second ongoing action by the same person. The ないまま clause is correct as it stands: the milk stayed un-refrigerated for a stretch of time.

～まま · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 返事へんじを書かかないで、手紙てがみを引ひき出だしにしまった。 (b) 返事へんじを書かかないまま、手紙てがみを引ひき出だしにしまった。

(a) reports a single choice at a single moment: rather than writing back, I put the letter away. It is a plain statement of what I did instead.

(b) puts weight on the reply never having been written. The un-answered state has been going on and is still going on, and the sentence leans faintly toward regret - the letter went into the drawer and the reply is still owed.

～っぱなし

Meaning · left in that state and never put right; kept going without a break

～っぱなし comes from 放はなす, "to let go", and it keeps the word's flavour. Something was set going or set open and then abandoned - the tap still running, the drawer still hanging out. A second use stretches an action across a whole span of time with no pause: on your feet the entire journey, losing every match for three years.

～っぱなし · Formation

っぱなし attaches to the ます-stem of a verb and produces a noun.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (ます-stem) ます-stem + っぱなし 出だします → 出だしっぱなし
る-verb (ます-stem) ます-stem + っぱなし 開あけます → 開あけっぱなし
Predicate ～っぱなし + だ / です 窓まどが開あけっぱなしだ
Before a noun ～っぱなし + の + Noun つけっぱなしのテレビ
Deliberate / accusing ～っぱなし + に + する 出だしっぱなしにする
Ending up that way ～っぱなし + に + なる つけっぱなしになる

～っぱなし · Formation (cont.)

Note - two families of verb take this suffix and they give the two senses. Transitive verbs that put something into a state give the "left that way" reading: つけっぱなし・開あけっぱなし・出だしっぱなし・脱ぬぎっぱなし・置おきっぱなし. Intransitive or durative verbs give the "without a break" reading: 立たちっぱなし・降ふりっぱなし・鳴なりっぱなし・負まけっぱなし・言いわれっぱなし. Verbs outside those two groups do not take it at all - ❌食たべっぱなし, ❌知しりっぱなし.

～っぱなし · Examples

テレビをつけっぱなしにして寝ねてしまった。
I fell asleep with the TV left on.

水みずを**出だしっぱなし**にしないでね。
Don't leave the tap running.

窓まどが**開あけっぱなし**だったので、部屋へやがすっかり冷ひえていた。
The window had been left open, so the room had gone completely cold.

東京とうきょうから名古屋なごやまで席せきが空あかず、立たちっぱなしだった。
No seat came free from Tokyo to Nagoya, so I was on my feet the whole way.

～っぱなし · Examples (cont.)

昨日きのうは朝あさから電話でんわが**鳴なりっぱなし**で、仕事しごとがまったく進すすまなかった。
The phone rang nonstop from morning yesterday and I got nothing done.

上着うわぎを**脱ぬぎっぱなし**にしておくと、母ははに必かならずしかられる。
If I leave my jacket where I took it off, my mother tells me off without fail.

会議かいぎでは部長ぶちょうが**話はなしっぱなし**で、こちらは一言ひとことも意見いけんを言いえなかった。
The manager talked for the whole meeting and I couldn't get a single word in.

今度こんどの試合しあいこそ勝かちたい。ここ三年さんねん、あのチームには**負まけっぱなし**なのだ。
I really want to win this one. We've lost to that team every single time for three years.

～っぱなし · Nuance & register

The first sense is never neutral. っぱなし is the word you use when you are annoyed, or admitting fault. Saying 電気でんきがつけっぱなしだ is not reporting that a light is on; it is saying somebody left it on and should not have. Parents, flatmates and office managers use it constantly, and it is one of the standard building blocks of Japanese nagging.

The second sense - "without a break" - trades blame for endurance. 立たちっぱなし, 歩あるきっぱなし, 走はしりっぱなし are complaints about being worn out. 負まけっぱなし and 言いわれっぱなし add a note of being on the receiving end and doing nothing about it, which is why 言いわれっぱなしではいられない - "I can't just take this lying down" - is a fixed expression.

～っぱなし · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is casual to neutral. It is perfectly normal at work between colleagues, but in a written report or a formal apology you would step up to something flatter: 電源でんげんを切きり忘わすれておりました rather than つけっぱなしでした. The kanji spelling ～っ放ぱなし exists but is rare; kana is the norm.

Watch the small tsu. 出だす gives 出だしっぱなし, not ❌出だすっぱなし; the っ belongs to the suffix and the stem stays short.

～っぱなし · ～っぱなし vs ～てある

Rule of thumb - てある says somebody did it on purpose and left it that way for a reason. っぱなし says somebody did it and couldn't be bothered to undo it. Same physical scene, opposite verdicts.

空気くうきを入いれかえるため、窓まどが開あけてある。
The window has been left open to air the room out.

誰だれも閉しめないので、窓まどが開あけっぱなしだ。
Nobody ever shuts it, so the window just stays open.

Both sentences describe a window that is open because a person opened it. てある supplies a purpose - and can be followed by one, as here. っぱなし supplies negligence, and it cannot take a purpose clause without sounding sarcastic.

～っぱなし · ～っぱなし vs ～てある (cont.)

This is the single most useful thing to know about the pattern, because the English "left open" covers both and gives you no clue which to choose.

～っぱなし · ～っぱなし vs ～たまま

Rule of thumb - たまま is the neutral version. っぱなし is the same state with a complaint attached, and it is limited to verb stems.

靴くつをはいたまま畳たたみに上あがってはいけない。
You mustn't step onto the tatami with your shoes on.

玄関げんかんに靴くつが**脱ぬぎっぱなし**で、足あしの踏ふみ場ばもない。
Shoes have been kicked off and left all over the entryway - there's nowhere to stand.

まま will also attach to nouns and adjectives（昔むかしのまま, 熱あついまま）, which っぱなし never does.

～っぱなし · ～っぱなし vs ～たまま (cont.)

And only っぱなし has the "without a break" sense. 立たったまま食たべる means eating in a standing posture; 立たちっぱなしだった means being on your feet for hours. Substituting one for the other changes the sentence completely.

～っぱなし · ～っぱなし vs ～ておく

Rule of thumb - ておく is leaving something as it is because that is the useful thing to do. っぱなし is leaving it as it is because nobody thought about it.

午後ごごもまた使つかうので、パソコンはつけておきます。
I'll leave the computer on, since I'll be using it again this afternoon.

三日みっかもつけっぱなしにしていたら、電気代でんきだいが大変たいへんなことになる。
Leave it on for three days and the electricity bill will be horrifying.

Notice that both can appear with the same verb in the same room. The choice is entirely about how the speaker wants the action judged - which makes っぱなし a useful thing to avoid when you are talking about your own deliberate decisions.

～っぱなし · Common mistakes

  • ❌ つけるっぱなし → ✅ つけっぱなし
    The suffix takes the ます-stem. Dictionary form in front of it is not possible.

  • ❌ 立たったっぱなし → ✅ 立たちっぱなし
    The た-form belongs to まま, not to っぱなし.

  • ❌ 開あけっぱなしな窓まど → ✅ 開あけっぱなしの窓まど
    The result is a noun, so a following noun is linked with の.

  • ❌ お客きゃくさんのために、入口いりぐちのドアを開あけっぱなしにしておきました。 → ✅ お客きゃくさんのために、入口いりぐちのドアを開あけておきました。
    You did it on purpose and for a good reason, so ておく. っぱなし would tell your boss you were careless.

～っぱなし · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ ご飯はんを食たべっぱなしで出でかけた → ✅ 食器しょっきを出だしっぱなしにして出でかけた
    Eating leaves no object in a state, so the verb does not take the suffix. Name the thing that was actually left - the dishes.

～っぱなし · Practice 1

1. 昨日きのうはエアコンを____にして出でかけてしまい、一日中いちにちじゅう動うごいていた。

エアコンをつけっぱなしにして出でかけてしまい…

The air conditioner was switched on and then abandoned for the day, which is the core "left that way" sense. ～っぱなしにする is the standard frame when a person is responsible for the state.

～っぱなし · Practice 2

This is the "no break" sense: the rain continued for a whole stretch of time. まま cannot express continuous action - 降ふったまま would try to mean "in the state left behind by raining", which is not a state anyone recognises.

～っぱなし · Practice 3

置おきっぱなし is the everyday way to complain about things left where they were put down. The にする frame makes the listener the one responsible, which is what a request of this kind needs.

～っぱなし · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 冷房れいぼうをつけっぱなしなので、部屋へやが寒さむすぎる。

✅ 冷房れいぼうがつけっぱなしなので、部屋へやが寒さむすぎる。

つけっぱなし describes the state the thing is in, so the thing takes が, not を. を would need a verb - つけっぱなしにしている - because を marks what someone is doing something to.

～っぱなし · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 机つくえの上うえに資料しりょうが置おいてある。 (b) 机つくえの上うえに資料しりょうが**置おきっぱなし**だ。

(a) says the papers were put there deliberately and are meant to stay - for you to read, or for the next meeting. It is neutral and often helpful information.

(b) says somebody dumped them there and never cleared up. The papers are clutter, and the sentence contains a small accusation. Said to the person who left them, it is a request to move them.

～てばかりいる

Meaning · do nothing but ～; be forever ～ing

～てばかりいる says a person spends their time on one activity to the exclusion of everything else - and that the speaker thinks they should be doing something different. It is a complaint dressed as a description of a schedule.

～てばかりいる · Formation

ばかり slots between the て-form and いる. The whole thing is an ordinary る-verb, so it conjugates exactly like いる.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb て-form + ばかり + いる 遊あそぶ → 遊あそんでばかりいる
Polite て-form + ばかり + います 寝ねてばかりいます
Past て-form + ばかり + いた 泣ないてばかりいた
Joining a clause て-form + ばかり + いて / いても 見みてばかりいても
Without いる て-form + ばかり + だ 謝あやまってばかりだ

～てばかりいる · Formation (cont.)

Note - ～てばかりいる and ～てばかりだ are close, and both are common. The version with いる presents the behaviour as going on right now or over a stretch of time; the version with だ is flatter, closer to a verdict on the person. Do not mix them into ❌～てばかりする, which is not a form.

～てばかりいる · Examples

弟おとうとは**遊あそんでばかりいる**。
My little brother does nothing but play.

彼かれは文句もんくを**言いってばかりいて**、自分じぶんでは何なにもしない。
He does nothing but complain and never does anything himself.

泣ないてばかりいても何なにも変かわらないよ。
Crying about it isn't going to change anything.

息子むすこはスマホを**見みてばかりいて**、話はなしかけても返事へんじをしない。
My son is forever staring at his phone and doesn't answer when I speak to him.

～てばかりいる · Examples (cont.)

休やすみの日ひに**寝ねてばかりいる**と、かえって疲つかれが取とれない。
If you do nothing but sleep on your days off, you actually end up less rested.

新あたらしい職場しょくばでは**失敗しっぱいしてばかりで**、毎日まいにち落おち込こんでいる。
At my new job I do nothing but make mistakes, and I'm down about it every day.

昔むかしのことを**思おもい出だしてばかりいて**も前まえに進すすめないので、何なにか新あたらしいことを始はじめたい。
Dwelling on the past won't get me anywhere, so I want to start something new.

部長ぶちょうは会議かいぎで**反対はんたいしてばかりいた**が、自分じぶんの案あんは一ひとつも出ださなかった。
The manager did nothing but object throughout the meeting, yet didn't offer a single idea of his own.

～てばかりいる · Nuance & register

The pattern is almost always critical. Even when the activity is one you would normally praise, てばかりいる turns it into an imbalance: 勉強べんきょうしてばかりいる is not "such a hard worker", it is "he never comes out of his room" - worry, not admiration. If you want the approving version, use a plain progressive: よく勉強べんきょうしています.

Aimed at yourself it becomes self-reproach, and this is extremely common: 謝あやまってばかりですみません, 迷惑めいわくをかけてばかりで. In that use it functions almost as an apology formula.

The casual contraction is ばっかり, and in fast speech it shortens further to ばっか: 遊あそんでばっかりいないで手伝てつだってよ. That is friends and family register; keep ばかり for anything else.

～てばかりいる · Nuance & register (cont.)

One genuine ambiguity to be aware of. ばかり has a second life after the た-form meaning "just did" - 着ついたばかりです, "I've only just arrived". The two are different patterns with nothing in common but the word, and the て-form versus た-form is what tells them apart. Confusing them produces sentences that are grammatical and mean the wrong thing, which is the worst kind of error.

Register is otherwise neutral: てばかりいる is at home in speech, in fiction and in a newspaper column about people who spend too long online.

～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs Noun + ばかり

Rule of thumb - after a noun, ばかり restricts what you do it to. After a て-form, ばかり restricts what you do - the whole activity.

弟おとうとは肉にくばかり食たべて、野菜やさいを残のこす。
My brother eats nothing but meat and leaves the vegetables.

弟おとうとは朝あさから**食たべてばかりいる**。
My brother has done nothing but eat since this morning.

～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs Noun + ばかり (cont.)

In the first, eating is fine; the problem is the menu. In the second, eating itself is the problem - he should be doing something else with his day. The noun version is also neutral enough to be used approvingly（野菜やさいばかり食たべている can be a compliment）, which the て-form version essentially never is.

～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs ～ている

Rule of thumb - ている reports what is happening. てばかりいる reports the same thing plus "and nothing else, and I object".

娘むすめは部屋へやでゲームをしている。
My daughter is playing a game in her room.

娘むすめは部屋へやでゲームをしてばかりいる。
My daughter does nothing but play games in her room.

～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs ～ている (cont.)

ている covers this moment or a neutral habit. てばかりいる needs a stretch of repeated behaviour, so it cannot describe a single instance: pointing at someone right now and saying 見みてばかりいます about one glance is wrong the way "he's forever looking" would be wrong about one look.

～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs ～がち

Rule of thumb - がち says it happens often, leaving room for other things. てばかりいる says it is all that happens.

忙いそがしい時期じきは外食がいしょくが多おおくなりがちだ。
When things get busy I tend to eat out more.

一人ひとり暮ぐらしを始はじめてから、外そとで**食たべてばかりいる**。
Since I started living alone I've done nothing but eat out.

～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs ～がち (cont.)

Both disapprove, but they measure different things - frequency against exclusivity. They also attach differently: がち takes the ます-stem and builds a な-adjective, while てばかりいる takes the て-form and stays a verb. That means only てばかりいる can carry tense and aspect of its own: 食たべてばかりいた, 食たべてばかりいるだろう.

～てばかりいる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 遊あそぶばかりいる → ✅ 遊あそんでばかりいる
    ばかり in this pattern follows the て-form, not the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 食たべてばかりする → ✅ 食たべてばかりいる
    The verb at the end is いる. している belongs to the noun pattern 心配しんぱいばかりしている.

  • ❌ お昼ひるを食たべてばかりいます。（meaning "I've just eaten lunch"） → ✅ お昼ひるを食たべたばかりです。
    "Just did" is た-form + ばかり. The て-form version says you have been eating all day.

  • ❌ 娘むすめは毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうしてばかりいて、えらいですね。 → ✅ 娘むすめさんは毎日まいにちよく練習れんしゅうしていて、えらいですね。
    The pattern carries disapproval, so it cannot be used to praise someone.

～てばかりいる · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 今いま、彼かれはこちらを見みてばかりいます。（about one moment） → ✅ 今いま、彼かれはこちらを見みています。
    てばかりいる needs behaviour repeated over time; it cannot report a single instant.

～てばかりいる · Practice 1

1. 試験しけんが近ちかいのに、兄あにはテレビを見み____いる。

兄あにはテレビを見みてばかりいる。

見みる goes into its て-form 見みて, then ばかり, then いる. The のに in the first half tells you a complaint is coming, which is exactly the tone this pattern supplies.

～てばかりいる · Practice 2

2. Choose: 山田やまださんは（甘あまい物ものばかり食たべている・食たべてばかりいる）ので、歯はが心配しんぱいだ。

The worry is about what he eats, not about him eating instead of doing other things - so ばかり goes after the noun. 食たべてばかりいる would make the eating itself the problem, which has nothing to do with his teeth.

～てばかりいる · Practice 3

いる takes its ordinary negative て-form いないで, which is how you tell someone to stop doing only that one thing. This ～てばかりいないで frame is very common in real speech.

～てばかりいる · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昨日きのう東京とうきょうに着ついてばかりなので、まだ何なにも見みていません。

✅ 昨日きのう東京とうきょうに着ついたばかりなので、まだ何なにも見みていません。

The meaning wanted is "I only arrived yesterday", which is た-form + ばかり. The て-form version would say the speaker has been arriving over and over - the same word, an entirely different pattern.

～てばかりいる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼女かのじょは最近さいきんよく本ほんを読よんでいる。 (b) 彼女かのじょは最近さいきん本ほんを**読よんでばかりいる**。

(a) is a neutral report of a habit, and in most contexts it is mildly positive - she has got into reading lately.

(b) says reading has crowded everything else out. Even though reading is a respectable activity, the pattern makes it sound like an escape or a neglect of something: she is not going out, not studying, not talking to anyone. The speaker is worried or exasperated, and that comes entirely from てばかりいる.

～かけ / ～かける

Meaning · started and not finished; on the point of ～ing

～かけ marks an action that got under way and then stopped short of its end. With an action you chose to start, that means half-done: a half-eaten sandwich, a half-written email. With a change that happens to you or to the world, it means almost happened: nearly fell, nearly forgot, sun starting to set.

～かけ / ～かける · Formation

The suffix has a noun form, かけ, and a verb form, かける (a る-verb, from 掛かける).

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (ます-stem) → noun ます-stem + かけ 読よみます → 読よみかけ
Before a noun ～かけ + の + Noun 読よみかけの本ほん
Predicate ～かけ + だ / です この本ほんは読よみかけだ
Verb (ます-stem) → verb ます-stem + かける 言いいます → 言いいかける
Past - began and stopped ～かけた 言いいかけた
Progressive - is on the verge ～かけている 沈しずみかけている
Before a noun (verb form) ～かけた + Noun 消きえかけた字じ

～かけ / ～かける · Formation (cont.)

Note - the verb has to name something with an endpoint to fall short of. 読よむ, 食たべる, 書かく, 言いう, 落おちる, 消きえる, 沈しずむ all qualify. States and open-ended activities do not: ❌住すみかけ, ❌遊あそびかけ, ❌知しりかけ. Note also that ます-stem + かける has a separate, purely lexical life in words like 話はなしかける ("speak to someone") and 出でかける ("go out") - those are vocabulary, not this pattern.

～かけ / ～かける · Examples

食たべかけのパンがテーブルに置おいてある。
There's a half-eaten piece of bread sitting on the table.

読よみかけの本ほんが何なん冊さつもたまっている。
I've got several books piled up that I started and never finished.

何なにか言いいかけて、彼かれは急きゅうに黙だまってしまった。
He started to say something and then suddenly went quiet.

階段かいだんで転ころびかけたが、手てすりをつかんで助たすかった。
I nearly fell on the stairs but grabbed the handrail and was fine.

～かけ / ～かける · Examples (cont.)

書かきかけのメールを送おくってしまい、あわてて謝あやまった。
I accidentally sent a half-written email and hurried to apologise.

日ひが沈しずみかけているので、そろそろ山やまを下おりましょう。
The sun is starting to go down, so let's head off the mountain.

祖父そふの声こえを忘わすれかけていたことに気きづいて、少すこし悲かなしくなった。
I realised I'd nearly forgotten what my grandfather's voice sounded like, and it made me sad.

やりかけの仕事しごとを机つくえに置おいたまま帰かえってしまったので、朝あさ一番いちばんで片かたづけます。
I went home leaving unfinished work on my desk, so I'll deal with it first thing in the morning.

～かけ / ～かける · Nuance & register

The noun form is a plain, everyday word - you can point at a plate and say 食たべかけ with no more ceremony than saying "leftovers". It does carry a faint sense of someone else's: a 飲のみかけのコーヒー is a cup you would rather not drink from, which is why offering one is slightly awkward.

The verb form is where the two senses separate cleanly, and the split follows the verb. With a volitional verb, かけた means the speaker began and was interrupted: 言いいかけた, 立たち上あがりかけた. With a non-volitional change, it means the process was under way and did not complete - or is still under way: 消きえかけた火ひ, 倒たおれかけた木き, 沈しずみかけている船ふね. Fiction leans on the second heavily.

～かけ / ～かける · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral throughout. Nothing about かけ is casual or formal; it turns up in conversation, in instructions and in literature alike.

One shape worth memorising because it is so useful: 死しにかける → 死しにかけた, "came close to dying". It is the standard way to say a near-miss with something serious, and it is much more common than any construction with "almost".

～かけ / ～かける · ～かけ vs ～ている途中

Rule of thumb - 途中とちゅう means you are in the middle of it right now. かけ means you stopped partway and are not doing it at the moment.

今いま読よんでいる**途中とちゅう**なので、あとで話はなしてもいいですか。
I'm in the middle of reading it, so can we talk later?

去年きょねん買かった小説しょうせつが、まだ**読よみかけ**のままだ。
The novel I bought last year is still sitting there half-read.

The first sentence has a person actively engaged. The second has a book on a shelf and nobody reading it. This is why かけ so often appears with まま and そのまま - the state has been abandoned and left standing.

～かけ / ～かける · ～かける vs ～そうだ

Rule of thumb - そうだ predicts from appearances: it looks like it will happen. かける reports that the process actually began.

棚たなの本ほんが落おちそうだから、少すこし奥おくに入いれて。
The books on the shelf look like they're going to fall - push them back a bit.

棚たなの本ほんが落おちかけたので、あわてて手てを出だした。
The books started to fall, so I grabbed at them.

Nothing has moved in the first sentence; it is a judgement about a risk. In the second, the books were on their way down. Learners who reach for かける when they mean "looks like" end up reporting events that never occurred.

～かけ / ～かける · ～かけ vs ～たばかり

Rule of thumb - opposite ends of the same action. かけ = begun, not finished. たばかり = finished a moment ago.

そのケーキ、まだ**食たべかけ**だから捨すてないで。
Don't throw that cake out - I haven't finished it.

ケーキなら、さっき**食たべたばかり**です。
If you mean cake, I've only just had some.

The forms make the difference visible: かけ sits on the ます-stem, ばかり in this sense sits on the た-form. If you can see a た, the action is over.

～かけ / ～かける · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 読よむかけの本ほん → ✅ 読よみかけの本ほん
    かけ attaches to the ます-stem.

  • ❌ 読よみかけな本ほん → ✅ 読よみかけの本ほん
    The noun form links to a following noun with の.

  • ❌ もう少すこしで事故じこにあいかけだった。 → ✅ もう少すこしで事故じこにあいかけた。
    The bare noun かけ is for objects left unfinished. The "on the verge" sense needs the verb かける, conjugated.

  • ❌ 昼ひるごはんは食たべかけです。（meaning "I've just eaten"） → ✅ 昼ひるごはんは食たべたばかりです。
    食たべかけ means the plate is still half full.

～かけ / ～かける · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 三年さんねんこの町まちに**住すみかけている**。 → ✅ 三年さんねんこの町まちに住すんでいる。
    住すむ has no endpoint to stop short of, so it does not take かける at all.

～かけ / ～かける · Practice 1

1. 机つくえの上うえに、書かき____の手紙てがみが置おいてあった。

The letter was started and abandoned, which is the noun form on the ます-stem, linked to 手紙てがみ with の. 書かきかけ is one of the commonest examples of this pattern.

～かけ / ～かける · Practice 2

2. Choose: 自転車じてんしゃが倒たおれ（そうだった・かけた）ので、手てで支ささえた。

The second half says the speaker physically caught it, so the bike must already have begun to go over - that is かける. 倒たおれそうだった would only say it looked unstable, and would not explain the sudden catch.

～かけ / ～かける · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Please don't leave your food half-eaten."

食たべかけ is the standard noun for food left partway through, and it joins the following clause with で exactly as any noun does. You could also say 食たべかけのまま残のこさないで, which adds the sense of the plate being left standing.

～かけ / ～かける · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼かれは何なにか言いいかけのに、途中とちゅうでやめてしまった。

An action that began and was cut short needs the verb かける, past tense かけた, before のに. The bare noun かけ cannot take のに directly and would in any case describe an object, not an event.

～かけ / ～かける · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 火ひが消きえそうだ。 (b) 火ひが消きえかけている。

(a) is a prediction from how the fire looks - small, low, unsteady. It might still recover, and the speaker is judging from the outside.

(b) says the going-out has already started: the flame is in the process of dying, and without intervention it will finish. It reports a change in progress rather than a risk, which makes it the more urgent of the two.

～たて

Meaning · freshly ～ed; only just ～ed

～たて says a thing came out of some process moments ago and is still in that condition. Bread out of the oven, rice out of the cooker, paint still wet, a graduate still blinking at the world. The freshness is presented as a quality of the thing, and it is a quality that will fade.

～たて · Formation

たて attaches to the ます-stem and produces a noun.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (ます-stem) ます-stem + たて 焼やきます → 焼やきたて
する-verb Noun + し + たて 卒業そつぎょうします → 卒業そつぎょうしたて
Before a noun ～たて + の + Noun 焼やきたてのパン
Predicate ～たて + だ / です ご飯はんは炊たきたてです
Joining a clause ～たて + で 習ならいたてで、まだ下手へただ

～たて · Formation (cont.)

Note - this is the most lexically restricted suffix in the group. Two small families use it and nothing else does. Things coming out of a process: 焼やきたて・揚あげたて・炊たきたて・入いれたて・塗ぬりたて・できたて・とれたて・生うまれたて. People who have just entered a status: 習ならいたて・覚おぼえたて・卒業そつぎょうしたて・入社にゅうしゃしたて・なりたて. Outside those, it simply is not said: ❌買かいたて, ❌読よみたて, ❌見みたて. (The existing words 見立みたて, 仕立したて and 組くみ立たて are separate vocabulary, not this suffix.)

～たて · Examples

焼やきたてのパンはやっぱりおいしい。
Freshly baked bread really is delicious.

ご飯はんは**炊たきたて**が一番いちばんだ。
Rice is best straight out of the cooker.

ペンキ**塗ぬりたて**なので、さわらないでください。
The paint is still wet, so please don't touch.

市場いちばでとれたての魚さかなを買かって帰かえった。
I bought some freshly caught fish at the market and took it home.

～たて · Examples (cont.)

入いれたてのコーヒーのにおいで目めが覚さめた。
The smell of freshly brewed coffee woke me up.

彼かれは運転うんてん免許めんきょをとりたてで、まだ高速こうそく道路どうろが怖こわいらしい。
He's only just got his driving licence and is apparently still nervous of the motorway.

覚おぼえたての敬語けいごを使つかおうとして、かえって変へんな日本語にほんごになってしまった。
I tried to use the keigo I'd just learned and ended up with odder Japanese than before.

この店みせは**揚あげたて**のコロッケを一ひとつずつ紙かみに包つつんでくれるので、帰かえり道みちでつい食たべてしまう。
This shop wraps each freshly fried croquette in paper for you, so I always end up eating one on the way home.

～たて · Nuance & register

たて is a warm, appetising word, and it is the only suffix in this group that is reliably positive. Shops and menus lean on it - 焼やきたてパン, できたてのお弁当べんとう - because it promises the best version of the thing. The two combinations 焼やきたて and できたて are so entrenched that they function as ordinary nouns.

The freshness has a short shelf life built in, and this is what makes it different from "new". Bread is 焼やきたて for an hour, not a week. A person is 卒業そつぎょうしたて for a few months. If the interval you mean is long, the word is wrong.

～たて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Applied to people, the tone is affectionate but a little indulgent: 新入社員しんにゅうしゃいんなりたて and 習ならいたて both carry "and it shows". It is not an insult, but it is not a compliment on competence either.

There is no register restriction - 塗ぬりたて appears on public signs, 揚あげたて on menus, 覚おぼえたて in casual conversation. Some combinations take the kanji 立たて in writing（焼やき立たて）, but kana is far more usual.

～たて · ～たて vs ～たばかり

Rule of thumb - たばかり works with any verb and measures time since an event. たて works with a short fixed list and describes the thing as being in a fresh condition.

この車くるまは先週せんしゅう買かったばかりだ。
I only bought this car last week.

朝あさ六時ろくじの店みせには、焼やきたてのパンが並ならんでいた。
At six in the morning the shop had freshly baked bread lined up.

～たて · ～たて vs ～たばかり (cont.)

Where both are available, they say slightly different things. 焼やいたばかりのパン tells you when the baking happened; 焼やきたてのパン tells you what the bread is like. The second is what you want on a sign, and the first is what you want in a sentence about your morning.

The safe rule for production: if the combination is not one of the fixed set, use たばかり. ❌買かいたての車くるま is not Japanese; 買かったばかりの車くるま is.

～たて · ～たて vs 新しい

Rule of thumb - 新あたらしい means not old. たて means straight out of the process that made it, with the heat still in it.

新あたらしいパンを二ふたつください。
Two of the fresh loaves, please.

昼ひるすぎに行いくと、もう**焼やきたて**は残のこっていない。
If you go after midday, there's nothing left that's just out of the oven.

～たて · ～たて vs 新しい (cont.)

新あたらしい covers anything baked today, and it would still be true tomorrow morning relative to last week's loaf. たて is a claim about minutes. This is also why 新あたらしい can describe things that were never made - 新あたらしい山やまの道みち - while たて needs a process and an agent.

～たて · ～たて vs ～かけ

Rule of thumb - the two ends of one action. かけ = begun and abandoned partway; たて = just completed.

飲のみかけのお茶ちゃが、まだテーブルに残のこっていた。
There was a half-drunk cup of tea still on the table.

入いれたてのお茶ちゃを、来客らいきゃくに出だした。
I served the visitors tea I'd just made.

～たて · ～たて vs ～かけ (cont.)

Structurally they are twins: both sit on the ます-stem, both produce a noun, both take の before a following noun. The difference is entirely one of where in the action they point - and, as a result, of mood. A 飲のみかけ cup is a small domestic mess; an 入いれたて cup is hospitality.

～たて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 焼やくたてのパン → ✅ 焼やきたてのパン
    たて attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

  • ❌ 焼やきたてなパン → ✅ 焼やきたてのパン
    The result is a noun and links with の.

  • ❌ 買かいたてのパソコン → ✅ 買かったばかりのパソコン
    Buying is not a process that leaves a thing freshly made, and 買かいたて is not used. The productive pattern is たばかり.

  • ❌ 去年きょねん卒業そつぎょうしたてなので、まだ仕事しごとに慣なれていません。 → ✅ 先月せんげつ卒業そつぎょうしたてなので、まだ仕事しごとに慣なれていません。
    The freshness must be genuinely recent. A year is too long for たて; use 去年きょねん卒業そつぎょうしたばかり if you really mean a year ago.

～たて · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この服ふくは洗あらいたてで古ふるいです。 → ✅ この服ふくは洗あらいたてでいいにおいがします。
    たて is a positive word about a fresh state. Pairing it with a negative assessment of the same thing pulls the sentence in two directions.

～たて · Practice 1

1. 駅前えきまえのパン屋やでは、夕方ゆうがたに____のパンが出でる。

夕方ゆうがたに**焼やきたて**のパンが出でる。

焼やく gives the stem 焼やき, and 焼やきたて is the most standard member of this small family. Note the の linking it to パン.

～たて · Practice 2

2. Choose: 山田やまださんは（入社にゅうしゃしたて・入社にゅうしゃしかけ）なので、まだ社内しゃないのことがよく分わかっていない。

山田やまださんは**入社にゅうしゃしたて**なので…

He has joined - the process is complete and he is fresh in the role, which is たて. 入社にゅうしゃしかけ would mean he started joining and stopped partway, which is not a situation that exists.

～たて · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Careful - the bench is freshly painted."

気きをつけて。ベンチはペンキ**塗ぬりたて**ですよ。

ペンキ塗ぬりたて is the set phrase you will actually see on signs in Japan. The result is a noun, so です attaches to it directly with no の and no な.

～たて · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 去年きょねん買かいたてのカメラが、もう動うごかなくなった。

✅ 去年きょねん買かったばかりのカメラが、もう動うごかなくなった。

Two things are wrong. 買かう is not one of the verbs that takes たて, and a year ago is far too long for the freshness たて claims. たばかり fixes both - it is productive and it can stretch to a year when the speaker feels it was recent.

～たて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) できたてのカレーを出だしてくれた。 (b) 作つくったばかりのカレーを出だしてくれた。

(a) presents the curry as being in its best possible state - hot, just off the stove, the way a restaurant would advertise it. The focus is on what the food is like now, and the sentence carries a small note of pleasure.

(b) simply reports when it was made: not long before it was served. It is the more neutral, factual phrasing, and it would fit equally well in a sentence explaining why there was none left over.

～きる

Meaning · do all of it, right to the end; be completely in a state

～きる (from 切きる, "to cut") takes a verb and adds completion with nothing left over. Not merely finishing, but getting through the whole quantity: the last page, the last spoonful, the last yen. A second use pushes a state to its limit - utterly exhausted, thoroughly cold, perfectly obvious.

～きる · Formation

きる attaches to the ます-stem and produces a verb. Because it is 切きる, it conjugates as an う-verb (a Group 1 / 五段ごだん verb), despite ending in -る.

～きる · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (ます-stem) ます-stem + きる 食たべます → 食たべきる
する-verb Noun + し + きる 整理せいりします → 整理せいりしきる
Past ～きった 読よみきった
て-form ～きって 使つかいきって
Negative (う-verb!) ～きらない 食たべきらない
Potential ～きれる / ～きれない 食たべきれない
State pushed to its limit ～きった + Noun 疲つかれきった顔かお

～きる · Formation (cont.)

Note - the completion sense needs a quantity or a course to run to the end of: a plateful, a book, a savings account, a race. The intensifying sense needs a verb of change of state: 疲つかれる, 冷ひえる, 弱よわる, 澄すむ, 困こまる. Verbs with neither - ❌見みきる in the sense "look completely", ❌住すみきる - do not take it.

～きる · Examples

大おおきなカレーを一人ひとりで**食たべきった**。
I finished off a huge plate of curry by myself.

今月こんげつのお金かねを、もう**使つかいきって**しまった。
I've already spent all of this month's money.

長ながい小説しょうせつを三日みっかで**読よみきった**。
I got through a long novel in three days.

最後さいごまで**走はしりきった**選手せんしゅに、大おおきな拍手はくしゅが送おくられた。
The runners who made it all the way to the finish got a big round of applause.

～きる · Examples (cont.)

引ひっ越こしの片かたづけで**疲つかれきって**、夕ゆうご飯はんも食たべずに寝ねた。
I was completely worn out from unpacking after the move and went to bed without dinner.

そんなことは**分わかりきった**話はなしだから、わざわざ言いわなくていい。
That's blindingly obvious, so there's no need to spell it out.

冬ふゆの朝あさ、冷ひえきった部屋へやでストーブをつけるのは、なかなかつらい。
On winter mornings, lighting the heater in a room that's gone stone cold is pretty grim.

三年さんねん分ぶんの資料しりょうを一いっ週間しゅうかんで**整理せいりしきる**のは、さすがに無理むりだと思おもいます。
Getting three years of documents completely sorted in one week honestly isn't going to happen.

～きる · Nuance & register

The completion sense carries a note of achievement or effort. 読よみきった is not a neutral "I finished reading"; it says the book was long and you got to the end of it. This is why the pattern is common in sports reporting and in talking about anything demanding - 走はしりきる, やりきる, 戦たたかいきる.

The intensifying sense produces a small set of very common ～きった adjectives. 疲つかれきった, 困こまりきった, 冷ひえきった, 澄すみきった空そら, 分わかりきったこと. These feel a shade literary before a noun and are entirely ordinary in speech as predicates （もう疲つかれきっています）.

～きる · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral. Several combinations have hardened into everyday vocabulary and no longer feel like grammar: 売うり切きれる ("sell out"), 締しめ切きり ("deadline"), 思おもい切きって ("taking the plunge"). You already know those as words; this page is about the productive pattern behind them.

The conjugation trap is worth stating plainly. 切きる is an う-verb, so the negative is 食たべきらない, not ❌食たべきない, and the polite past is 食たべきりました. Learners who see the -る and treat it as a る-verb get every form wrong at once.

～きる · ～きる vs ～てしまう

Rule of thumb - てしまう is about the action being over and irreversible, often with regret. きる is about the quantity being exhausted, often with satisfaction.

冷蔵庫れいぞうこのケーキを食たべてしまった。
I've gone and eaten the cake that was in the fridge.

大おおきなケーキだったが、みんなで食たべきった。
It was a big cake, but between us we finished the lot.

～きる · ～きる vs ～てしまう (cont.)

The first invites "…and I shouldn't have". The second invites "…and we're rather pleased". They stack, too, and the combination is very common: 使つかいきってしまった - used it all up, and now there is none left.

～きる · ～きる vs ～終わる

Rule of thumb - 終おわる means the activity stopped because you reached the end of it. きる means the whole amount was got through.

昨日きのうやっとレポートを書かき**終おわった**。
I finally finished writing the report yesterday.

三さん時間じかんかけて、残のこっていた問題もんだいを全部ぜんぶやりきった。
I spent three hours on it and got through every one of the remaining questions.

～きる · ～きる vs ～終わる (cont.)

終おわる is the neutral, always-available choice and attaches to almost any verb. きる adds difficulty and completeness, and it needs something countable to be complete of. That is why 書かき終おわる is unremarkable while 書かききる sounds like the report nearly defeated you.

～きる · ～きる vs ～抜く

Rule of thumb - both mean "to the very end". きる stresses that nothing was left; 抜ぬく stresses that you kept going despite hardship.

苦くるしかったが、最後さいごまで**走はしりきった**。
It hurt, but I made it all the way to the end.

どんなに反対はんたいされても、最後さいごまでやり**抜ぬく**つもりだ。
However much people object, I intend to see it through to the end.

～きる · ～きる vs ～抜く (cont.)

With a race, either works and they shade differently: 走はしりきる is about covering the whole distance, 走はしり抜ぬく about enduring it. With a quantity - money, food, paper - only きる fits, because there is nothing to endure. 抜ぬく belongs to a later level, but the pair is worth seeing now so that きる does not get stretched to cover perseverance.

～きる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 食たべるきる → ✅ 食たべきる
    きる attaches to the ます-stem.

  • ❌ 食たべきない → ✅ 食たべきらない
    切きる is an う-verb, so its negative is きらない. The same goes for きります, きって, きった.

  • ❌ 財布さいふをなくしきった。 → ✅ 財布さいふをなくしてしまった。
    A wallet is one object, not a quantity to work through, so there is nothing for きる to exhaust. Regret over a single completed action is てしまう.

  • ❌ この町まちに三十年さんじゅうねん住すみきった。 → ✅ この町まちに三十年さんじゅうねん住すんでいる。
    住すむ has no endpoint, so it takes neither sense of きる.

～きる · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 疲つかれきる顔かお → ✅ 疲つかれきった顔かお
    In the intensifying sense, the state has already been reached, so the form before a noun is the past きった.

～きる · Practice 1

1. 冷蔵庫れいぞうこに残のこっていた野菜やさいを、全部ぜんぶスープに入いれて使つかい____。

使つかいきった。

The point is that every last vegetable went into the pot - a quantity worked through to nothing, which is exactly what きる marks. The past is きった, formed as for any う-verb ending in -る.

～きる · Practice 2

2. Choose: 昨日きのうのレポートは夜中よなかに（書かき終おわった・書かききった）ので、今日きょうは出だせます。

昨日きのうのレポートは夜中よなかに**書かき終おわった**ので、今日きょうは出だせます。

The sentence is a plain report that the writing reached its end, and 書かき終おわる is the neutral way to say that. 書かききった would add a note of struggle that the matter-of-fact second half does not support.

～きる · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "By the time the match ended, every player was completely worn out."

試合しあいが終おわるころには、選手せんしゅは全員ぜんいん疲つかれきっていた。

This is the intensifying sense: the tiredness has been pushed to its limit. 疲つかれきっていた is much stronger than 疲つかれていた, and it is the standard way to say "utterly exhausted".

～きる · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この量りょうなら、一人ひとりでも食たべきないことはないと思おもう。

✅ この量りょうなら、一人ひとりでも食たべきらないことはないと思おもう。

きる is 切きる, an う-verb, so its plain negative is きらない. Treating it as a る-verb and writing きない is the single commonest error with this pattern.

～きる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) ビールを全部ぜんぶ飲のんでしまった。 (b) ビールを全部ぜんぶ飲のみきった。

(a) reports that the beer is gone and colours it with the speaker's feeling about that - most often "oops", sometimes just finality. The focus is on the situation now: there is none left.

(b) focuses on the drinking having covered the whole amount, and it carries a hint of effort or of a job done. It is what you would say about finishing a large crate between friends, not about carelessly emptying the fridge.

～きれない

Meaning · there is too much of it to get through; more than one can manage

～きれない is the negative potential of 切きる in its "do it all" sense. It does not say you are incapable of the action - it says the amount defeats you. You can eat; there is simply more food than you can eat. You can count; there are simply more stars than you can count.

～きれない · Formation

きれない attaches to the ます-stem. It is already a negative potential, so it never takes a second potential ending.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (ます-stem) ます-stem + きれない 食たべます → 食たべきれない
する-verb Noun + し + きれない 説明せつめいします → 説明せつめいしきれない
Polite ～きれません 覚おぼえきれません
Past ～きれなかった 読よみきれなかった
Before a noun (with ほど/くらい) ～きれない + ほど / くらい + の 数かぞえきれないほどの星ほし
Affirmative counterpart ～きれる 一人ひとりで運はこびきれる
Literary negative て-form ～きれず(に) 待まちきれずに出でかけた

～きれない · Formation (cont.)

Note - the affirmative きれる exists but is far less common than the negative. When you mean "I managed to finish it", the ordinary 食たべきった is what people actually say; 食たべきれた is reserved for contexts where the capacity is the point（一人ひとりでも食たべきれる量りょうです）. The pattern is lopsided, and it is worth learning it as a negative first.

～きれない · Examples

料理りょうりが多おおすぎて、食たべきれない。
There's too much food - I can't finish it.

漢字かんじが多おおくて、一日いちにちでは**覚おぼえきれない**。
There are so many kanji that I can't learn them all in one day.

空そらには**数かぞえきれない**ほどの星ほしが光ひかっていた。
There were countless stars shining in the sky.

来週らいしゅうの旅行りょこうが楽たのしみで、待まちきれない。
I'm so looking forward to next week's trip that I can hardly wait.

～きれない · Examples (cont.)

荷物にもつが多おおくて一度いちどでは**運はこびきれない**ので、二回にかいに分わけます。
There's too much to carry in one trip, so I'll split it into two.

お世話せわになった方々かたがたへの感謝かんしゃは、言葉ことばでは**伝つたえきれません**。
I can't put into words how grateful I am to everyone who looked after me.

資料しりょうが多おおすぎて会議かいぎまでに**読よみきれなかった**ので、要点ようてんだけまとめてもらった。
There was too much material to get through before the meeting, so I had someone summarise the key points.

～きれない · Examples (cont.)

一人ひとりでは**抱かかえきれない**問題もんだいを、何年なんねんも誰だれにも言いわずにいたそうだ。
Apparently he kept a problem too big for one person to handle to himself for years, without telling anyone.

～きれない · Nuance & register

The core meaning is capacity overrun, and it is the amount that overruns, not your skill. That distinction does real work in a sentence: 読よみきれない means the book was too long, while 読よめない would mean you cannot read it at all - bad eyesight, wrong language, handwriting you cannot decipher. Learners who use きれない for lack of ability end up claiming that a two-line note is too long for them.

きれない is register-neutral and it turns up constantly in one particular shape: ～きれないほど, "so many/much that you couldn't ～". 数かぞえきれないほど is the standard Japanese for "countless", and 食たべきれないほどの料理りょうり is what you say about a table that groans.

～きれない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two idiomatic uses do not follow the "too much" template, and both are worth knowing. 待まちきれない is eager anticipation - the waiting exceeds what you can bear - and it is a warm, positive expression. 信しんじきれない and 割わりきれない (in its emotional sense) describe a feeling you cannot fully settle into; they are more literary.

In writing you will also meet the て-form きれず(に): 我慢がまんしきれずに笑わらってしまった. That is standard written Japanese and sounds slightly formal in speech.

～きれない · ～きれない vs the plain potential negative

Rule of thumb - られない denies the ability. きれない grants the ability and denies that it is enough for the quantity.

辛からい物ものが苦手にがてなので、このカレーは**食たべられない**。
I'm no good with spicy food, so I can't eat this curry.

おいしいけれど量りょうが多おおくて、このカレーは**食たべきれない**。
It's delicious, but there's so much of it that I can't finish this curry.

In the first sentence the speaker cannot eat one spoonful. In the second they have been eating happily and have simply run out of room. Notice that きれない presupposes the action is under way or possible - which is why it fits so naturally with a half-empty plate.

～きれない · ～きれない vs ～すぎる

Rule of thumb - すぎる describes the thing: there is too much of it. きれない describes the person: they cannot get to the end of it.

今日きょうの宿題しゅくだいは**多おおすぎる**。
There's far too much homework today.

今日きょうの宿題しゅくだいは夜よるまでにやりきれない。
I won't get through today's homework by tonight.

～きれない · ～きれない vs ～すぎる (cont.)

They are two views of the same problem and they combine constantly - the commonest frame in the whole pattern is ～すぎて～きれない: 多おおすぎて食たべきれない. Using both is not redundant; the first names the cause and the second the consequence.

～きれない · ～きれない vs ～きった

Rule of thumb - these are the two halves of one pattern. きった = got through the whole lot. きれない = could not.

大おおきなピザだったが、三人さんにんで**食たべきった**。
It was a big pizza, but the three of us polished it off.

大おおきなピザで、二人ふたりでは**食たべきれなかった**。
It was a big pizza, and the two of us couldn't finish it.

～きれない · ～きれない vs ～きった (cont.)

The asymmetry to remember is that the affirmative side normally uses the plain form 食たべきる / 食たべきった, not the potential 食たべきれる, while the negative side almost always uses the potential 食たべきれない rather than the plain 食たべきらない. Both plain negatives exist, but they sound deliberate - "doesn't finish it" rather than "can't finish it".

～きれない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 食たべるきれない → ✅ 食たべきれない
    The suffix goes on the ます-stem.

  • ❌ 食たべきれられない → ✅ 食たべきれない
    きれない is already the potential negative. Adding another potential ending doubles it.

  • ❌ この字じは小ちいさくて**読よみきれない**。 → ✅ この字じは小ちいさくて**読よめない**。
    Small print is a problem of ability, not of quantity. きれない needs an amount to fall short of.

  • ❌ 数かぞえきれないの星ほし → ✅ 数かぞえきれないほどの星ほし
    きれない is a negative い-adjective form, so it modifies a noun directly（数かぞえきれない星ほし）or through ほどの. It never takes a bare の.

～きれない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 来週らいしゅうのライブが**待まちきれる**! → ✅ 来週らいしゅうのライブが**待まちきれない**!
    The excited "I can't wait!" is the negative. The affirmative would mean you are perfectly capable of waiting, which rather kills the mood.

～きれない · Practice 1

1. パーティーの料理りょうりが多おおすぎて、全部ぜんぶは食たべ____。

全部ぜんぶは食たべきれなかった。

多おおすぎて names the cause and きれない names the consequence - the standard pairing. The past きれなかった is needed because the party is over.

～きれない · Practice 2

2. Choose: 目めが悪わるくて、遠とおくの字じが（読よめない・読よみきれない）。

目めが悪わるくて、遠とおくの字じが**読よめない**。

Poor eyesight removes the ability altogether, which is the plain potential negative. きれない would claim that the text is too long to finish, and it says nothing about eyes.

～きれない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I got so many messages that I couldn't reply to them all."

メッセージが多おおすぎて、全部ぜんぶには**返事へんじしきれなかった**。

返事へんじする is a する-verb, so the stem is 返事へんじし. The sentence is about volume defeating you rather than about not knowing how to reply, which is precisely きれない.

～きれない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: おみやげが多おおくて、一いっ回かいでは持もちきれられませんでした。

✅ おみやげが多おおくて、一いっ回かいでは持もちきれませんでした。

きれる already carries the potential meaning, so きれられる stacks two potentials. The polite negative past is simply きれませんでした.

～きれない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この本ほんは一日いちにちでは読よめない。 (b) この本ほんは一日いちにちでは読よみきれない。

(a) says reading it in a day is beyond you - which could be because it is long, but could equally be because it is in a language you do not have, or too difficult. The sentence does not say which.

(b) narrows it to volume. You can read the book perfectly well; there are just more pages than fit into a day. It implicitly grants that you would get through it given longer, which (a) does not.

～がたい

Meaning · hard to bring oneself to ～; almost impossible to ～

～がたい (from 難かたい, voiced to がたい as a suffix) marks a difficulty that lives in the mind, not in the hands. Something is hard to believe, hard to accept, hard to part with, hard to put into words. It is not about a task being physically awkward - for that Japanese has a different suffix entirely.

～がたい · Formation

がたい attaches to the ます-stem and produces an い-adjective: 信しんじがたい → 信しんじがたかった → 信しんじがたく → no だ.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (ます-stem) ます-stem + がたい 信しんじます → 信しんじがたい
する-verb Noun + し + がたい 理解りかいします → 理解りかいしがたい
Before a noun ～がたい + Noun 忘わすれがたい思おもい出で
Past ～がたかった 受うけ入いれがたかった
Polite ～がたいです 信しんじがたいです
Adverbial ～がたく 捨すてがたく思おもう

～がたい · Formation (cont.)

Note - this suffix is not productive. It attaches to a short list of verbs of thinking, feeling, saying and judging: 信しんじる・理解りかいする・想像そうぞうする・忘わすれる・捨すてる・言いう・認みとめる・近寄ちかよる・動うごかす・得える・耐たえる. Everyday physical verbs do not take it: ❌開あけがたい, ❌歩あるきがたい, ❌食たべがたい. If you are unsure whether a combination exists, it almost certainly does not - use にくい.

～がたい · Examples

あの人ひとの話はなしは**信しんじがたい**。
His story is hard to believe.

一年間いちねんかんの留学りゅうがくは**忘わすれがたい**経験けいけんになった。
The year abroad turned into an experience I'll never forget.

どちらの案あんも**捨すてがたい**ので、もう少すこし考かんがえさせてください。
Both proposals have something going for them, so let me think a bit longer.

なぜ彼かれがあんな嘘うそをついたのか、どうしても**理解りかいしがたい**。
I really cannot understand why he told a lie like that.

～がたい · Examples (cont.)

部長ぶちょうは少すこし**近寄ちかよりがたい**雰囲気ふんいきがあるが、話はなしてみると優やさしい人ひとだ。
The manager comes across as a bit unapproachable, but he's kind once you talk to him.

今いまの段階だんかいでは、この計画けいかくが成功せいこうしたとは**言いいがたい**。
At this stage it would be hard to call the project a success.

信頼しんらいできる同僚どうりょうは**得えがたい**存在そんざいだと、転職てんしょくして初はじめて気きづいた。
It took changing jobs for me to see how rare a colleague you can trust really is.

数字すうじがこれだけそろっているのだから、この結論けつろんは**動うごかしがたい**だろう。
With this many figures lined up, this conclusion is going to be hard to shift.

～がたい · Nuance & register

がたい is noticeably formal. It belongs to written Japanese, prepared speech, news copy and careful argument. In everyday conversation the same idea comes out as 信しんじられない, ちょっとわからない, 捨すてるのはもったいない. Saying 信しんじがたいですね in a chat with a friend is not wrong, but it lands like "that strains credulity" in English - correct, and a little theatrical.

The kind of difficulty it names is psychological or principled. A conclusion that is 動うごかしがたい is not heavy; it is well supported. A person who is 近寄ちかよりがたい is not physically distant; they are intimidating. Note that this is a comment on how they come across and not necessarily a criticism - 近寄ちかよりがたい often shades into "commands respect".

～がたい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The suffix is also useful for hedged negatives, and this is where business Japanese likes it. 成功せいこうしたとは言いいがたい means "I would not go so far as to call it a success" - a way of saying it failed without saying it failed. 賛成さんせいしがたい softens a refusal in the same way.

Two lexical footnotes. ありがたい ("grateful, welcome") comes from this suffix historically but is now simply a word, and it is not built the same way. And the reading voices: がたい, not かたい. 固かたい and 硬かたい are unrelated adjectives about hardness.

～がたい · ～がたい vs ～にくい

Rule of thumb - にくい is practical difficulty and attaches to almost any verb. がたい is psychological or moral difficulty and attaches to a short fixed list. When in doubt, にくい is nearly always the right answer.

この靴くつは小ちいさすぎて**歩あるきにくい**。
These shoes are too small and hard to walk in.

事故じこのことは家族かぞくには**言いいがたい**。
It's hard to bring myself to tell my family about the accident.

～がたい · ～がたい vs ～にくい (cont.)

Where a verb takes both, the difference is exactly this. 言いいにくい is awkward to pronounce or awkward to raise - a practical obstacle. 言いいがたい is something you cannot in conscience assert. Likewise 信しんじにくい ("hard to credit, on the evidence") is milder than 信しんじがたい ("beyond belief").

Register follows the same line: にくい is at home everywhere, がたい is formal. Learners producing ❌使つかいがたいアプリ for "an app that's hard to use" are picking the wrong suffix twice over - wrong kind of difficulty and wrong level of formality.

～がたい · ～がたい vs ～ようがない

Rule of thumb - がたい says it is hard and you would rather not. ようがない says there is no method at all, so the question of willingness never arises.

彼かれの言いい訳わけは**信しんじがたい**が、証拠しょうこもない。
His excuse is hard to believe, though there's no evidence either way.

連絡先れんらくさきを知しらないので、知しらせようがない。
I don't have his contact details, so there's no way to let him know.

The first speaker could believe the excuse and is declining to. The second speaker would happily make the call and cannot. That is why がたい combines naturally with が and けれど - it admits the other side - while ようがない tends to close a topic down.

～がたい · ～がたい vs ～られない

Rule of thumb - られない reports that you cannot. がたい reports that it is hard to, leaving the possibility open.

そんな話はなし、とても**信しんじられない**。
I simply can't believe a story like that.

報告ほうこくの内容ないようには、いくつか**信しんじがたい**点てんがある。
There are several points in the report that are hard to credit.

The first is an emotional reaction, spoken, and it slams the door. The second is an assessment, written, and it leaves room for the report to turn out right after all. Choosing がたい where られない was wanted makes you sound as though you are drafting a memo in the middle of an argument; choosing られない in a formal document sounds hot-headed.

～がたい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 信しんじるがたい → ✅ 信しんじがたい
    がたい attaches to the ます-stem.

  • ❌ 忘わすれがたいだ → ✅ 忘わすれがたい
    The result is an い-adjective. The polite form is 忘わすれがたいです.

  • ❌ この席せきは狭せまくて**座すわりがたい**。 → ✅ この席せきは狭せまくて**座すわりにくい**。
    Physical awkwardness is にくい. がたい is for difficulty of the mind.

  • ❌ しんじかたい → ✅ しんじがたい
    The suffix voices. Unvoiced かたい belongs to 固かたい and 硬かたい, which mean "hard" in the physical sense.

～がたい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この漢字かんじは**読よみがたい**。 → ✅ この漢字かんじは**読よみにくい**。
    A character that is hard to make out is a practical problem, and 読よみがたい is not a combination Japanese uses.

～がたい · Practice 1

1. 三十年さんじゅうねん前まえの写真しゃしんを見みると、あの夏なつの旅行りょこうは今いまでも忘わすれ____思おもい出でだ。

忘わすれがたい思おもい出でだ。

忘わすれがたい is one of the fixed combinations, and as an い-adjective it sits directly in front of the noun with no の and no な. It is warmer and more literary than 忘わすれられない.

～がたい · Practice 2

2. Choose: この説明書せつめいしょは字じが小ちいさくて（読よみにくい・読よみがたい）。

この説明書せつめいしょは字じが小ちいさくて**読よみにくい**。

Small print is a practical obstacle, and practical obstacles take にくい. 読よみがたい is not used at all - the verb 読よむ is outside the small set that takes がたい.

～がたい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "At this point it would be hard to say the plan has gone well."

現時点げんじてんでは、計画けいかくがうまくいっているとは**言いいがたい**。

The frame ～とは言いいがたい is the standard formal way to reject a claim without flatly contradicting it, and it is exactly what the hedged English sentence calls for.

～がたい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼かれの態度たいどは理解りかいしがたいだと思おもいます。

✅ 彼かれの態度たいどは理解りかいしがたいと思おもいます。

がたい builds an い-adjective, and い-adjectives take no だ before と思おもう. The だ in the wrong sentence is the same error as ❌高たかいだと思おもう.

～がたい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部長ぶちょうは**近寄ちかよりがたい**人ひとだ。 (b) 部長ぶちょうは話はなしかけにくい人ひとだ。

(a) describes the impression the manager gives off - a certain distance or gravity that makes people hesitate. It is formal in tone and can shade into respect: an imposing person is often described this way.

(b) is a practical complaint from the speaker's side: it is awkward to start a conversation with him, perhaps because he is always busy or always frowning. It is the more everyday phrasing, and it is about the speaker's difficulty rather than about his aura.

～ようがない / ～ようもない

Meaning · there is no way of doing it; no method exists

よう here is 様よう, "manner, way of doing". ～ようがない therefore says that the means are missing: not that you lack the skill, not that you are unwilling, but that there is nothing available to do it with. No address, so no way to send it. No pieces left, so no way to mend it.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Formation

ようがない attaches to the ます-stem and the result is an い-adjective, because it ends in ない.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (ます-stem) ます-stem + ようがない 直なおします → 直なおしようがない
する-verb Noun + し + ようがない 連絡れんらくします → 連絡れんらくしようがない

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Formation (cont.)

Note - ようもない is the same pattern with も in place of が, and the も adds "not even". It is a shade stronger and a shade more literary; everything else is identical. Do not confuse either with the volitional 行いこう, 食たべよう - those come from a different base and cannot take this ending.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Examples

住所じゅうしょが分わからないので、送おくりようがない。
I don't know the address, so there's no way to send it.

こんなに壊こわれていては、直なおしようがない。
Broken like this, there's no fixing it.

電話でんわ番号ばんごうも知しらないから、連絡れんらくしようがない。
I don't even know his phone number, so there's no way to get in touch.

名前なまえが大おおきく書かいてあるので、間違まちがえようがない。
The name is written in big letters, so there's no way you can get it wrong.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Examples (cont.)

本人ほんにんが話はなしてくれない以上いじょう、こちらからは助たすけようがない。
As long as he won't talk to us, there's nothing we can do to help.

材料ざいりょうが何なにもないのだから、作つくりようがないじゃないか。
There aren't any ingredients, so how exactly am I supposed to make anything?

あのときの彼女かのじょの気持きもちは、想像そうぞうしようもない。
I cannot even begin to imagine how she felt at the time.

データがすべて消きえてしまったので、報告書ほうこくしょを書かきようがなく、締しめ切きりを延のばしてもらった。
All the data was wiped, so there was simply no way to write the report and I had the deadline pushed back.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Nuance & register

The feeling is resignation without excuse-making. Because the sentence claims that no method exists, it removes blame from everybody: 直なおしようがない is not "I failed to fix it" but "it was not fixable". That makes the pattern useful for declining responsibility gracefully, and it also makes it a strong claim - if a method did exist, the sentence is simply untrue.

There is a mirror-image use that surprises learners: ようがない on a verb of error or failure produces a guarantee. 間違まちがえようがない means "you couldn't get it wrong if you tried"; 負まけようがない, "there's no way we lose". The logic is the same - no method available - but the effect is confident rather than defeated.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral, leaning slightly written for ようもない. The one thing that is not neutral is the contraction. 仕方しかたがない has a spoken cousin しょうがない, and しょうがない is a contraction of しようがない - so the two patterns share a form. In practice, しょうがない has drifted away into a fixed phrase meaning "oh well, nothing to be done", and it is quite casual; if you mean specifically "there is no way to do it", say しようがない with the full vowel.

Note also that しょうがない人ひと means "hopeless case, incorrigible" - an affectionate insult, not a comment on methods.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · ～ようがない vs the potential negative

Rule of thumb - られない says I can't. ようがない says nobody can, because there is nothing to do it with.

道具どうぐがないので、私わたしには**直なおせない**。
I haven't got the tools, so I can't fix it.

部品ぶひんがもう作つくられていないので、直なおしようがない。
The parts aren't made any more, so it simply can't be repaired.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · ～ようがない vs the potential negative (cont.)

In the first sentence, a repair shop could still do it. In the second, nobody can. This is why ようがない is the right choice when you want to close off the possibility entirely - and the wrong choice when the obstacle is personal. ❌忙いそがしくて行いきようがない is a common learner error; being busy is your circumstance, not an absence of method, so it should be 忙いそがしくて行いけない.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · ～ようがない vs ～がたい

Rule of thumb - がたい is hard; ようがない is impossible. がたい concedes that the thing could be done and says you can hardly bring yourself to; ようがない says the question does not arise.

彼かれの説明せつめいは**理解りかいしがたい**が、一応いちおう聞きいておこう。
His explanation is hard to make sense of, but let's hear him out anyway.

資料しりょうが一いち枚まいもなくては、理解りかいしようがない。
Without a single document to go on, there's no way to understand it.

The give-away is what can follow. がたい sits comfortably before が or けれど, because there is another side. ようがない normally ends the matter.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · ～ようがない vs 仕方がない

Rule of thumb - 仕方しかたがない is about accepting a situation: it can't be helped. ようがない is about an action having no method: there's no way to do it.

電車でんしゃが止とまってしまったのだから、仕方しかたがない。
The trains have stopped, so there's nothing for it.

地図ちずも電波でんぱもないので、道みちを調しらべようがない。
With no map and no signal, there's no way to look up the route.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · ～ようがない vs 仕方がない (cont.)

仕方しかたがない stands alone as a comment on the whole state of affairs and attaches to nothing. ようがない must attach to a verb stem and names a specific action that cannot be carried out. They often appear in the same breath: 調しらべようがないのだから、仕方しかたがない.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 直なおすようがない → ✅ 直なおしようがない
    The suffix takes the ます-stem.

  • ❌ 行いこうがない → ✅ 行いきようがない
    The volitional 行いこう is a different form entirely. よう here is the noun 様よう, and it follows the stem.

  • ❌ 連絡れんらくするようがない → ✅ 連絡れんらくしようがない
    With a する-verb, the stem is し.

  • ❌ 直なおしようがないだ → ✅ 直なおしようがない
    It ends in ない, so it is an い-adjective and takes no だ. Polite is 直なおしようがありません.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 眠ねむくて勉強べんきょうしようがない。 → ✅ 眠ねむくて勉強べんきょうできない。
    Being sleepy is a personal state, not a missing method. ようがない needs the means themselves to be absent.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Practice 1

1. メールアドレスも電話でんわ番号ばんごうも分わからないので、知しらせ____。

知しらせようがない。

Every possible channel is missing, so there is literally no method - which is what ようがない claims. Note the ます-stem 知しらせ, and that the whole thing behaves as an い-adjective.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Practice 2

2. Choose: 説明せつめいが丁寧ていねいすぎるくらいなので、作つくり方かたを（間違まちがえようがない・間違まちがえがたい）。

作つくり方かたを**間違まちがえようがない**。

This is the confident use: the instructions are so thorough that no route to a mistake exists. 間違まちがえがたい is not a combination Japanese uses, and がたい would in any case only mean "hard to", not "impossible".

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The shop has closed down, so there's no way to complain about it now."

店みせがなくなってしまったので、今いまとなっては文句もんくの言いいようがない。

The shop's disappearance removes the means, not the speaker's ability - exactly the distinction the pattern draws. 言いいようがない sits on the ます-stem of 言いう.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 時間じかんがなくて、昨日きのうは返事へんじしようがなかったです。

✅ 時間じかんがなくて、昨日きのうは返事へんじできませんでした。

Lack of time is a personal circumstance, so the potential negative is what is wanted. ようがない would claim that no channel for replying existed at all - which would be true if the phone were dead, but not merely because you were busy.

～ようがない / ～ようもない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この時計とけいは**直なおせない**。 (b) この時計とけいは**直なおしようがない**。

(a) says the speaker cannot repair it. Somebody with more skill, better tools or the right parts might well be able to, and the sentence quietly invites you to take it somewhere else.

(b) closes that door. There is no method - the parts do not exist, or the damage is past any repair - so taking it elsewhere would be pointless. It is a statement about the watch rather than about the speaker.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる

Meaning · four suffixes that bolt a direction, a partner, a second attempt or a habit onto a verb

Japanese builds a great deal of its vocabulary by welding a second verb onto the ます-stem of a first. These four are the ones an N3 learner meets constantly: ～込こむ sends the action inward or deep, ～合あう makes it mutual, ～直なおす makes it a second go, and ～慣なれる says you have done it often enough to be comfortable.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Formation

All four attach to the ます-stem and produce an ordinary verb. 込こむ, 合あう and 直なおす are う-verbs; 慣なれる is a る-verb.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (ます-stem) - inward ます-stem + 込こむ 飛とびます → 飛とび込こむ
Verb (ます-stem) - deeply, at length ます-stem + 込こむ 考かんがえます → 考かんがえ込こむ
Verb (ます-stem) - mutually ます-stem + 合あう 話はなします → 話はなし合あう
Verb (ます-stem) - do over ます-stem + 直なおす 書かきます → 書かき直なおす
Verb (ます-stem) - get used to doing ます-stem + 慣なれる 使つかいます → 使つかい慣なれる
Usual shape of the last one ～慣なれて + いる 使つかい慣なれている
Noun instead of a stem Noun + に + 慣なれる 生活せいかつに慣なれる

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Formation (cont.)

Note - these are word-formation, not free grammar. Each combination is a separate item of vocabulary, and only some combinations exist: 考かんがえ込こむ is a word, ❌走はしり込こむ in the sense "run inward" mostly is not. 込こむ is the least predictable of the four - 使つかい込こむ means both "use for years" and "embezzle" - so treat new combinations as vocabulary to look up rather than forms to generate. 直なおす and 合あう are the two you can most safely build yourself.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Examples

この漢字かんじは間違まちがえたので、書かき直なおします。
I got this kanji wrong, so I'll write it again.

クラスのみんなで**話はなし合あった**。
The whole class talked it over together.

子こどもが水みずたまりに**飛とび込こんだ**。
The child jumped into the puddle.

この道具どうぐは十年じゅうねん使つかっているので、すっかり**使つかい慣なれて**いる。
I've been using this tool for ten years, so it's completely second nature by now.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Examples (cont.)

部長ぶちょうは質問しつもんを聞きいて、しばらく**考かんがえ込こんで**いた。
The manager heard the question and sat there thinking hard for a while.

留学りゅうがくをやめるかどうか、もう一度いちど考かんがえ直なおしてみることにした。
I decided to give the question of quitting the exchange programme another think.

困こまったときに**助たすけ合あえる**友ともだちが近ちかくにいるのは、ありがたいことだ。
It's a blessing to have friends nearby who can help each other out when things go wrong.

東京とうきょうの満員まんいん電車でんしゃにもだんだん**乗のり慣なれて**きて、今いまでは本ほんも読よめる。
I've gradually got used to Tokyo's packed trains, and these days I can even read on them.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Examples (cont.)

駅えきで昔むかしの友人ゆうじんに会あい、喫茶店きっさてんで二に時間じかんも**話はなし込こんで**しまった。
I ran into an old friend at the station and ended up talking for two solid hours in a coffee shop.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Nuance & register

All four are ordinary, register-neutral vocabulary. Nothing about them is formal or casual; what varies is how freely you can invent new combinations.

～直なおす is the most productive and the most useful for a learner. Almost any deliberate action can be redone: 書かき直なおす, 読よみ直なおす, 考かんがえ直なおす, 作つくり直なおす, やり直なおす. It always implies the first attempt was unsatisfactory, which is why it does not work for pleasant repetition - you do not 聞きき直なおす a song you love, you listen again.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Nuance & register (cont.)

～合あう requires two or more parties acting on each other. That is a real constraint: 助たすけ合あう needs both sides helping, and if only one person helps, it is plain 助たすける. This use produces a warm, cooperative tone, which is why 話はなし合あい is the word for a proper discussion rather than an argument.

～込こむ splits into a physical direction（飛とび込こむ, 乗のり込こむ, 流ながれ込こむ）and an intensity or duration sense（考かんがえ込こむ, 話はなし込こむ, 黙だまり込こむ, 教おしえ込こむ）. The second group tends to suggest being absorbed to the point of losing track of the outside world.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Nuance & register (cont.)

～慣なれる almost always appears as ～慣なれている or ～慣なれてくる, because it names a state you have arrived at rather than an event. And keep it apart from its noun-taking twin: 日本にほんの生活せいかつに慣なれる takes a noun and に; 日本語にほんごで話はなし慣なれる takes a stem.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～直す vs もう一度～する

Rule of thumb - もう一度いちど is neutral repetition. ～直なおす says the first attempt was wrong or inadequate and this is the corrected version.

好すきな映画えいがなので、もう一度いちど見みたい。
It's a film I love, so I'd like to watch it again.

名前なまえが抜ぬけていたので、申込書もうしこみしょを**書かき直なおした**。
The name was missing, so I filled the application form in again.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～直す vs もう一度～する (cont.)

Swap them and the meanings shift audibly. 映画えいがを見み直なおす would mean watching it again to check something, or - in another sense - coming to think better of it. もう一度いちど書かく is simply writing a second copy, with no implication that the first was faulty.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～合う vs 一緒に～する

Rule of thumb - ～合あう means the action goes between the participants. 一緒いっしょに means they do it side by side, usually to something or someone else.

三人さんにんで荷物にもつを**一緒いっしょに**運はこんだ。
The three of us carried the luggage together.

困こまっている人ひとを、みんなで**助たすけ合あおう**。
Let's all help each other out when we're in trouble.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～合う vs 一緒に～する (cont.)

The luggage is a third party; nobody is carrying anybody. That is why ❌運はこび合あう is odd. With 助たすける, the sentence only takes 合あう if the helping flows in both directions - 一緒いっしょに助たすける would mean two people helping a third.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～込む vs ～出す

Rule of thumb - as directional suffixes they are opposites: 込こむ points in, 出だす points out. Note that 出だす also has the unrelated "suddenly begin" sense you met at N4.

かばんから財布さいふを**取とり出だした**。
I took my wallet out of my bag.

ポケットに切符きっぷを**押おし込こんだ**。
I shoved the ticket into my pocket.

The same verb takes both and the pair is worth memorising: 飛とび出だす ("dash out, leap out") against 飛とび込こむ ("dive in").

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～込む vs ～出す (cont.)

The trap is that 出だす has a second job - marking the sudden start of an action, as in 泣なき出だす - and that job has nothing to do with direction. Context tells them apart: 走はしり出だす is "break into a run", not "run outwards".

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 書かく**直なおす** → ✅ 書かき**直なおす**
    All four attach to the ます-stem.

  • ❌ 先生せんせいに相談そうだんし**合あった**。 → ✅ 先生せんせいに相談そうだんした。
    ～合あう needs the action to run both ways. Consulting a teacher goes one way, so the plain verb is correct.

  • ❌ 日本にほんの生活せいかつを住すみ**慣なれた**。 → ✅ 日本にほんの生活せいかつに慣なれた。
    With a noun, use に慣なれる. The stem form needs a verb in front of it: この町まちに住すみ慣なれた.

  • ❌ この問題もんだいをもう一度いちど考かんがえ込こもう。 → ✅ この問題もんだいをもう一度いちど考かんがえ直なおそう。
    考かんがえ込こむ is to brood, absorbed and silent. To rethink something is 考かんがえ直なおす.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ このパソコンは**使つかい慣なれる**。 → ✅ このパソコンは**使つかい慣なれている**。
    The suffix names a state you have reached, so the ている form is what you almost always want.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Practice 1

1. 漢字かんじを一いち文字もじ間違まちがえたので、はじめから書かき____。

はじめから書かき**直なおした**。

The first version was faulty, which is exactly what ～直なおす marks. 直なおす is an う-verb, so the past is 直なおした.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Practice 2

2. Choose: 会議かいぎでは全員ぜんいんで意見いけんを（言いい合あった・一緒いっしょに言いった）。

全員ぜんいんで意見いけんを**言いい合あった**。

Opinions were exchanged between the participants, which is the mutual sense. 一緒いっしょに言いった would mean everyone said the same thing in unison, which is not what a meeting is.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I've got used to cooking for myself, so it doesn't bother me any more."

自分じぶんで料理りょうりを**作つくり慣なれた**ので、もう苦くにならない。

作つくり慣なれる takes the ます-stem because the thing you are used to is an action. Had the sentence been about being used to life alone, it would need the noun pattern 一人ひとり暮ぐらしに慣なれる.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 電車でんしゃが来きたので、いそいで乗のり出だした。

✅ 電車でんしゃが来きたので、いそいで乗のり**込こんだ**。

Getting onto a train is movement inward, so the suffix is 込こむ. 乗のり出だす exists but means "lean out" or "launch into a venture" - not boarding.

～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれは黙だまって下したを見みていた。 (b) 彼かれは**黙だまり込こんで**しまった。

(a) simply describes what he was doing: not speaking, eyes down. It is a neutral piece of stage direction and says nothing about why.

(b) says he shut down into silence and stayed there. The 込こむ supplies both depth and duration - he has gone somewhere inward - and しまった adds that this was not what anyone wanted. It is a much heavier silence than (a).
