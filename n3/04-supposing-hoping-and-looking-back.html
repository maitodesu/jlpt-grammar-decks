～としたら / ～とすれば

Meaning · supposing that A is true, then B - a premise granted for the sake of working out what follows

The と here is the quotative と, and する is "take it as". ～としたら literally means if we take it that…. It does not report a condition on its way to happening; it grants a proposition and asks the listener to see what falls out of it. That is why the main clause is nearly always a judgement - a deduction, an estimate, a question, a "then there's nothing for it but…" - rather than a plan or an instruction.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Formation

Everything goes into its plain form and takes と. Nouns and な-adjectives need だ in front - the opposite of なら, which forbids it.

Note - the adverbs もし, 仮かりに and 万一まんいち sit naturally in front of this pattern and announce the supposition early: 仮かりに勝かったとしたら… They are optional, but they make a long sentence much easier to follow.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Examples

今いまから行いくとしたら、何時なんじに着つきますか。
If we were to set off now, what time would we get there?

これが本当ほんとうだとすれば、大おおきな問題もんだいです。
If this is true, then it's a serious problem.

一人ひとりで全部ぜんぶやるとしたら、三日みっかはかかるだろう。
If I had to do the whole thing alone, it would take a good three days.

仮かりに宝たからくじが当あたったとしたら、何なにに使つかいますか。
Supposing you won the lottery, what would you spend it on?

～としたら / ～とすれば · Examples (cont.)

彼かれが来こないとすれば、司会しかいは私わたしがやるしかない。
If he isn't coming, then I have no choice but to run the thing myself.

この数字すうじが正ただしいとすると、去年きょねんより売うり上あげが減へっていることになる。
If these figures are right, it means sales are down on last year.

留学りゅうがくするとしたら、やっぱり英語えいごが通つうじる国くにがいいなあ。
If I were going to study abroad, I'd still want somewhere English gets you by.

台風たいふうで新幹線しんかんせんが止とまっているとしたら、明日あしたの会議かいぎは延期えんきするほかないでしょう。
If the bullet trains are down because of the typhoon, there'll be nothing for it but to postpone tomorrow's meeting.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Examples (cont.)

今いまの条件じょうけんのまま引ひき受うけるとすれば、人ひとを二人ふたり増ふやしていただく必要ひつようがあります。
If we're to take this on under the current terms, we'd need two more people on it.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Nuance & register

The family is register-neutral in the sense that no member is rude or childish, but the three are not evenly distributed. としたら is the everyday spoken member and the one that handles frankly unreal suppositions - 生うまれ変かわったとしたら, 私わたしが社長しゃちょうだとしたら. とすれば leans towards written argument, reports and analysis, where it introduces an inference the writer is prepared to defend. とすると tends to mark a consequence the speaker has just this second worked out, and often carries a small "ah - then…" in it.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because the clause is subordinate, it stays plain even in the politest sentence: 事実じじつだとすれば、大変たいへん申もうし訳わけございません。 If you want the politeness to reach into the clause itself - service-counter Japanese, a careful reply to a superior - the forms are としましたら and, at the top of the scale, といたしますと. These are common at a bank or a travel desk and sound stiff anywhere else.

One restriction is worth internalising. The main clause is where the speaker's judgement goes: だろう, はずだ, ことになる, しかない, ～たい, or a question. A bare statement of scheduled fact does not belong there, and neither does a request. If what you mean is "let me know once you get home", the sentence wants たら, not としたら - you are not supposing anything, you are waiting for something.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, としたら freely takes a past clause in front without referring to past time: 落おとしたとしたら、どこだろう。 The た marks the supposed situation as already complete at the moment you are reasoning about it, exactly as it does with たら.

～としたら / ～とすれば · ～としたら vs ～たら

Rule of thumb - たら supposes an event that could actually turn up; としたら supposes a proposition so that you can reason from it. If you could replace the clause with "assuming that…", you want としたら.

駅えきに**着ついたら**電話でんわしてね。
Give me a call when you get to the station.

That one cannot take としたら: arriving is not a hypothesis, it is the plan. Swap in a genuine supposition and the pattern comes back:

終電しゅうでんに乗のり遅おくれたとしたら、彼女かのじょはどこで泊とまるつもりだろう。
Supposing she did miss the last train, where is she planning to stay?

～としたら / ～とすれば · ～としたら vs ～たら (cont.)

The same split explains why としたら sounds odd with an instruction after it. You cannot act on a premise that is only being entertained.

～としたら / ～とすれば · ～としたら vs ～なら

Rule of thumb - なら picks up something the other person has just put on the table and answers it, usually with advice or an offer. としたら may suppose something nobody has claimed, and answers itself with a conclusion.

京都きょうとに**行いくなら**、朝あさ早はやく出でたほうがいいよ。
If you're going to Kyoto, you'd do better to leave early.

京都きょうとに**行いくとしたら**、日帰ひがえりでは無理むりだろうね。
If one were to go to Kyoto, a day trip probably wouldn't work.

～としたら / ～とすれば · ～としたら vs ～なら (cont.)

The first is aimed at a person with a plan; the second weighs an idea. Note also the form: なら refuses だ (本当ほんとうなら) while としたら requires it (本当ほんとうだとしたら). They look like a pair and conjugate as opposites.

～としたら / ～とすれば · ～としたら vs ～とすれば vs ～とすると

Rule of thumb - all three grant a premise. としたら is the freest and most spoken; とすれば is the reasoning one and dominates writing; とすると presents the consequence as something that simply falls out, and often marks a realisation.

もし私わたしが部長ぶちょうだとしたら、まずこの制度せいどを変かえますね。
If I were the manager, the first thing I'd change is this system.

この説明せつめいが正ただしいとすれば、原因げんいんは一ひとつしか考かんがえられない。
If this account is correct, only one cause is conceivable.

木村きむらさんがもう出でたとすると、さっき廊下ろうかですれ違ちがったのかな。
If Kimura's already left, then maybe that was him I passed in the corridor.

～としたら / ～とすれば · ～としたら vs ～とすれば vs ～とすると (cont.)

Note - となると is a fourth cousin and is not interchangeable. It takes a situation that has genuinely arisen and asks what now becomes of things: 来週らいしゅう出張しゅっちょうとなると、準備じゅんびが間まに合あわない。 Where としたら supposes, となると concedes.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 雨あめとしたら、中止ちゅうしです。 → ✅ 雨あめだとしたら、中止ちゅうしです。
    Nouns and な-adjectives take だ before と. なら is the one that drops it, and learners routinely apply the wrong half of the pair.

  • ❌ 安やすいだとすれば買かいます。 → ✅ 安やすいとすれば買かいます。
    い-adjectives are already complete predicates. No だ.

  • ❌ 家いえに着ついたとしたら、連絡れんらくしてください。 → ✅ 家いえに着ついたら、連絡れんらくしてください。
    Arriving home is expected, not supposed, and the main clause is a request. Both point away from としたら and towards plain たら.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 春はるになるとしたら、桜さくらが咲さきます。 → ✅ 春はるになると、桜さくらが咲さきます。
    A regularity that always holds is と. Nothing is being supposed.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Practice 1

1. 仮かりに明日あした大雪おおゆき____、学校がっこうはどうなりますか。

大雪おおゆきだとしたら（or だとすれば）

The clause ends in a noun, so だ is obligatory before と. The main clause is a question about consequences, which is exactly the slot this pattern was built for.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Practice 2

2. Choose: 荷物にもつが届とどい（たら・たとしたら）、すぐ開あけてみてください。

荷物にもつが届とどいたら、すぐ開あけてみてください。

The parcel is on its way - this is an expected event, not a hypothesis - and the main clause is a request. としたら refuses both of those.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "If this report is accurate, we'll have to change the plan."

この報告ほうこくが正ただしいとすれば、計画けいかくを変かえなければなりません。

とすれば is the reasoning member of the family and fits a written or workplace inference. The main clause states what necessarily follows, which is the natural partner for this pattern.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼かれが犯人はんにんとしたら、どうして逃にげなかったんだろう。

✅ 彼かれが犯人はんにんだとしたら、どうして逃にげなかったんだろう。

犯人はんにん is a noun, so だ has to appear. Everything else about the sentence is right: a supposition followed by a puzzled question is a textbook use.

～としたら / ～とすれば · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 留学りゅうがくするなら、アメリカがいいですよ。 (b) 留学りゅうがくするとしたら、アメリカがいいですね。

(a) responds to a plan the listener has already announced. You have heard that they intend to study abroad, and you are advising them where to go - hence the listener-directed よ.

(b) entertains the idea without attributing it to anyone. The speaker is weighing a hypothetical, quite possibly about themselves, and ね invites the listener to weigh it too. Nobody has necessarily decided anything.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば

Meaning · if only A, then B - A named as the single condition that is enough on its own

さえ picks out one thing and the ば-clause says that this one thing is all it takes. Everything else is waved away: it may be missing, it may be against you, it does not matter. The main clause then reports that the result is assured - or, in the past, that it would have been, which is why this pattern is also how Japanese sighs over a small thing that was not done.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Formation

Two halves have to arrive together: the particle さえ on the crucial element, and a ば-form on the verb or adjective that governs it. Which helper you need depends on what the crucial element is.

Rule of thumb - さえ replaces が and を, and follows every other particle. ❌お金かねがさえ, ❌これをさえ; but 駅えきにさえ, ここでさえ, 友ともだちからさえ are all fine.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Examples

お金かねさえあれば、問題もんだいないんだけどな。
If only we had the money, there'd be no problem.

これさえ覚おぼえれば、テストは大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。
Learn just this and you'll be fine on the test.

天気てんきさえよければ、明日あしたの朝あさ出発しゅっぱつできます。
As long as the weather holds, we can set off tomorrow morning.

静しずかでさえあれば、部屋へやは狭せまくてもかまいません。
So long as it's quiet, I don't mind the room being small.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Examples (cont.)

説明書せつめいしょを読よみさえすれば、だれでも組くみ立たてられますよ。
Anyone can put it together, as long as they actually read the instructions.

薬くすりを飲のんでさえいれば、熱ねつはもっと早はやく下さがっていたはずです。
If you'd just kept taking the medicine, the fever would have come down sooner.

あなたが元気げんきでさえいてくれれば、ほかには何なにも望のぞみません。
As long as you stay well, I don't ask for anything else.

締しめ切きりさえ守まもってくれれば、やり方かたはそちらに任まかせます。
Provided you hit the deadline, I'll leave the method entirely to you.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Examples (cont.)

あの時とき、一言ひとこと謝あやまりさえすれば、こんな大おおごとにはならなかったのに。
If I had just said one word of apology back then, none of this would have blown up.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral and extremely common in speech, but it always carries an attitude, and the attitude changes with the tense of the main clause.

With a non-past main clause it is reassuring or businesslike: here is the one hurdle, clear it and the rest takes care of itself. This is the tone of instructions, of a manager delegating, of somebody talking a nervous friend down - 落おち着つきさえすれば、できるよ。

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Nuance & register (cont.)

With a past main clause, and especially with のに or んだけど hanging off the end, it turns wistful or accusing. The clause names a trivially small thing that was not done, and the main clause names the disaster that followed. Said about yourself it is regret; said about the person in front of you it is a reproach, and a fairly sharp one - 連絡れんらくさえしてくれれば is not a neutral remark. Soften it, or say it about the situation rather than the person.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two spoken habits worth knowing. First, the ます-stem construction (読よみさえすれば) sounds slightly emphatic and bookish; in casual speech people more often hang さえ on a noun and leave the verb alone (それさえ読よめば). Second, すら is a near-synonym of さえ in the "even" sense but is confined to writing and almost never appears in this minimum-condition frame - do not swap them.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · ～さえ○○ば vs plain ～ば

Rule of thumb - plain ば says this is a condition. さえ…ば says this is the only condition. Removing さえ costs you the whole point of the sentence.

時間じかんがあれば、手伝てつだいます。
If I have time, I'll help.

時間じかんさえあれば、手伝てつだえるんですが。
It's purely a question of time - if I had it, I could help.

The second sentence claims that willingness, skill and permission are all already in place, so that time is the last thing standing in the way. The first claims nothing of the sort. Notice too that が disappears once さえ arrives.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · ～さえ○○ば vs ～だけで

Rule of thumb - だけ counts; さえ selects. だけで says the amount is small; さえ…ば says this particular thing is the one that matters, however large it is.

名前なまえを書かくだけで参加さんかできます。
You can take part just by writing your name.

名前なまえさえ書かけば、あとはこちらでやっておきます。
Once you've put your name down, we'll take care of the rest.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · ～さえ○○ば vs ～だけで (cont.)

だけで is about how little effort is involved and is a natural thing to put in an advertisement. さえ…ば is about clearing the one remaining obstacle, and it implies a list of other things that are already settled. That is also why さえ…ば can take a heavy condition - 一億円いちおくえんさえあれば - where だけで would sound absurd.

～さえ○○ば vs さえ meaning "even"

Rule of thumb - same particle, opposite ends of the scale. Without a ば after it, さえ picks an extreme case ("even X"). With a ば after it, it picks the minimum sufficient case ("if only X").

子こどもさえ知しっている言葉ことばです。
It's a word even children know.

子こどもさえ静しずかにしていれば、いい店みせなんですけどね。
It's a lovely restaurant, as long as the children keep quiet.

The first ranks children at the bottom of a scale of knowledge and says the word reaches even there. The second says the children are the sole problem. The ば is what flips the reading, so leaving it off does not weaken the sentence - it changes it into a different one.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Common mistakes

  • ❌ お金かねがさえあれば → ✅ お金かねさえあれば
    さえ takes the place of が and を. It stacks only on に, で, から, と and the like.

  • ❌ 来くるさえすれば → ✅ 来きさえすれば
    さえする attaches to the ます-stem, never the dictionary form. For a する verb built on a noun, put さえ after the noun instead: 連絡れんらくさえすれば.

  • ❌ 安やすいさえあれば買かいます。 → ✅ 安やすくさえあれば買かいます。
    An い-adjective goes into its く form before さえある, exactly as it does before ない.

  • ❌ 時間じかんさえある、終おわります。 → ✅ 時間じかんさえあれば、終おわります。
    The ば is not decoration. Without it the sentence says "even time exists", which is not what anybody means.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この書類しょるいさえ出だせば、受付うけつけに行いってください。 → ✅ この書類しょるいを出だしたら、受付うけつけに行いってください。
    A sufficient-condition clause is a claim about what will follow, not a step in a set of instructions. Like plain ば with an action verb, it does not take a request or a command after it.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Practice 1

1. パスポート____、あとは何なにもいりません。

パスポートさえあれば

The sentence lists one requirement and dismisses everything else, which is the core job of this pattern. が disappears in front of さえ.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Practice 2

2. Choose: この本ほんを（読よむだけで・読よみさえすれば）、全体ぜんたいの流ながれが分わかります。

Both are grammatical, but they say different things. 読よむだけで advertises how little work it is; 読よみさえすれば implies you have been putting it off and the reading is the one thing left to do. Pick the second if the sentence is aimed at somebody who has not opened the book yet.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "If only he'd told me earlier, I could have changed the booking."

もっと早はやく言いってくれさえすれば、予約よやくを変かえられたのに。

A past counterfactual with のに is the standard shape for this feeling. Note that くれる is what makes the complaint land on him rather than on the situation.

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 部屋へやが明あかるいさえあれば、古ふるくてもいいです。

✅ 部屋へやが明あかるくさえあれば、古ふるくてもいいです。

An い-adjective must become く before さえある. A tidier version moves the particle too: 部屋へやさえ明あかるければ、古ふるくてもいいです。

～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれさえ来こなかった。 (b) 彼かれさえ来こなければ、楽たのしい会かいだったのに。

(a) is the "even" さえ: of all the people who might have failed to show up, even he did - he was the last person you expected to miss it.

(b) is the sufficient-condition さえ…ば: everything else about the party was fine, and his presence was the single thing that spoiled it. The ば and the counterfactual のに together turn the same particle into a complaint about one man.

～ばいい

Meaning · that's all you have to do; you should just ~; it's fine if ~

ば names a condition and いい says that meeting it is enough. Put together they give advice not by ordering someone about but by pointing at the one action that will settle the matter: do that, and we're done. The same shape asks the question in reverse - どうすればいいですか, "what would be enough here?" - which is the single most useful sentence in the book when you are lost.

～ばいい · Formation

The first half is the ordinary ば-form. Nouns and な-adjectives use なら, since なら is the copula's ば-form.

～ばいい · Examples

分わからなければ、先生せんせいに**聞きけばいい**よ。
If you don't know, just ask the teacher.

ここに名前なまえを**書かけばいい**ですか。
Do I just write my name here?

寒さむかったら、そこの毛布もうふを**使つかえばいい**からね。
If you get cold, just use that blanket over there.

道みちに迷まよったときは、どうすればいいですか。
What should I do if I get lost?

～ばいい · Examples (cont.)

全部ぜんぶ覚おぼえなくても、大事だいじなところだけ**見みておけばいい**んです。
You don't have to memorise all of it - going over the important bits is enough.

文句もんくがあるなら、自分じぶんでやればいいじゃないか。
If you've got complaints, do it yourself then.

返事へんじは急いそぎませんので、来週らいしゅうまでに**決きめていただければいい**です。
There's no rush on the reply - it's fine if you decide by next week.

コピーで受うけ付つけますから、原本げんぽんは後あとから**送おくっていただければいい**んですよ。
We'll accept a copy, so you can just send the original on afterwards.

～ばいい · Nuance & register

ばいい is short, and short can be cold. Said flatly to somebody who has asked for help, 自分じぶんで調しらべればいい is not friendly advice - it means "look it up yourself, then." The pattern's structure is partly to blame: naming the sufficient action implies the listener could have worked it out. Softeners matter more here than with most patterns.

～ばいい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The usual repairs, roughly from casual to careful: ばいいよ (warm, to a friend), ばいいんじゃない? (a suggestion offered rather than handed down), ばいいですよ, ばいいと思おもいます, and - with a superior or a customer - dropping the pattern entirely for ～ていただければと思おもいます. Telling your section head 「メールで送おくればいいです」 is technically an answer to their question and still lands badly.

There is a deliberately dismissive use, and you should recognise it even if you never say it: 勝手かってにすればいい, 帰かえりたければ帰かえればいい. Attached to a ～たければ clause and delivered flatly, ばいい means "suit yourself" and is a way of ending a conversation badly.

～ばいい · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two everyday frames run in the opposite direction and are entirely safe. ばいいんです is the reassuring "all you have to do is…" of a helpful colleague. And どうすればいいですか is the standard polite request for instructions - neutral, businesslike, usable with anyone.

Finally, note what the past tense does. ばいい has no ❌ばいいでした. Its past is ばよかった, and that form means regret, not advice.

～ばいい · ～ばいい vs ～たらいい

Rule of thumb - ばいい presents the action as sufficient: that, and nothing more, will do it. たらいい presents it as a suggestion: how about doing that? The first is closer to a solution, the second to a proposal.

きっぷは券売機けんばいきで**買かえばいい**んです。
You buy your ticket at the machine - that's all there is to it.

疲つかれてるなら、今日きょうは早はやく**寝ねたらいい**よ。
If you're tired, why not get an early night today.

～ばいい · ～ばいい vs ～たらいい (cont.)

Swapping them is not ungrammatical, but the flavour shifts: 寝ねればいい sounds like you are closing the topic, while 買かったらいい sounds oddly tentative about a fixed procedure. The same split shows in the two questions: どうすればいいですか asks for the correct procedure, while どうしたらいいですか more often comes from somebody genuinely at a loss. That is a tendency rather than a rule, and either is acceptable in either situation.

～ばいい · ～ばいい vs ～たほうがいい

Rule of thumb - たほうがいい compares: this course is better than the other one, and there is an implication that you might be about to choose wrongly. ばいい does not compare; it simply supplies an answer.

熱ねつがあるなら、病院びょういんに**行いったほうがいい**よ。
If you've got a fever you'd better go to a clinic.

保険証ほけんしょうがなければ、後あとで**出だせばいい**そうです。
Apparently if you don't have your insurance card you can just hand it in later.

The first weighs going against not going and comes down on one side, which is why it can sound like mild pressure. The second answers a procedural worry and applies no pressure at all. Use たほうがいい when there is a real risk in the alternative.

～ばいい · ～ばいい vs ～てもいい

Rule of thumb - てもいい grants permission: doing it would not be a problem. ばいい states sufficiency: doing it is the thing that solves this.

この席せき、座すわってもいいですか。
Is it all right if I sit here?

席せきが空あいていなければ、後うしろに**立たてばいい**ですよ。
If there are no seats free, you can just stand at the back.

～ばいい · ～ばいい vs ～てもいい (cont.)

English "you can" covers both, which is exactly why learners mix them up. Ask yourself whether you are being allowed to do something or told what will work. Answering 「はい、座すわればいいです」 to a request for permission sounds brusque, almost like "well, sit down then."

～ばいい · Common mistakes

  • ❌ ここに書かくばいいですか。 → ✅ ここに書かけばいいですか。
    いい follows the ば-form, not the dictionary form. The ば is part of the verb, not a separate particle.

  • ❌ 部屋へやが静しずかだばいいです。 → ✅ 部屋へやが静しずかならいいです。
    Nouns and な-adjectives have no ば-form of their own; they borrow the copula's, which is なら.

  • ❌ もっと早はやく出でればいいでした。 → ✅ もっと早はやく出でればよかったです。
    いい cannot be put into the past behind ば. The past of this pattern is ばよかった, and it means "I should have", not "it was fine to".

～ばいい · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ (課長かちょうに) 「この書類しょるいはメールで送おくればいいです。」 → ✅ 「この書類しょるいはメールでお送おくりいただければと思おもいます。」
    Grammatical, but telling a superior what is sufficient sounds like issuing them instructions. Move to a ていただければ frame.

～ばいい · Practice 1

1. 「この機械きかい、どう使つかうんですか。」「このボタンを____だけです。」

このボタンを**押おせばいい**だけです。

The question asks for a procedure, and the answer names the one sufficient action. だけ reinforces the "and nothing else" that ばいい already implies.

～ばいい · Practice 2

2. Choose: 熱ねつが下さがらないなら、今日きょうは（休やすめばいい・休やすんだほうがいい）ですよ。

今日きょうは**休やすんだほうがいい**ですよ。

There is a real risk in the alternative - going in with a fever - so the sentence should weigh the two courses. ばいい would sound as though you were merely informing them that taking the day off is permitted.

～ばいい · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "What should I say when I answer the phone?"

電話でんわに出でるとき、何なんと**言いえばいい**ですか。

～ばいいですか is the standard way of asking for the correct procedure, and this is a procedure question rather than a request for permission. 何なんと is the quotative と, not を.

～ばいい · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: あの時とき、田中たなかさんに相談そうだんすればいいでした。

✅ あの時とき、田中たなかさんに相談そうだんすればよかったです。

ばいい has no past form; you put いい itself into the past. The result no longer means "it was enough to consult him" but "I wish I had consulted him", which is what the past of this pattern always means.

～ばいい · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 嫌いやなら、行いかなくてもいいよ。 (b) 嫌いやなら、行いかなければいいじゃない。

(a) releases the listener from an obligation: if you don't want to go, you are not required to. It is kind, and the speaker is taking responsibility for the permission.

(b) hands the problem back. It says the solution was obvious all along - just don't go - and with じゃない on the end it carries a distinct edge of impatience. The same words in a gentler voice can be genuinely helpful, but the default reading is a brush-off.

～たらいい / ～といい - should

Meaning · you should ~; why not ~ - advice given by describing a good outcome rather than issuing an order

Read the words literally and they say if you did it, that would be good. That is the whole trick. Instead of telling somebody what to do, you sketch a small hypothetical world in which they have already done it, and pronounce that world good. The listener is left to draw the obvious conclusion, which is far easier to accept than an instruction.

This page covers the advice reading only. The identical string also means "I hope" and "it would be nice if"; what tells them apart is who acts and whether they can.

～たらいい / ～といい - should · Formation

The advice reading is essentially a verb pattern, and the verb has to be something the listener could actually decide to do.

If it's souvenirs you're after, I'd buy them in the basement floor at the station.

～たらいい / ～といい - should · Examples (cont.)

迷まよっているなら、いちど見みに**行いってみたらいい**んじゃないですか。
If you're torn about it, why not just go and have a look once?

If polite language is your weak spot, start by copying the dialogue in dramas.

There's no rush to settle your future. Work for three years or so and think about it then.

パソコンが重おもいなら、要いらないアプリを**消けしてみたらいい**ですよ。驚おどろくほど速はやくなります。
If your laptop is sluggish, try deleting the apps you don't use. It gets startlingly faster.

～たらいい / ～といい - should · Nuance & register

Because the pattern hides advice inside a conditional, it is gentler than ～なさい or the plain imperative, and slightly gentler than ～たほうがいい. It is the register of a helpful colleague, a friendly teacher, a shop assistant who has decided to be kind to you.

たらいい and といい are close, with a small difference of angle. たらいい responds to this person's situation - the conditional takes their circumstances as read. といい states something closer to a general good practice, which is why it turns up so often in teacherly and written advice: 辞書じしょはときどき引ひくといい。 Both are entirely ordinary in speech.

～たらいい / ～といい - should · Nuance & register (cont.)

The pattern is still advice, and advice flows downhill in Japanese. To a friend or a junior, 休やすんだらいいよ is warm. To your section head the same words are presumptuous, and you climb the ladder of hedges instead: たらいいと思おもいます → たらいいんじゃないでしょうか → dropping the pattern altogether for ～てはいかがでしょうか.

たらいいでしょう deserves a warning. It is grammatical, but it lands as distant and lecturing - a teacher addressing a hall rather than a person addressing a person. In conversation prefer たらいいですよ.

Two things do not happen. いい here is an い-adjective, so it never takes だ (❌たらいいだ). And the pattern has no past form of its own: putting いい into the past gives you たらよかった, which is regret, not advice.

～たらいい / ～といい - should · ～たらいい vs ～ばいい

Rule of thumb - ばいい names a sufficient action: do that, and the matter is settled. たらいい makes a suggestion: how about doing that? The first closes a question, the second opens one.

きっぷは自動じどう券売機けんばいきで**買かえばいい**んです。
You buy the ticket from the machine - that's all there is to it.

時間じかんがあるなら、窓口まどぐちで**聞きいてみたらいい**ですよ。
If you've got time, you might try asking at the counter.

Neither swap is ungrammatical, but the tone moves. ばいい on a suggestion can sound like you are shutting the conversation down; たらいい on a fixed procedure sounds oddly unsure of a thing everybody knows.

～たらいい / ～といい - should · ～たらいい vs ～たほうがいい

Rule of thumb - たほうがいい weighs this course against the alternative and warns you off the other one. たらいい does not weigh anything; it simply offers an idea.

夜中よなかに一人ひとりであの道みちを**通とおらないほうがいい**よ。
You'd better not take that road alone at night.

時間じかんがかかってもいいなら、あの道みちを**通とおったらいい**よ。
If you don't mind it taking longer, take that road.

The first implies a risk; the second implies a choice. That is also why たほうがいい has a natural negative and たらいい does not: warning somebody off is exactly the job たほうがいい was built for.

The advice reading vs the "I hope" reading

Rule of thumb - if the listener could decide to do it, the sentence is advice. If nobody in the room controls it, the sentence is a hope. Same words, and the test is about the world, not the grammar.

早はやめに**予約よやくしたらいい**ですよ。
You'd do well to book early.

席せきが**空あいているといい**ですね。
I hope there are seats free.

The advice reading vs the "I hope" reading (cont.)

Booking is up to the listener, so the first is advice. Whether seats are free is up to nobody, so the second is a hope - and note that hopes take ですね, which invites the listener to share the feeling, while advice takes ですよ, which hands them something they did not have. The final particle is often the fastest clue.

～たらいい / ～といい - should · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 少すこし休やすむたらいいですよ。 → ✅ 少すこし休やすんだらいいですよ。
    たら attaches to the plain past. The dictionary form goes with と, not with たら.

  • ❌ 早はやく寝ねたらいいだ。 → ✅ 早はやく寝ねたらいい。
    いい is an い-adjective and is already a complete predicate. No だ - in the polite form it becomes いいです.

  • ❌ 無理むりしないといいですよ。（meaning "you shouldn't push yourself"） → ✅ 無理むりしないほうがいいですよ。
    A negative clause in front of といい is read as a wish, so your advice turns into "I hope you don't", which is not what you meant.

～たらいい / ～といい - should · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ (部長ぶちょうに)「この方法ほうほうで進すすめたらいいです。」 → ✅ 「この方法ほうほうで進すすめてはいかがでしょうか。」
    The grammar is fine; the direction is not. Advice aimed upwards needs a different frame, not just a です on the end.

1. 日本語にほんごが上手じょうずになりたいなら、毎日まいにち声こえに出だして____いいですよ。

Reading aloud is something the listener can decide to do, so this is the advice reading. Either conditional works; と sounds a little more like a general recommendation.

The listener has a concrete problem with one obvious fix, and ばいい is the form that names a sufficient solution. 買かったらいい is not wrong, but it frames an obvious remedy as a tentative idea.

The action is fully under the listener's control, so this is advice rather than hope, and ですよ is the particle that goes with handing somebody a new idea.

～たらいい / ～といい - should · Practice 4

飲のまないといい would be a hope that the person avoids medicine, which makes no sense as advice for a cold that won't clear. If you really do want negative advice, the form is 飲のまないほうがいい.

～たらいい / ～といい - should · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 明日あした、早はやく**来きたらいいですよ。 (b) 明日あした、早はやく来こられるといい**ですね。

(a) is advice. Coming early is the listener's decision, and ですよ presents the idea as something the speaker is giving them.

(b) is a hope. The potential form 来こられる puts the outcome partly outside the listener's hands - traffic, work, whatever - so the speaker can only wish for it, and ですね invites the listener to wish along. Swapping the two particles would make both sentences sound slightly off.

～たらいい / ～といい - I hope

Meaning · I hope ~ ; here's hoping ~ - a wish about an outcome nobody in the conversation controls

Same words as the advice pattern, different job. The conditional still sketches a world and calls it good; what changes is that neither speaker nor listener can bring that world about. The weather, an exam result, somebody's recovery, whether the train is on time - once the outcome is out of human hands, "you should" is impossible and the only reading left is hope.

This is the everyday Japanese for "I hope so", and the fixed phrase ～といいですね is worth learning as a single unit.

～たらいい / ～といい - I hope · Nuance & register

The pattern is neutral and usable at every level of politeness, but whose hope it is changes the ending, and getting that wrong is the most audible mistake learners make here.

For a hope about the listener's situation, the ending is ですね (or plain ね). ね asks the listener to share a feeling, and since the feeling is about them, that works: 試合しあいに勝かてるといいですね。 This is the standard way of wishing somebody luck, and it is far more common than 「がんばってください」 in careful adult speech.

～たらいい / ～といい - I hope · Nuance & register (cont.)

For a hope of your own, the natural ending trails off: ～といいんですが, ～といいのですが, ～といいんですけど. The unfinished が/けど does the work of English "…though" - it stops the sentence from sounding like a demand. 間まに合あうといいんですが is modest; 間まに合あうといいですね about your own train sounds like you are asking a stranger to agree with your wishes.

なあ is the private version. It is spoken to yourself or to somebody very close, it draws the vowel out, and it is one of the most natural-sounding things a learner can say: 明日あした休やすみだといいなあ。 In writing it appears mostly in dialogue and diaries.

～たらいい / ～といい - I hope · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two small points of substance. First, the negative ないといい is fully idiomatic for hoping something doesn't happen - 渋滞じゅうたいしていないといいですね - and is the normal way to say it. Second, when the outcome is already settled but unknown to you, put the clause into ている/ていた: 忘わすれずに持もっていってくれて いるといいんですが. The hope is not that it will happen, but that it already did.

～といい vs ～たらいい in the hope reading

Rule of thumb - both are used and neither is wrong. と presents the hoped-for thing as simply happening, and is the default in the set phrases. たら frames it a little more as a condition being met, so it leans slightly further from expectation.

来月らいげつの旅行りょこう、楽たのしいといいですね。
I hope next month's trip is fun.

宝たからくじ、当あたったらいいなあ。
Wouldn't it be something if the lottery came in.

～といい vs ～たらいい in the hope reading (cont.)

The trip is a real event with a decent chance of being fun; と fits. Winning the lottery is remote, and たら sits more comfortably with remoteness. Do not turn this into a rule - 当あたるといいなあ is perfectly natural too. What is close to fixed is the polite formula: 「～といいですね」 is heard far more often than 「～たらいいですね」.

～たらいい / ～といい - I hope · ～といいですね vs ～といいんですが

Rule of thumb - ですね for somebody else's hope, んですが for your own. ね asks the listener to agree, and you cannot ask people to agree that your wishes should come true.

面接めんせつ、うまくいくといいですね。
I hope your interview goes well.

面接めんせつ、うまくいくといいんですが。
I hope my interview goes well - we'll see.

The second is what you say about yourself, and the trailing が is not laziness; it is the politeness. Ending flatly on 「うまくいくといいです」 about your own affairs sounds blunt in a way that is hard to explain and easy to hear.

～たらいい / ～といい - I hope · ～といい vs ～てほしい

Rule of thumb - てほしい aims at a person and asks them to act. といい aims at the world and asks nothing of anybody.

明日あしたまでに返事へんじをもらえるといいんですが。
I hope I can get an answer by tomorrow.

明日あしたまでに返事へんじをもらいたいんですが。
I'd like an answer by tomorrow, please.

The first leaves the other party free; the second is a request wearing a soft coat. Said to the person who owes you the answer, といい is a hint and てほしい／たい is a push. Choosing the wrong one either fails to get you your answer or annoys the person who has to give it.

～たらいい / ～といい - I hope · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日あした、いい天気てんきといいですね。 → ✅ 明日あした、いい天気てんきだといいですね。
    A noun needs だ before this と. The same goes for な-adjectives: 元気げんきだといいですね.

  • ❌ 私わたしの荷物にもつが無事ぶじに着つくといいですね。 → ✅ …着つくといいんですが。
    ですね invites agreement, which only works when the hope is about the listener. About your own luggage it sounds like you are recruiting them into your worry.

  • ❌ 手伝てつだってくれるといいです。（meaning "I want you to help"） → ✅ 手伝てつだってほしいです。
    Said to the person who could help, a hope is not a request. Use てほしい when you actually want them to act.

～たらいい / ～といい - I hope · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 雨あめが降ふらないといいだね。 → ✅ 雨あめが降ふらないといいね。
    いい is an い-adjective and never takes だ in front of ね.

～たらいい / ～といい - I hope · Practice 1

1. 「来週らいしゅう、北海道ほっかいどうに行いくんです。」「そうですか。雪ゆきが____ですね。」

The hope is about the listener's trip and about something no one controls, so といい plus ですね is exactly right. ですね is what makes it a wish for them rather than a remark about yourself.

～たらいい / ～といい - I hope · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 早はやく元気げんきになるといいですね。 (b) 早はやく元気げんきになってほしいです。

(a) is a hope handed to the person you are talking to, or to somebody's family on their behalf. It asks nothing of anyone and is the safe thing to say to a patient or their relatives.

(b) puts the recovery on the other person as something you want from them. Between close family that reads as affection; said to a colleague who is ill it can sound like you need them back at work. The grammar is fine either way - it is the direction of the wish that differs.

～たらいい / ～といい - it would be nice if

Meaning · it would be nice if ~; I wish ~ were the case - a wish aimed at something that is not so and probably won't be

The third reading of the same words. Advice supposes the listener will act; hope supposes the world might oblige. This one supposes neither. The clause describes a state that is not this one - a bigger flat, a shorter commute, a father who listens - and the speaker looks at it and sighs.

Nothing in the conjugation marks the difference. What marks it is のに (and its spoken relatives なあ, んだけど), which quietly supplies the second half of the thought: …but it isn't.

～たらいい / ～といい - it would be nice if · Formation

The wish reading takes anything that can be a state, and normally carries のに or なあ on the end. Without one of those, the sentence usually slides back into the plain "I hope" reading.

I always find myself wishing our cat could just tell me what he's thinking.

～たらいい / ～といい - it would be nice if · Nuance & register

This is the emotional member of the family, and it sounds like it. のに on its own is already a small complaint; のになあ drawn out is a sigh; んだけど trailing away is the resigned version that adults use in conversation. All of them are casual, and none of them belongs in a report.

The polite equivalent is ～たらいいのですが, which sounds measured rather than wistful, and is what you would write in an email. Note that ～のに does not politely become ～のにです; if you need politeness, the sentence has to be rebuilt.

～たらいい / ～といい - it would be nice if · Nuance & register (cont.)

Direction matters more here than in the other two readings. Aimed at yourself or at the world, のに is harmless. Aimed at a person present, it becomes a reproach: 早はやく言いってくれたらいいのに is not a wish, it is a complaint that they didn't. That can be affectionate between friends and is close to insubordinate said upwards at work. If a superior did something inconvenient, do not reach for this pattern.

～たらいい / ～といい - it would be nice if · Nuance & register (cont.)

One consequence of the "…but it isn't" is worth spelling out: using のに about something that could still easily happen sounds defeatist, and occasionally rude. Saying 「明日あしたのピクニック、晴はれたらいいのに」 to the person who organised it tells them you have written the day off. The live-hope form 「晴はれるといいですね」 is what you actually want.

Finally, note that the wish reading loves the potential form - 話はなせたら, 住すめたら, 飛とべたら. That is not an accident: the things we wish for are usually things we cannot do, and the potential is where that shows up in the grammar.

～たらいいのに vs ～といいですね

Rule of thumb - the difference is expectation, not form. といいですね treats the outcome as still open; たらいいのに treats it as closed and disappointing.

来週らいしゅうの試合しあい、勝かてるといいですね。
I hope we can win next week's match.

うちのチームにもう一人ひとりいい選手せんしゅがいたらいいのに。
If only our team had one more decent player.

The match has not been played, so hope is available. The squad is what it is, so only a wish is. Learners who use のに for every wish end up sounding pessimistic about perfectly ordinary plans.

～たらいい / ～といい - it would be nice if · ～たらいいのに vs ～たい

Rule of thumb - たい is for what you could go and do. たらいいのに is for what you cannot arrange: a state of the world, somebody else's behaviour, an ability you do not have.

沖縄おきなわに**行いきたい**なあ。
I want to go to Okinawa.

沖縄おきなわが電車でんしゃで**行いけたらいい**のになあ。
I wish you could get to Okinawa by train.

The first is a desire you could act on tomorrow with a plane ticket. The second wishes the map were different. Using たらいいのに where たい belongs makes you sound oddly helpless about your own life; using たい where the world is the obstacle simply doesn't parse.

～たらいいのに vs ～ばよかった

Rule of thumb - たらいいのに wishes about now: the state is wrong at this moment. ばよかった regrets the past: a decision went the wrong way and cannot be retaken.

この辞書じしょ、もっと軽かるかったらいいのに。
I wish this dictionary were lighter.

軽かるいほうを**買かえばよかった**。
I should have bought the lighter one.

The dictionary's weight is a present fact you are stuck with; the purchase is a past act you are second-guessing. If you can name the moment you would go back and change, you want ばよかった.

～たらいい / ～といい - it would be nice if · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 部屋へやが広ひろいだったらいいのに。 → ✅ 部屋へやが広ひろかったらいいのに。
    An い-adjective forms its own past - ひろかった - and never borrows だった.

  • ❌ あたたかいスープが食たべたらいいなあ。 → ✅ あたたかいスープが食たべたいなあ。
    Eating soup is something you can go and do, so the desire form is たい. The wish pattern is for what you cannot arrange.

  • ❌ (運動会うんどうかいの前日ぜんじつに)「明日あした晴はれたらいいのに。」 → ✅ 「明日あした晴はれるといいですね。」
    のに announces that you expect it not to happen. For a genuinely open outcome, use the live-hope form.

～たらいい / ～といい - it would be nice if · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ (部長ぶちょうに)「もっと早はやく教おしえてくれたらいいのに。」 → ✅ 「もう少すこし早はやくお知しらせいただけると助たすかります。」
    Pointed at a person, のに is a complaint about what they did. Upwards, rebuild the sentence as a request.

～たらいい / ～といい - it would be nice if · Practice 5

～ば○○のに

Meaning · if only A were so, B would be - and A is not, which is exactly the problem

Two pieces do the work. The ば-clause names a condition the speaker asserts is not met; the main clause names what would follow if it were. のに then sits at the end and marks the gap between those two worlds as a grievance.

This is the same のに you met at N4 meaning "although" - a marker of a contrast that ought not to be there. Here the contrast is between the reality you are stuck with and the one you can see perfectly well from where you are standing.

～ば○○のに · Formation

The front half is an ordinary ば-form. The back half is a plain clause plus のに, and its tense is what tells you whether the wish is about now or about the past.

Rule of thumb - 安やすければ買かうのに = it isn't cheap, so I'm not buying. 安やすければ買かったのに = it wasn't cheap, so I didn't. Only the main verb moves; the ば-clause looks identical in both.

This is one of the places where ば is at its most natural.

～ば○○のに · Examples

If it were a bit cheaper I'd buy it.

時間じかんがあれば手伝てつだえるのに。
I'd help if I had the time.

天気てんきがよければ、ここから富士山ふじさんが見みえたのに。
If the weather had been better, you'd have been able to see Mt Fuji from here.

車くるまがあれば、こんなに荷物にもつで苦労くろうしないのに。
If we had a car we wouldn't be struggling with the luggage like this.

～ば○○のに · Examples (cont.)

もっと早はやく病院びょういんに**行いっていれば**、こんなに長ながく休やすまずにすんだのに。
If I'd gone to the doctor sooner, I wouldn't have had to take so much time off.

駅えきまで走はしれば間まに合あったのに、彼かれはのんびり歩あるいていた。
He'd have made it if he'd run to the station, but he just strolled along.

一言ひとこと相談そうだんしてくれれば、力ちからになれたのにと今いまでも思おもう。
I still think that if she'd said one word to me, I could have helped.

この町まちにもう一本いっぽん電車でんしゃが通とおっていれば、若わかい人ひともこんなに出でていかなかったのに。
If this town had one more rail line, the young people wouldn't have left in these numbers.

～ば○○のに · Nuance & register

のに is not neutral. It is the audible sound of dissatisfaction, and the sentence is usually delivered with a fall on the last syllable and nothing after it. That is also why the main clause is so often left out entirely - 「もう少すこし早はやく言いってくれれば…」 - with the trailing silence doing what のに would have done. Both versions are casual.

～ば○○のに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Who the complaint lands on is the thing to watch. When the unmet condition is about the speaker or about the world, the sentence is simply regret, and nobody is hurt by it. When the condition is about the person you are talking to - 「連絡れんらくして くれれば迎むかえに行いったのに」 - it is a reproach, however gently you say it. Between friends and family that is normal and often affectionate. Upwards at work it is close to unusable; rebuild the thought as 「次回じかいはご連絡れんらくいただければ幸さいわいです」 and let the complaint go.

～ば○○のに · Nuance & register (cont.)

There is no polite form of のに itself. If you need the register lifted, the sentence becomes ～のですが - 時間じかんがあればお手伝てつだいできるのですが - which is measured and slightly formal, and loses most of the sting.

ば and たら are both usable in this frame and neither is wrong. ば is the tighter, more reasoned one and dominates when the sentence sounds like an argument; たら is a shade more conversational. なら is the odd one out: it can appear (私わたしが社長しゃちょうならこんな決定けっていはしないのに) but only where the condition is genuinely a premise about identity or circumstance, not an event.

～ば○○のに · ～ば○○のに vs ～ばよかった

Rule of thumb - ばよかった names the action you wish had been taken and stops there. ば…のに names the consequence you were denied. The first is self-directed regret; the second easily points at somebody else.

もっと早はやく**出でればよかった**。
I should have left earlier.

もっと早はやく**出でれば**、渋滞じゅうたいに巻まき込こまれなかったのに。
If we'd left earlier we wouldn't have got caught in the traffic.

～ば○○のに · ～ば○○のに vs ～ばよかった (cont.)

The first is a verdict on a decision. The second spells out what the decision cost. Note that only the second can comfortably take a second person as its subject - turn ばよかった on somebody else and you have to add のに anyway to make it land: 言いってくれればよかったのに.

～ば○○のに · ～ば○○のに vs ～ばいいのに

Rule of thumb - ばいいのに is about now: I wish this were the case, and it still could be. ば…のに with a past main clause is about a chance that has closed.

山田やまださんも来くればいいのに。
I wish Yamada would come too.

山田やまださんも来くれば、みんなで写真しゃしんが撮とれたのに。
If Yamada had come too, we could have had a photo with everyone in it.

The first is aimed at a party that has not started; the second at one that is over. In speech the difference is carried entirely by the tense of the main verb, so it is worth practising the pair aloud until the switch is automatic.

～ば○○のに · ～ば○○のに vs plain ～ば

Rule of thumb - without のに, the condition is left open and might yet be met. With のに, the speaker has asserted that it is not.

明日あした晴はれれば、海うみに行いきます。
If it's fine tomorrow, we'll go to the sea.

明日あした晴はれれば海うみに行いけるのに。
We could go to the sea if only it were going to be fine tomorrow.

The first is a plan with a condition attached. The second has already read the forecast. That is why のに cannot be added to a sentence about something you are genuinely waiting to find out - it gives away an answer you do not have.

～ば○○のに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ もっと安やすいければ買かうのに。 → ✅ もっと安やすければ買かうのに。
    The い of an い-adjective is replaced, not kept. やすい → やすければ.

  • ❌ 明日あしたが休やすみだなら、海うみに行いくのに。 → ✅ 明日あしたが休やすみなら、海うみに行いくのに。
    なら is itself the copula's ば-form, so no だ goes in front of it. Compare としたら, which requires one.

  • ❌ (終おわった試験しけんについて) もっと勉強べんきょうすれば合格ごうかくするのに。 → ✅ …合格ごうかくしたのに。
    The main clause carries the time. A non-past main verb says the chance is still open, which contradicts an exam you have already sat.

～ば○○のに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ (部長ぶちょうに)「手伝てつだってくださればもっと早はやく終おわったのに。」 → ✅ 「次回じかいはお手てを貸かしていただけると助たすかります。」
    Aimed upwards, のに is a complaint about a superior's conduct. Convert it into a forward-looking request.

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 早はやく言いってくれればよかった。 (b) 早はやく言いってくれれば、準備じゅんびできたのに。

(a) is a bare verdict: telling me sooner would have been the better course. Without のに it stays fairly mild and can even sound like the speaker blaming themselves for not asking.

(b) supplies the damage. It says exactly what the silence cost - the speaker could have been ready - and のに puts that cost on the person who stayed quiet. The second is noticeably sharper, and between colleagues you would soften it or leave it unsaid.

～ばよかった

Meaning · I should have ~; I wish I had ~ - looking back at a choice and wanting it back

ば names an action; よかった is いい in the past, passing judgement on the world in which that action was taken. Put together they say: doing it would have been the good outcome - and since you are saying so afterwards, you didn't.

This is the most useful single thing an intermediate learner can say about their own past. English needs four different constructions - "I should have", "I wish I'd", "I ought to have", "why didn't I" - and Japanese does all of them with one ending. It is worth over-practising.

～ばよかった · Formation

Any ば-form plus よかった. The よかった is frozen: it is always past, and it never becomes ❌ばいいでした.

～ばよかった · Examples

もっと早はやく**寝ねればよかった**。
I should have gone to bed earlier.

こんなに降ふるなら、傘かさを**持もってくればよかった**。
If it was going to rain like this, I should have brought an umbrella.

あんなこと、言いわなければよかった。
I wish I hadn't said that.

人気にんきの店みせだから、前まえの日ひに**予約よやくしておけばよかった**ですね。
It's a popular place - we should have booked the day before.

～ばよかった · Examples (cont.)

地図ちずを**確たしかめておけばよかった**。おかげで３０分ぷんも迷まよってしまった。
I should have checked the map. As it is I spent half an hour lost.

こんなに時間じかんがかかると分わかっていたら、朝あさのうちに**始はじめればよかった**。
If I'd known it would take this long, I'd have started first thing.

大学だいがくのとき、もっといろいろな人ひとと**話はなしておけばよかった**と今いまになって思おもいます。
I find myself thinking now that I should have talked to more people at university.

せっかく京都きょうとまで行いったのだから、もう一日いちにち泊とまればよかったですね。
Since we'd gone all the way to Kyoto, we really should have stayed one more night.

～ばよかった · Examples (cont.)

あのとき会社かいしゃをやめなければよかったのかな、と時々ときどき考かんがえることがあります。
Now and then I wonder whether I should have stayed at that company after all.

～ばよかった · Nuance & register

Bare ばよかった is casual, self-directed and extremely common. With なあ on the end it is close to a sigh - 買かっておけばよかったなあ - and that is how it most often turns up in speech, half muttered, with nobody expected to answer.

The polite ばよかったです is perfectly usable and you will hear it, though in careful or written Japanese ～ばよかったと思おもいます sounds more measured and less like an outburst. In an apology at work the natural frame is a humble verb: 早はやめにご報告ほうこくすればよかったです、申もうし訳わけありません。

～ばよかった · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two combinations carry most of the traffic. ておけばよかった - "should have done it in advance" - is the workhorse, because most regret is about preparation you skipped. And なければよかった is the regret of having done something, which learners chronically avoid because the double negative feels backwards in English.

Direction is what changes the temperature. Pointed at yourself, ばよかった is harmless and even endearing. Pointed at somebody else it needs のに, and then it becomes a gentle "why on earth didn't you": 言いってくれればよかったのに。 That is milder than it looks - it often functions as consolation rather than blame - but it is still a comment on their decision, so aim it downwards or sideways, not upwards.

～ばよかった · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, a limit worth respecting. ばよかった is about a choice, so its most natural subject is somebody who could have chosen otherwise. For things nobody controlled - the weather, an illness, a train - a bare ばよかった sounds incomplete; add のに and let it be a wish instead: 昨日きのう晴はれればよかったのに。

たらよかった exists and is heard, particularly in casual speech and in western Japan. ばよかった is the standard set form and the one to produce; treat たらよかった as something to recognise.

～ばよかった · ～ばよかった vs ～てよかった

Rule of thumb - てよかった looks at what did happen and is glad. ばよかった looks at what didn't and wishes it had. One is relief, the other is regret, and the only visible difference is the conditional.

早はやめに家いえを**出でてよかった**。
I'm glad I left the house early.

早はやめに家いえを**出でればよかった**。
I should have left the house early.

Both end in よかった, and beginners hear them as the same sentence. The て-form reports something real; the ば-form floats something imaginary. If you are ever unsure which one you have just said, check whether the thing actually happened.

～ばよかった · ～ばよかった vs ～ばよかったのに

Rule of thumb - without のに, the regret is yours. With のに, it is aimed at somebody else and means "you should have".

会かいに**来くればよかった**。
I should have come to the gathering.

会かいに**来くればよかったのに**。
You should have come to the gathering - it's a shame you didn't.

The second is what you say to a friend who stayed home, and it usually reads as warmth rather than criticism: we'd have liked you there. Drop the のに while talking about somebody else and the sentence sounds like you are regretting their existence at your own event, which is not the effect anyone wants.

～ばよかった · ～ばよかった vs ～べきだった / ～たほうがよかった

Rule of thumb - ばよかった is felt: it is a wish. べきだった is judged: it is an obligation you failed. たほうがよかった is weighed: of two options, the other one was better.

親おやに**相談そうだんすればよかった**。
I wish I'd talked to my parents about it.

親おやに**相談そうだんすべきだった**。
I ought to have consulted my parents.

電車でんしゃより、バスで**行いったほうがよかった**ですね。
It would have been better to go by bus than by train.

～ばよかった · ～ばよかった vs ～べきだった / ～たほうがよかった (cont.)

べきだった is stiffer, more written, and carries a moral edge - it is the form of a public apology and of self-reproach. たほうがよかった is the coolest of the three; it compares two courses without much feeling and is what you use when reviewing a decision with colleagues. ばよかった is the one that sounds like a person.

～ばよかった · Common mistakes

  • ❌ もっと野菜やさいを食たべるばよかった。 → ✅ もっと野菜やさいを食たべればよかった。
    よかった follows the ば-form. The dictionary form never appears here.

  • ❌ 傘かさを持もってこなければよかった。（meaning "I should have brought an umbrella"） → ✅ 傘かさを持もってくればよかった。
    The negative reverses the regret. As written, the sentence says you wish you had left the umbrella at home - which, standing in the rain, is the opposite of your point.

  • ❌ もっと勉強べんきょうすればいいでした。 → ✅ もっと勉強べんきょうすればよかったです。
    いい cannot be put into the past behind ば while staying いい. The past of いい is よかった, and it takes the whole meaning with it.

～ばよかった · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ (課長かちょうに)「課長かちょうが先さきに言いってくれればよかったのに。」 → ✅ 「次つぎからは早はやめに確認かくにんするようにいたします。」
    ばよかったのに is a judgement on the other person's choice. Aimed at a superior that is insubordinate however politely you say it; convert it into what you will do next time.

～ばよかった · Practice 1

1. 映画えいがのチケットが売うり切きれだった。ネットで先さきに____。

Buying in advance is exactly the "preparation I skipped" case, which is why ておけばよかった is the natural combination. A bare 買かえばよかった works too but loses the sense of advance planning.

～ばよかった · Practice 2

2. Choose: 傘かさを持もってきたから、濡ぬれずにすんだ。（持もってくればよかった・持もってきてよかった）。

The umbrella was brought, so the speaker is relieved, not regretful. 持もってくればよかった would contradict the first half of the sentence by claiming the umbrella stayed at home.

～ばよかった · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I wish I hadn't sent that email."

あのメール、送おくらなければよかった。

The regret is about something the speaker did, so the ば-form has to be negative. In a more measured register: 送おくらなければよかったと思おもっています。

～ばよかった · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 友ともだちが一人ひとりで大変たいへんそうだった。手伝てつだわなければよかった。

The negative says the speaker wishes they had not helped, which contradicts the first sentence. The regret is about help that was never offered, so the ば-form must be positive - and てあげる is what makes it clear the help was for the friend.

～ばよかった · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) もっと早はやく**言いえばよかった**。 (b) もっと早はやく**言いってくれればよかったのに**。

(a) is the speaker taking it on themselves: I had the information and I sat on it. It is an apology in embryo, and in a workplace it is often the polite thing to say even when the fault was shared.

(b) hands it back. くれる makes the other person the one who failed to speak, and のに supplies the disappointment. Between friends it is close to "you should have said!" and is usually said kindly - but it is still an allocation of blame, so it does not travel upwards.

～つもりだった

Meaning · I had meant to (but didn't); I thought I had (but I hadn't) - an intention or a belief that reality declined to confirm

つもり is what is in your head. Put it in the past and you announce that what was in your head and what happened in the world came apart. Nothing in the words says the plan failed - but the past tense sets up a "but" so reliably that a Japanese listener hears it before you finish the sentence.

The pattern has two readings, and which one you get depends entirely on the tense of the verb in front:

～つもりだった · Overview

  • 出だすつもりだった - I meant to send it. (I didn't.)
  • 出だしたつもりだった - I thought I had sent it. (I hadn't.)

～つもりだった · Formation

つもり is a noun, so what precedes it takes the shape a noun-modifying clause takes: な-adjectives get な, nouns get の.

Verb - mistaken belief plain past + つもりだった かけた → かけたつもりだった
Verb - belief about a state ている + つもりだった やっている → やっているつもりだった
い-adjective plain form + つもりだった 優やさしい → 優やさしいつもりだった

Rule of thumb - つもり needs a will behind it. Only something that can decide things can have one. A train, a parcel or the weather cannot: for those the pattern is はずだった.

～つもりだった · Examples

今日きょう銀行ぎんこうに行いくつもりだったんですが、時間じかんがありませんでした。
I'd meant to go to the bank today, but I didn't have time.

鍵かぎをかけたつもりだったのに、ドアが開あいていた。
I could have sworn I locked up, but the door was open.

あれは冗談じょうだんのつもりだったんです。本当ほんとうにすみませんでした。
It was meant as a joke. I really am sorry.

自分じぶんでは丁寧ていねいに説明せつめいしたつもりだったが、まったく伝つたわっていなかった。
I thought I'd explained it carefully, but none of it had got through.

～つもりだった · Examples (cont.)

大学だいがくを出でたら地元じもとに帰かえるつもりだったけれど、結局けっきょく東京とうきょうに残のこった。
I'd intended to go back home after university, but in the end I stayed in Tokyo.

お金かねは全部ぜんぶ返かえしたつもりでしたが、まだ三千円さんぜんえん残のこっていたようです。
I was under the impression I'd paid it all back, but apparently three thousand yen is still outstanding.

週末しゅうまつは何なにもしないつもりだったのに、気きがついたら一日中いちにちじゅう片付かたづけをしていた。
I'd planned to do nothing all weekend, and then found I'd spent the whole day tidying.

部長ぶちょうにはお伝つたえしたつもりでしたが、私わたしの勘違かんちがいだったようです。申もうし訳わけございません。
I was sure I'd passed it on to the manager, but it seems I was mistaken. My apologies.

～つもりだった · Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral - つもりでした sits comfortably in a business email - but it is socially loaded, because most of the time you are using it to explain yourself.

The intention reading is the everyday softener of excuses. The full apology shape is ～つもりだったんですが with a trailing clause: 連絡れんらくするつもりだったんです が、会議かいぎが長ながびいてしまって。 The unfinished ending is deliberate. It lets the listener supply the conclusion, which is more polite than announcing it.

～つもりだった · Nuance & register (cont.)

The mistaken-belief reading (～たつもりだった) is the Japanese for "I could have sworn I…". It is honest and unglamorous, and it is what you say when you are the one who dropped something. Its slightly sharper cousin is ～ているつもりだった - "I thought I was doing that" - which comes up when somebody tells you your effort has not shown: 気きをつけているつもりだったんですが。

There is one construction here you should learn as a unit: ～つもりはなかった, "I didn't mean to." 傷きずつけるつもりはありませんでした is the standard way to apologise for an accidental hurt. Note the は: it is contrastive, and it does the work of "whatever else I may have done, that was not my intention." The casual version is つもりじゃなかった.

～つもりだった · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - do not confuse this with ～たつもりで, a separate idiom meaning "as if you had": 旅行りょこうに行いったつもりで貯金ちょきんする - "save the money as though you'd taken the trip." The で form is a way of pretending on purpose; the だった form is finding out you were wrong.

～つもりだった · ～つもりだった vs ～はずだった

Rule of thumb - つもり is what you decided. はず is what the facts led you to expect. If somebody asked どうして？ and your answer would be "because I'd made up my mind to", it is つもり; if it would be "because that's what the schedule said", it is はず.

七時しちじの電車でんしゃに乗のるつもりだったんだけど、寝坊ねぼうした。
I'd meant to catch the seven o'clock train, but I overslept.

七時しちじの電車でんしゃは八時はちじに着つくはずだったのに、まだ動うごかない。
The seven o'clock was supposed to arrive at eight, and it still hasn't moved.

Only a person can hold an intention, which is why the second sentence cannot take つもり. Trains have timetables, not plans.

～つもりだった · ～るつもりだった vs ～たつもりだった

Rule of thumb - non-past in front means the action was never performed. Past in front means you believe it was performed, and you were wrong.

窓まどを**閉しめるつもりだった**。
I meant to close the window.

窓まどを**閉しめたつもりだった**。
I thought I'd closed the window.

In the first, the window was never touched. In the second, you did something to it and it wasn't enough. That is a real difference in what you are confessing to, and getting it backwards in an apology gives entirely the wrong impression of how careful you were.

～つもりだった · ～つもりだった vs ～(よ)うと思っていた

Rule of thumb - つもりだった is a decision you were carrying around. ～(よ)うと思おもっていた is a thought you were entertaining. The second is vaguer, and therefore softer.

来月らいげつ引ひっ越こすつもりだったんですが、事情じじょうが変かわりました。
I'd been planning to move next month, but circumstances changed.

来月らいげつ引ひっ越こそうと思おもっていたんですが、まだ決きめていません。
I'd been thinking of moving next month, but I haven't decided yet.

～つもりだった · ～つもりだった vs ～(よ)うと思っていた (cont.)

Because つもり reports something settled, it can sound firm when you are asked about your plans by somebody with authority over them. If you want to leave yourself room, 思おもっていた is the more diplomatic choice.

～つもりだった · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 行いきますつもりでした。 → ✅ 行いくつもりでした。
    つもり is a noun and takes a plain noun-modifying clause. The politeness goes on the end of the sentence, never inside it.

  • ❌ 冗談じょうだんつもりだったんです。 → ✅ 冗談じょうだんのつもりだったんです。
    A noun in front of つもり needs の; a な-adjective needs な (親切しんせつなつもり).

  • ❌ 電車でんしゃは三時さんじに着つくつもりだった。 → ✅ 電車でんしゃは三時さんじに着つくはずだった。
    つもり requires somebody with a will. Timetables, deliveries and the weather take はずだった.

～つもりだった · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 怒おこらせるつもりじゃなかったです。 → ✅ 怒おこらせるつもりはありませんでした。
    The meaning is right but the registers clash: casual じゃなかった under a polite です. In an apology, use the full form.

The reply was never written, so the verb in front stays non-past. んですが with a trailing explanation is the standard apology frame for an intention that fell through.

The past-tense verb in front is what gives the "I thought I had" reading. With 消けすつもりだった the sentence would mean you never touched it, which contradicts the second half.

As written, the sentence apologises by announcing that being rude was the plan. To deny an intention you need つもりはない in the past, with the contrastive は carrying "that, at least, was not what I meant."

～つもりだった · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 部屋へやを**片付かたづけるつもりだった**。 (b) 部屋へやを**片付かたづけたつもりだった**。

(b) says the speaker did tidy - and then somebody looked at the room and disagreed. It is a confession of a judgement that was wrong rather than a plan that was dropped, and it carries a faint note of being caught out.

～はずだった

Meaning · it was supposed to ~, and it didn't - a well-grounded expectation that the world declined to meet

At N4, はずだ was an expectation you could justify if asked: a timetable, a promise, a rule, a fact somebody told you. はずだった keeps the grounds and loses the outcome. It says this is what should have followed from what I knew - and the fact that you are saying it in the past tense tells the listener that it did not.

Where つもりだった reports a plan of your own that came to nothing, はずだった reports the world failing to behave as the evidence promised. Nobody's will is involved, which is why it works for parcels, trains and weather forecasts.

～はずだった · Formation

はず is a noun, so the clause in front takes noun-modifying shapes: な for な-adjectives, の for nouns.

～はずだった · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb plain non-past + はずだった 届とどく → 届とどくはずだった
Verb (past) た-form + はずだった 入いれた → 入いれたはずだった

い-adjective plain form + はずだった 安やすい → 安やすいはずだった

Noun Noun + の + はずだった 休やすみ → 休やすみのはずだった

～はずだった · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - say はずだった and you have as good as said the rest. If the expectation was met, you do not use this pattern at all - you report the fact, or say 予定よていどおり. Using はずだった about something that worked out puzzles the listener, who is still waiting for the "but".

～はずだった · Examples

会議かいぎは三時さんじに終おわるはずだった。
The meeting was supposed to finish at three.

今日きょうは休やすみのはずだったのに、急きゅうに呼よび出だされた。
Today was supposed to be my day off, and then I got called in out of nowhere.

財布さいふにお金かねが入はいっているはずだったんですが、空からっぽでした。
There was supposed to be money in my wallet, but it was empty.

簡単かんたんな仕事しごとのはずでしたが、けっきょく三日みっかもかかりました。
It was meant to be a simple job, and it ended up taking three days.

～はずだった · Examples (cont.)

新あたらしいパソコンは昨日きのう届とどくはずでしたが、まだ届とどいておりません。
The new laptop was due to arrive yesterday, but it still hasn't come.

あの試合しあいは、最後さいごの５分ふんまでは、うちのチームが勝かつはずだった。
Until the last five minutes, that match was ours to win.

留学りゅうがくして一年いちねんで帰かえってくるはずだった弟おとうとが、もう五年ごねん向むこうに住すんでいる。
My brother, who was supposed to study abroad for a year and come home, has been living over there for five now.

書類しょるいは先週せんしゅうのうちに全部ぜんぶお渡わたししたはずでしたが、一枚いちまい足たりなかったようです。
I was sure I'd handed over all the documents last week, but it seems one was missing.

～はずだった · Nuance & register

はずだった is register-neutral and travels everywhere, which makes it unusually useful at work. The polite はずでした plus a trailing が is the standard way of raising a problem without accusing anybody: 本日ほんじつ午前中ごぜんちゅうに届とどく はずでしたが、まだ確認かくにんできておりません。 It is firm - you are pointing out that a commitment was not kept - while attributing the failure to the situation rather than to the person you are speaking to. That combination is hard to achieve in English and easy here.

～はずだった · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two flavours are worth separating. With a non-past clause in front, the pattern is about a scheduled or predicted event that failed to occur. With a past clause (渡わたしたはずだった) it is about your own confident memory being wrong - "I was sure I had." That second one shades into ～たつもりだった, and the difference is where the confidence came from: つもり from your intention, はず from evidence you thought you had.

The negative deserves its own line. こんなはずではなかった - "this isn't how it was supposed to go" - is a fixed phrase, slightly dramatic, and extremely common in novels, films and genuine bad days. Its casual form is こんなはずじゃなかった.

～はずだった · Nuance & register (cont.)

One honest qualification. In a narrative, はずだった can simply report what was expected at a point in the story, and the next sentence may confirm it. But in conversation, standing alone, it is heard as a defeated expectation almost without exception - so treat the failure reading as the default and do not rely on the neutral one.

～はずだった · ～はずだった vs ～つもりだった

Rule of thumb - つもり is what somebody decided; はず is what the evidence implied. Only a being with a will can have a つもり; anything at all can have a はず.

明日あした休やすみを取とるつもりだったんですが、仕事しごとが入はいりました。
I'd been meaning to take tomorrow off, but work came up.

明日あしたは会社かいしゃが休やすみのはずだったんですが、出勤しゅっきんになりました。
Tomorrow was supposed to be a company holiday, but now we're working.

～はずだった · ～はずだった vs ～つもりだった (cont.)

The first is about your own decision; the second about the calendar. Note what happens when you get it backwards: ❌電車でんしゃが遅おくれるつもりだった attributes an intention to a train, which is not merely unusual but funny.

～はずだった · ～はずだった vs ～予定だった

Rule of thumb - 予定よていだった reports a schedule and says nothing about whether it held. はずだった reports an expectation and implies it did not.

来週らいしゅう大阪おおさかに出張しゅっちょうする**予定よていでした**が、中止ちゅうしになりました。
I was scheduled to go to Osaka next week, but it's been called off.

飛行機ひこうきは十時じゅうじに着つくはずでした。
The plane was supposed to land at ten.

～はずだった · ～はずだった vs ～予定だった (cont.)

予定よていだった can be followed by "…and that's what happened", which is why it is the neutral word for an itinerary. はずでした said on its own already tells the listener the plane is late. If the flight landed on time, you say 予定よていどおり十時じゅうじに着つきました and the はず never appears.

～はずだった · ～はずだった vs ～ことになっていた

Rule of thumb - ことになっていた reports an arrangement somebody made - a rule, an agreement, a rota. はずだった reports your inference from whatever grounds you had, which may include such an arrangement.

受付うけつけは私わたしが担当たんとうすることになっていたのに、誰だれも教おしえてくれなかった。
I was down to cover reception, and nobody told me.

受付うけつけには誰だれかいるはずだったのに、席せきが空あいていた。
There was supposed to be somebody on reception, but the desk was empty.

The first points at a decision that was taken; the second at a conclusion you drew. ことになっていた is the better choice whenever you can name who agreed to what, and it is the safer one in a workplace because it appeals to a shared arrangement rather than to your own reasoning.

～はずだった · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 簡単かんたんはずだったのに。 → ✅ 簡単かんたんなはずだったのに。
    はず is a noun, so a な-adjective needs な and a noun needs の (休やすみのはず). Compare かもしれない, which takes both bare.

  • ❌ 明日あしたは七時しちじに起おきるはずだった。（meaning "I meant to get up at seven"） → ✅ 明日あしたは七時しちじに起おきるつもりだった。
    Your own resolve is つもり. はず would need external grounds - an alarm somebody else set, a schedule you were given.

  • ❌ 予定よていどおり三時さんじに着つくはずでした。（meaning "and we did"） → ✅ 予定よていどおり三時さんじに**着つきました**。
    はずでした announces that the expectation was not met. If it was, simply report what happened.

～はずだった · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ こんなはずじゃなかったです。 → ✅ こんなはずではありませんでした。
    The casual contraction under a polite です clashes. In careful speech, expand it - or keep the whole sentence casual.

～はずだった · Practice 1

1. この電車でんしゃは各駅かくえきに止とまらない____のに、次つぎの駅えきで止とまった。

止とまらないはずだったのに

The expectation came from what kind of train it is, not from anyone's intention, so はず is the right noun. のに then supplies the contradiction that the second half spells out.

～はずだった · Practice 2

2. Choose: 今年ことしこそ海外かいがい旅行りょこうに行いく（つもりだった・はずだった）のに、結局けっきょく行いけなかった。

行いくつもりだったのに

今年ことしこそ marks this as the speaker's own resolve, and resolve is つもり territory. はずだった would need something external - a booking already made, a trip somebody else had arranged for you.

～はずだった · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The package was supposed to arrive on Friday, but it still hasn't come."

荷物にもつは金曜日きんようびに届とどくはずでしたが、まだ届とどいていません。

The expectation rests on a delivery date, so はず rather than つもり. The polite はずでした plus が is the standard shape for raising this kind of problem without blaming the person you are speaking to.

～はずだった · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 昨日きのうの会議かいぎは休やすみはずでしたが、急きゅうに行おこなわれました。

✅ 昨日きのうの会議かいぎは**中止ちゅうしのはず**でしたが、急きゅうに行おこなわれました。

Two things: a noun before はず needs の, and 休やすみ is odd for a meeting - a cancelled meeting is 中止ちゅうし. The はずでした frame itself is exactly right.

～はずだった · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この部屋へやの鍵かぎは閉しめたはずだった。 (b) この部屋へやの鍵かぎは閉しめたつもりだった。

(a) rests on evidence: the speaker remembers the click, or knows it is on their checklist every evening. The surprise is that the facts they were relying on have let them down.

(b) rests on intention: the speaker meant to lock it and believed they had followed through. It admits more personal responsibility, which is why (b) is the better sentence in an apology and (a) the better one when you are genuinely baffled.
