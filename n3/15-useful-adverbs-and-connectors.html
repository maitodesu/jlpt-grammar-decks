だけど

Meaning · but; however - the casual sentence-opening "but"

だけど opens a new sentence that pushes back on the one just said. It is the everyday spoken "however": friends use it constantly, and it is one of the few words you should deliberately leave outside a job interview.

だけど · Usage

だけど stands at the head of a sentence, with a comma after it in writing, and the objection follows. It does not conjugate, take particles or attach to anything - it is a fixed one-word opener.

  • 「明日あした、海うみに行いこうよ。」「だけど、台風たいふうが来くるらしいよ。」

Historically it is the copula だ plus the clause-final けど you met at N5, and that history explains the one place learners reliably go wrong. After a noun or な-adjective the same string turns up inside a sentence, because the だ belongs to the first clause: 学生がくせいだけど、もう働はたらいている. After a verb or い-adjective there is no だ available, so it is plain けど: 安やすいけど使つかいにくい - never ❌安やすいだけど.

だけど · Usage (cont.)

Sentence-initially, then, だけど is always available. Mid-sentence it is only available after a noun or な-adjective, and there it is really two words wearing one coat.

One more very common shape: ～んだけど, which prefaces a request or a piece of news - ちょっと聞ききたいことがあるんだけど… - where the trailing だけど softens the approach rather than contradicting anything.

だけど · Examples

今日きょうは寒さむい。だけど、雪ゆきは降ふらないと思おもう。
It's cold today. But I don't think it'll snow.

「もう一杯いっぱいどう?」「だけど、明日あした早はやいんだよね。」
"Another drink?" "But I've got an early start tomorrow."

三年さんねん習ならった。だけど、まだ電話でんわで話はなすのはこわい。
I studied it for three years. But phone calls still terrify me.

部屋へやは狭せまいし、駅えきからも遠とおい。だけど、この家賃やちんなら文句もんくは言いえない。
The flat is cramped and far from the station. But at this rent I can't complain.

だけど · Nuance & register

Casual. だけど belongs to conversation with friends and family, to messages, and to relaxed writing such as blog posts. In a business email, an essay or anything said to a customer, use ですが or しかし instead. Roughly, the ladder runs しかし (formal, written) - ですが (polite) - でも (neutral casual) - だけど (casual) - けど (very casual, often trailing off).

Against でも, だけど tends to be the firmer of the two. でも can register a mild reservation; だけど picks up the previous statement and argues with it, so opening several sentences in a row with it comes across as combative.

だけれど and だけれども are the unshortened forms: a shade more careful and slightly bookish, and they lean older in speech. だけどさ, だけどね do the opposite, softening the pushback with a final particle. None of these are marked for gender.

だけど · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note also that だけど is not a subordinating conjunction. It cannot join two clauses into one sentence the way が or けど do; it needs a full stop in front of it, or at least the pause that a full stop would represent.

だけど · だけど vs でも

Rule of thumb - same slot, same register, different force. でも raises a qualification; だけど contradicts.

「これ、おいしいね。」「でも、ちょっと甘あますぎない?」
"This is good." "Though - isn't it a bit too sweet?"

「これ、おいしいね。」「だけど、値段ねだんを見みた?」
"This is good." "But did you see the price?"

Both are natural; the second sounds more like the start of an argument. When you are not sure which you want, でも is the safer default.

だけど · だけど vs けど

Rule of thumb - だけど begins a sentence; けど ends a clause. They are not interchangeable positions.

時間じかんはあるけど、お金かねがない。
I have the time, but not the money.

時間じかんはある。だけど、お金かねがない。
I have the time. But I don't have the money.

だけど · だけど vs けど (cont.)

The content is identical; the second version breaks it into two beats and lands harder. Sentence-initial けど alone does exist in very casual speech - 「けど、無理むりでしょ」 - but だけど is the standard choice there.

だけど · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 時間じかんがないだけど、行いきます。 → ✅ 時間じかんがないけど、行いきます。
    A verb or い-adjective cannot be followed by だ. Only nouns and な-adjectives can carry the だ that makes だけど.

  • ❌ 静しずかですだけど → ✅ 静しずかですけど
    です is already the copula; adding だ on top of it doubles up. In polite speech the clause-final connector is ですけど or ですが.

  • ❌（取引先とりひきさきへのメールで）だけど、納期のうきは来週らいしゅうになります。 → ✅ しかし、納期のうきは来週らいしゅうになります。
    だけど in business writing reads as though you forgot who you were talking to. しかし or ですが carries the same meaning at the right register.

だけど · Practice 1

1. 「明日あしたのパーティー、来くる?」「行いきたい。____、バイトなんだ。」

「行いきたい。だけど、バイトなんだ。」

The speaker has finished one sentence and starts another that contradicts it, so the connector has to be the sentence-initial kind. でも would also fit here and would sound a little softer.

だけど · Practice 2

2. Find and fix the error: 高たかいだけど、けっきょく買かうことにした。

✅ 高たかいけど、けっきょく買かうことにした。

高たかい is an い-adjective, and い-adjectives are never followed by だ. Splitting it into two sentences - 高たかい。だけど、買かうことにした。 - works too.

だけど · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) だけど、私わたしはそうは思おもわない。 (b) ですが、私わたしはそうは思おもいません。

The grammar is the same and the meaning is the same: both reject what was just said. The difference is entirely register. Sentence (a) is what you say to a friend over coffee, while (b) is what you say in a meeting or write in an essay. Using (a) in a formal discussion would sound abrupt and a little rude, and using (b) with a close friend would sound stiff and distant.

ところで

Meaning · by the way - putting the current topic down and picking up a new one

ところで is a signal, not a link. It tells the listener that what follows has nothing to do with what came before, and that the speaker is doing it on purpose. Nine times out of ten, what follows is a question the speaker has been sitting on.

ところで · Usage

ところで goes at the head of a sentence, followed by a comma or a pause. It does not attach to a word, does not conjugate, and never appears in the middle of a clause.

  • ところで、来月らいげつの旅行りょこうはどうなりましたか。
  • ところで、あの話はなし、覚おぼえてる?

ところで · Usage (cont.)

Two things it is not. It is not "however" - it contradicts nothing, so ❌ところで、私わたしは反対はんたいです is wrong where しかし was wanted. And it is not the ところ family of grammar points: the ～たところで of 急いそいだところで間まに合あわない ("even if you hurry…") and the ～ているところ of 今いま食たべているところです ("in the middle of eating") are separate constructions that merely look alike. The ところで of this page always begins a sentence and never follows a verb.

Because the whole point of the word is to close one subject and open another, it usually appears once in a conversation, at a natural seam - after a topic has run its course, not on top of someone else who is still talking.

ところで · Examples

ところで、山田やまださんは元気げんき?
By the way, how's Yamada doing?

ところで、昼ひるごはんはもう食たべましたか。
By the way, have you had lunch yet?

資料しりょうは以上いじょうです。ところで、来週らいしゅうの会議かいぎは何時なんじからでしたか。
That's everything in the handouts. By the way, what time was next week's meeting again?

写真しゃしん、ありがとう。すごくよく撮とれてるね。ところで、あのカメラっていくらぐらいしたの?
Thanks for the photos - they came out really well. By the way, roughly how much was that camera?

ところで · Nuance & register

ところで is register-neutral in itself: it works in ます-form speech and in plain speech, and it appears in writing wherever a writer changes tack. What is not neutral is the act it performs. Announcing a change of subject is a small claim on the floor, so it lands most comfortably when you have the standing to make it - among equals, or from whoever is hosting the conversation.

Speaking to a customer or someone well above you, the same move is usually padded: 話はなしは変かわりますが、… or それはそうと、… or simply 一ひとつお伺うかがいしてもよろしいでしょうか. ところで itself is not rude - it is used in business meetings constantly - but it is brisk, and dropped straight after someone has said something emotionally weighty it can sound as though you were not listening.

There is no casual contraction of it, and it is not marked for age or gender.

ところで · ところで vs ちなみに

Rule of thumb - ところで drops the current topic. ちなみに stays on it and adds a footnote.

このカフェ、安やすくておいしいよ。ちなみに、夜よるは十時じゅうじまでやってる。
This café is cheap and good. Incidentally, it's open until ten at night.

このカフェ、安やすくておいしいよ。ところで、引ひっ越こしの準備じゅんびは進すすんでる?
This café is cheap and good. Anyway - how's the packing for your move coming along?

Swapping them breaks both sentences: the opening hours are plainly still about the café, and the packing plainly is not. A quick test - if the new sentence would also work with そういえば in front of it, ところで is the right choice.

ところで · Common mistakes

  • ❌ ところで、この案あんには賛成さんせいできません。 → ✅ しかし、この案あんには賛成さんせいできません。
    ところで changes the subject; it does not disagree. Learners reach for it because it looks like a conjunction, but it carries no adversative meaning at all.

  • ❌ 映画えいがを見みたところで、晩ばんごはんは何なににする?（meaning "by the way"）→ ✅ 映画えいが、面白おもしろかったね。ところで、晩ばんごはんは何なににする?
    Placed after a verb, the same syllables become the pattern meaning "even if", and the sentence turns into "even if I watch the film…". Keep it at the front.

  • ❌ ところでですが、… → ✅ ところで、…
    ところで already contains everything it needs, and ですが does not politen it. If you want a politer version, replace the whole word with 話はなしは変かわりますが.

ところで · Practice 1

1. ____、田中たなかさんの連絡先れんらくさきを知しっていますか。

ところで、田中たなかさんの連絡先れんらくさきを知しっていますか。

A question with no connection to the previous topic needs a marker warning the listener to switch tracks, and that is exactly the job this word does.

ところで · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural connector: 明日あしたの会場かいじょうは市民しみんホールです。（ところで・ちなみに）、駐車場ちゅうしゃじょうは有料ゆうりょうです。

ちなみに、駐車場ちゅうしゃじょうは有料ゆうりょうです。

The parking charge is a further detail about the same venue, so the topic has not changed at all. ところで would promise the listener something unrelated and then fail to deliver it.

ところで · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) ところで、部長ぶちょう、そろそろ失礼しつれいしてもよろしいでしょうか。 (b) 話はなしは変かわりますが、部長ぶちょう、そろそろ失礼しつれいしてもよろしいでしょうか。

Both change the subject and both are grammatical. Sentence (a) is brisk: the speaker announces the switch in one word and moves on, which is fine between colleagues but a little bald when addressing a superior. Sentence (b) spends a few extra syllables acknowledging that a switch is being made, and that small detour is what makes it sound considerate.

つまり

Meaning · in other words; which means - restating something more plainly, or spelling out what it amounts to

つまり announces that the same content is coming round again in a clearer shape. It does two jobs with one word: rewording something complicated, and stating the consequence nobody has said out loud yet.

つまり · Usage

つまり is an adverb and sits at the front of the clause it introduces. Nothing attaches to it, and it never changes form.

The habit worth building is what comes at the end of that clause. A つまり sentence very often closes with ということだ, わけだ or のだ, because you are labelling a conclusion rather than reporting a fresh fact:

  • つまり、来こられないということですね。
  • 三年さんねん住すんでいた。つまり、地元じもとのようなものだ。

The two jobs, one line each:

つまり · Usage (cont.)

  • Rewording - the second half says the same thing in easier words.
    検討けんとうします。つまり、今いまは決きめられないということです。
  • Consequence - the second half is what the first one comes to.
    終電しゅうでんはもう出でた。つまり、歩あるいて帰かえるしかない。

As a standalone check on your understanding, つまり with a rising question is extremely common: 「つまり、明日あしたは休やすみってこと?」

つまり · Examples

父ちちの姉あね、つまりおばです。
My father's older sister - my aunt, in other words.

返事へんじがない。つまり、断ことわられたということだ。
There's been no reply. Which means I've been turned down.

材料費ざいりょうひも送料そうりょうも上あがっている。つまり、値上ねあげは避さけられないわけです。
Materials and shipping have both gone up. In other words, a price rise can't be avoided.

「前向まえむきに検討けんとうします」って言いわれたんだけど、つまり断ことわられたってこと?
They said they'd "consider it positively" - so does that mean I've been refused?

つまり · Nuance & register

Register-neutral and very frequent. It is at home in a lecture, a contract summary, a news broadcast and a conversation between friends, with no change of form.

In speech it has a second life as a filler. Some speakers open every third sentence with つまり、つまりですね… while they assemble a thought, and the word then carries no summarising meaning at all. Worth recognising, not worth imitating.

One genuine risk: aiming つまり at what somebody else just said can sound like a correction - "so what you actually meant was…". A softener fixes it: つまり、こういうことでしょうか. Aimed at your own words it is completely safe, and in a presentation it is a useful signpost that the summary has arrived.

つまり · Nuance & register (cont.)

つまり is not a cause-and-effect connector. だから and したがって say "therefore, here is a new fact"; つまり says "the same fact, re-expressed". When the second sentence contains information the first one did not, you want だから.

つまり · つまり vs 要するに

Rule of thumb - つまり re-expresses; 要ようするに compresses. The second one claims the right to decide what the point was.

会場かいじょうが変かわりました。つまり、駅えきの南口みなみぐちに集合しゅうごうです。
The venue has changed - that is, we meet at the south exit of the station.

説明せつめいは長ながかったが、要ようするに予算よさんが足たりないのだ。
The explanation went on a while, but the gist is that there isn't enough budget.

要ようするに implies the original was long-winded, so pointing it at a superior's remarks is risky. つまり carries no such judgement.

つまり · つまり vs すなわち

Rule of thumb - same job, different clothes. すなわち is the written, formal register: it puts an equals sign between two terms.

日本にほんの首都しゅと、すなわち東京とうきょうに本社ほんしゃを置おいている。
Our head office is in the capital of Japan, namely Tokyo.

In conversation すなわち sounds like a legal document being read aloud. Reach for つまり when you are speaking, and keep すなわち for formal writing, where it is usually left in kana.

つまり · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 雨あめが降ふっていた。つまり、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしになった。 → ✅ 雨あめが降ふっていた。だから、試合しあいは中止ちゅうしになった。
    The cancellation is a new event, not a restatement of the rain. つまり needs the second sentence to be the same content in another form.

  • ❌ つまり三人さんにん来きました。 → ✅ 全部ぜんぶで三人さんにん来きました。
    つまり cannot introduce a plain new fact out of nowhere. Something has to have been said already for it to restate.

  • ❌ 要ようするに、先生せんせいのおっしゃりたいことは…（said to your own teacher）→ ✅ つまり、先生せんせいのおっしゃりたいことは…
    Summarising a superior's words with 要ようするに hints that they rambled. つまり is the neutral choice whenever the words being restated are not your own.

つまり · Practice 1

1. 木曜もくようも金曜きんようも予定よていが入はいっている。____、今週こんしゅうはもう時間じかんがない。

つまり、今週こんしゅうはもう時間じかんがない。

The second sentence adds no new information - it is the first one seen from a step back. That is exactly the restating job, and だから would also be possible if you wanted to present it as a result rather than a rewording.

つまり · Practice 2

2. Find and fix the error: 部長ぶちょうのお話はなしは、要ようするに予算よさんを減へらせということですね。

✅ 部長ぶちょうのお話はなしは、つまり予算よさんを減へらせということですね。

Grammatically the original is fine, but 要ようするに tells your manager that his explanation needed boiling down. つまり restates without passing judgement on the length of the original.

つまり · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 会員かいいんは無料むりょうです。つまり、お金かねはかかりません。 (b) 会員かいいんは無料むりょうです。だから、みんな入はいりたがります。

In (a) the two halves are the same fact twice, the second time in plainer words, so つまり is doing a translation job for the listener. In (b) the second half is a separate fact that follows from the first, which calls for a causal connector. The test is simple: if deleting the second clause loses no information at all, つまり is the word you want.

たとえば

Meaning · for example; say - pulling one concrete case out of a general statement

たとえば warns the listener that what follows is an illustration, not a new claim. The example is offered as one of several possibilities, which is why it so often arrives with とか or など trailing behind it.

たとえば · Usage

たとえば normally stands at the head of the sentence, but it can also sit directly in front of the noun phrase it is introducing:

  • 果物くだものが好すきです。たとえば、みかんとかぶどうとか。
  • 近ちかくの店みせ、たとえばコンビニでも買かえます。

Three shapes carry most of its use:

たとえば · Usage (cont.)

  • たとえば～とか／など - a list left deliberately open.
    たとえば漢字かんじのアプリなど、毎日まいにち使つかえるものがいいです。
  • たとえば～のような／みたいな + noun - the example modifies a noun.
    たとえば京都きょうとのような古ふるい町まち
  • たとえば～なら - set up a hypothetical case and then say what happens in it.
    たとえば雨あめなら、中なかでやりましょう。

In kanji it is 例たとえば, and both spellings are completely normal: printed prose and official documents tend to use the kanji, casual writing the kana. The reading and the meaning are identical either way.

たとえば · Usage (cont.)

Do not confuse it with たとえ, which is a different word entirely - see the contrast below.

たとえば · Examples

甘あまいものが好すきです。たとえば、チョコレートやアイスです。
I like sweet things - chocolate and ice cream, for example.

たとえば、この漢字かんじは読よみ方かたが三みっつあります。
This kanji, for instance, has three different readings.

休やすみの日ひにできること、たとえば散歩さんぽとか料理りょうりとかを増ふやしたい。
I want more things I can do on a day off - walks, cooking, that sort of thing.

たとえば地震じしんが起おきたら、まず机つくえの下したに入はいってください。
Say an earthquake happens - the first thing to do is get under a desk.

たとえば · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. Nothing about たとえば marks formality, age or gender, and it appears unchanged in academic writing and in chat with friends.

Two conversational uses are worth memorising whole. 「たとえばの話はなしだけど」 prefaces a hypothetical you are not committing to - "just supposing, though". And 「たとえば?」 said alone is a request for one: "such as?" That one-word question is enormously useful and very common.

In writing, a たとえば sentence is usually short and ends with など, といった or a plain noun phrase, and the paragraph then returns to the general point. If you find yourself giving three sentences of example, consider 具体ぐたい的てきには ("specifically") instead, which promises a fuller account.

たとえば · Nuance & register (cont.)

たとえば presents one member of a set. When you want to say that your example is the representative one - the first of many, the flagship - Japanese has をはじめ for that, and it is not interchangeable.

たとえば · たとえば vs たとえ○○ても

Rule of thumb - たとえば introduces an example. たとえ introduces a concession, and it always has a ても waiting for it later in the sentence.

たとえば明日あした雨あめが降ふったら、どうしますか。
If it rains tomorrow, say, what will you do?

たとえ明日あした雨あめが降ふっても、試合しあいはやります。
Even if it rains tomorrow, the match is going ahead.

The first sentence asks about one possible case among many; the second brushes the case aside. The ば is the whole difference in writing, and in speech the giveaway is the ても at the far end: if you hear one, the word at the front was たとえ.

たとえば · Common mistakes

  • ❌ たとえ日本料理にほんりょうりが好すきです。 → ✅ たとえば日本料理にほんりょうりが好すきです。
    Without the ば the word means "even if", and the sentence is left dangling with nothing for it to concede to.

  • ❌ たとえば、日本にほんの人口じんこうは約やく一億いちおく二千万人にせんまんにんです。 → ✅ 日本にほんの人口じんこうは約やく一億いちおく二千万人にせんまんにんです。
    A single hard fact is not an example of anything. たとえば needs a general statement in front of it for the example to illustrate.

  • ❌ 野菜やさい、たとえばにんじんをはじめ買かいました。 → ✅ 野菜やさい、たとえばにんじんなどを買かいました。
    たとえば pairs with など, とか and といった. をはじめ is a different pattern meaning "starting with", and the two cannot be stacked.

たとえば · Practice 1

1. 運動うんどう不足ぶそくなので、____階段かいだんを使つかうようにしています。

たとえば階段かいだんを使つかうようにしています。

The stairs are one concrete instance of the general problem stated first, which is what this word is for. Note that the example still needs the general statement in front of it to lean on.

たとえば · Practice 2

2. Choose the right word: （たとえ・たとえば）失敗しっぱいしても、やってみる価値かちはある。

たとえ失敗しっぱいしても、やってみる価値かちはある。

The ても later in the sentence is the tell: this is a concession, "even if I fail", not an illustration. たとえば would leave the ても with no partner.

たとえば · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 静しずかな町まち、たとえば奈良ならのような町まちに住すみたい。 (b) 静しずかな町まち、奈良ならに住すみたい。

Sentence (a) uses Nara as an illustration of the kind of town the speaker wants; other quiet towns would do just as well, and nobody would be surprised if they ended up somewhere else. Sentence (b) names Nara as the actual destination and describes it as quiet along the way. The example frame is what keeps the choice open.

確かに

Meaning · certainly; admittedly; you're right - vouching for something, or granting a point before you argue with it

確たしかに has two lives. It can mean "definitely, without doubt", said of something you are sure of. Far more often at this level it means "granted" - you concede what the other person said, and then the が or けど arrives.

確かに · Usage

確たしかに is an adverb: it goes in front of the predicate, or at the head of the sentence, and never changes shape. Three uses:

  • Conceding - the big one. 確たしかに～が／けど、～. The first half agrees,
    the second half pushes back. 確たしかに値段ねだんは高たかいが、質しつはいい。
  • Vouching - "for certain", of an event you witnessed or a fact you can stand
    behind. 昨日きのう、確たしかに渡わたしました。
  • Agreeing, on its own - 「確たしかに。」 is a complete turn in
    conversation, meaning "true" or "fair point". This is very common in modern casual speech.

確かに · Usage (cont.)

The single most useful thing to remember is the concessive frame. Whenever an essay or a discussion needs "it's true that…, but…", 確たしかに at the front and が in the middle is the standard shape, and a listener hears the が coming the moment you say the first word.

Kanji 確たしかに and kana たしかに are both normal; the kanji is the default in print.

確かに · Examples

「この店みせ、安やすいね。」「確たしかに。」
"This place is cheap." "It is, isn't it."

確たしかに寒さむいけど、雪ゆきは降ふらないよ。
It's cold, admittedly, but it isn't going to snow.

書類しょるいは**確たしかに**お預あずかりしました。
I have received the documents - they are safely with me.

確たしかに彼かれの説明せつめいは分わかりやすかったが、肝心かんじんの費用ひようの話はなしが抜ぬけていた。
His explanation was certainly easy to follow, but it left out the crucial question of cost.

確かに · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and equally at home in an academic paper and in a text message. The concessive frame 確たしかに～が is a staple of written argument; the one-word 「確たしかに。」 reply is casual but not slangy, and works in the office as well as with friends.

The vouching use has a slightly formal flavour, because it is what you say when responsibility is at stake: 確たしかに承うけたまわりました, "I have definitely taken note of that", is standard business phrasing. In everyday talk about the future, きっと is the natural word instead.

確かに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Be aware of what conceding does to the shape of a conversation. Opening with 確たしかに sets up an expectation that a が is coming; if you concede and then stop, the listener will wait for the rest of the sentence. That is often exactly the effect you want when disagreeing politely - the agreement takes the sting out of what follows - but it does mean 確たしかに is rarely the whole of your position.

確かに · 確かに vs 確か

Rule of thumb - one に reverses the meaning. 確たしかに is confidence; 確たしか is a memory you are not sure of.

確たしか会議かいぎは三時さんじからだったと思おもいます。
The meeting was at three, if I remember rightly.

会議かいぎは**確たしかに**三時さんじから始はじまりました。
The meeting definitely started at three.

確かに · 確かに vs 確か (cont.)

確たしか is an adverb of hedged recollection and normally comes with と思おもう, はず or だったよね. 確たしかに asserts. Mixing them up tells your listener the opposite of what you meant, which is why this pair is worth drilling.

確かに · 確かに vs きっと

Rule of thumb - 確たしかに is about what is already established. きっと is a confident guess about what is not.

彼女かのじょはきっと来きます。
She'll definitely come.

彼女かのじょは**確たしかに**来きました。
She did come - I can vouch for it.

きっと looks forward and is a prediction, however confident; 確たしかに looks back at evidence. Saying ❌確たしかに来きます about a future event sounds like you are promising on someone else's behalf.

確かに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 確たしかに明日あしたは雨あめでしょう。 → ✅ きっと明日あしたは雨あめでしょう。
    A forecast is a guess, not something you can vouch for. 確たしかに needs evidence already in hand.

  • ❌ 確たしかに田中たなかさんの傘かさだったと思おもいます。 → ✅ 確たしか田中たなかさんの傘かさだったと思おもいます。
    と思おもいます signals that you are not sure, so the hedging 確たしか is the form that fits. With に the sentence contradicts itself.

  • ❌ 確たしかにです。 → ✅ 確たしかにそうですね。
    確たしかに is an adverb and cannot take です directly. To agree politely, give it something to modify.

確かに · Practice 1

1. ____不便ふべんな場所ばしょだが、家賃やちんが安やすいのは魅力みりょくだ。

確たしかに不便ふべんな場所ばしょだが、家賃やちんが安やすいのは魅力みりょくだ。

The が in the middle is the giveaway: the writer grants the drawback first and then argues past it, which is the standard concessive frame this word builds.

確かに · Practice 2

2. Choose the right form: （確たしか・確たしかに）去年きょねんも同おなじ問題もんだいが出でたはずです。

確たしか去年きょねんも同おなじ問題もんだいが出でたはずです。

はず marks this as inference from a hazy memory rather than a fact the speaker can guarantee, so the hedging form without に is the one that fits.

確かに · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 確たしかにこの方法ほうほうは速はやい。 (b) この方法ほうほうは速はやい。

Sentence (b) is a flat statement of the speaker's own view. Sentence (a) sounds like a reply: it accepts a point somebody else has made, and it leaves the door open for a "but" - about cost, or safety, or whatever the speaker is really driving at. Ending there without the "but" makes the agreement sound reluctant.

おそらく

Meaning · in all likelihood; probably - a considered estimate, stated carefully

おそらく is the measured, slightly formal cousin of たぶん. It says the speaker has weighed what they know and arrived at a probable answer. The weighing is the point: おそらく sounds like a judgement, not a shrug.

おそらく · Usage

おそらく is an adverb and stands at the head of the sentence or immediately before the predicate. It never conjugates and takes no particles.

What matters is the end of the sentence. おそらく almost always pairs with a marker of conjecture:

  • おそらく～だろう／でしょう - the default.
    おそらく明日あしたには届とどくでしょう。
  • おそらく～と思おもう／と思おもわれる - reasoning out loud, or in
    writing. おそらく原因げんいんは配線はいせんだと思おもわれます。
  • おそらく～はずだ／に違ちがいない - when the estimate is close to a
    conclusion. おそらく彼かれはもう家いえに着ついているはずだ。

おそらく · Usage (cont.)

Ending a plain assertion with nothing at all - ❌おそらく雨あめが降ふる - sounds unfinished to a native ear. The hedge at the end is not decoration; it is what おそらく is agreeing with.

In kanji it is 恐おそらく. The kanji spelling is common in print; the kana is common in ordinary writing. It has nothing to do with fear despite the character.

おそらく · Examples

おそらく彼かれはもう知しっているでしょう。
He probably already knows.

この時間じかんなら、おそらく道みちは空すいているだろう。
At this hour the roads are most likely clear.

返事へんじがないところを見みると、おそらく忙いそがしいのだろう。
Judging by the lack of a reply, he's probably swamped.

今回こんかいの不具合ふぐあいは、おそらく先月せんげつの更新こうしんが原因げんいんだと考かんがえられます。
This fault is in all likelihood caused by last month's update.

おそらく · Nuance & register

おそらく leans formal. It is the natural choice in a report, a news article, a presentation or a careful explanation to a client, and it sounds a little stiff between friends deciding where to eat, where たぶん is what people actually say.

It also leans toward inference about circumstances rather than announcements about your own plans. たぶん行いきます ("I'll probably go") is ordinary; おそらく行いきます sounds oddly detached, as though you were forecasting the behaviour of a stranger who happens to be you. Reserve おそらく for things outside your control.

On the confidence scale, おそらく sits high - above もしかしたら, roughly level with or a shade above たぶん - but it is still a guess. If you know, say so plainly; hedging a fact with おそらく reads as evasive, and in business that impression is hard to shake off.

おそらく · おそらく vs たぶん

Rule of thumb - same probability, different room. たぶん is spoken and casual; おそらく is written and considered.

たぶん間まに合あうと思おもうよ。
We'll probably make it, I reckon.

おそらく間まに合あうものと思おもわれます。
We are likely to arrive in time.

たぶん can be tossed off with no evidence at all; おそらく implies you looked at something first. In casual conversation おそらく is not wrong, merely bookish.

おそらく · おそらく vs きっと

Rule of thumb - おそらく estimates. きっと believes.

きっと喜よろこんでくれるよ。
She's going to love it.

おそらく喜よろこんでくれるだろう。
She will probably be pleased.

きっと carries the speaker's feeling - hope, conviction, encouragement - and is what you say when reassuring someone. おそらく carries none of that warmth, which makes it excellent for analysis and poor for comfort.

おそらく · Common mistakes

  • ❌ おそらく明日あした雨あめが降ふります。 → ✅ おそらく明日あしたは雨あめが降ふるでしょう。
    A flat ます-ending states a fact, which contradicts the word at the front. おそらく needs でしょう, だろう or と思おもいます to lean on.

  • ❌ おそらく私わたしが作つくりました。 → ✅ たぶん私わたしが作つくったと思おもいます。
    You do not estimate your own past actions from the outside. おそらく is for inference about things you did not do.

  • ❌ 明日あしたのパーティー、おそらく行いくね。 → ✅ 明日あしたのパーティー、たぶん行いくね。
    Announcing your own plans to a friend calls for the casual word. おそらく with the final particle ね mixes two registers in one short sentence.

おそらく · Practice 1

1. 電気でんきが消きえているから、____もう寝ねているだろう。

おそらくもう寝ねているだろう。

The speaker reasons from visible evidence - the lights are off - to a probable conclusion, and the だろう at the end is the partner this adverb expects.

おそらく · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural word: この結果けっかは、（きっと・おそらく）気温きおんの影響えいきょうによるものと思おもわれる。

おそらく気温きおんの影響えいきょうによるものと思おもわれる。

The sentence is written analysis, complete with the formal と思おもわれる ending. きっと would import a note of personal conviction that does not belong in a report.

おそらく · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) おそらく彼かれは来こないでしょう。 (b) もしかしたら彼かれは来こないかもしれません。

Sentence (a) is a considered estimate: the speaker has reasons and expects him not to turn up. Sentence (b) merely floats the possibility, and the speaker would not be surprised either way. In practice (a) is what you say when planning around his absence, and (b) is what you say when raising a risk nobody had mentioned yet.

もしかしたら

Meaning · maybe; there's a chance that - raising a possibility the speaker has just thought of

もしかしたら opens the door to something that has not been considered yet, often something slightly unexpected or unwelcome. It is a low-confidence word: the speaker is not predicting, only pointing at a possibility.

もしかしたら · Usage

もしかしたら sits at the head of the sentence. Like every adverb of conjecture, its real lesson is the partner at the other end of the sentence:

  • もしかしたら～かもしれない／かも - the default pairing, by a wide margin.
    もしかしたら明日あしたは休やすみかもしれない。
  • もしかしたら～のではないか - the same idea in careful or written style.
    もしかしたら何なにか誤解ごかいがあるのではないでしょうか。
  • もしかしたら～かな／かしら - thinking aloud.
    もしかしたら道みちに迷まよったのかな。

もしかしたら · Usage (cont.)

Leaving the sentence to end flatly - ❌もしかしたら雨あめが降ふります - is the single most common error. The adverb only sets up the possibility; something at the end has to keep it open.

Three variants mean essentially the same thing and are interchangeable in most sentences: もしかすると (a shade more formal), ひょっとしたら and ひょっとすると (more colloquial, slightly more surprised). もしかして looks like a fourth but behaves differently - see the contrast below.

もしかしたら · Examples

もしかしたら明日あしたは雪ゆきかもしれない。
There's a chance it'll snow tomorrow.

もしかしたら、鍵かぎを会社かいしゃに忘わすれてきたかも。
I might have left my keys at the office.

何なんの連絡れんらくもないので、もしかしたら日ひにちを間違まちがえているのではないでしょうか。
There's been no word at all, so I wonder if she hasn't got the date wrong.

もしかしたら来年らいねん、大阪おおさかに転勤てんきんになるかもしれないと上司じょうしに言いわれた。
My boss told me there's a chance I'll be transferred to Osaka next year.

もしかしたら · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and usable in ます-form or plain form without changing shape. In careful business speech it usually comes with the fuller ending のではないでしょうか rather than かも, which is where the register is actually carried.

The word has a distinctly tentative colour. Because it presents an idea as one that has only just occurred to the speaker, it is a soft way to raise something uncomfortable: もしかしたら私わたしの勘違かんちがいかもしれませんが… lets you question someone's claim while taking the blame for the doubt yourself. That politeness trick is worth stealing.

もしかしたら · Nuance & register (cont.)

Contracted もしかしたら～かも is very common in speech and messages, and the whole sentence is often left trailing: 「もしかしたら無理むりかも…」 The trailing-off is part of the effect - you have floated the bad news without stating it.

On the confidence ladder, もしかしたら sits at the bottom: below たぶん and well below おそらく. If you are more than about half sure, it undersells you.

もしかしたら · もしかしたら vs もしかして

Rule of thumb - もしかしたら floats a possibility to the world and ends in かもしれない. もしかして aims a guess at the listener and ends in a question.

もしかして、田中たなかさんですか。
Excuse me - are you Tanaka, by any chance?

もしかしたら田中たなかさんも来くるかもしれません。
There's a chance Tanaka will come too.

もしかしたら · もしかしたら vs もしかして (cont.)

もしかして is what you say when you suspect something about the person in front of you - that they are the one you were told to meet, that they are angry, that they have not eaten. It expects an answer. Using もしかしたら there （❌もしかしたら田中たなかさんですか）sounds like you are guessing at a stranger rather than asking one.

もしかしたら · Common mistakes

  • ❌ もしかしたら彼女かのじょは来きます。 → ✅ もしかしたら彼女かのじょは来こないかもしれません。
    A bare ます-ending asserts, which cancels the doubt the adverb just raised. The possibility has to stay open at the end of the sentence.

  • ❌ もしかしたら明日あした会議かいぎがありますか。 → ✅ もしかして明日あした会議かいぎがありますか。
    A direct question to the listener takes もしかして. もしかしたら is for possibilities you are laying out, not for questions you are putting.

  • ❌ もしかしたら絶対ぜったい遅おくれる。 → ✅ きっと遅おくれる。
    You cannot hedge and insist in the same breath. If you are sure, drop the adverb of possibility entirely.

もしかしたら · Practice 1

1. 電気でんきがついているから、誰だれか残のこっている。

電気でんきがついているから、もしかしたら誰だれか残のこっているかもしれない。

Both blanks belong to one frame. The adverb opens the possibility and かもしれない keeps it open; filling only one of them leaves the sentence lopsided.

もしかしたら · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural word: （もしかして・もしかしたら）今いま、お時間じかんありませんか。

もしかして今いま、お時間じかんありませんか。

The speaker is putting a guess directly to the person in front of them and wants an answer, which is the question-shaped use. もしかしたら would be floating an idea into the air with nobody to answer it.

もしかしたら · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) もしかしたら間まに合あわないかもしれません。 (b) たぶん間まに合あわないと思おもいます。

Sentence (a) raises a risk: the speaker still expects to make it, but wants the listener warned in case. Sentence (b) is an actual expectation - the speaker believes they will miss it. If you say (a) and then arrive late, nobody is surprised; if you say (b), the listener will already have made other plans.

まるで

Meaning · just like; as if - flagging a comparison that everyone knows is not literally true

まるで is the word that turns a plain statement into a simile. It tells the listener in advance: what I am about to say is an exaggeration, take it as a picture rather than a fact.

まるで · Usage

まるで is an adverb and normally opens the clause. The lesson of this page is the ending, because まるで cannot stand alone - it must be answered by a likeness-marker:

  • まるで～ようだ／ような／ように - the standard, and the one to learn first.
    まるで夢ゆめのようだ。
  • まるで～みたいだ／みたいな／みたいに - the same thing in casual speech.
    まるで子供こどもみたいに喜よろこんでいた。
  • まるで～かのようだ - literary and a shade more emphatic; common in writing.
    まるで何なにもなかったかのように話はなし続つづけた。

まるで · Usage (cont.)

A sentence like ❌まるで夢ゆめだ is broken. Whatever comes after まるで has to be marked as a comparison; without that, you have asserted the impossible thing instead of comparing to it.

There is a second, quite different use. With a negative predicate, まるで means "not at all": まるで話はなしにならない, まるで覚おぼえていない. Here it is an intensifier of the negation, and no ようだ appears. This use is emphatic and a little literary; ぜんぜん and まったく are the everyday equivalents.

まるで · Examples

今日きょうはまるで春はるのように暖あたたかい。
It's as warm as spring today.

その犬いぬはまるでぬいぐるみみたいだった。
That dog looked just like a stuffed toy.

一年いちねんぶりに来きたのに、まるで昨日きのうのことのように覚おぼえている。
I hadn't been back in a year, yet I remember it as though it were yesterday.

質問しつもんには一ひとつも答こたえず、まるで聞きこえていないかのように窓まどの外そとを見みていた。
He answered not one question and stared out of the window as if he could not hear us.

まるで · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and common in speech, novels and journalism alike. The choice of ending carries the register instead: みたい is casual, ようだ is neutral, かのようだ is written and rather literary.

まるで is an exaggeration marker, so the listener understands the comparison to be false. That makes it a good tool for complaint and for praise, and a bad one for description. If the resemblance is real - a child who genuinely looks like her mother - the natural word is そっくり: 母親ははおやにそっくりだ. Saying まるで母親ははおやのようだ suggests the likeness is uncanny rather than simply present.

Also note that まるで is optional. 夢ゆめのようだ is a complete and natural sentence; adding まるで in front of it only turns the volume up. Overusing it in writing quickly sounds breathless.

まるで · Nuance & register (cont.)

The negative use is a different register again. まるで分わからない is emphatic, slightly old-fashioned in flavour, and more common in writing and in grumbling than in neutral conversation.

まるで · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 彼かれはまるで王様おうさまだ。 → ✅ 彼かれはまるで王様おうさまのようだ。
    Without a likeness-marker the sentence claims he is a king. まるで sets up a comparison and needs ようだ, みたいだ or かのようだ to complete it.

  • ❌ 姉あねはまるで母ははのようです。（meaning they really do look alike）→ ✅ 姉あねは母ははにそっくりです。
    まるで says the likeness is a figure of speech. For an actual family resemblance it undercuts what you meant.

  • ❌ まるで漢字かんじが読よめます。 → ✅ まるで漢字かんじが読よめません。
    In its intensifier use, まるで attaches only to a negative. There is no affirmative version of that sense.

まるで · Practice 1

1. 雨あめにぬれて、ぬれた猫ねこ姿すがただった。

雨あめにぬれて、まるでぬれた猫ねこのような姿すがただった。

The two blanks are one frame: the adverb opens the comparison and のような attaches it to the noun. Because a noun follows, のように would be wrong here.

まるで · Practice 2

2. Find and fix the error: 彼女かのじょの部屋へやはまるでホテルだ。

✅ 彼女かのじょの部屋へやはまるでホテルのようだ。

As written, the sentence says her room is a hotel. Adding のようだ - or みたいだ in casual speech - turns it back into the comparison the speaker intended.

まるで · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれの説明せつめいはまるで分わからなかった。 (b) 彼かれの説明せつめいはあまり分わからなかった。

Sentence (a) uses the intensifier sense: nothing at all got through, and the tone is emphatic, close to a complaint. Sentence (b) is measured - some of it landed, much of it did not. Note that (a) contains no comparison at all, which is why no ようだ appears in it.

なるべく

Meaning · as far as possible; if you can - a preference stated without making it an order

なるべく softens whatever follows it into an aim rather than a requirement. Put it in front of a request and the request becomes a wish; put it in front of your own plan and the plan becomes an intention you will try to keep.

なるべく · Usage

なるべく is an adverb. It goes immediately in front of the part it qualifies - usually a verb, often an adverb of degree such as 早はやく or 多おおく - and it does not change form.

Three places it lives:

なるべく · Usage (cont.)

  • Requests. なるべく早はやめにお返事へんじをください。 The deadline is
    still a deadline, but you have signalled that you understand life happens.
  • Negative requests. なるべく音おとを立たてないでください。 Very common
    on notices, where an outright prohibition would be too strong.
  • Resolutions and habits. なるべく歩あるくようにしています。 Here it pairs
    naturally with ようにする, which is itself a "make an effort to" pattern.

なるべく · Usage (cont.)

なるべく belongs with things that are still open - requests, intentions, advice. It sits badly on a finished fact: ❌なるべく早はやく来きました reports what you did, and there is nothing left to try for. Use できるだけ急いそいで 来きました, or simply 急いそいで来きました.

In kanji it is 成なるべく, but the kana spelling is overwhelmingly the norm and the kanji looks archaic.

なるべく · Examples

なるべく早はやく帰かえります。
I'll be home as early as I can.

夜よるはなるべくコーヒーを飲のまないようにしている。
I try not to drink coffee at night if I can help it.

会場かいじょうが狭せまいので、なるべく荷物にもつは少すくなめにお願ねがいします。
The venue is small, so please keep your luggage to a minimum if you can.

返事へんじは急いそぎませんが、なるべく今週中こんしゅうちゅうにいただけると助たすかります。
There's no rush on the reply, but it would help if I could have it this week if at all possible.

なるべく · Nuance & register

なるべく is neutral to slightly informal, and works fine inside ます-form speech - なるべく早はやくお送おくりします is perfectly good business Japanese. It is common in notices and instructions, where its softness is the whole point.

The softness cuts both ways. Attach なるべく to something that genuinely must happen and you have accidentally made it optional: a hard deadline announced as なるべく明日あしたまでに will be read as "tomorrow would be nice". When the requirement is firm, drop the adverb, or use 必かならず.

なるべく · Nuance & register (cont.)

Received rather than given, なるべく is a small courtesy: the speaker is telling you they know what they are asking may be inconvenient. Answering 「はい、なるべく早はやく」 in return is a natural way to accept without promising a time.

なるべく · なるべく vs できるだけ

Rule of thumb - the same request, measured differently. なるべく asks for willingness; できるだけ asks for the maximum that is actually possible.

なるべく静しずかにしてください。
Please keep it down if you can.

できるだけ静しずかにしてください。
Please be as quiet as you possibly can.

なるべく · なるべく vs できるだけ (cont.)

Both are polite and both are common; できるだけ is the firmer of the two because it names the limit of your ability and asks you to go to it. It is also the one that suits objective, written contexts - できるだけ正確せいかくに記入きにゅうして ください on a form. Stronger still, and distinctly formal, is できる限かぎり. Where a difference is felt, the ordering runs なるべく - できるだけ - できる限かぎり, but in most everyday sentences the first two are interchangeable.

なるべく · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 明日あしたは九時くじに来きてください。なるべく遅おくれないでください。 → ✅ …絶対ぜったいに遅おくれないでください。
    If lateness is genuinely not acceptable, なるべく gives permission you did not mean to give. Save it for things you would forgive.

  • ❌ なるべく昨日きのう片付かたづけました。 → ✅ できるだけ片付かたづけました。
    A completed action leaves nothing to aim at. なるべく needs a request, a plan or a habit - something still in front of you.

  • ❌ なるべくの早はやい時間じかんにお願ねがいします。 → ✅ なるべく早はやい時間じかんにお願ねがいします。
    なるべく is an adverb and takes no の. It attaches straight to what it modifies.

なるべく · Practice 1

1. 席せきが少すくないので、____早はやめにお越こしください。

なるべく早はやめにお越こしください。

The request is real but the host is not in a position to insist, so the softening adverb fits exactly. できるだけ would also work and would push a little harder.

なるべく · Practice 2

2. Find and fix the error: 昨日きのうはなるべく急いそいで作つくりました。

✅ 昨日きのうはできるだけ急いそいで作つくりました。

The action is finished, so there is no remaining effort for なるべく to point at. できるだけ works because it describes the extent of the effort that was actually made.

なるべく · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) なるべく明日あしたまでに出だしてください。 (b) 必かならず明日あしたまでに出だしてください。

Sentence (a) states a preferred deadline and quietly accepts that it might slip; the listener will not feel they have broken a promise if they submit on the day after. Sentence (b) is an instruction with no give in it. Choosing between them is really a choice about what you will do if the deadline is missed.

めったに

Meaning · hardly ever; rarely - and only ever with a negative verb

めったに says that something happens so seldom it is almost worth remarking on. The frequency it describes is very low - lower than あまり, close to ほとんど - and the word is grammatically incomplete without a negative to attach to.

めったに · Usage

めったに is an adverb and goes in front of the predicate. The rule that matters:

Rule of thumb - めったに requires a negative predicate. めったに～ない, めったに～ません. There is no affirmative version.

❌めったに行いく and ❌めったに見みます are not "rarely go" - they are not Japanese at all. The negative carries the frequency; めったに only tells you how far down the scale it goes.

  • 外食がいしょくはめったにしません。
  • 彼かれはめったに怒おこらない。
  • 雪ゆきはめったに降ふりませんでした。 - past tense works normally.

めったに · Usage (cont.)

One fixed expression is worth learning as a unit: めったにない used before a noun means "rare, exceptional" - めったにないチャンス, "a chance you don't get twice". The same negative rule is still doing the work; it has simply become a noun-modifier.

In kanji it is 滅多めったに, an ateji spelling that appears in print, but the kana is more usual.

めったに · Examples

父ちちはめったに映画えいがを見みません。
My father hardly ever watches films.

この辺へんではめったに雪ゆきが降ふらない。
It almost never snows around here.

忙いそがしくて、平日へいじつに家族かぞくそろって夕食ゆうしょくをとることはめったにない。
We're so busy that the whole family is hardly ever at dinner together on a weekday.

あの寡黙かもくな部長ぶちょうがめったに見みせない笑顔えがおを見みせた。
Our famously silent department head produced a smile of a kind he almost never shows.

めったに · Nuance & register

Register-neutral: it appears in ます-form and plain form, in speech and in writing, with no change. It is not marked for age or gender.

What it does carry is a faint note of comment. Saying めったに来こない tends to imply that the rarity is notable - surprising, disappointing, or precious. When you simply want to report a low frequency with no colour at all, あまり～ない is flatter and safer.

Because the frequency is so low, めったに and a stated number sit badly together. 月つきに一回いっかいめったに行いかない is contradictory: if you can count them, they are not "hardly ever". Use たまに行いきます for a countable occasional habit.

めったに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note finally that めったに describes frequency, not degree. It cannot modify an adjective: ❌めったに高たかくない is wrong for "not very expensive"; that is あまり高たかくない.

めったに · めったに vs あまり○○ない

Rule of thumb - both need a negative, but あまり measures degree or moderate frequency, and めったに measures very low frequency only.

お酒さけはあまり飲のみません。
I don't drink much.

お酒さけはめったに飲のみません。
I almost never drink.

めったに · めったに vs あまり○○ない (cont.)

The first speaker probably has a beer now and then; the second is close to teetotal. And because あまり can also scale degree, it works with adjectives - あまり辛からくない - where めったに cannot. The everyday positive counterpart of both is たまに, "occasionally", which takes an ordinary affirmative verb: たまに飲のみます.

めったに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私わたしはめったに外食がいしょくします。 → ✅ 私わたしはめったに外食がいしょくしません。
    The adverb cannot appear with an affirmative predicate. If you mean "I eat out occasionally", the word you want is たまに.

  • ❌ この店みせはめったに高たかくない。 → ✅ この店みせはあまり高たかくない。
    めったに counts occasions, not degrees, so it cannot modify an adjective.

  • ❌ 年ねんに二回にかいぐらいめったに会あわない。 → ✅ 年ねんに二回にかいぐらいしか会あわない。
    Naming a frequency and then calling it "hardly ever" pulls in two directions. Pick one: either the number, or the adverb.

めったに · Practice 1

1. 忙いそがしいので、旅行りょこうには____行いき____。

旅行りょこうにはめったに行いきません。

The two blanks are a single frame - the adverb is only half a statement until the negative arrives at the end. Filling the first blank and leaving an affirmative verb would be ungrammatical rather than merely odd.

めったに · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural word: 甘あまいものは（めったに・あまり）好すきじゃない。

甘あまいものはあまり好すきじゃない。

好すき is a state, not a repeated event, so there are no occasions to count. あまり scales the degree of liking, which is what the sentence needs.

めったに · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 田中たなかさんはめったに遅刻ちこくしない。 (b) 田中たなかさんは遅刻ちこくしない。

Sentence (b) is a flat description of a reliable person. Sentence (a) admits that it has happened once or twice and, by pointing at how seldom it happens, quietly turns the remark into praise - or, depending on the conversation, into a way of saying that today's lateness is out of character.

決して～ない

Meaning · never; not under any circumstances - a categorical denial or an absolute commitment

決けっして does not make a sentence negative; the verb does that. What 決けっして adds is finality. It closes the door on exceptions, which is why it belongs to promises, warnings, rules and firm denials rather than to ordinary reporting.

決して～ない · Formation

決けっして is an adverb placed before the predicate, and the predicate must be negative. Everything else about the sentence is unchanged.

決して～ない · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (plain) 決けっして + ない-form 忘わすれる → 決けっして忘わすれない
Verb (polite) 決けっして + ません 言いう → 決けっして言いいません
Verb (past) 決けっして + なかった／ませんでした 許ゆるす → 決けっして許ゆるさなかった

い-adjective 決けっして + くない 安やすい → 決けっして安やすくない
な-adjective・Noun 決けっして + ではない／じゃない 無駄むだ → 決けっして無駄むだではない

決して～ない · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - no negative, no 決けっして. There is no affirmative use of this word in modern Japanese, so ❌決けっして行いきます is not a weaker version of anything - it is simply broken.

Note - the negative does not have to sit immediately next to 決けっして, but it does have to be in the same clause. 決けっして人ひとの悪口わるぐちを言いわない is fine: the adverb is at the front, the negation at the end, and everything between them is inside the scope.

決して～ない · Examples

この約束やくそくは**決けっして忘わすれません**。
I will never forget this promise.

彼かれは**決けっして**弱音よわねを吐はかない。
He never lets on that he's struggling.

値段ねだんは**決けっして**安やすくないが、作つくりはしっかりしている。
It is by no means cheap, but it's solidly made.

中なかの水みずは**決けっして**飲のまないでください。
Under no circumstances drink the water inside.

決して～ない · Examples (cont.)

あのときいただいたご恩おんを、私わたしは**決けっして**忘わすれません。
I will never forget the kindness you showed me then.

失敗しっぱいしたからといって、それが**決けっして**無駄むだだったわけではありません。
Just because it failed doesn't mean it was wasted effort.

安全あんぜんのため、走行中そうこうちゅうは**決けっして**席せきをお立たちにならないでください。
For your safety, please do not leave your seat at any time while the vehicle is moving.

決して～ない · Nuance & register

決けっして is emphatic and leans formal. It is the natural word in written rules, safety notices, apologies, contracts and public statements, and it is what you reach for in speech when you want to be heard as making a commitment rather than a remark. Among friends, the everyday word for the same force is 絶対ぜったいに.

Because it is absolute, it is also a favourite of denial. A politician saying 決けっしてそのような事実じじつはございません is not saying "probably not" - and the very strength of the word is why it can sound defensive when overused.

決して～ない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two smaller points. The kana spelling けっして is common in casual writing, and the kanji 決けっして in print; the reading is the same. And in formal writing you will meet 決けっして～まい, an old-fashioned negative-volitional pairing meaning "I shall never": 決けっして忘わすれまい. Recognise it; you do not need to produce it.

決して～ない · 決して vs 絶対に

Rule of thumb - 絶対ぜったいに works with both affirmatives and negatives. 決けっして works only with negatives, and sounds more formal doing it.

絶対ぜったいに**勝かちます**。
We are absolutely going to win.

決けっしてあきらめません。
I will never give up.

決して～ない · 決して vs 絶対に (cont.)

Note that ❌決けっして勝かちます is impossible while 絶対ぜったいに負まけません is perfectly natural - so 絶対ぜったいに is the more flexible word, and the one you will hear far more often in conversation. In writing, 決けっして is the more dignified of the two.

決して～ない · 決して vs 全然

Rule of thumb - 全然ぜんぜん measures degree: not at all, right now. 決けっして makes a claim about every occasion: not ever, no exceptions.

この問題もんだいは**全然ぜんぜん分わかりません**。
I don't understand this question at all.

嘘うそは**決けっしてつきません**。
I never tell lies.

決して～ない · 決して vs 全然 (cont.)

Putting 全然ぜんぜん in the second sentence would say your lies are unconvincing rather than absent. And 決けっして in the first would turn a statement about today's homework into a lifelong vow of incomprehension.

決して～ない · 決して vs めったに

Rule of thumb - both take a negative, but めったに leaves room for exceptions and 決けっして does not.

父ちちはめったに怒おこらない。
My father hardly ever gets angry.

父ちちは**決けっして**怒おこらない。
My father never gets angry - not once.

めったに is a frequency report; 決けっして is a claim about principle or character. If you can imagine a counter-example, you want めったに.

決して～ない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 決けっして行いきます。 → ✅ 絶対ぜったいに行いきます。
    The word cannot appear with an affirmative predicate at all. For an emphatic positive promise, 絶対ぜったいに is the word.

  • ❌ 決けっして好すきです。 → ✅ 決けっして嫌きらいではありません。
    The same trap with an adjective. Note how the natural fix flips the sentence into a denial, which is the shape this adverb always wants.

  • ❌ 週しゅうに一回いっかいぐらいしか決けっして行いきません。 → ✅ 週しゅうに一回いっかいぐらいしか行いきません。
    A stated frequency contradicts an absolute denial. If exceptions exist, use めったに or あまり instead.

決して～ない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 決けっして忘わすれないでください、大丈夫だいじょうぶです。 → ✅ 忘わすれないでくださいね。
    In a light, friendly reminder 決けっして is far too heavy - it sounds as though a serious consequence is waiting. Keep it for real warnings.

決して～ない · Practice 1

1. 安全あんぜんのため、工事中こうじちゅうはこの線せんより中なかに____入はいら____。

この線せんより中なかに**決けっして**入はいらないでください。

The adverb and the negative are one frame: the first blank supplies the absolute force and the second supplies the negation it needs. A safety notice is precisely the register this word belongs to.

決して～ない · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural word: 母ははは（決けっして・めったに）映画館えいがかんに行いきません。年ねんに一度いちどぐらいでしょうか。

母はははめったに映画館えいがかんに行いきません。

The second sentence admits about one visit a year, so the denial cannot be absolute. 決けっして would contradict the very next sentence.

決して～ない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I will never say anything like that again."

決けっして二度にどとそんなことは言いいません。 （or simply 決けっしてそんなことは言いいません。）

The English "never … again" is a promise about all future occasions, which is what this adverb is for. 二度にどと reinforces it, and the sentence still needs the negative ending to be grammatical.

決して～ない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 彼かれの作品さくひんは決けっして素晴すばらしいです。

✅ 彼かれの作品さくひんは決けっして悪わるくありません。 （or, if praise was intended: 彼かれの作品さくひんは本当ほんとうに素晴すばらしいです。）

The original pairs an absolute-denial adverb with a positive adjective, which is ungrammatical. Either make the predicate negative, or drop the adverb and use an ordinary intensifier.

決して～ない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この方法ほうほうは決けっして難むずかしくありません。 (b) この方法ほうほうは全然ぜんぜん難むずかしくありません。

Both deny that the method is hard. Sentence (b) is a plain, casual measurement of difficulty - there is none. Sentence (a) sounds like a rebuttal: someone has suggested the method is difficult, and the speaker is closing that idea down, in the slightly formal register of a manual or a presentation.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない

Meaning · not really; not particularly - denying that there is anything special to report

べつに does not deny the fact so much as deny that the fact matters. It says there is nothing worth singling out here: no strong feeling, no problem, no reason worth going into. That is what makes it so useful for deflecting a question - and so easy to sound sulky with.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Formation

べつに is an adverb placed before the predicate, and the predicate is negative. Nothing attaches to it and it never changes shape.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (plain) べつに + ない-form 困こまる → べつに困こまらない
Verb (polite) べつに + ません 急いそぐ → べつに急いそぎません
い-adjective べつに + くない 忙いそがしい → べつに忙いそがしくない
な-adjective・Noun べつに + じゃない／ではない 嫌きらい → べつに嫌きらいじゃない
Permission べつに + なくてもいい 来くる → べつに来こなくてもいい
Standalone べつに。 「どうしたの?」「べつに。」

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - like 決けっして and めったに, べつに needs a negative predicate. The standalone reply 「べつに。」 is not an exception: the negative is simply left unsaid, and both speakers hear it.

Note - there is a second, unrelated 別べつに meaning "separately", and it takes an ordinary affirmative verb: 別べつに包つつんでいただけますか, "could you wrap them separately?" Same characters, different word. Context separates them without any effort: if the predicate is negative, it is the "not particularly" one.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Examples

「怒おこってる?」「べつに怒おこってないよ。」
"Are you angry?" "Not particularly, no."

べつに急いそぎませんから、ゆっくりどうぞ。
There's no rush at all - please take your time.

「どうしたの?」「……べつに。」
"What's wrong?" "…Nothing."

べつに嫌きらいじゃないけど、自分じぶんでは買かわないかな。
It's not that I dislike it - I just wouldn't buy one myself.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Examples (cont.)

べつに深ふかい意味いみはありません。ただ気きになっただけです。
There's no deeper meaning to it. I was just curious.

遅おくれた理由りゆうを聞きいただけで、べつに責せめるつもりはありません。
I only asked why you were late; I'm not blaming you or anything.

「手伝てつだおうか?」「ありがとう。でもべつに一人ひとりでも平気へいきだから、休やすんでて。」
"Want a hand?" "Thanks, but I'm honestly fine on my own - go and rest."

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Nuance & register

べつに is casual to neutral. It is entirely normal inside ます-form speech - べつに問題もんだいありません is ordinary office Japanese - but the flavour is conversational, and in a formal document 特とくに is the better word.

Everything depends on delivery. Said warmly and followed by the rest of the sentence, べつに reassures: べつに迷惑めいわくじゃありませんよ. Said flat, alone, and clipped, 「べつに。」 is the classic teenage non-answer - "nothing", meaning "something, and I'm not telling you". Learners sometimes pick up the standalone form from television and use it with a teacher or a manager, where it lands as rude. Keep the rest of the sentence, and the risk disappears.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Nuance & register (cont.)

The word also does real politeness work. Because it denies that anything special is going on, it lowers the temperature: telling someone べつに怒おこっていませんよ is gentler than a bare 怒おこっていません, which can sound like a protest.

Both spellings, 別べつに and べつに, are common. The kana version looks slightly softer and is usual in messages.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · べつに vs 特に

Rule of thumb - 特とくに～ない is a neutral "nothing in particular". べつに adds a shrug, and sometimes a hint of defensiveness.

質問しつもんは**特とくに**ありません。
I have no questions in particular.

質問しつもんはべつにないけど。
Nah, I haven't got any questions.

In a meeting, on a form, or replying to a customer, 特とくに is the safe choice. べつに in the same slot is not wrong, but it sounds like you could not be bothered to think about it.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · べつに vs あまり

Rule of thumb - あまり～ない scales the degree: "not very". べつに denies that the thing is notable at all.

この映画えいがはあまり面白おもしろくなかった。
That film wasn't very good.

この映画えいがはべつに面白おもしろくなかった。
That film was nothing special.

The first is a measured judgement; the second dismisses the film, and often implies the speaker is contradicting someone who praised it.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · べつに vs 全然

Rule of thumb - 全然ぜんぜん is emphatic: not at all, none whatsoever. べつに is deliberately unemphatic.

全然ぜんぜん気きにしてないよ。
I don't mind in the slightest.

べつに気きにしてないよ。
I don't especially mind.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · べつに vs 全然 (cont.)

Reassuring a friend who has apologised, 全然ぜんぜん is warmer, because it sounds like you mean it. べつに can come out cooler than you intended - which is exactly why it works when you want to sound unbothered.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Common mistakes

  • ❌ べつに好すきです。 → ✅ べつに嫌きらいじゃないです。
    The predicate must be negative. Learners who translate "not really" one word at a time often leave the verb affirmative and reverse the meaning.

  • ❌（部長ぶちょうに）「何なにかありましたか。」「べつに。」 → ✅ 「いえ、特とくにありません。」
    The one-word reply is fine among friends and abrupt to anyone above you. Give the full sentence, or switch to 特とくに.

  • ❌ べつに包つつまないでください。（meaning "please wrap them separately"）→ ✅ 別べつに包つつんでください。
    With an affirmative verb the word means "separately", so a negative here says the opposite of what you wanted.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ べつに三人さんにんしか来こなかった。 → ✅ 三人さんにんしか来こなかった。
    べつに denies that anything is noteworthy, so it clashes with しか, which points out that the number is small.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Practice 1

1. 「この色いろ、変へんかな?」「____変へんじゃないと思おもうよ。」

「べつに変へんじゃないと思おもうよ。」

The speaker is playing the worry down - there is nothing odd worth remarking on - and the negative じゃない is the partner the adverb requires.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural word: 会議かいぎで「ご意見いけんは?」と聞きかれて → 「（べつに・特とくに）ございません。」

「特とくにございません。」

A meeting calls for the neutral, formal word. べつに alongside the very polite ございません would mix registers and make the reply sound offhand.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "It's not that I don't want to go - I just have no money."

べつに行いきたくないわけじゃない。ただお金かねがないだけ。

べつに sets up the denial and わけじゃない completes it, which is a very common pairing when you are correcting an impression somebody has formed.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: べつに問題もんだいがあります。

✅ べつに問題もんだいはありません。

べつに cannot stand with an affirmative predicate in this sense. If you did mean "there is a separate problem", the natural phrasing is 別べつの問題もんだいがあります.

べつに～ない・べつに～じゃない · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 「どうかした?」「べつに。」 (b) 「どうかした?」「ううん、なんでもないよ。」

Both mean "nothing". Reply (a) is clipped, and to a Japanese ear it usually signals the opposite of what it says: something is wrong and the speaker does not want to discuss it. Reply (b) closes the subject warmly and invites no follow-up. If you genuinely have nothing to report, (b) is much the safer choice.

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own

Meaning · suddenly; out of nowhere - used of a thought, a memory or a feeling that surfaces without being summoned

ふと is the word for the moment when something occurs to you. Nobody made it happen, you were not trying to remember it, and there is no cause worth naming - it simply came.

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own · Usage

ふと is an adverb and stands directly in front of the verb. It does not conjugate and takes no particles.

The lesson of the page is which verbs it goes with. In this sense ふと selects verbs of the mind:

  • 思おもう - ふと思おもったんだけど…
  • 思おもい出だす - ふと昔むかしのことを思おもい出だした。
  • 気きづく - ふと気きづくと、外そとは暗くらくなっていた。
  • 考かんがえる, 不安ふあんになる, 心配しんぱいになる, 頭あたまに浮うかぶ

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own · Usage (cont.)

Two shapes are near-fixed and worth learning whole:

  • ふと思おもうことがある - "the thought sometimes strikes me".
  • ふと気きづくと～ていた - "before I knew it, …". The と here is the
    N4 conditional, and the second half is almost always in a past or ていた form.

ふと describes a mental event happening to you, so it is almost always narrated in the past. ❌ふと思おもいます, as a statement about the present, does not work; a thought you are having on purpose is not a ふと thought.

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own · Usage (cont.)

It cannot be used of external events. Rain, noises and visitors do not arrive ふと - that is 急きゅうに or 突然とつぜん.

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own · Examples

ふと母ははの声こえを思おもい出だした。
My mother's voice suddenly came back to me.

ふと気きづくと、電車でんしゃはもう終点しゅうてんだった。
Before I knew it, the train had reached the last stop.

洗あらい物ものをしているとき、ふと来年らいねんのことが心配しんぱいになった。
While I was doing the washing-up, a worry about next year came over me out of nowhere.

自分じぶんは何なにのために働はたらいているのだろうと、ふと考かんがえてしまうことがある。
Now and then the thought creeps up on me: what exactly am I working for?

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own · Nuance & register

ふと is neutral in politeness - it fits ます-form and plain form equally - but it is not evenly distributed. It belongs to reflective narration: novels, essays, diary entries, and the kind of conversation where somebody is telling you what went through their head. You will hear it far less in brisk, practical talk.

Because of that, ふと colours a sentence. It signals that the speaker was not in control of the thought, which makes it useful for softening: ふと思おもった んだけど、… is a gentle way to introduce a suggestion, since you are presenting it as something that merely occurred to you rather than a position you have taken.

The word is written in kana. It has no kanji in ordinary use, no contraction, and no politeness variants.

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own · ふと vs 急に・突然

Rule of thumb - 急きゅうに and 突然とつぜん describe an event changing suddenly. ふと describes something surfacing in your own mind.

急きゅうに雨あめが降ふり出だした。
It suddenly started to rain.

ふと、傘かさを持もってきていないことに気きづいた。
It struck me that I hadn't brought an umbrella.

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own · ふと vs 急に・突然 (cont.)

The rain is out there in the world, so it takes 急きゅうに; the realisation is inside the speaker's head, so it takes ふと. Note also the difference in force: 急きゅうに is abrupt and often unwelcome, while ふと is quiet - a thought drifting in, not a door slamming.

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own · Common mistakes

  • ❌ ふと電話でんわが鳴なった。 → ✅ 急きゅうに電話でんわが鳴なった。
    A ringing phone is an event in the world, not a thought in your mind. ふと cannot take an external subject.

  • ❌ ふと思おもい出だそうとした。 → ✅ ふと思おもい出だした。
    Trying to remember is deliberate, and ふと means precisely that no effort was involved. The two cancel each other out.

  • ❌ 毎朝まいあさふと母ははのことを思おもい出だします。 → ✅ 毎朝まいあさ母ははのことを思おもい出だします。
    Something that happens every morning is a habit, not a thought arriving unbidden. ふと needs the occasion to be one-off and unplanned.

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own · Practice 1

1. 散歩さんぽをしていて、____学生時代がくせいじだいのことを思おもい出だした。

散歩さんぽをしていて、ふと学生時代がくせいじだいのことを思おもい出だした。

A memory surfacing during an unrelated activity, with no trigger the speaker cares to name, is the core use of this adverb. The past tense is part of the pattern.

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural word: （ふと・急きゅうに）後うしろから名前なまえを呼よばれた。

急きゅうに後うしろから名前なまえを呼よばれた。

Someone else calling your name is an external event, so it needs the adverb for sudden happenings. ふと would only work if the sentence described something arising inside the speaker's own head.

ふと - a thought that arrives on its own · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) ふと彼かれの言葉ことばを思おもい出だした。 (b) 彼かれの言葉ことばを思おもい出だした。

Sentence (b) is neutral and would also fit someone deliberately casting their mind back. Sentence (a) says the memory arrived by itself, unprompted, in the middle of something else - and because the speaker did not choose to have it, the remark carries a hint that the words have been sitting with them.

ふと - without meaning to

Meaning · unintentionally; by chance - of an action done with no purpose behind it, and of what you happen to notice as a result

This is the same word as the ふと of thoughts arriving unbidden, pointed outward. Here it modifies something you did - glancing up, stopping, saying a thing - and its job is to strip the action of any intention.

ふと - without meaning to · Usage

ふと stands directly in front of the verb. In this sense it selects small, momentary, involuntary actions:

  • 見みる・見み上あげる - ふと空そらを見み上あげた。
  • 目めが合あう - ふと目めが合あって、気きまずかった。
  • 立たち止どまる - ふと立たち止どまって振ふり返かえった。
  • 口くちにする, 手てに取とる, 足あしを止とめる

It also appears before a noun as ふとした, an adnominal form that means "chance, trifling":

ふと - without meaning to · Usage (cont.)

  • ふとしたことから知しり合あった。 - "we met through some small chance"
  • ふとした瞬間しゅんかんに思おもい出だす

Two limits are worth stating plainly. First, the action has to be small and momentary: you can ふと glance at a shop window, but not ふと buy the shop. ❌ふと会社かいしゃを辞やめた is wrong, because quitting a job takes decisions. Second, ふと implies no carelessness and no damage. It is neutral - for an unintended action with a bad outcome, Japanese uses うっかり.

Like the other sense, ふと is almost always narrated in the past.

ふと - without meaning to · Examples

ふと空そらを見み上あげると、星ほしが見みえた。
I happened to look up, and there were stars.

ふと隣となりの人ひとと目めが合あった。
My eyes met the person's next to me, quite by chance.

本屋ほんやでふと手てに取とった本ほんが、今いまでも一番いちばん好すきな一冊いっさつだ。
A book I picked up at random in a shop is still my favourite one.

会議かいぎの帰かえりにふと漏もらした一言ひとことが、思おもわぬ誤解ごかいを招まねいてしまった。
An offhand remark I let slip on the way back from the meeting caused an unexpected misunderstanding.

ふと - without meaning to · Nuance & register

Register-neutral in politeness, but stylistically marked: ふと belongs to narration. It is common in novels, essays and anecdotes, and rarer in brisk practical speech, where people simply say なんとなく ("for no particular reason") or たまたま ("as it happened").

The word is quiet. It gives an action a drifting, unhurried feel, which is why writers use it to open a scene - ふと窓まどの外そとを見みると… - and why it sits oddly in a hurried context.

Note that ふと says nothing about the consequences. In the fourth example above the remark caused trouble, but the trouble is carried by the rest of the sentence, not by ふと. If you want the listener to hear that you were careless and are sorry, you need うっかり.

ふと is written in kana, has no contraction, and is not marked for age or gender.

ふと - without meaning to · ふと vs うっかり

Rule of thumb - both mean "without intending to", but うっかり adds carelessness and a bad result. ふと is neutral and blames nobody.

うっかり傘かさを電車でんしゃに忘わすれてしまった。
I carelessly left my umbrella on the train.

ふと窓まどの外そとに目めをやった。
I let my eyes drift to the window.

ふと - without meaning to · ふと vs うっかり (cont.)

うっかり goes with mistakes - forgetting, mixing things up, pressing the wrong button - and typically appears with てしまう, which is itself the marker of regret. ふと goes with harmless small actions and does not take てしまう for that reason. Swapping them is immediately audible: ❌うっかり空そらを見み上あげた makes looking at the sky sound like an error.

ふと - without meaning to · Common mistakes

  • ❌ ふとパスワードを間違まちがえた。 → ✅ うっかりパスワードを間違まちがえた。
    A mistake with a consequence calls for the careless-action adverb. ふと says only that there was no intention, which is not what you want to convey when apologising.

  • ❌ ふと三時間さんじかん散歩さんぽした。 → ✅ なんとなく三時間さんじかん散歩さんぽしてしまった。
    ふと covers an instant, not a stretch of time. It marks the moment an action begins, so a three-hour activity is outside its range.

  • ❌ ふとの一言ひとことが問題もんだいになった。 → ✅ ふとした一言ひとことが問題もんだいになった。
    Before a noun the form is ふとした, not ❌ふとの. As an adverb it modifies verbs only.

ふと - without meaning to · Practice 1

1. 駅えきの前まえで____立たち止どまって、来きた道みちを振ふり返かえった。

駅えきの前まえでふと立たち止どまって、来きた道みちを振ふり返かえった。

Stopping for no reason the speaker could name is exactly the unintended, momentary action this adverb marks, and the past-tense narration is typical of it.

ふと - without meaning to · Practice 2

2. Choose the natural word: （ふと・うっかり）大事だいじなファイルを消けしてしまった。

うっかり大事だいじなファイルを消けしてしまった。

Deleting an important file is a mistake with a cost, and てしまう at the end is already signalling regret. ふと would strangely report the deletion as an idle gesture with no consequence attached.

ふと - without meaning to · Practice 3

3. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) ふとした質問しつもんから話はなしが広ひろがった。 (b) ある質問しつもんから話はなしが広ひろがった。

Sentence (b) simply says a question started the conversation. Sentence (a) adds that the question was a trivial one, asked with no plan behind it - and the implication is that the interesting turn the conversation took was pure accident. ふとした is doing that work in one word.
