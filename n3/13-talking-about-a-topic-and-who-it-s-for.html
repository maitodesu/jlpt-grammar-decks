～について

Meaning · about; concerning - names the subject matter that the talking, writing, thinking or investigating is about

について marks the topic of an activity. Something is being said, asked, written, researched or thought - and について says what it is about. It does not say who it is aimed at, and it does not say whose point of view we are taking. Those are jobs for other members of this same に family, and keeping them apart is most of the work at N3.

～について · Formation

について is a compound: the particle に plus the て-form of つく ("attach to"). It behaves as a unit and attaches to a bare noun - never to a verb or an adjective.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun, before a verb Noun + について + verb 事故じこ → 事故じこについて話はなす
Noun, modifying another noun Noun + についての + Noun 環境かんきょう → 環境かんきょうについての記事きじ
Noun, as topic Noun + については 費用ひよう → 費用ひようについては後あとで
Noun, as addition Noun + についても 安全あんぜん → 安全あんぜんについても考かんがえる
Noun, formal / business Noun + につきまして（は） 日程にってい → 日程にっていにつきましては
Question word 何なに / どこ など + について 何なに → 何なにについて書かきますか

～について · Formation (cont.)

Note - the の in についての is not optional. について is adverbial: it can modify a verb on its own, but to hang it on a noun you have to convert it with の, exactly as you would with 東京とうきょうでの会議かいぎ.

～について · Examples

地震じしんについて調しらべています。
I'm looking into earthquakes.

その件けんについて質問しつもんがあります。
I have a question about that matter.

先生せんせいに進路しんろについて相談そうだんしました。
I talked to my teacher about which path to take after school.

日本にほんの祭まつりについての番組ばんぐみを録画ろくがしておいた。
I recorded a programme about Japanese festivals.

～について · Examples (cont.)

旅行りょこうの費用ひようについては、あとでまとめてメールで知しらせます。
As for the cost of the trip, I'll send you the whole thing by email later.

新あたらしい規則きそくについて、社員しゃいんからいくつも意見いけんが出でている。
Staff have raised a number of points about the new rules.

その画家がかについて何なにも知しらないまま展覧会てんらんかいに行いっても、たぶん楽たのしめないだろう。
If I go to the exhibition knowing nothing about the painter, I probably won't enjoy it.

明日あすの説明会せつめいかいでは、来年度らいねんどの予算よさんにつきましてご説明せつめいいたします。
At tomorrow's briefing I will go over next year's budget.

～について · Nuance & register

について sits in the middle of the register scale, and that is why it is the one to learn first. It is at home in a polite conversation, an email, a report, a university seminar and a news broadcast alike. Nothing about it is stiff; nothing about it is casual either.

Casual speech mostly does without it. Friends say 昨日きのうのこと, あの人ひとのこと, or just ～って:

  • あの映画えいがのこと、どう思おもった? - "What did you think about that film?"
  • 試験しけんって、いつだっけ。 - "The exam - when was it again?"

～について · Nuance & register (cont.)

Using について there is not wrong, but it lifts the sentence a register: it sounds like you are opening an agenda item rather than chatting. Conversely, an email to a client that says 納期のうきのことですが where it could have said 納期のうきにつきましては reads as slightly under-dressed.

The につきまして form is worth recognising even if you never produce it. It is the same word with a humble-polite ます stem inserted, and it belongs to shop announcements, customer service and business correspondence: 本日ほんじつの営業えいぎょう時間じかんにつきましては…. In ordinary polite speech it would sound over-formal, almost parodic.

～について · Nuance & register (cont.)

One structural habit to build: について likes to be followed by a verb of speaking, writing or finding out - 話はなす, 書かく, 説明せつめいする, 調しらべる, 考かんがえる, 議論ぎろんする, 質問しつもんする, 発表はっぴょうする. If the predicate is an emotion or an evaluation instead, について is usually the wrong tool.

～について · ～について vs ～に関して

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register and different width. について points at a specific topic and is fine in speech; に関かんして is more formal, mostly written, and takes in the surrounding area of a topic as well.

You can swap them in most sentences without producing anything ungrammatical. What changes is how the sentence sounds, and how tightly the topic is drawn.

この漢字かんじの読よみ方かたについて先生せんせいに聞きいた。
I asked the teacher about how this kanji is read.

～について · ～について vs ～に関して (cont.)

外国人がいこくじんの受うけ入いれに**関かんして**、市しは新あたらしい方針ほうしんを示しめした。
The city has set out a new policy on accepting foreign residents.

The first is one narrow question, asked out loud - について fits. The second covers a whole policy area, in writing, in a public register - に関かんして fits. Reverse them and neither is wrong, but the first would sound like a committee paper and the second like small talk.

～について · ～について vs ～に対して

Rule of thumb - について is the topic; に対たいして is the target. "Talk about the customer" is について; "be polite to the customer" is に対たいして.

This is the pair that costs learners the most marks, because English "about" and "toward" both come out as "regarding" in a dictionary.

部長ぶちょうは新人しんじんについて何なにか言いっていましたか。
Did the manager say anything about the new hire?

部長ぶちょうは新人しんじんに**対たいして**厳きびしすぎると思おもう。
I think the manager is too hard on the new hire.

～について · ～について vs ～に対して (cont.)

In the first, the new hire is the subject matter of some remarks. In the second, the new hire is the person the manager's behaviour is aimed at. A useful test: if you can replace the phrase with "on the subject of", it is について. If you can replace it with "at / to / against", it is に対たいして.

～について · ～について vs ～のこと

Rule of thumb - のこと is the everyday equivalent and, unlike について, it can be the object of a feeling. Verbs and adjectives of emotion take のこと (or a plain particle), never について.

弟おとうとのことを心配しんぱいしています。
I'm worried about my little brother.

弟おとうとについて心配しんぱいな点てんを先生せんせいに伝つたえました。
I told the teacher the points about my brother that concern me.

について needs the sentence to be presenting information on a topic. In the first sentence there is no presenting going on - there is only the feeling, and the brother is its object. The second sentence adds an act of communication, and について becomes available again.

～について · ～について vs ～のこと (cont.)

The same trap catches 好すき, 嫌きらい, 怒おこる and 覚おぼえている. ❌この歌手かしゅについて好すきです is simply not Japanese; you want この歌手かしゅが好すきです.

～について · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 事故じこについて記事きじを読よんだ。（intending "an article about the accident"） → ✅ 事故じこについての記事きじを読よんだ。
    Without の, について modifies the verb 読よんだ, which gives "I read an article, about the accident" - a different and odd sentence. To attach it to a noun you need についての.

  • ❌ アニメについて好すきです。 → ✅ アニメが好すきです。
    について introduces the subject matter of an act of communication or thought. A feeling takes its object with が or を, not with について.

  • ❌ 私わたしについてこの仕事しごとは大切たいせつです。 → ✅ 私わたしにとってこの仕事しごとは大切たいせつです。
    "For me, this job is important" is a standpoint, not a topic. Standpoints take にとって.

～について · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 客きゃくについて失礼しつれいな態度たいどを取とった。 → ✅ 客きゃくに**対たいして**失礼しつれいな態度たいどを取とった。
    An attitude is directed at somebody. That is に対たいして; について would mean the rudeness was somehow on the topic of the customer.

～について · Practice 1

1. 来週らいしゅうの会議かいぎでは、工場こうじょうの移転いてん____話はなし合あいます。

工場こうじょうの移転いてんについて話はなし合あいます。

The factory move is the subject matter of the discussion, and 話はなし合あう is exactly the kind of communication verb について was built for. に関かんして would also be grammatical, just a notch more formal than a normal meeting warrants.

～について · Practice 2

2. Choose: 店長てんちょうはアルバイトの学生がくせい（について・に対たいして）とても親切しんせつだ。

店長てんちょうはアルバイトの学生がくせいに対たいしてとても親切しんせつだ。

親切しんせつだ describes how the manager behaves toward somebody, so the students are the target of an attitude, not a topic of conversation. Say について and the sentence would try to mean that he is kind on the subject of part-timers, which is nonsense.

～について · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'd like to hear your opinion about the new plan."

新あたらしい計画けいかくについてご意見いけんを伺うかがいたいのですが。

ご意見いけん is information being given on a topic, so について is the natural choice. In a written request you could raise the register with 新あたらしい計画けいかくにつきましては, and in casual speech you would drop the whole thing: 新あたらしい計画けいかく、どう思おもう?

～について · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この問題もんだいについてのレポートを来週らいしゅうまでに出だしてください。それから、締しめ切きりについて質問しつもんは受うけ付つけません。

✅ …締しめ切きりについての質問しつもんは受うけ付つけません。

The first について is correct because it already carries の before the noun レポート. The second one sits directly in front of the noun 質問しつもん and needs the same の. This is the single most common slip with this pattern.

～について · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 田中たなかさんについて話はなしました。 (b) 田中たなかさんと話はなしました。

(a) makes 田中たなかさん the subject matter: somebody discussed him, and he may well not have been in the room. Depending on the context it can sound slightly ominous, in the same way English "we talked about you" does.

(b) makes him the conversation partner - you and he talked, about anything at all. と marks the person you do something jointly with; について marks what the doing is on the topic of. The two can even be stacked: 山田やまださんと田中たなかさんについて話はなしました - "I talked with Yamada about Tanaka."

～に関する / ～に関して

Meaning · concerning; relating to; in connection with - the formal, largely written cousin of について（かんする・かんして）

に関かんする and に関かんして mark the subject matter of a statement, a document, a rule or a piece of research. They mean what について means, but they belong to a different register - reports, notices, contracts, academic writing, the news - and they draw the topic wider: not just the thing named, but everything connected to it.

The two forms are one word doing two jobs. 関かんする is the dictionary form of a verb, so it modifies nouns. 関かんして is its て-form, so it modifies verbs.

～に関する / ～に関して · Formation

に + 関かんする ("to be connected to"). The noun in front takes に, never を.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + に関かんする + Noun 安全あんぜん → 安全あんぜんに関かんする規則きそく
Noun, modifying a verb Noun + に関かんして + verb 費用ひよう → 費用ひように関かんして話はなす
Noun, as topic Noun + に関かんしては その点てん → その点てんに関かんしては
Noun, as addition Noun + に関かんしても 人数にんずう → 人数にんずうに関かんしても
Noun, business / keigo Noun + に関かんしまして（は） 納期のうき → 納期のうきに関かんしましては
Noun, modifying a noun (rarer) Noun + に関かんしての + Noun 事故じこ → 事故じこに関かんしての報告ほうこく

～に関する / ～に関して · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - 関かんする before a noun, 関かんして before a verb. 関かんするの does not exist: the の you are reaching for is already built into the verb form.

～に関する / ～に関して · Examples

これは安全あんぜんに**関かんする**規則きそくです。
These are the safety rules.

その点てんに**関かんして**は、後のちほどお答こたえします。
I'll answer that point later.

健康けんこうに**関かんする**本ほんを何冊なんさつも買かってしまった。
I've ended up buying any number of books on health.

料金りょうきんに**関かんして**ご質問しつもんのある方かたは、受付うけつけまでお越こしください。
If you have questions concerning the fees, please come to the reception desk.

～に関する / ～に関して · Examples (cont.)

気候きこうの変化へんかに**関かんする**研究けんきゅうが世界中せかいじゅうで進すすめられている。
Research relating to climate change is being carried forward all over the world.

この事件じけんに**関かんして**、警察けいさつはまだ何なにも発表はっぴょうしていない。
The police have released nothing in connection with this incident.

給与きゅうよに**関かんしては不満ふまんはないが、残業ざんぎょうの多おおさに関かんして**は会社かいしゃに考かんがえ直なおしてほしい。
I have no complaints where pay is concerned, but on the amount of overtime I'd like the company to think again.

個人こじん情報じょうほうの扱あつかいに**関かんしまして**は、当社とうしゃのホームページをご覧らんください。
Regarding the handling of personal data, please see our website.

～に関する / ～に関して · Nuance & register

This is a written pattern first and a formal-spoken pattern second. Its natural homes are notices, headings, minutes, contracts, textbooks, news copy and the polished parts of a presentation. If you say it to a friend over coffee you will sound like you are reading from a slide.

A useful gauge: the more institutional the speaker, the more likely に関かんして is. A company says 停電ていでんに関かんしてお知しらせします; a housemate says 停電ていでんのことなんだけど. Both are correct in their own place, and swapping them is the mistake.

に関かんしまして raises it one more step, into customer-facing business Japanese. It is extremely common in announcements and service email, and you should expect to hear it far more often than you produce it.

～に関する / ～に関して · Nuance & register (cont.)

There is also a width difference that survives the register difference. について picks out a topic; に関かんして draws a circle around it. 台風たいふうに関かんする情報じょうほう covers the typhoon, the train stoppages, the school closures and the shop opening hours - everything connected to it. 台風たいふうについての情報じょうほう is more likely to be information about the typhoon itself. This is a tendency rather than a hard line, but it is why headings and document titles overwhelmingly prefer に関かんする.

Finally, do not confuse the phrase with the noun 関係かんけい. 関係かんけいがある claims a real-world connection between two things; に関かんする merely announces a topic. この問題もんだいに関かんする資料しりょう is "material on this problem"; この問題もんだいと関係かんけいがある資料しりょう is "material that turns out to be connected to this problem."

～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関して vs ～について

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different clothes. について is the everyday form and works in speech; に関かんして is formal, mostly written, and covers a broader area around the topic.

Nearly every sentence accepts both. Choose by asking who is speaking and where.

夏休なつやすみの予定よていについて家族かぞくで話はなした。
The family talked about plans for the summer holiday.

市しの予算よさんに**関かんして**、議会ぎかいで激はげしい議論ぎろんがあった。
There was a fierce debate in the assembly concerning the city budget.

～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関して vs ～について (cont.)

Put に関かんして in the first sentence and the family sounds like a board of directors. Put について in the second and nothing breaks - it is simply one notch less formal than assembly minutes would be.

～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関する + Noun vs ～についての + Noun

Rule of thumb - both attach a topic to a noun, but the shapes differ: に関かんする takes no の, について takes an obligatory one. Titles, headings and formal documents prefer に関かんする.

留学生りゅうがくせいの住すまいに**関かんする**アンケート
A survey concerning housing for international students

留学生りゅうがくせいの住すまいについてのアンケート
A survey about housing for international students

～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関する + Noun vs ～についての + Noun (cont.)

The second is perfectly good Japanese and is what you would say out loud. The first is what would be printed at the top of the form. Note the ❌に関かんするの error is impossible to make with について, and the ❌について質問しつもん error (dropping の) is impossible to make with に関かんする - the two patterns fail in opposite directions.

～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関して vs ～において

Rule of thumb - に関かんして gives the topic; において gives the setting - the place, the era, the field or the situation in which something holds.

Both are formal, both translate as "in" or "regarding" at some point in a dictionary, and both appear in the same kind of document, so they blur together.

安全あんぜん対策たいさくに**関かんして**、新あたらしい案あんが出だされた。
A new proposal has been put forward concerning safety measures.

建設けんせつ現場げんばにおいて、安全あんぜん対策たいさくは何なによりも優先ゆうせんされる。
On construction sites, safety measures take priority over everything.

～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関して vs ～において (cont.)

In the first, safety measures are what the proposal is about. In the second, the building site is where the rule applies. If the noun could be preceded by "on the subject of", it is に関かんして; if it could be preceded by "in" or "at", it is において.

～に関する / ～に関して · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 環境かんきょうに関かんするの問題もんだい → ✅ 環境かんきょうに**関かんする**問題もんだい
    関かんする is already a verb in its noun-modifying form, so it attaches to the noun directly. The の belongs to についての, not here.

  • ❌ 事故じこに関かんして報告書ほうこくしょを見みせてください。（intending "the report on the accident"） → ✅ 事故じこに**関かんする**報告書ほうこくしょを見みせてください。
    Before a noun you need 関かんする. 関かんして would attach the topic to 見みせる instead - "show me a report, on the subject of the accident."

  • ❌ ゆうべの飲のみ会かいに関かんして話はなそうよ。 → ✅ ゆうべの飲のみ会かいのこと話はなそうよ。
    Not a grammar error - a register error. Casual invitations do not take formal written vocabulary; the sentence sounds like a parody of a manager.

～に関する / ～に関して · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 私わたしに関かんして、この決定けっていは残念ざんねんです。 → ✅ 私わたしにとって、この決定けっていは残念ざんねんです。
    "For me" as a standpoint is にとって. に関かんして would make you the subject matter of the decision.

～に関する / ～に関して · Practice 1

1. 会社かいしゃは社員しゃいんの健康けんこうに____調査ちょうさを行おこなった。

健康けんこうに**関かんする**調査ちょうさを行おこなった。

The topic is being hung on the noun 調査ちょうさ, so you need the noun-modifying form 関かんする - with no の after it. 健康けんこうについての調査ちょうさ would also work and would sound slightly less official.

～に関する / ～に関して · Practice 2

2. Choose: 会議かいぎの場所ばしょ（に関かんして・において）は、また連絡れんらくします。

会議かいぎの場所ばしょに関かんしては、また連絡れんらくします。

The venue is the subject matter of the message you will send, not the place where the sending happens. において would have to mean "I will contact you at the meeting venue", which is not what the sentence is doing.

～に関する / ～に関して · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, in the register of a company notice: "We are currently investigating the cause of the problem. We will inform you separately regarding compensation."

現在げんざい、問題もんだいの原因げんいんを調査ちょうさしております。補償ほしょうに**関かんしまして**は、別べつにご連絡れんらくいたします。

A public notice is exactly where に関かんしまして belongs, and the は after it marks compensation as a separate item being set aside for later. 補償ほしょうについては would be acceptable but slightly plainer than a formal apology notice usually goes.

～に関する / ～に関して · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 新あたらしい制度せいどに関かんしてのついて説明せつめいは、来週らいしゅう行おこないます。

✅ 新あたらしい制度せいどに**関かんする**説明せつめいは、来週らいしゅう行おこないます。

Two patterns have been welded together. Pick one: 制度せいどに関かんする説明せつめい or 制度せいどについての説明せつめい. Both mean "the explanation of the new system"; the first is the one you would print on an agenda.

～に関する / ～に関して · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 地震じしんに**関かんする**情報じょうほう (b) 地震じしんについての情報じょうほう

(a) is the phrase a broadcaster or a city website would use, and it takes in everything connected to the earthquake - the tremor itself, the damage, the trains, the evacuation centres. The topic is drawn as a field.

(b) is what you would say in conversation, and it points more narrowly at information about the earthquake itself: how big it was, where it struck. The difference is a tendency rather than a rule, but the register difference is real and reliable: (a) is written and institutional, (b) is spoken and ordinary.

～において / ～における

Meaning · in; at; on - the formal written stand-in for で, naming the place, time, situation or field in which something holds（において・における）

において sets the stage. It tells you where something happens, when it happens, or within what field or respect a statement is true. Everything it does, the particle で also does - but で is the everyday word and において is the one that appears in announcements, papers, contracts and ceremonies.

における is the same word in noun-modifying dress: 会場かいじょうにおいて行おこなう ("carry out at the venue") but 会場かいじょうにおける注意ちゅうい事項じこう ("points to note at the venue").

～において / ～における · Formation

Attaches to a bare noun. The に is part of the pattern and is not replaceable.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (place), modifying a verb Noun + において 本社ほんしゃ → 本社ほんしゃにおいて行おこなう
Noun (time or era) Noun + において 明治めいじ時代じだい → 明治めいじ時代じだいにおいて
Noun (field or respect) Noun + において 技術ぎじゅつ面めん → 技術ぎじゅつ面めんにおいて
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + における + Noun 職場しょくば → 職場しょくばにおける関係かんけい
Noun, as topic Noun + においては 日本にほん → 日本にほんにおいては
Noun, as addition Noun + においても 海外かいがい → 海外かいがいにおいても

～において / ～における · Formation (cont.)

Note - においての exists but is uncommon and slightly clumsy. Before a noun, reach for における. Before a verb or an adjective, において.

～において / ～における · Examples

会議かいぎは本社ほんしゃにおいて行おこなわれます。
The meeting will be held at head office.

この点てんにおいて、両者りょうしゃの意見いけんは一致いっちしている。
On this point, the two sides are in agreement.

大会たいかいは来月らいげつ七日なのか、市民しみんホールにおいて開催かいさいいたします。
The tournament will be held on the seventh of next month at the civic hall.

江戸えど時代じだいにおける町人ちょうにんの暮くらしを調しらべています。
I'm researching the lives of townspeople in the Edo period.

～において / ～における · Examples (cont.)

医療いりょうの分野ぶんやにおけるAIの利用りようが急速きゅうそくに広ひろがっている。
The use of AI in the field of medicine is spreading rapidly.

面接めんせつにおいては、話はなす内容ないようだけでなく話はなし方かたも見みられている。
In an interview it is not only what you say but how you say it that is being judged.

我わが国くににおいても、人口じんこうの減少げんしょうは深刻しんこくな問題もんだいとなりつつある。
In our country too, the falling population is becoming a serious problem.

価格かかくにおいては他社たしゃに負まけているが、品質ひんしつにおいては自信じしんがある。
We lose to our competitors on price, but where quality is concerned we are confident.

～において / ～における · Nuance & register

This pattern is formal and written, with essentially no casual life at all. Notices, minutes, academic papers, legal text, ceremonial speech and the polished register of the news are its habitat. Saying ❌コンビニにおいてパンを買かった is not a grammar error - it is a tonal disaster, roughly like writing "I did procure bread at the convenience store."

Two practical consequences.

First, において covers only some of what で covers. で marks place, but also means ("with a knife"), cause ("because of a cold"), material, and limit ("within a week"). において inherits only the place, time, situation and field senses. There is no ❌ナイフにおいて切きる.

～において / ～における · Nuance & register (cont.)

Second, the "field or respect" sense is where において earns its keep, because で there can be ambiguous. この点てんにおいて - "in this respect" - is crisper in formal prose than この点てんで, which could momentarily be read as a location.

において also carries a faint ceremonial weight when used of an event. A wedding invitation says ホテルの大だいホールにおいて; a text to a friend says ホテルで. The formality is doing real work in the first case - it signals that this is an occasion.

～において / ～における · Nuance & register (cont.)

One last thing worth noticing: における is a fixture of titles. 現代げんだい社会しゃかいにおける家族かぞくの役割やくわり is exactly the shape of an essay title, a chapter heading, a conference paper. If you are writing academic Japanese, this is a phrase you will use constantly.

～において / ～における · ～において vs ～で

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register, narrower coverage. において can replace the place / time / situation / field uses of で and nothing else, and only in formal writing or formal speech.

試合しあいは大阪おおさかで行おこなわれた。
The match was held in Osaka.

試合しあいは大阪おおさかにおいて行おこなわれた。
The match was held in Osaka. (announcement register)

Identical in meaning; the second belongs in a written report. Now watch it fail:

～において / ～における · ～において vs ～で (cont.)

電車でんしゃが事故じこで止とまっている。
The trains are stopped because of an accident.

❌事故じこにおいて止とまっている is ungrammatical, because this で marks a cause, and において has no cause sense. The same goes for バスで行いく (means) and 木きで作つくる (material) - neither converts.

～において / ～における · ～における + Noun vs ～の + Noun

Rule of thumb - の says the two nouns are connected somehow; における says specifically "within / inside / in the context of". When the relationship needs pinning down, における does it.

日本にほんの教育きょういく
Japanese education

日本にほんにおける外国語がいこくご教育きょういく
foreign-language education in Japan

The first could mean education that is Japanese in style, education owned by Japan, education about Japan - の does not say. The second is unambiguous: it is education happening inside Japan, seen as a setting. This precision is why academic titles reach for における rather than の, and why replacing it with の in an essay title makes the title vaguer even though it stays grammatical.

～において / ～における · ～における + Noun vs ～の + Noun (cont.)

Between the two sits での, which is the neutral-register version of the same idea: 日本にほんでの生活せいかつ, "life in Japan". Use での when you are speaking, における when you are writing formally.

～において / ～における · ～において vs ～について

Rule of thumb - において is the setting; について is the subject matter. "Discussed at the meeting" is において; "discussed about the meeting" is について.

Both are formal-ish, both hang off に, and English "regarding" is offered as a translation of both, which is how learners end up swapping them.

その問題もんだいは先日せんじつの会議かいぎにおいて議論ぎろんされた。
That issue was debated at the meeting the other day.

先日せんじつの会議かいぎについて報告書ほうこくしょを書かいた。
I wrote a report about the meeting the other day.

～において / ～における · ～において vs ～について (cont.)

In the first, the meeting is the setting the debating happened in. In the second, the meeting is what the report is on. Substituting one for the other produces a sentence that is grammatical and simply says something else - which is the most dangerous kind of mistake.

～において / ～における · Common mistakes

  • ❌ はさみにおいて紙かみを切きった。 → ✅ はさみで紙かみを切きった。
    において inherits only the place, time, situation and field senses of で. It never marks an instrument.

  • ❌ 台風たいふうにおいて学校がっこうが休やすみになった。 → ✅ 台風たいふうで学校がっこうが休やすみになった。
    This で is a cause, and there is no cause sense in において. If you want the formal register, use 台風たいふうのため.

  • ❌ 職場しょくばにおいて問題もんだいが増ふえている。（intending "problems in the workplace") → ✅ 職場しょくばにおける問題もんだいが増ふえている。
    Before a noun you need における. As written, the sentence says the increasing is happening at the workplace, which is not quite the same claim.

～において / ～における · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 昨日きのう、駅前えきまえのカフェにおいて友ともだちに会あった。 → ✅ 駅前えきまえのカフェで友ともだちに会あった。
    Grammatically fine, tonally absurd. A private, everyday event does not take announcement vocabulary.

A century is a time setting, which is one of the four senses において inherits from で. The sentence is written prose, so the formal form fits; 20世紀せいきに would also be possible and plainer.

4. Find and fix the error: 大雨おおあめにおいて試合しあいが中止ちゅうしになり、来週らいしゅう、同おなじ球場きゅうじょうにおいて行おこなわれることになった。

✅ 大雨おおあめのため試合しあいが中止ちゅうしになり、来週らいしゅう、同おなじ球場きゅうじょうにおいて行おこなわれることになった。

～において / ～における · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 会場かいじょうでのアンケート (b) 会場かいじょうにおけるアンケート

Both mean "a survey at the venue", and neither is wrong. The difference is register and, faintly, framing.

(a) is ordinary Japanese - spoken, or written in an email to colleagues. での is just で fitted to modify a noun.

(b) is document Japanese. It is what would be printed on the cover of the report, and it frames the venue as a defined setting whose conditions matter, rather than merely as the spot where the clipboards were handed out. Choosing (b) in conversation would sound as though you were quoting the report out loud.

～に対して

Meaning · toward; at; in response to - and, from a clause, "whereas"（にたいして）

に対たいして points. It names the person or thing an action, an attitude or a reaction is aimed at: who you are polite to, what you are objecting to, what a reply is a reply to. Say "about" in your head and you will reach for it wrongly - this is not the topic of the sentence, it is the target.

A second use falls out of the same image. If you set one thing opposite another, you get contrast: Aのに対たいして、B - "A, whereas B". And a third, narrower use gives ratios: "per one participant, two bottles."

～に対して · Formation

対たいする is a verb ("face, confront"), so it inflects: the て-form modifies verbs and adjectives, the dictionary form modifies nouns.

～に対して · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (target) Noun + に対たいして 子こども → 子こどもに対たいしてやさしい
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + に対たいする + Noun 客きゃく → 客きゃくに対たいする態度たいど
Verb / い-adjective (contrast) Plain form + のに対たいして 高たかい → 高たかいのに対たいして
な-adjective (contrast) ～な + のに対たいして 静しずかだ → 静しずかなのに対たいして

～に対して · Formation (cont.)

Note - the contrastive use needs の before it. A clause cannot attach directly: ❌高たかいに対たいして. The の turns the clause into something noun-like, which is what に対たいして requires.

～に対して · Examples

子こどもに対たいしてやさしい人ひとです。
He's a person who is kind to children.

その質問しつもんに対たいして、彼かれは何なにも答こたえなかった。
He gave no answer to that question.

目上めうえの人ひとに対たいしてそんな言いい方かたをしてはいけない。
You mustn't speak that way to someone senior to you.

市しの決定けっていに対たいして、住民じゅうみんが強つよく反発はんぱつしている。
Residents are pushing back hard against the city's decision.

～に対して · Examples (cont.)

参加者さんかしゃ一人ひとりに対たいして、飲のみ物ものを二本にほんずつ用意よういした。
We laid on two drinks per participant.

姉あねが外そとで体からだを動うごかすのが好すきなのに対たいして、妹いもうとは一日中いちにちじゅう本ほんを読よんでいる。
Where my older sister loves being outside and moving about, my younger sister reads all day.

お客様きゃくさまに対たいする言葉遣ことばづかいについては、入社にゅうしゃしたばかりのころに厳きびしく注意ちゅういされた。
I got told off sharply about how I spoke to customers when I had only just joined.

去年きょねんの売上うりあげがまったく伸のびなかったのに対たいして、今年ことしは春はるからずっと好調こうちょうだ。
Whereas last year's sales didn't grow at all, this year has been strong right through from spring.

～に対して · Nuance & register

The target use leans formal, but not so far that it is out of place in ordinary polite conversation. 部下ぶかに対たいして厳きびしい is something a colleague might say out loud. What marks it as slightly weighty is that plain に would usually do the job: 子こどもにやさしい and 子こどもに対たいしてやさしい mean the same thing, and the second is more deliberate - it puts the relationship itself under a spotlight. In relaxed speech you would normally drop it.

Where に対たいして is not optional is when the noun is the target of a reaction: a protest, a reply, a complaint, a demand, an evaluation. 説明せつめいに対たいして質問しつもんが出でた - the questions came back at the explanation. Bare に there is thinner and sometimes ambiguous.

～に対して · Nuance & register (cont.)

The contrastive use is squarely written. It belongs in comparisons, reports and essays, and it has a cool, analytical tone: the writer is laying two facts side by side, not complaining. That distinguishes it from のに, which contrasts and carries dissatisfaction.

に対たいし, without て, is the clipped written form. It is standard in newspapers and official documents - 組合くみあいは会社かいしゃに対たいし回答かいとうを求もとめた - and sounds stiff if spoken aloud outside a formal announcement.

Note - the ratio use is common in explanations of quantity and in statistics: 人口じんこう千人せんにんに対たいして医師いしが三人さんにん. It is the one sense where the noun in front is a number, so it is easy to spot and easy to keep separate from the other two.

～に対して · ～に対して vs ～について

Rule of thumb - に対たいして is the target of an action or attitude; について is the subject matter of a statement. Aim vs topic.

新あたらしい制度せいどについて社員しゃいんに説明せつめいした。
I explained the new system to the staff.

新あたらしい制度せいどに対たいして社員しゃいんから不満ふまんが出でた。
Complaints came from the staff against the new system.

～に対して · ～に対して vs ～について (cont.)

In the first the system is what the explanation is about. In the second it is what the complaints are aimed at. Note that the two sentences have different verbs, and that is the reliable clue: verbs of saying, writing and investigating take について, while verbs of reacting - 反対はんたいする, 抗議こうぎする, 答こたえる, 文句もんくを言いう - take に対たいして.

～に対して · ～に対して vs ～にとって

Rule of thumb - に対たいして looks outward from the subject toward the noun; にとって looks outward from the noun at the world. Target vs standpoint.

親おやに対たいしてあんな口くちをきくべきではない。
You shouldn't talk to your parents like that.

親おやにとって、子こどもの健康けんこうが一番いちばんの心配しんぱいだ。
For parents, their children's health is the biggest worry.

～に対して · ～に対して vs ～にとって (cont.)

In the first, the parents are on the receiving end of the speaking. In the second, nothing is aimed at them at all - we have simply climbed inside their heads to judge what matters. A quick test: にとって is nearly always followed by an evaluation (大切たいせつだ, 難むずかしい, ありがたい), whereas に対たいして is followed by an action or a stance.

～に対して · ～のに対して vs ～のに

Rule of thumb - both put two clauses against each other, but のに対たいして is a neutral comparison and のに is a complaint. If you are annoyed, you want のに.

東京とうきょうの冬ふゆは晴はれの日ひが多おおいのに対たいして、日本海側にほんかいがわは雪ゆきの日ひが続つづく。
Winter in Tokyo has many clear days, whereas on the Japan Sea side the snow goes on and on.

約束やくそくしたのに、彼かれは来こなかった。
He promised, and yet he didn't come.

～に対して · ～のに対して vs ～のに (cont.)

The first sentence has no feeling in it: two regions, two climates, stated side by side. The second is a grievance. There is also a structural difference - のに対たいして compares two things of the same kind along the same axis (winters with winters, sales with sales), while のに simply says the second clause is unexpected given the first.

For the same neutral comparison you will also meet 一方いっぽう（で）, which does much the same work and joins sentences rather than clauses.

～に対して · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この問題もんだいに対たいして話はなし合あいましょう。 → ✅ この問題もんだいについて話はなし合あいましょう。
    話はなし合あう takes a topic, not a target. Use に対たいして only when something is being aimed at the noun.

  • ❌ 私わたしに対たいして、この写真しゃしんは宝物たからものです。 → ✅ 私わたしにとって、この写真しゃしんは宝物たからものです。
    "For me" meaning "from my point of view" is にとって. 私わたしに対たいして would mean the photograph is doing something to you.

  • ❌ 兄あには背せが高たかいに対たいして、弟おとうとは小ちいさい。 → ✅ 兄あには背せが高たかいのに対たいして、弟おとうとは小ちいさい。
    The contrastive use attaches to a nominalised clause. The の is compulsory, and with a な-adjective it becomes な+のに対たいして.

～に対して · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 上司じょうしに対たいして態度たいどが悪わるい。（intending "his attitude toward his boss is bad"） → ✅ 上司じょうしに**対たいする**態度たいどが悪わるい。
    Before a noun the verb takes its dictionary form. As written, the sentence attaches the target to 悪わるい and drifts toward "he is badly behaved in the direction of his boss."

～に対して · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれは後輩こうはいに厳きびしい。 (b) 彼かれは後輩こうはいに対たいして厳きびしい。

Both say he is hard on his juniors, and both are correct. (a) is the ordinary spoken sentence: plain に is enough, and nobody would think twice about it.

(b) makes the relationship itself the point. The longer phrase draws attention to the direction of the strictness - juniors specifically, as opposed to anyone else - and it raises the register, so it fits a written assessment, a complaint made formally, or a sentence that goes on to contrast his behaviour toward someone else: 後輩こうはいに対たいしては厳きびしいが、上司じょうしには何なにも言いわない.

～にとって

Meaning · for; from the standpoint of - the sentence is judged from inside the position of the person or group named

にとって hands the microphone to somebody else. It says: seen from where they stand, here is how things look. What follows is almost always an evaluation - important, hard, welcome, painful, worthless - because that is the only kind of statement a standpoint can change.

It cannot mean "about", and it cannot mark the target of an action. Those two facts do most of the work of keeping it apart from its neighbours.

～にとって · Formation

Attaches to a bare noun naming somebody capable of having a stake in the matter: a person, a group, a company, a country, sometimes a living thing.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (person or group) Noun + にとって 私わたし → 私わたしにとって大切たいせつだ
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + にとっての + Noun 学生がくせい → 学生がくせいにとっての理想りそう
Noun, as topic Noun + にとっては 彼かれ → 彼かれにとっては簡単かんたんだ
Noun, as addition Noun + にとっても 会社かいしゃ → 会社かいしゃにとっても損そんだ
Noun, business keigo Noun + にとりまして 弊社へいしゃ → 弊社へいしゃにとりましては

～にとって · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - after にとって you need an evaluation, not an action. 大切たいせつだ・必要ひつようだ・難むずかしい・ありがたい・問題もんだいだ・忘わすれられない all work. A plain report of what somebody did does not: ❌私わたしにとって毎日まいにち走はしっています.

～にとって · Examples

私わたしにとって、家族かぞくが一番いちばん大切たいせつです。
For me, family comes first.

子こどもにとってこの本ほんは難むずかしすぎる。
This book is too hard for a child.

私わたしにとっては少すこし高たかいが、買かう価値かちはあると思おもう。
It's a bit pricey as far as I'm concerned, but I think it's worth buying.

会社かいしゃにとって、人ひとを育そだてることは一番いちばん確実かくじつな投資とうしだ。
For a company, developing its people is the surest investment there is.

～にとって · Examples (cont.)

北国きたぐにで育そだった人ひとにとって、東京とうきょうの冬ふゆはほとんど寒さむくないらしい。
Apparently, for people raised in the far north, a Tokyo winter is barely cold at all.

あのときの一言ひとことは、彼女かのじょにとって一生いっしょう忘わすれられないものになった。
That one remark became something she would never forget as long as she lived.

留学生りゅうがくせいにとっての最初さいしょの壁かべは、言葉ことばよりも生活せいかつ習慣しゅうかんかもしれない。
The first wall for an exchange student may be daily customs rather than language.

地方ちほうの小ちいさな町まちにとって、若わかい人ひとが出でていくことは放ほうっておけない問題もんだいだ。
For a small rural town, young people leaving is a problem that cannot be left alone.

～にとって · Nuance & register

にとって is close to register-neutral. It is at home in an essay, a news article, a job interview and a conversation with a friend. Nothing about it is stiff, and unlike に関かんして or において it has not been pushed out of speech by a plainer alternative.

What it does carry is a mild rhetorical weight. Because it explicitly frames a judgement as belonging to somebody, it invites the reader to notice that other people might see it differently. 私わたしにとっては高たかい is politer than 高たかい for exactly that reason: it presents the verdict as personal rather than absolute. Sales staff and diplomats lean on this.

～にとって · Nuance & register (cont.)

In casual speech, には covers most of the same ground with fewer syllables - 僕ぼくにはちょっと無理むりかな - and younger speakers also reach for 個人的こじんてきには, "personally speaking". にとって is not unnatural in conversation, but a whole conversation full of it would sound like an essay read aloud.

にとりまして is the business-keigo form, common in apologies and in speeches: 弊社へいしゃにとりまして大変たいへんありがたいお話はなしです. Recognise it; you will rarely need to produce it.

～にとって · Nuance & register (cont.)

One restriction worth stating plainly: the noun has to be something that can have interests. People, groups, companies, countries, and living things all qualify - 植物しょくぶつにとって水みずは欠かかせない is perfectly normal. A pure abstraction or an inanimate object usually does not: ❌この机つくえにとって is odd, because a desk has no point of view to speak from.

～にとって · ～にとって vs ～には

Rule of thumb - they overlap heavily, and には is the shorter, more spoken option. にとって is unambiguous; には is a stack of two particles and can also mean "to" or "in", so context has to sort it out.

私わたしにはこの説明せつめいがよく分わからなかった。
I didn't really follow this explanation.

初心者しょしんしゃにとって、この説明せつめいは不親切ふしんせつだ。
For a beginner, this explanation is unhelpful.

～にとって · ～にとって vs ～には (cont.)

Both frame a judgement from somebody's position. The difference shows up when the sentence could be read another way. 山田やまださんには話はなした means "I told Yamada" - に marking the recipient, は marking contrast - and has nothing to do with standpoints. 山田やまださんにとって can only ever mean "from Yamada's point of view". In writing, where you cannot lean on intonation, that certainty is worth the extra syllables.

～にとって · ～にとって vs ～に対して

Rule of thumb - にとって is the viewer; に対たいして is the target. Nothing is being aimed at the noun in a にとって sentence.

新人しんじんにとって、最初さいしょの一年いちねんは覚おぼえることばかりだ。
For a new employee, the first year is nothing but things to learn.

新人しんじんに対たいして、もう少すこし丁寧ていねいに説明せつめいしたほうがいい。
You'd do better to explain things to a new employee a bit more carefully.

～にとって · ～にとって vs ～に対して (cont.)

The first sentence describes the world as the newcomer experiences it. The second describes what somebody else should do to them. A quick test: if you can add "…and they may be the only one who sees it that way", it is にとって. If you can add "…and they are on the receiving end", it is に対たいして.

～にとって · ～にとって vs ～として

Rule of thumb - にとって = "for X, from X's side of the fence". として = "as X, in the role of X". にとって looks out from the noun; として puts the subject into the noun.

Both come out as English "as" or "for" often enough to blur, and they can even sit in the same slot with different meanings.

親おやにとって、子こどもの進路しんろは口くちを出だしにくい問題もんだいだ。
For a parent, a child's career choice is a hard thing to weigh in on.

親おやとして、一度いちどは本音ほんねを伝つたえておきたい。
As a parent, I'd like to say what I really think at least once.

～にとって · ～にとって vs ～として (cont.)

In the first, parents are the group whose viewpoint we are borrowing; the speaker need not be one. In the second, the speaker is a parent and is acting in that capacity. Notice also what follows each: にとって is followed by an evaluation, として by an action.

～にとって · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私わたしにとって毎朝まいあさコーヒーを飲のみます。 → ✅ 私わたしは毎朝まいあさコーヒーを飲のみます。
    にとって frames a judgement, so it needs an evaluative predicate. A plain report of what you do takes an ordinary topic は.

  • ❌ この計画けいかくにとって意見いけんを聞きかせてください。 → ✅ この計画けいかくについて意見いけんを聞きかせてください。
    にとって cannot mean "about". A plan is not a viewpoint-holder; it is a topic.

  • ❌ 先生せんせいにとって失礼しつれいなことを言いってしまった。 → ✅ 先生せんせいに**対たいして**失礼しつれいなことを言いってしまった。
    Rudeness is aimed at someone. That is に対たいして; にとって would put the teacher in the judge's seat instead of the victim's.

～にとって · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 彼かれにとって感謝かんしゃしています。 → ✅ 彼かれに感謝かんしゃしています。
    Gratitude has a recipient, not a standpoint. Plain に is what 感謝かんしゃする takes.

～にとって · Practice 1

1. 受験生じゅけんせい____、冬ふゆは一年いちねんで一番いちばんつらい季節きせつだろう。

受験生じゅけんせいにとって、冬ふゆは一年いちねんで一番いちばんつらい季節きせつだろう。

The sentence rates winter from the exam candidates' position - for everyone else winter is just winter. つらい is an evaluation, which is exactly what にとって requires.

～にとって · Practice 2

2. Choose: 外国がいこくのお客様きゃくさま（にとって・に対たいして）は、英語えいごのメニューを用意よういしています。

外国がいこくのお客様きゃくさまに対たいしては、英語えいごのメニューを用意よういしています。

The restaurant is doing something for - that is, aimed at - those customers, so they are a target, not a viewpoint. にとって would need a judgement to follow, such as 外国がいこくのお客様きゃくさまにとって注文ちゅうもんは難むずかしい.

～にとって · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "For me, that failure ended up being a good experience."

私わたしにとって、あの失敗しっぱいはいい経験けいけんになりました。

The whole point of the sentence is that the verdict is personal - someone else might well call the same failure a disaster. That is precisely the work にとって does. 私わたしには would also be natural in speech.

～にとって · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 会社かいしゃにとって、社員しゃいんにとって新あたらしい制度せいどを説明せつめいする義務ぎむがある。

✅ 会社かいしゃには、社員しゃいんに対たいして新あたらしい制度せいどを説明せつめいする義務ぎむがある。

The second にとって is wrong: the employees are who the explanation is delivered to, so they need に対たいして. The first is better replaced by plain には, since 義務ぎむがある states a fact about the company rather than judging something from its viewpoint.

～にとって · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 田中たなかさんにとってこの仕事しごとは簡単かんたんだ。 (b) この仕事しごとは簡単かんたんだ。

(b) states the job's difficulty as a plain fact about the job - anyone would find it easy.

(a) attaches the verdict to one person. It says the job is easy as Tanaka experiences it, and it quietly implies that this is not everybody's experience: someone else might struggle. That built-in "…though not necessarily for others" is the whole reason the pattern exists, and it is why にとって is useful for softening an opinion you do not want to present as objective truth.

～として

Meaning · as; in the capacity of; in the role of - the hat somebody or something is wearing while it does what it does

として assigns a role. A person acts as a representative, a building is used as a gallery, a sum of money is handed over as a gift. The thing itself does not change; what として supplies is the capacity in which it is being treated.

Two extensions follow naturally. としては sets a standard of judgement - "measured as an X" - and, with a person, "speaking for myself". And a handful of fixed adverbs like 原則げんそくとして and 参考さんこうとして are simply this pattern frozen solid.

～として · Formation

Attaches to a bare noun. No な, no の before it.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (role), modifying a verb Noun + として 医者いしゃ → 医者いしゃとして働はたらく
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + としての + Noun 親おや → 親おやとしての責任せきにん
Noun, as standard or standpoint Noun + としては 新人しんじん → 新人しんじんとしてはよくやっている
Noun, as addition Noun + としても 趣味しゅみ → 趣味しゅみとしても楽たのしめる
Noun, ruling one out Noun + としてではなく 仕事しごと → 仕事しごととしてではなく
Fixed adverbs 原則げんそく／例外れいがい／参考さんこう + として 原則げんそく → 原則げんそくとして禁止きんしです

～として · Formation (cont.)

Note - beyond N3 you will meet 一人ひとりとして…ない, "not a single person", where として combines with a counter and a negative. It is the same word doing an emphatic job, and it is worth recognising even though you do not need to produce it yet.

～として · Examples

兄あには医者いしゃとして働はたらいています。
My older brother works as a doctor.

趣味しゅみとして絵えを描かいています。
I paint as a hobby.

会社かいしゃの代表だいひょうとして式しきに出席しゅっせきしました。
I attended the ceremony as the company's representative.

この建物たてものは今いま、美術館びじゅつかんとして使つかわれている。
This building is now used as an art museum.

～として · Examples (cont.)

参考さんこうとして、去年きょねんの資料しりょうもお送おくりしておきます。
I'll send last year's materials along as well, for reference.

入はいったばかりの人ひととしては、よくやっているほうだと思おもう。
For someone who's only just joined, I'd say he's doing well.

彼女かのじょは友ともだちとしては最高さいこうだが、仕事しごとのパートナーとしては少すこし心配しんぱいだ。
She's wonderful as a friend, but as a working partner I have my doubts.

留学りゅうがくは語学ごがくの勉強べんきょうとしてだけでなく、人生じんせいの経験けいけんとしても大おおきな意味いみがある。
Studying abroad matters not only as language study but as a life experience too.

～として · Nuance & register

として is register-neutral. It appears in casual conversation, in job interviews, in newspapers and in contracts without changing shape or feeling. Nothing about it is stiff.

What is register-marked is the company it keeps. The fixed adverbs - 原則げんそくとして ("as a rule"), 例外れいがいとして ("by way of exception"), 結果けっかとして ("as a result") - belong to written and formal speech. A friend says だいたいダメだよ; a notice says 原則げんそくとしてお断ことわりしております.

としては deserves its own line, because it does two different things and speakers rely on both.

～として · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • As a category standard: 中古ちゅうことしては高たかい -
    "expensive for a second-hand one." The judgement is made against what is normal for the category.
  • As a personal position: 私わたしとしては反対はんたいです -
    "my own position is that I'm against it." This is a politeness device. It marks the opinion as yours alone and leaves room for the group to decide otherwise, which is why it is everywhere in meetings.

The second of those is worth practising. Where English says "personally, I…", Japanese very often says 私わたしとしては, and a learner who reaches for 私わたしは instead sounds more insistent than they meant to.

～として · ～として vs ～にとって

Rule of thumb - として puts the subject into the role; にとって looks out from the noun's viewpoint. "As a teacher, I…" is として; "for a teacher, this is…" is にとって.

教師きょうしとして、生徒せいとの話はなしはきちんと聞ききたい。
As a teacher, I want to listen properly to what my students say.

教師きょうしにとって、生徒せいとに信頼しんらいされることほどうれしいことはない。
For a teacher, nothing is more gratifying than being trusted by the students.

In the first sentence the speaker is a teacher and is acting in that capacity. In the second the speaker is standing outside, describing how the world looks to teachers - and need not be one. The predicate gives it away too: として is followed by an action or an intention, にとって by an evaluation.

～として · ～として vs ～で

Rule of thumb - で can also mark a capacity, but only loosely and only in casual speech. として states the role explicitly and works at any register.

去年きょねんはアルバイトで入はいっていました。
I was there on a part-time basis last year.

去年きょねんはアルバイト職員しょくいんとして採用さいようされました。
Last year I was taken on as a part-time member of staff.

～として · ～として vs ～で (cont.)

Both are natural; the second is what you would write on a form. The gap widens when the role is formal or contested - ❌代表だいひょうで意見いけんを述のべた is at best vague about whether you were the representative, while 代表だいひょうとして意見いけんを述のべた states it plainly. When the role is what matters, use として.

～として · ～としては vs ～にしては

Rule of thumb - としては measures something within its category; にしては says the result is surprising for that category. Add "considering" and a raised eyebrow, and you have にしては.

子こどもとしてはよく考かんがえているほうだ。
As children go, he thinks things through well.

子こどもにしてはよく考かんがえている。
He thinks things through well, for a child.

～として · ～としては vs ～にしては (cont.)

The first ranks him among children - a neutral placement inside the category. The second is mildly astonished: given that he is a child, this is more than expected. Where they part company completely is the personal-position use. 私わたしとしては賛成さんせいです means "my position is that I'm in favour"; ❌私わたしにしては賛成さんせいです would have to mean "surprisingly for me, I'm in favour", which is rarely what anyone intends.

～として · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 通訳つうやくになって会議かいぎに出でました。（intending "attended as an interpreter"） → ✅ 通訳つうやくとして会議かいぎに出でました。
    になる means become: the wrong version says you turned into an interpreter and then went. として names the capacity you attended in.

  • ❌ この部屋へやは倉庫そうこにして使つかっています。 → ✅ この部屋へやは倉庫そうことして使つかっています。
    にする is "turn it into"; として使つかう is "use it as". Only the second describes an ongoing role.

  • ❌ 先生せんせいとしての働はたらいています。 → ✅ 先生せんせいとして働はたらいています。
    としての exists only in front of a noun (先生せんせいとしての経験けいけん). Before a verb, drop the の.

～として · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 私わたしとして、この写真しゃしんはとても大切たいせつです。 → ✅ 私わたしにとって、この写真しゃしんはとても大切たいせつです。
    This is a judgement made from your side, not a role you are performing. 私わたしとしては would be possible only if you were stating a position in a discussion.

～として · Practice 1

1. その画家がかは長ながい間あいだ、教師きょうし____生活せいかつしていた。

教師きょうしとして生活せいかつしていた。

The painter was living in the capacity of a teacher - a role, held over a long period. 教師きょうしになって would say he became one at a particular moment, which is a different claim.

～として · Practice 2

2. Choose: 初はじめて作つくった(としては・にしては)、なかなかうまくできた。

初はじめて作つくったにしては、なかなかうまくできた。

The sentence is expressing surprise: given that it was a first attempt, the result beat expectations. That is にしては. としては also attaches to nouns rather than clauses, so 初はじめて作つくったとしては would not work here anyway.

～として · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'd like to send this as a thank-you, not as a gift."

これはプレゼントとしてではなく、お礼れいとして送おくりたいのです。

としてではなく rules out one role before naming the intended one. The は is the ordinary contrastive は sitting on top of the pattern, which is why the shape is として+では+なく rather than としてじゃない.

～として · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 山田やまださんはリーダーにとって優すぐれているが、技術者ぎじゅつしゃとしてこの仕事しごとは向むいていないと思おもう。

✅ 山田やまださんはリーダーとしては優すぐれているが、技術者ぎじゅつしゃとしてはこの仕事しごとは向むいていないと思おもう。

Both slots are judging him within a role, so both need として - and は on each, because the whole sentence is built on setting one role against the other. にとって would make the leader the one doing the judging.

～として · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 私わたしはこの案あんに反対はんたいです。 (b) 私わたしとしてはこの案あんに反対はんたいです。

(a) is a flat statement of opposition. In a meeting it lands as a personal stand and can sound confrontational, especially if others have already spoken in favour.

(b) frames the same opposition as your position within the discussion - "my own view is against it" - which implicitly grants that the group may decide otherwise. It is softer and more collegial, and it is the standard shape for disagreeing in a Japanese meeting. The propositional content is identical; only the social move differs.

～向き - facing

Meaning · facing such-and-such a way - the direction a thing is turned toward（むき）

向むき is the noun form of 向むく, "to face". Stuck onto a direction word it gives you the orientation of something that is standing still: a south-facing room, an arrow pointing down, an envelope placed face up.

There is no movement in it. A south-facing window is not going anywhere; it is simply turned that way. That single fact separates this pattern from に向むかって, and it is where learners slip.

～向き - facing · Overview

The same word has a second, unrelated-looking life meaning "suited to", as in 子こども向むきの本ほん. That sense is treated separately; this page is only about direction.

～向き - facing · Formation

Attaches to Form Example
Direction word 南みなみ・北きた・東ひがし・西にし + 向むき 南みなみ → 南みなみ向むきの部屋へや
Up / down / sideways 上うわ・下した・横よこ + 向むき 下した → 下した向むきの矢印やじるし
Front / back (also figurative) 前まえ・後うしろ + 向むき 前まえ → 前まえ向むきに考かんがえる
As a bare noun Noun + の + 向むき いす → いすの向むきを変かえる
Adverbially, before a verb ～向むきに + verb 上うわ向むき → 上うわ向むきに置おく
As a predicate ～向むきだ／である 東ひがし → 玄関げんかんは東ひがし向むきだ

～向き - facing · Formation (cont.)

Note on readings - two of these are worth memorising as whole words rather than as sums. 上うわ向むき is standardly うわむき, not うえむき. And 風かざ向むき is かざむき: 風かぜ takes its bound form かざ inside compounds, as it does in 風車かざぐるま and 風上かざかみ. In both words the 向むき half is regular. 下した向むき is regular throughout.

～向き - facing · Examples

この部屋へやは南みなみ向むきで、日当ひあたりがいい。
This room faces south and gets plenty of sun.

いすの**向むき**を変かえてください。
Please turn the chair around.

矢印やじるしが下した向むきになっている。
The arrow is pointing downward.

封筒ふうとうは表おもてを上うわ向むきにして箱はこに入いれてください。
Put the envelopes in the box face up.

～向き - facing · Examples (cont.)

玄関げんかんが東ひがし向むきなので、朝日あさひがよく入はいる。
The entrance faces east, so the morning sun comes right in.

風かざ向むきが変かわって、煙けむりがこちらに流ながれてきた。
The wind shifted and the smoke drifted over to our side.

失敗しっぱいしても、いつまでも後うしろ**向むきでいるより前まえ向むき**に考かんがえたほうがいい。
Even when things go wrong, it's better to think forward than to stay stuck looking backward.

部屋へやを借かりるときは、家賃やちんや広ひろさだけでなく、窓まどの**向むき**も確たしかめておいたほうがいい。
When you rent a flat, it's worth checking which way the windows face, not just the rent and the floor space.

～向き - facing · Nuance & register

This is an ordinary, register-neutral noun. Nothing about it is formal or casual; it turns up in estate agents' listings, in assembly instructions, in weather reports and in conversation, all in the same shape.

Two of its habitats are worth knowing about, because they are where you will actually meet it.

Property. 南みなみ向むき is a selling point in Japan, and listings advertise it the way an English listing advertises a garden. The reason is practical - sun for the laundry and warmth in winter - and the vocabulary is fixed: 南みなみ向むきバルコニー, 東ひがし向むきの窓まど.

～向き - facing · Nuance & register (cont.)

Attitude. 前まえ向むき and 後うしろ向むき have drifted from literal facing to mean "positive / constructive" and "negative / defeatist". These are extremely common in work Japanese: 前まえ向むきに検討けんとうします is what a company says when it wants to sound willing.

Note - 前まえ向むきに検討けんとうします is a famous soft refusal. Literally "we will consider it positively", it very often means the matter will quietly go nowhere. Take it as encouragement, not as a promise.

～向き - facing · Nuance & register (cont.)

The bare noun 向むき also appears in a fixed phrase you will hear in shops and offices: 向むきを変かえる, "turn it round". And 風かざ向むき has its own figurative use - 風かざ向むきが変かわった, "the wind has changed", said of a situation turning in someone's favour.

～向き - facing · ～向き vs ～に向かって

Rule of thumb - 向むき is a static orientation; に向むかって is movement or action toward something. If anything is travelling, you need に向むかって.

その像ぞうは海うみのほうを向むいて立たっている。
The statue stands facing the sea.

船ふねは島しまに**向むかって**進すすんでいる。
The ship is heading toward the island.

～向き - facing · ～向き vs ～に向かって (cont.)

The statue is not going to the sea; it is merely turned that way, and 海うみ向むき would describe it. The ship is under way, so に向むかって is the only option: ❌島しま向むきに進すすむ does not work. Note also the grammatical shape - 向むき attaches directly to the noun with no particle, while 向むかって demands に.

～向き - facing · 向き vs 方向

Rule of thumb - 向むき is which way a thing is turned; 方向ほうこう is a direction out in the world, the way you go. A chair has a 向むき; a road has a 方向ほうこう.

テーブルの**向むき**を少すこし右みぎに回まわした。
I turned the table a little to the right.

駅えきはこの**方向ほうこう**でいいですか。
Is the station this way?

～向き - facing · 向き vs 方向 (cont.)

Swapping them produces sentences that sound off rather than wrong. ❌テーブルの方向ほうこうを変かえる suggests moving the table somewhere else, not rotating it; ❌駅えきはこの向むきですか sounds as though the station is facing you. The pair 向むきを変かえる (rotate) versus 方向ほうこうを変かえる (change course) is the cleanest way to hold them apart.

～向き - facing · ～向き vs ～向け

Rule of thumb - only 向むき does direction. 向むけ means "made for, aimed at an audience" and never means "facing".

北きた向むきの窓まどからは一日中いちにちじゅう日ひが入はいらない。
A north-facing window gets no sun all day.

これは海外かいがい向むけに作つくられたモデルです。
This is a model built for overseas markets.

～向き - facing · ～向き vs ～向け (cont.)

❌北きた向むけの窓まど is not Japanese. The two suffixes do overlap in the other sense of 向むき - "suitable for" - where 子こども向むき and 子こども向むけ are both possible and differ subtly. In the direction sense there is no overlap at all, so here at least the choice is easy.

～向き - facing · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この部屋へやは南みなみに向むきです。 → ✅ この部屋へやは南みなみ向むきです。
    The direction word joins directly to 向むき as a compound noun. There is no particle in between. With に you would need the verb: 南みなみに向むいています.

  • ❌ 北きた向むけのベランダは洗濯物せんたくものが乾かわきにくい。 → ✅ 北きた向むきのベランダは洗濯物せんたくものが乾かわきにくい。
    向むけ means "intended for" and cannot express facing. Only 向むき takes a compass point.

  • ❌ 子こどもたちは公園こうえん向むきに走はしっていった。 → ✅ 子こどもたちは公園こうえんに**向むかって**走はしっていった。
    Running is movement, and 向むき has no movement in it. Anything that travels takes に向むかって.

～向き - facing · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ カメラの方向ほうこうを上うえにしてください。 → ✅ カメラの**向むきを上うわ向むき**にしてください。
    Tilting the camera changes how it is turned, not where it is going. 方向ほうこう would suggest carrying it somewhere.

～向き - facing · Practice 1

1. この建物たてものの入口いりぐちは西にし____なので、夕方ゆうがたはまぶしい。

入口いりぐちは西にし向むきなので、夕方ゆうがたはまぶしい。

A compass point plus 向むき gives the orientation of a fixed thing. No particle comes between the two, and 西にし向むけ would be meaningless here.

～向き - facing · Practice 2

2. Choose: 台風たいふうは北きた（向むきに・に向むかって）進すすんでいます。

A typhoon is moving, and 向むき describes only static facing. Weather forecasts say 北きたに向むかって or 北きたへ進すすむ; ❌北きた向むきに進すすむ would try to give a storm a fixed orientation.

～向き - facing · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Please lay the photographs face up on the desk."

上うわ向むき - note the うわ reading - plus にする gives "put it into a face-up orientation". A common alternative is 表おもてを上うえにして, which says the same thing by naming the front rather than the direction.

～向き - facing · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 寝ねるときは下した向むきより上うえ向むきのほうが体からだにいいと言いわれている。

The grammar is fine; the reading is not. The standard reading of 上うわ向むき is うわむき, even though 下した向むき is the regular したむき. This is a compound whose first element keeps the older うわ form, as in 上着うわぎ and 上手うわて.

～向き - facing · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) いすを窓まどのほうに**向むけた**。 (b) いすの**向むき**を変かえた。

(a) uses the transitive verb 向むける and names the destination of the turning: the chair now faces the window specifically. The window is the point of the sentence.

(b) uses the noun and says only that the orientation is now different from what it was. Where it ended up is left open. In practice (b) is what you say while doing it and (a) is what you say when the direction matters - for instance because you wanted to look outside.

～向き - suited to

Meaning · suitable for; a good fit for - it happens to suit that person, group or purpose, whether or not anyone planned it that way（むき）

This is the same 向むき that gives you 南みなみ向むきの部屋へや, but with the direction sense worn away. Here it says: this thing and that audience match. A book that suits beginners, a dish that suits summer, a job that suits people who like talking.

The key word is happens to. 向むき is the speaker's assessment of a fit. It makes no claim that anybody designed the thing for that audience - and that is exactly what separates it from 向むけ, which does.

～向き - suited to · Formation

These clothes are light and cool, so they're good for summer.

It's quiet and the seats are roomy, so I'd call it a good café for studying.

～向き - suited to · Examples (cont.)

The paths on this mountain are well maintained, and many of the routes suit families.

Sales suits people who enjoy talking to others; I don't think it's for me.

This app has big text and few controls, so it suits people who aren't used to smartphones.

～向き - suited to · Nuance & register

Register-neutral and fully at home in speech. You can say これ、子こども向むきだね to a friend in a shop without sounding formal, and the same phrase can appear in a product review.

What the word carries is a judgement, and judgements belong to somebody. When you say 初心者しょしんしゃ向むきだ you are giving your reading of how well the thing fits - you are not reporting a fact about the publisher's intentions. This is why 向むき works so naturally in reviews, recommendations and warnings, and why it so often appears with hedges: 向むきかもしれない, 向むきだと思おもう, 向むきとは言いえない.

～向き - suited to · Nuance & register (cont.)

The negative 不向ふむき is a single word, not 向むき plus a prefix you can apply freely, and it is used above all of people and jobs: 彼かれは接客せっきゃくには不向ふむきだ. It is blunter than 向むきではない and can sting, so in careful speech people soften it: あまり向むいていないかもしれませんね.

向むき不向ふむき as a pair is a small fixed noun meaning "aptitude" - literally the suits and the does-not-suits. 人ひとには向むき不向ふむきがある is a stock consoling phrase: "people are cut out for different things."

～向き - suited to · ～向き vs ～向け

Rule of thumb - 向むき = it happens to suit them. 向むけ = it was made for them. Fit versus intention.

This is the pair the whole page exists for, and the difference is real even though many sentences accept both.

この辞書じしょは字じが大おおきいので高齢者こうれいしゃ向むきだ。
This dictionary has large print, so it works well for older readers.

この辞書じしょは高齢者こうれいしゃ向むけに作つくられている。
This dictionary was made for older readers.

～向き - suited to · ～向き vs ～向け (cont.)

The first is a reader's verdict: whatever the publisher had in mind, the big print suits them. The second is a statement about the design brief. Consequently 向むけ is what appears in business and marketing language - 海外かいがい向むけ, 法人ほうじん向むけ - while 向むき is what appears in reviews and advice.

One useful consequence: 向むけ takes に before a verb naturally (向むけに作つくる, 向むけに開ひらく) because intention modifies an action. 向むき mostly stays a predicate or a noun modifier, which is why ❌子こども向むきに作つくる sounds wrong to a native ear.

～向き - suited to · ～向き vs ～用

Rule of thumb - 用よう states a designated use: this item is for that and not for anything else. 向むき only rates a fit.

子こども**用よう**のいすはあちらにございます。
The children's chairs are over there.

この映画えいがは子こども**向むき**だと思おもう。
I think this film is one for children.

～向き - suited to · ～向き vs ～用 (cont.)

子こども用よう is a category label - the chair is a child's chair, and an adult using it is misusing it. 子こども向むき is a recommendation; an adult can watch the film perfectly well, it just fits children better. Labels on shelves and menus overwhelmingly use 用よう: 業務用ぎょうむよう, 洗濯用せんたくよう, 家庭用かていよう - it is the suffix of designated purpose.

～向き - suited to · ～向きだ vs ～に向いている

Rule of thumb - the two say the same thing from opposite ends. A は B 向むきだ: the thing is described, B is the audience. B は A に向むいている: the person is described, A is the activity.

この仕事しごとは細こまかい作業さぎょうが好すきな人ひと向むきだ。
This job suits people who like detailed work.

妹いもうとは細こまかい作業さぎょうに**向むいている**。
My younger sister is well suited to detailed work.

～向き - suited to · ～向きだ vs ～に向いている (cont.)

Getting these crossed is the commonest error with the pattern. Remember that 向むいている is a verb and needs に, while 向むき is a suffix and needs nothing at all. Also note that with a named individual, に向むいている is far more natural: 妹いもうと向むきだ would be odd, because 向むき prefers a type of person rather than a particular one.

～向き - suited to · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 子こども向むきに作つくられた番組ばんぐみです。 → ✅ 子こども**向むけ**に作つくられた番組ばんぐみです。
    Being made for an audience is intention, and intention is 向むけ. 向むき would only rate the result.

  • ❌ 彼かれはこの仕事しごと向むきです。 → ✅ 彼かれはこの仕事しごとに**向むいています**。
    The two ends are reversed. 向むき attaches to the audience, so the correct form with this word order would be この仕事しごとは彼かれのような人ひと向むきです.

  • ❌ 初心者しょしんしゃ向むきな教材きょうざいを探さがしています。 → ✅ 初心者しょしんしゃ向むきの教材きょうざいを探さがしています。
    向むき is a noun and links to the next noun with の. Only 前まえ向むき and 後うしろ向むき take な.

～向き - suited to · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ このコースは体力たいりょくに自信じしんがない人ひとに向むきです。 → ✅ …自信じしんがない人ひと向むきです。
    No particle stands between the audience and 向むき; they form a compound noun. The に belongs to the verb version, 人ひとに向むいています.

～向き - suited to · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この雑誌ざっしは若わかい女性じょせい向むきだ。 (b) この雑誌ざっしは若わかい女性じょせい向むけだ。

It is the sentence an editor or a bookshop clerk would use, and it says nothing about whether it actually suits them. ～向け

Meaning · intended for; aimed at - made, written, priced or organised with that audience or market in mind（むけ）

向むけ comes from 向むける, "to point something at". Somebody has aimed this thing at somebody: a programme at children, a car at the export market, a seminar at second-year students. The word carries intention, and that is its whole meaning.

It says nothing about whether the aim succeeded. A textbook can be 初心者しょしんしゃ向むけ and still be far too hard for a beginner - the publisher aimed, and missed. Judging the fit is the job of 向むき.

～向け · Formation

Attaches to a bare noun naming an audience, a market, a region or a customer type.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (audience), as predicate Noun + 向むけだ 海外かいがい → この商品しょうひんは海外かいがい向むけだ
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + 向むけの + Noun 子こども → 子こども向むけの番組ばんぐみ
Noun, modifying a verb Noun + 向むけに + verb 学生がくせい → 学生がくせい向むけに作つくる

Rule of thumb - 向むけに before a verb, 向むけの before a noun, 向むけだ at the end of the sentence. There is no 向むけな.

The company held a briefing session for students.

This magazine was originally produced for specialists, but these days most of its readers are ordinary members of the public.

～向け · Nuance & register

The word itself is register-neutral, but its habitat is not. You will meet it in product descriptions, publishers' catalogues, course listings, TV schedules, press releases and company presentations, far more often than in casual chat. That is not because it sounds formal - it is because talking about target audiences is mostly a business activity.

In conversation it turns up whenever the topic is products or content: これ、大人おとな向むけだよね is perfectly ordinary spoken Japanese. What you would not do is use it about your own private arrangements - see the contrast with のために below.

～向け · Nuance & register (cont.)

A few collocations are worth having ready-made, because they are how the pattern actually appears in the wild: 海外かいがい向むけ, 国内こくない向むけ, 輸出ゆしゅつ向むけ, 法人ほうじん向むけ ("for businesses"), 個人こじん向むけ ("for individuals"), 初心者しょしんしゃ向むけ, 女性じょせい向むけ. The last of these has become slightly loaded in recent years - marketing categories drawn along gender lines attract comment - so it is worth noticing that it describes a marketing decision and not a claim about who may buy the thing.

Note - 向むけ wants a group or market, not a named individual. 部長ぶちょう向むけの資料しりょう is odd unless you mean managers as a category. For one person, use 部長ぶちょうに見みせる資料しりょう or 部長ぶちょうのための資料しりょう.

～向け · ～向け vs ～向き

Rule of thumb - 向むけ = somebody aimed it there. 向むき = it turns out to suit them. Intention versus fit.

この問題集もんだいしゅうは受験生じゅけんせい向むけに作つくられた。
This question book was produced for exam candidates.

この問題集もんだいしゅうは説明せつめいが丁寧ていねいなので独学どくがくの人ひと向むきだ。
The explanations are thorough, so this question book is good for people studying alone.

～向け · ～向け vs ～向き (cont.)

The first reports a decision made by the publisher. The second is the speaker's verdict, and it could easily be about an audience the publisher never considered. This is why a shop sign says 子こども向むけコーナー - the shop has designated it - while a review says 子こども向むきだと思おもう.

Only 向むけ combines comfortably with に plus a verb of making, writing, opening or selling, precisely because those verbs describe the act of aiming. And only 向むき can be preceded by a compass point: 南みなみ向むき is a direction, and ❌南みなみ向むけ is nothing at all.

～向け · ～向け vs ～用

Rule of thumb - 向むけ names the audience something was aimed at; 用よう names the use something is for. Who versus what-for.

自宅じたく**用ようですか、贈おくり物もの用よう**ですか。
Is it for yourself, or is it a gift?

会社かいしゃ向むけのサービスも始はじめました。
We've launched a service for businesses as well.

～向け · ～向け vs ～用 (cont.)

The shop assistant's question is about the purpose the item will serve, so 用よう. The second sentence names the customer segment, so 向むけ. They can even stack in the same phrase without redundancy: 学生がくせい向むけの試験しけん用ようアプリ - an exam-taking app aimed at students.

Note that 用よう attaches to activities and occasions as readily as to people (旅行用りょこうよう, 非常用ひじょうよう), whereas 向むけ is essentially restricted to audiences and markets.

～向け · ～向けに vs ～のために

Rule of thumb - のために is for someone's sake, personal and warm. 向むけに is targeted at a market, impersonal and commercial.

母ははのために大おおきい字じの本ほんを買かった。
I bought a large-print book for my mother.

目めが悪わるい人ひと向むけに大おおきい字じの本ほんが出だされている。
Large-print books are published for people with poor eyesight.

～向け · ～向けに vs ～のために (cont.)

Swapping them is a tonal mistake rather than a grammatical one, and it is a memorable one. ❌母はは向むけに本ほんを買かった makes your mother sound like a demographic segment. Conversely, a publisher's catalogue would not write 目めが悪わるい人ひとのために unless it wanted to sound charitable rather than commercial - which, occasionally, it does.

～向け · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 南みなみ向むけのベランダがある部屋へやを探さがしています。 → ✅ 南みなみ向むきのベランダがある部屋へやを探さがしています。
    Compass points take 向むき. A balcony faces south; nobody aimed it at south as a market.

  • ❌ 初心者しょしんしゃ向むけな教科書きょうかしょを使つかっています。 → ✅ 初心者しょしんしゃ向むけの教科書きょうかしょを使つかっています。
    向むけ is a noun and joins the next noun with の. There is no な-adjective form.

  • ❌ 今夜こんやは家族かぞく向むけにカレーを作つくりました。 → ✅ 今夜こんやは家族かぞくのためにカレーを作つくりました。
    Cooking dinner at home is done for people's sake, not aimed at a market segment. 向むけに here sounds as though you are running a canteen.

～向け · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この靴くつは私わたし向むけです。 → ✅ この靴くつは私わたしに**合あっています**。
    向むけ takes a group or a market, never a single named person. For an individual fit, use 合あう, or 向むいている for an aptitude.

The museum has aimed a service at a defined group of visitors - a planning decision, so 向むけ. You could equally write 外国人がいこくじん観光客かんこうきゃく向むけに英語えいごのツアーを始はじめました, using the に form before the verb.

～向け · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この辞書じしょは小学生しょうがくせい向むけに作つくられているが、説明せつめいが短みじかくて分わかりやすいので、大人おとな向むけにもいい。

✅ …説明せつめいが短みじかくて分わかりやすいので、大人おとな向むきでもある。

The first 向むけ is right - the publisher aimed it at primary school pupils. The second is not, because nobody aimed it at adults; the speaker is judging that it happens to suit them. That is exactly the 向むき job.

～向け · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 子こども**向むけに書かかれた説明せつめい (b) 子こどものために**書かかれた説明せつめい

(a) is a statement about the target readership: the writer pitched the level, vocabulary and length at children. It is neutral and professional, the kind of thing said about published material, and it implies nothing about the writer's feelings.

(b) is about benefit. Somebody wrote it so that children would be helped, and the phrase carries warmth - it would suit a hospital leaflet or a note written by a teacher for their own class. In practice (b) also allows the reader and the beneficiary to differ: an explanation written for the sake of children might in fact be addressed to their parents, which (a) rules out.
