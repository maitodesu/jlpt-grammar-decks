～について

Meaning · about; concerning - names the subject matter that the talking, writing, thinking or investigating is about

について marks the topic of an activity. Something is being said, asked, written, researched or thought - and について says what it is about. It does not say who it is aimed at, and it does not say whose point of view we are taking. Those are jobs for other members of this same に family, and keeping them apart is most of the work at N3.

～について · Formation

について is a compound: the particle に plus the て-form of つく ("attach to"). It behaves as a unit and attaches to a bare noun - never to a verb or an adjective.

～について · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun, before a verb Noun + について + verb 事故(じこ) → 事故(じこ)について話(はな)す
Noun, modifying another noun Noun + についての + Noun 環境(かんきょう) → 環境(かんきょう)についての記事(きじ)
Noun, as topic Noun + については 費用(ひよう) → 費用(ひよう)については後(あと)で
Noun, as addition Noun + についても 安全(あんぜん) → 安全(あんぜん)についても考(かんが)える
Noun, formal / business Noun + につきまして（は） 日程(にってい) → 日程(にってい)につきましては
Question word 何(なに) / どこ など + について 何(なに) → 何(なに)について書(か)きますか

～について · Formation (cont.)

Note - the の in についての is not optional. について is adverbial: it can modify a verb on its own, but to hang it on a noun you have to convert it with の, exactly as you would with 東京(とうきょう)での会議(かいぎ).

～について · Examples

地震(じしん)について調(しら)べています。
I'm looking into earthquakes.

その件(けん)について質問(しつもん)があります。
I have a question about that matter.

先生(せんせい)に進路(しんろ)について相談(そうだん)しました。
I talked to my teacher about which path to take after school.

日本(にほん)の祭(まつ)りについての番組(ばんぐみ)を録画(ろくが)しておいた。
I recorded a programme about Japanese festivals.

旅行(りょこう)の費用(ひよう)については、あとでまとめてメールで知(し)らせます。
As for the cost of the trip, I'll send you the whole thing by email later.

～について · Examples (cont.)

新(あたら)しい規則(きそく)について、社員(しゃいん)からいくつも意見(いけん)が出(で)ている。
Staff have raised a number of points about the new rules.

その画家(がか)について何(なに)も知(し)らないまま展覧会(てんらんかい)に行(い)っても、たぶん楽(たの)しめないだろう。
If I go to the exhibition knowing nothing about the painter, I probably won't enjoy it.

明日(あす)の説明会(せつめいかい)では、来年度(らいねんど)の予算(よさん)につきましてご説明(せつめい)いたします。
At tomorrow's briefing I will go over next year's budget.

～について · Nuance & register

について sits in the middle of the register scale, and that is why it is the one to learn first. It is at home in a polite conversation, an email, a report, a university seminar and a news broadcast alike. Nothing about it is stiff; nothing about it is casual either.

Casual speech mostly does without it. Friends say 昨日(きのう)のこと, あの人(ひと)のこと, or just ～って:

  • あの映画(えいが)のこと、どう思(おも)った? - "What did you think about that film?"
  • 試験(しけん)って、いつだっけ。 - "The exam - when was it again?"

Using について there is not wrong, but it lifts the sentence a register: it sounds like you are opening an agenda item rather than chatting. Conversely, an email to a client that says 納期(のうき)のことですが where it could have said 納期(のうき)につきましては reads as slightly under-dressed.

～について · Nuance & register (cont.)

The につきまして form is worth recognising even if you never produce it. It is the same word with a humble-polite ます stem inserted, and it belongs to shop announcements, customer service and business correspondence: 本日(ほんじつ)の営業(えいぎょう)時間(じかん)につきましては…. In ordinary polite speech it would sound over-formal, almost parodic.

One structural habit to build: について likes to be followed by a verb of speaking, writing or finding out - 話(はな)す, 書(か)く, 説明(せつめい)する, 調(しら)べる, 考(かんが)える, 議論(ぎろん)する, 質問(しつもん)する, 発表(はっぴょう)する. If the predicate is an emotion or an evaluation instead, について is usually the wrong tool.

～について · ～について vs ～に関(かん)して

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register and different width. について points at a specific topic and is fine in speech; に関(かん)して is more formal, mostly written, and takes in the surrounding area of a topic as well.

You can swap them in most sentences without producing anything ungrammatical. What changes is how the sentence sounds, and how tightly the topic is drawn.

この漢字(かんじ)の読(よ)み方(かた)について先生(せんせい)に聞(き)いた。
I asked the teacher about how this kanji is read.

外国人(がいこくじん)の受(う)け入(い)れに関(かん)して、市(し)は新(あたら)しい方針(ほうしん)を示(しめ)した。
The city has set out a new policy on accepting foreign residents.

～について · ～について vs ～に関(かん)して (cont.)

The first is one narrow question, asked out loud - について fits. The second covers a whole policy area, in writing, in a public register - に関(かん)して fits. Reverse them and neither is wrong, but the first would sound like a committee paper and the second like small talk.

～について · ～について vs ～に対(たい)して

Rule of thumb - について is the topic; に対(たい)して is the target. "Talk about the customer" is について; "be polite to the customer" is に対(たい)して.

This is the pair that costs learners the most marks, because English "about" and "toward" both come out as "regarding" in a dictionary.

部長(ぶちょう)は新人(しんじん)について何(なに)か言(い)っていましたか。
Did the manager say anything about the new hire?

部長(ぶちょう)は新人(しんじん)に対(たい)して厳(きび)しすぎると思(おも)う。
I think the manager is too hard on the new hire.

～について · ～について vs ～に対(たい)して (cont.)

In the first, the new hire is the subject matter of some remarks. In the second, the new hire is the person the manager's behaviour is aimed at. A useful test: if you can replace the phrase with "on the subject of", it is について. If you can replace it with "at / to / against", it is に対(たい)して.

～について · ～について vs ～のこと

Rule of thumb - のこと is the everyday equivalent and, unlike について, it can be the object of a feeling. Verbs and adjectives of emotion take のこと (or a plain particle), never について.

弟(おとうと)のことを心配(しんぱい)しています。
I'm worried about my little brother.

弟(おとうと)について心配(しんぱい)な点(てん)を先生(せんせい)に伝(つた)えました。
I told the teacher the points about my brother that concern me.

について needs the sentence to be presenting information on a topic. In the first sentence there is no presenting going on - there is only the feeling, and the brother is its object. The second sentence adds an act of communication, and について becomes available again.

～について · ～について vs ～のこと (cont.)

The same trap catches 好(す)き, 嫌(きら)い, 怒(おこ)る and 覚(おぼ)えている. ❌この歌手(かしゅ)について好(す)きです is simply not Japanese; you want この歌手(かしゅ)が好(す)きです.

～について · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 事故(じこ)について記事(きじ)を読(よ)んだ。（intending "an article about the accident"） → ✅ 事故(じこ)についての記事(きじ)を読(よ)んだ。
    Without の, について modifies the verb 読(よ)んだ, which gives "I read an article, about the accident" - a different and odd sentence. To attach it to a noun you need についての.

  • ❌ アニメについて好(す)きです。 → ✅ アニメが好(す)きです。
    について introduces the subject matter of an act of communication or thought. A feeling takes its object with が or を, not with について.

  • ❌ 私(わたし)についてこの仕事(しごと)は大切(たいせつ)です。 → ✅ 私(わたし)にとってこの仕事(しごと)は大切(たいせつ)です。
    "For me, this job is important" is a standpoint, not a topic. Standpoints take にとって.

～について · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 客(きゃく)について失礼(しつれい)な態度(たいど)を取(と)った。 → ✅ 客(きゃく)に対(たい)して失礼(しつれい)な態度(たいど)を取(と)った。
    An attitude is directed at somebody. That is に対(たい)して; について would mean the rudeness was somehow on the topic of the customer.

～について · Practice 1

1. 来週(らいしゅう)の会議(かいぎ)では、工場(こうじょう)の移転(いてん)____話(はな)し合(あ)います。

工場(こうじょう)の移転(いてん)について話(はな)し合(あ)います。

The factory move is the subject matter of the discussion, and 話(はな)し合(あ)う is exactly the kind of communication verb について was built for. に関(かん)して would also be grammatical, just a notch more formal than a normal meeting warrants.

～について · Practice 2

2. Choose: 店長(てんちょう)はアルバイトの学生(がくせい)（について・に対(たい)して）とても親切(しんせつ)だ。

店長(てんちょう)はアルバイトの学生(がくせい)に対(たい)してとても親切(しんせつ)だ。

親切(しんせつ)だ describes how the manager behaves toward somebody, so the students are the target of an attitude, not a topic of conversation. Say について and the sentence would try to mean that he is kind on the subject of part-timers, which is nonsense.

～について · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I'd like to hear your opinion about the new plan."

新(あたら)しい計画(けいかく)についてご意見(いけん)を伺(うかが)いたいのですが。

ご意見(いけん) is information being given on a topic, so について is the natural choice. In a written request you could raise the register with 新(あたら)しい計画(けいかく)につきましては, and in casual speech you would drop the whole thing: 新(あたら)しい計画(けいかく)、どう思(おも)う?

～について · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この問題(もんだい)についてのレポートを来週(らいしゅう)までに出(だ)してください。それから、締(し)め切(き)りについて質問(しつもん)は受(う)け付(つ)けません。

✅ …締(し)め切(き)りについての質問(しつもん)は受(う)け付(つ)けません。

The first について is correct because it already carries の before the noun レポート. The second one sits directly in front of the noun 質問(しつもん) and needs the same の. This is the single most common slip with this pattern.

～について · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 田中(たなか)さんについて話(はな)しました。 (b) 田中(たなか)さんと話(はな)しました。

(a) makes 田中(たなか)さん the subject matter: somebody discussed him, and he may well not have been in the room. Depending on the context it can sound slightly ominous, in the same way English "we talked about you" does.

(b) makes him the conversation partner - you and he talked, about anything at all. と marks the person you do something jointly with; について marks what the doing is on the topic of. The two can even be stacked: 山田(やまだ)さんと田中(たなか)さんについて話(はな)しました - "I talked with Yamada about Tanaka."

～に関する / ～に関して

Meaning · concerning; relating to; in connection with - the formal, largely written cousin of について（かんする・かんして）

に関(かん)する and に関(かん)して mark the subject matter of a statement, a document, a rule or a piece of research. They mean what について means, but they belong to a different register - reports, notices, contracts, academic writing, the news - and they draw the topic wider: not just the thing named, but everything connected to it.

The two forms are one word doing two jobs. 関(かん)する is the dictionary form of a verb, so it modifies nouns. 関(かん)して is its て-form, so it modifies verbs.

～に関する / ～に関して · Formation

に + 関(かん)する ("to be connected to"). The noun in front takes に, never を.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + に関(かん)する + Noun 安全(あんぜん) → 安全(あんぜん)に関(かん)する規則(きそく)
Noun, modifying a verb Noun + に関(かん)して + verb 費用(ひよう) → 費用(ひよう)に関(かん)して話(はな)す
Noun, as topic Noun + に関(かん)しては その点(てん) → その点(てん)に関(かん)しては
Noun, as addition Noun + に関(かん)しても 人数(にんずう) → 人数(にんずう)に関(かん)しても
Noun, business / keigo Noun + に関(かん)しまして（は） 納期(のうき) → 納期(のうき)に関(かん)しましては

～に関する / ～に関して · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun, modifying a noun (rarer) Noun + に関(かん)しての + Noun 事故(じこ) → 事故(じこ)に関(かん)しての報告(ほうこく)

Rule of thumb - 関(かん)する before a noun, 関(かん)して before a verb. 関(かん)するの does not exist: the の you are reaching for is already built into the verb form.

～に関する / ～に関して · Examples

これは安全(あんぜん)に関(かん)する規則(きそく)です。
These are the safety rules.

その点(てん)に関(かん)しては、後(のち)ほどお答(こた)えします。
I'll answer that point later.

健康(けんこう)に関(かん)する本(ほん)を何冊(なんさつ)も買(か)ってしまった。
I've ended up buying any number of books on health.

料金(りょうきん)に関(かん)してご質問(しつもん)のある方(かた)は、受付(うけつけ)までお越(こ)しください。
If you have questions concerning the fees, please come to the reception desk.

～に関する / ～に関して · Examples (cont.)

気候(きこう)の変化(へんか)に関(かん)する研究(けんきゅう)が世界中(せかいじゅう)で進(すす)められている。
Research relating to climate change is being carried forward all over the world.

この事件(じけん)に関(かん)して、警察(けいさつ)はまだ何(なに)も発表(はっぴょう)していない。
The police have released nothing in connection with this incident.

給与(きゅうよ)に関(かん)しては不満(ふまん)はないが、残業(ざんぎょう)の多(おお)さに関(かん)しては会社(かいしゃ)に考(かんが)え直(なお)してほしい。
I have no complaints where pay is concerned, but on the amount of overtime I'd like the company to think again.

～に関する / ～に関して · Examples (cont.)

個人(こじん)情報(じょうほう)の扱(あつか)いに関(かん)しましては、当社(とうしゃ)のホームページをご覧(らん)ください。
Regarding the handling of personal data, please see our website.

～に関する / ～に関して · Nuance & register

This is a written pattern first and a formal-spoken pattern second. Its natural homes are notices, headings, minutes, contracts, textbooks, news copy and the polished parts of a presentation. If you say it to a friend over coffee you will sound like you are reading from a slide.

A useful gauge: the more institutional the speaker, the more likely に関(かん)して is. A company says 停電(ていでん)に関(かん)してお知(し)らせします; a housemate says 停電(ていでん)のことなんだけど. Both are correct in their own place, and swapping them is the mistake.

に関(かん)しまして raises it one more step, into customer-facing business Japanese. It is extremely common in announcements and service email, and you should expect to hear it far more often than you produce it.

～に関する / ～に関して · Nuance & register (cont.)

There is also a width difference that survives the register difference. について picks out a topic; に関(かん)して draws a circle around it. 台風(たいふう)に関(かん)する情報(じょうほう) covers the typhoon, the train stoppages, the school closures and the shop opening hours - everything connected to it. 台風(たいふう)についての情報(じょうほう) is more likely to be information about the typhoon itself. This is a tendency rather than a hard line, but it is why headings and document titles overwhelmingly prefer に関(かん)する.

Finally, do not confuse the phrase with the noun 関係(かんけい). 関係(かんけい)がある claims a real-world connection between two things; に関(かん)する merely announces a topic. この問題(もんだい)に関(かん)する資料(しりょう) is "material on this problem"; この問題(もんだい)と関係(かんけい)がある資料(しりょう) is "material that turns out to be connected to this problem."

～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関(かん)して vs ～について

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different clothes. について is the everyday form and works in speech; に関(かん)して is formal, mostly written, and covers a broader area around the topic.

Nearly every sentence accepts both. Choose by asking who is speaking and where.

夏休(なつやす)みの予定(よてい)について家族(かぞく)で話(はな)した。
The family talked about plans for the summer holiday.

市(し)の予算(よさん)に関(かん)して、議会(ぎかい)で激(はげ)しい議論(ぎろん)があった。
There was a fierce debate in the assembly concerning the city budget.

Put に関(かん)して in the first sentence and the family sounds like a board of directors. Put について in the second and nothing breaks - it is simply one notch less formal than assembly minutes would be.

～に関(かん)する + Noun vs ～についての + Noun

Rule of thumb - both attach a topic to a noun, but the shapes differ: に関(かん)する takes no の, について takes an obligatory one. Titles, headings and formal documents prefer に関(かん)する.

留学生(りゅうがくせい)の住(す)まいに関(かん)するアンケート
A survey concerning housing for international students

留学生(りゅうがくせい)の住(す)まいについてのアンケート
A survey about housing for international students

The second is perfectly good Japanese and is what you would say out loud. The first is what would be printed at the top of the form. Note the ❌に関(かん)するの error is impossible to make with について, and the ❌について質問(しつもん) error (dropping の) is impossible to make with に関(かん)する - the two patterns fail in opposite directions.

～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関(かん)して vs ～において

Rule of thumb - に関(かん)して gives the topic; において gives the setting - the place, the era, the field or the situation in which something holds.

Both are formal, both translate as "in" or "regarding" at some point in a dictionary, and both appear in the same kind of document, so they blur together.

安全(あんぜん)対策(たいさく)に関(かん)して、新(あたら)しい案(あん)が出(だ)された。
A new proposal has been put forward concerning safety measures.

建設(けんせつ)現場(げんば)において、安全(あんぜん)対策(たいさく)は何(なに)よりも優先(ゆうせん)される。
On construction sites, safety measures take priority over everything.

～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関(かん)して vs ～において (cont.)

In the first, safety measures are what the proposal is about. In the second, the building site is where the rule applies. If the noun could be preceded by "on the subject of", it is に関(かん)して; if it could be preceded by "in" or "at", it is において.

～に関する / ～に関して · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 環境(かんきょう)に関(かん)するの問題(もんだい) → ✅ 環境(かんきょう)に関(かん)する問題(もんだい)
    関(かん)する is already a verb in its noun-modifying form, so it attaches to the noun directly. The の belongs to についての, not here.

  • ❌ 事故(じこ)に関(かん)して報告書(ほうこくしょ)を見(み)せてください。（intending "the report on the accident"） → ✅ 事故(じこ)に関(かん)する報告書(ほうこくしょ)を見(み)せてください。
    Before a noun you need 関(かん)する. 関(かん)して would attach the topic to 見(み)せる instead - "show me a report, on the subject of the accident."

  • ❌ ゆうべの飲(の)み会(かい)に関(かん)して話(はな)そうよ。 → ✅ ゆうべの飲(の)み会(かい)のこと話(はな)そうよ。
    Not a grammar error - a register error. Casual invitations do not take formal written vocabulary; the sentence sounds like a parody of a manager.

～に関する / ～に関して · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 私(わたし)に関(かん)して、この決定(けってい)は残念(ざんねん)です。 → ✅ 私(わたし)にとって、この決定(けってい)は残念(ざんねん)です。
    "For me" as a standpoint is にとって. に関(かん)して would make you the subject matter of the decision.

～に関する / ～に関して · Practice 1

1. 会社(かいしゃ)は社員(しゃいん)の健康(けんこう)に____調査(ちょうさ)を行(おこな)った。

健康(けんこう)に関(かん)する調査(ちょうさ)を行(おこな)った。

The topic is being hung on the noun 調査(ちょうさ), so you need the noun-modifying form 関(かん)する - with no の after it. 健康(けんこう)についての調査(ちょうさ) would also work and would sound slightly less official.

～に関する / ～に関して · Practice 2

2. Choose: 会議(かいぎ)の場所(ばしょ)（に関(かん)して・において）は、また連絡(れんらく)します。

会議(かいぎ)の場所(ばしょ)に関(かん)しては、また連絡(れんらく)します。

The venue is the subject matter of the message you will send, not the place where the sending happens. において would have to mean "I will contact you at the meeting venue", which is not what the sentence is doing.

～に関する / ～に関して · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese, in the register of a company notice: "We are currently investigating the cause of the problem. We will inform you separately regarding compensation."

現在(げんざい)、問題(もんだい)の原因(げんいん)を調査(ちょうさ)しております。補償(ほしょう)に関(かん)しましては、別(べつ)にご連絡(れんらく)いたします。

A public notice is exactly where に関(かん)しまして belongs, and the は after it marks compensation as a separate item being set aside for later. 補償(ほしょう)については would be acceptable but slightly plainer than a formal apology notice usually goes.

～に関する / ～に関して · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 新(あたら)しい制度(せいど)に関(かん)してのついて説明(せつめい)は、来週(らいしゅう)行(おこな)います。

✅ 新(あたら)しい制度(せいど)に関(かん)する説明(せつめい)は、来週(らいしゅう)行(おこな)います。

Two patterns have been welded together. Pick one: 制度(せいど)に関(かん)する説明(せつめい) or 制度(せいど)についての説明(せつめい). Both mean "the explanation of the new system"; the first is the one you would print on an agenda.

～に関する / ～に関して · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 地震(じしん)に関(かん)する情報(じょうほう) (b) 地震(じしん)についての情報(じょうほう)

(a) is the phrase a broadcaster or a city website would use, and it takes in everything connected to the earthquake - the tremor itself, the damage, the trains, the evacuation centres. The topic is drawn as a field.

(b) is what you would say in conversation, and it points more narrowly at information about the earthquake itself: how big it was, where it struck. The difference is a tendency rather than a rule, but the register difference is real and reliable: (a) is written and institutional, (b) is spoken and ordinary.

～において / ～における

Meaning · in; at; on - the formal written stand-in for で, naming the place, time, situation or field in which something holds（において・における）

において sets the stage. It tells you where something happens, when it happens, or within what field or respect a statement is true. Everything it does, the particle で also does - but で is the everyday word and において is the one that appears in announcements, papers, contracts and ceremonies.

における is the same word in noun-modifying dress: 会場(かいじょう)において行(おこな)う ("carry out at the venue") but 会場(かいじょう)における注意(ちゅうい)事項(じこう) ("points to note at the venue").

～において / ～における · Formation

Attaches to a bare noun. The に is part of the pattern and is not replaceable.

～において / ～における · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (place), modifying a verb Noun + において 本社(ほんしゃ) → 本社(ほんしゃ)において行(おこな)う
Noun (time or era) Noun + において 明治(めいじ)時代(じだい) → 明治(めいじ)時代(じだい)において
Noun (field or respect) Noun + において 技術(ぎじゅつ)面(めん) → 技術(ぎじゅつ)面(めん)において
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + における + Noun 職場(しょくば) → 職場(しょくば)における関係(かんけい)
Noun, as topic Noun + においては 日本(にほん) → 日本(にほん)においては

Attaches to Form Example
Noun, as addition Noun + においても 海外(かいがい) → 海外(かいがい)においても

Note - においての exists but is uncommon and slightly clumsy. Before a noun, reach for における. Before a verb or an adjective, において.

～において / ～における · Examples

会議(かいぎ)は本社(ほんしゃ)において行(おこな)われます。
The meeting will be held at head office.

この点(てん)において、両者(りょうしゃ)の意見(いけん)は一致(いっち)している。
On this point, the two sides are in agreement.

大会(たいかい)は来月(らいげつ)七日(なのか)、市民(しみん)ホールにおいて開催(かいさい)いたします。
The tournament will be held on the seventh of next month at the civic hall.

江戸(えど)時代(じだい)における町人(ちょうにん)の暮(く)らしを調(しら)べています。
I'm researching the lives of townspeople in the Edo period.

医療(いりょう)の分野(ぶんや)におけるAIの利用(りよう)が急速(きゅうそく)に広(ひろ)がっている。
The use of AI in the field of medicine is spreading rapidly.

～において / ～における · Examples (cont.)

面接(めんせつ)においては、話(はな)す内容(ないよう)だけでなく話(はな)し方(かた)も見(み)られている。
In an interview it is not only what you say but how you say it that is being judged.

我(わ)が国(くに)においても、人口(じんこう)の減少(げんしょう)は深刻(しんこく)な問題(もんだい)となりつつある。
In our country too, the falling population is becoming a serious problem.

価格(かかく)においては他社(たしゃ)に負(ま)けているが、品質(ひんしつ)においては自信(じしん)がある。
We lose to our competitors on price, but where quality is concerned we are confident.

～において / ～における · Nuance & register

This pattern is formal and written, with essentially no casual life at all. Notices, minutes, academic papers, legal text, ceremonial speech and the polished register of the news are its habitat. Saying ❌コンビニにおいてパンを買(か)った is not a grammar error - it is a tonal disaster, roughly like writing "I did procure bread at the convenience store."

Two practical consequences.

First, において covers only some of what で covers. で marks place, but also means ("with a knife"), cause ("because of a cold"), material, and limit ("within a week"). において inherits only the place, time, situation and field senses. There is no ❌ナイフにおいて切(き)る.

Second, the "field or respect" sense is where において earns its keep, because で there can be ambiguous. この点(てん)において - "in this respect" - is crisper in formal prose than この点(てん)で, which could momentarily be read as a location.

～において / ～における · Nuance & register (cont.)

において also carries a faint ceremonial weight when used of an event. A wedding invitation says ホテルの大(だい)ホールにおいて; a text to a friend says ホテルで. The formality is doing real work in the first case - it signals that this is an occasion.

One last thing worth noticing: における is a fixture of titles. 現代(げんだい)社会(しゃかい)における家族(かぞく)の役割(やくわり) is exactly the shape of an essay title, a chapter heading, a conference paper. If you are writing academic Japanese, this is a phrase you will use constantly.

～において / ～における · ～において vs ～で

Rule of thumb - same meaning, different register, narrower coverage. において can replace the place / time / situation / field uses of で and nothing else, and only in formal writing or formal speech.

試合(しあい)は大阪(おおさか)で行(おこな)われた。
The match was held in Osaka.

試合(しあい)は大阪(おおさか)において行(おこな)われた。
The match was held in Osaka. (announcement register)

Identical in meaning; the second belongs in a written report. Now watch it fail:

電車(でんしゃ)が事故(じこ)で止(と)まっている。
The trains are stopped because of an accident.

～において / ～における · ～において vs ～で (cont.)

❌事故(じこ)において止(と)まっている is ungrammatical, because this で marks a cause, and において has no cause sense. The same goes for バスで行(い)く (means) and 木(き)で作(つく)る (material) - neither converts.

～において / ～における · ～における + Noun vs ～の + Noun

Rule of thumb - の says the two nouns are connected somehow; における says specifically "within / inside / in the context of". When the relationship needs pinning down, における does it.

日本(にほん)の教育(きょういく)
Japanese education

日本(にほん)における外国語(がいこくご)教育(きょういく)
foreign-language education in Japan

The first could mean education that is Japanese in style, education owned by Japan, education about Japan - の does not say. The second is unambiguous: it is education happening inside Japan, seen as a setting. This precision is why academic titles reach for における rather than の, and why replacing it with の in an essay title makes the title vaguer even though it stays grammatical.

～において / ～における · ～における + Noun vs ～の + Noun (cont.)

Between the two sits での, which is the neutral-register version of the same idea: 日本(にほん)での生活(せいかつ), "life in Japan". Use での when you are speaking, における when you are writing formally.

～において / ～における · ～において vs ～について

Rule of thumb - において is the setting; について is the subject matter. "Discussed at the meeting" is において; "discussed about the meeting" is について.

Both are formal-ish, both hang off に, and English "regarding" is offered as a translation of both, which is how learners end up swapping them.

その問題(もんだい)は先日(せんじつ)の会議(かいぎ)において議論(ぎろん)された。
That issue was debated at the meeting the other day.

先日(せんじつ)の会議(かいぎ)について報告書(ほうこくしょ)を書(か)いた。
I wrote a report about the meeting the other day.

～において / ～における · ～において vs ～について (cont.)

In the first, the meeting is the setting the debating happened in. In the second, the meeting is what the report is on. Substituting one for the other produces a sentence that is grammatical and simply says something else - which is the most dangerous kind of mistake.

～において / ～における · Common mistakes

  • ❌ はさみにおいて紙(かみ)を切(き)った。 → ✅ はさみで紙(かみ)を切(き)った。
    において inherits only the place, time, situation and field senses of で. It never marks an instrument.

  • ❌ 台風(たいふう)において学校(がっこう)が休(やす)みになった。 → ✅ 台風(たいふう)で学校(がっこう)が休(やす)みになった。
    This で is a cause, and there is no cause sense in において. If you want the formal register, use 台風(たいふう)のため.

  • ❌ 職場(しょくば)において問題(もんだい)が増(ふ)えている。（intending "problems in the workplace") → ✅ 職場(しょくば)における問題(もんだい)が増(ふ)えている。
    Before a noun you need における. As written, the sentence says the increasing is happening at the workplace, which is not quite the same claim.

～において / ～における · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 昨日(きのう)、駅前(えきまえ)のカフェにおいて友(とも)だちに会(あ)った。 → ✅ 駅前(えきまえ)のカフェで友(とも)だちに会(あ)った。
    Grammatically fine, tonally absurd. A private, everyday event does not take announcement vocabulary.

～において / ～における · Practice 1

～において / ～における · Practice 5

Both mean "a survey at the venue", and neither is wrong. The difference is register and, faintly, framing.

(a) is ordinary Japanese - spoken, or written in an email to colleagues. での is just で fitted to modify a noun.

(b) is document Japanese. It is what would be printed on the cover of the report, and it frames the venue as a defined setting whose conditions matter, rather than merely as the spot where the clipboards were handed out. Choosing (b) in conversation would sound as though you were quoting the report out loud.

～に対して

Meaning · toward; at; in response to - and, from a clause, "whereas"（にたいして）

に対(たい)して points. It names the person or thing an action, an attitude or a reaction is aimed at: who you are polite to, what you are objecting to, what a reply is a reply to. Say "about" in your head and you will reach for it wrongly - this is not the topic of the sentence, it is the target.

A second use falls out of the same image. If you set one thing opposite another, you get contrast: Aのに対(たい)して、B - "A, whereas B". And a third, narrower use gives ratios: "per one participant, two bottles."

～に対して · Formation

対(たい)する is a verb ("face, confront"), so it inflects: the て-form modifies verbs and adjectives, the dictionary form modifies nouns.

～に対して · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (target) Noun + に対(たい)して 子(こ)ども → 子(こ)どもに対(たい)してやさしい
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + に対(たい)する + Noun 客(きゃく) → 客(きゃく)に対(たい)する態度(たいど)
Verb / い-adjective (contrast) Plain form + のに対(たい)して 高(たか)い → 高(たか)いのに対(たい)して
な-adjective (contrast) ～な + のに対(たい)して 静(しず)かだ → 静(しず)かなのに対(たい)して
Noun (contrast) Noun + なのに / であるのに + 対(たい)して 学生(がくせい) → 学生(がくせい)なのに対(たい)して

～に対して · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Quantity (ratio) Number + に対(たい)して + Number 一人(ひとり) → 一人(ひとり)に対(たい)して二枚(にまい)
Written / curt variant Noun + に対(たい)し 会社(かいしゃ) → 会社(かいしゃ)に対(たい)し求(もと)める

Note - the contrastive use needs の before it. A clause cannot attach directly: ❌高(たか)いに対(たい)して. The の turns the clause into something noun-like, which is what に対(たい)して requires.

～に対して · Examples

子(こ)どもに対(たい)してやさしい人(ひと)です。
He's a person who is kind to children.

その質問(しつもん)に対(たい)して、彼(かれ)は何(なに)も答(こた)えなかった。
He gave no answer to that question.

目上(めうえ)の人(ひと)に対(たい)してそんな言(い)い方(かた)をしてはいけない。
You mustn't speak that way to someone senior to you.

市(し)の決定(けってい)に対(たい)して、住民(じゅうみん)が強(つよ)く反発(はんぱつ)している。
Residents are pushing back hard against the city's decision.

参加者(さんかしゃ)一人(ひとり)に対(たい)して、飲(の)み物(もの)を二本(にほん)ずつ用意(ようい)した。
We laid on two drinks per participant.

～に対して · Examples (cont.)

姉(あね)が外(そと)で体(からだ)を動(うご)かすのが好(す)きなのに対(たい)して、妹(いもうと)は一日中(いちにちじゅう)本(ほん)を読(よ)んでいる。
Where my older sister loves being outside and moving about, my younger sister reads all day.

お客様(きゃくさま)に対(たい)する言葉遣(ことばづか)いについては、入社(にゅうしゃ)したばかりのころに厳(きび)しく注意(ちゅうい)された。
I got told off sharply about how I spoke to customers when I had only just joined.

去年(きょねん)の売上(うりあげ)がまったく伸(の)びなかったのに対(たい)して、今年(ことし)は春(はる)からずっと好調(こうちょう)だ。
Whereas last year's sales didn't grow at all, this year has been strong right through from spring.

～に対して · Nuance & register

The target use leans formal, but not so far that it is out of place in ordinary polite conversation. 部下(ぶか)に対(たい)して厳(きび)しい is something a colleague might say out loud. What marks it as slightly weighty is that plain に would usually do the job: 子(こ)どもにやさしい and 子(こ)どもに対(たい)してやさしい mean the same thing, and the second is more deliberate - it puts the relationship itself under a spotlight. In relaxed speech you would normally drop it.

Where に対(たい)して is not optional is when the noun is the target of a reaction: a protest, a reply, a complaint, a demand, an evaluation. 説明(せつめい)に対(たい)して質問(しつもん)が出(で)た - the questions came back at the explanation. Bare に there is thinner and sometimes ambiguous.

～に対して · Nuance & register (cont.)

The contrastive use is squarely written. It belongs in comparisons, reports and essays, and it has a cool, analytical tone: the writer is laying two facts side by side, not complaining. That distinguishes it from のに, which contrasts and carries dissatisfaction.

に対(たい)し, without て, is the clipped written form. It is standard in newspapers and official documents - 組合(くみあい)は会社(かいしゃ)に対(たい)し回答(かいとう)を求(もと)めた - and sounds stiff if spoken aloud outside a formal announcement.

Note - the ratio use is common in explanations of quantity and in statistics: 人口(じんこう)千人(せんにん)に対(たい)して医師(いし)が三人(さんにん). It is the one sense where the noun in front is a number, so it is easy to spot and easy to keep separate from the other two.

～に対して · ～に対(たい)して vs ～について

Rule of thumb - に対(たい)して is the target of an action or attitude; について is the subject matter of a statement. Aim vs topic.

新(あたら)しい制度(せいど)について社員(しゃいん)に説明(せつめい)した。
I explained the new system to the staff.

新(あたら)しい制度(せいど)に対(たい)して社員(しゃいん)から不満(ふまん)が出(で)た。
Complaints came from the staff against the new system.

In the first the system is what the explanation is about. In the second it is what the complaints are aimed at. Note that the two sentences have different verbs, and that is the reliable clue: verbs of saying, writing and investigating take について, while verbs of reacting - 反対(はんたい)する, 抗議(こうぎ)する, 答(こた)える, 文句(もんく)を言(い)う - take に対(たい)して.

～に対して · ～に対(たい)して vs ～にとって

Rule of thumb - に対(たい)して looks outward from the subject toward the noun; にとって looks outward from the noun at the world. Target vs standpoint.

親(おや)に対(たい)してあんな口(くち)をきくべきではない。
You shouldn't talk to your parents like that.

親(おや)にとって、子(こ)どもの健康(けんこう)が一番(いちばん)の心配(しんぱい)だ。
For parents, their children's health is the biggest worry.

In the first, the parents are on the receiving end of the speaking. In the second, nothing is aimed at them at all - we have simply climbed inside their heads to judge what matters. A quick test: にとって is nearly always followed by an evaluation (大切(たいせつ)だ, 難(むずか)しい, ありがたい), whereas に対(たい)して is followed by an action or a stance.

～に対して · ～のに対(たい)して vs ～のに

Rule of thumb - both put two clauses against each other, but のに対(たい)して is a neutral comparison and のに is a complaint. If you are annoyed, you want のに.

東京(とうきょう)の冬(ふゆ)は晴(は)れの日(ひ)が多(おお)いのに対(たい)して、日本海側(にほんかいがわ)は雪(ゆき)の日(ひ)が続(つづ)く。
Winter in Tokyo has many clear days, whereas on the Japan Sea side the snow goes on and on.

約束(やくそく)したのに、彼(かれ)は来(こ)なかった。
He promised, and yet he didn't come.

The first sentence has no feeling in it: two regions, two climates, stated side by side. The second is a grievance. There is also a structural difference - のに対(たい)して compares two things of the same kind along the same axis (winters with winters, sales with sales), while のに simply says the second clause is unexpected given the first.

～に対して · ～のに対(たい)して vs ～のに (cont.)

For the same neutral comparison you will also meet 一方(いっぽう)（で）, which does much the same work and joins sentences rather than clauses.

～に対して · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この問題(もんだい)に対(たい)して話(はな)し合(あ)いましょう。 → ✅ この問題(もんだい)について話(はな)し合(あ)いましょう。
    話(はな)し合(あ)う takes a topic, not a target. Use に対(たい)して only when something is being aimed at the noun.

  • ❌ 私(わたし)に対(たい)して、この写真(しゃしん)は宝物(たからもの)です。 → ✅ 私(わたし)にとって、この写真(しゃしん)は宝物(たからもの)です。
    "For me" meaning "from my point of view" is にとって. 私(わたし)に対(たい)して would mean the photograph is doing something to you.

  • ❌ 兄(あに)は背(せ)が高(たか)いに対(たい)して、弟(おとうと)は小(ちい)さい。 → ✅ 兄(あに)は背(せ)が高(たか)いのに対(たい)して、弟(おとうと)は小(ちい)さい。
    The contrastive use attaches to a nominalised clause. The の is compulsory, and with a な-adjective it becomes な+のに対(たい)して.

～に対して · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 上司(じょうし)に対(たい)して態度(たいど)が悪(わる)い。（intending "his attitude toward his boss is bad"） → ✅ 上司(じょうし)に対(たい)する態度(たいど)が悪(わる)い。
    Before a noun the verb takes its dictionary form. As written, the sentence attaches the target to 悪(わる)い and drifts toward "he is badly behaved in the direction of his boss."

3. Translate into Japanese: "Whereas the first floor is a shop, the second floor is used as an office."

一階(いっかい)が店(みせ)であるのに対(たい)して、二階(にかい)は事務所(じむしょ)として使(つか)われている。

A neutral side-by-side comparison of two floors, so のに対(たい)して rather than のに. After a noun the clause needs である (or な) before のに - ❌店(みせ)のに対(たい)して is not possible.

～に対して · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 会社(かいしゃ)は事故(じこ)に対(たい)して説明(せつめい)を行(おこな)ったが、被害者(ひがいしゃ)について十分(じゅうぶん)な補償(ほしょう)をしていない。

✅ 会社(かいしゃ)は事故(じこ)について説明(せつめい)を行(おこな)ったが、被害者(ひがいしゃ)に対(たい)して十分(じゅうぶん)な補償(ほしょう)をしていない。

The two are the wrong way round. An explanation is about the accident - topic, so について. Compensation is paid to the victims - target, so に対(たい)して.

～に対して · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼(かれ)は後輩(こうはい)に厳(きび)しい。 (b) 彼(かれ)は後輩(こうはい)に対(たい)して厳(きび)しい。

Both say he is hard on his juniors, and both are correct. (a) is the ordinary spoken sentence: plain に is enough, and nobody would think twice about it.

(b) makes the relationship itself the point. The longer phrase draws attention to the direction of the strictness - juniors specifically, as opposed to anyone else - and it raises the register, so it fits a written assessment, a complaint made formally, or a sentence that goes on to contrast his behaviour toward someone else: 後輩(こうはい)に対(たい)しては厳(きび)しいが、上司(じょうし)には何(なに)も言(い)わない.

～にとって

Meaning · for; from the standpoint of - the sentence is judged from inside the position of the person or group named

にとって hands the microphone to somebody else. It says: seen from where they stand, here is how things look. What follows is almost always an evaluation - important, hard, welcome, painful, worthless - because that is the only kind of statement a standpoint can change.

It cannot mean "about", and it cannot mark the target of an action. Those two facts do most of the work of keeping it apart from its neighbours.

～にとって · Formation

Attaches to a bare noun naming somebody capable of having a stake in the matter: a person, a group, a company, a country, sometimes a living thing.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (person or group) Noun + にとって 私(わたし) → 私(わたし)にとって大切(たいせつ)だ
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + にとっての + Noun 学生(がくせい) → 学生(がくせい)にとっての理想(りそう)
Noun, as topic Noun + にとっては 彼(かれ) → 彼(かれ)にとっては簡単(かんたん)だ
Noun, as addition Noun + にとっても 会社(かいしゃ) → 会社(かいしゃ)にとっても損(そん)だ
Noun, business keigo Noun + にとりまして 弊社(へいしゃ) → 弊社(へいしゃ)にとりましては

～にとって · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - after にとって you need an evaluation, not an action. 大切(たいせつ)だ・必要(ひつよう)だ・難(むずか)しい・ありがたい・問題(もんだい)だ・忘(わす)れられない all work. A plain report of what somebody did does not: ❌私(わたし)にとって毎日(まいにち)走(はし)っています.

～にとって · Examples

私(わたし)にとって、家族(かぞく)が一番(いちばん)大切(たいせつ)です。
For me, family comes first.

子(こ)どもにとってこの本(ほん)は難(むずか)しすぎる。
This book is too hard for a child.

私(わたし)にとっては少(すこ)し高(たか)いが、買(か)う価値(かち)はあると思(おも)う。
It's a bit pricey as far as I'm concerned, but I think it's worth buying.

会社(かいしゃ)にとって、人(ひと)を育(そだ)てることは一番(いちばん)確実(かくじつ)な投資(とうし)だ。
For a company, developing its people is the surest investment there is.

～にとって · Examples (cont.)

北国(きたぐに)で育(そだ)った人(ひと)にとって、東京(とうきょう)の冬(ふゆ)はほとんど寒(さむ)くないらしい。
Apparently, for people raised in the far north, a Tokyo winter is barely cold at all.

あのときの一言(ひとこと)は、彼女(かのじょ)にとって一生(いっしょう)忘(わす)れられないものになった。
That one remark became something she would never forget as long as she lived.

留学生(りゅうがくせい)にとっての最初(さいしょ)の壁(かべ)は、言葉(ことば)よりも生活(せいかつ)習慣(しゅうかん)かもしれない。
The first wall for an exchange student may be daily customs rather than language.

～にとって · Examples (cont.)

地方(ちほう)の小(ちい)さな町(まち)にとって、若(わか)い人(ひと)が出(で)ていくことは放(ほう)っておけない問題(もんだい)だ。
For a small rural town, young people leaving is a problem that cannot be left alone.

～にとって · Nuance & register

にとって is close to register-neutral. It is at home in an essay, a news article, a job interview and a conversation with a friend. Nothing about it is stiff, and unlike に関(かん)して or において it has not been pushed out of speech by a plainer alternative.

What it does carry is a mild rhetorical weight. Because it explicitly frames a judgement as belonging to somebody, it invites the reader to notice that other people might see it differently. 私(わたし)にとっては高(たか)い is politer than 高(たか)い for exactly that reason: it presents the verdict as personal rather than absolute. Sales staff and diplomats lean on this.

In casual speech, には covers most of the same ground with fewer syllables - 僕(ぼく)にはちょっと無理(むり)かな - and younger speakers also reach for 個人的(こじんてき)には, "personally speaking". にとって is not unnatural in conversation, but a whole conversation full of it would sound like an essay read aloud.

～にとって · Nuance & register (cont.)

にとりまして is the business-keigo form, common in apologies and in speeches: 弊社(へいしゃ)にとりまして大変(たいへん)ありがたいお話(はなし)です. Recognise it; you will rarely need to produce it.

One restriction worth stating plainly: the noun has to be something that can have interests. People, groups, companies, countries, and living things all qualify - 植物(しょくぶつ)にとって水(みず)は欠(か)かせない is perfectly normal. A pure abstraction or an inanimate object usually does not: ❌この机(つくえ)にとって is odd, because a desk has no point of view to speak from.

～にとって · ～にとって vs ～には

Rule of thumb - they overlap heavily, and には is the shorter, more spoken option. にとって is unambiguous; には is a stack of two particles and can also mean "to" or "in", so context has to sort it out.

私(わたし)にはこの説明(せつめい)がよく分(わ)からなかった。
I didn't really follow this explanation.

初心者(しょしんしゃ)にとって、この説明(せつめい)は不親切(ふしんせつ)だ。
For a beginner, this explanation is unhelpful.

～にとって · ～にとって vs ～には (cont.)

Both frame a judgement from somebody's position. The difference shows up when the sentence could be read another way. 山田(やまだ)さんには話(はな)した means "I told Yamada" - に marking the recipient, は marking contrast - and has nothing to do with standpoints. 山田(やまだ)さんにとって can only ever mean "from Yamada's point of view". In writing, where you cannot lean on intonation, that certainty is worth the extra syllables.

～にとって · ～にとって vs ～に対(たい)して

Rule of thumb - にとって is the viewer; に対(たい)して is the target. Nothing is being aimed at the noun in a にとって sentence.

新人(しんじん)にとって、最初(さいしょ)の一年(いちねん)は覚(おぼ)えることばかりだ。
For a new employee, the first year is nothing but things to learn.

新人(しんじん)に対(たい)して、もう少(すこ)し丁寧(ていねい)に説明(せつめい)したほうがいい。
You'd do better to explain things to a new employee a bit more carefully.

The first sentence describes the world as the newcomer experiences it. The second describes what somebody else should do to them. A quick test: if you can add "…and they may be the only one who sees it that way", it is にとって. If you can add "…and they are on the receiving end", it is に対(たい)して.

～にとって · ～にとって vs ～として

Rule of thumb - にとって = "for X, from X's side of the fence". として = "as X, in the role of X". にとって looks out from the noun; として puts the subject into the noun.

Both come out as English "as" or "for" often enough to blur, and they can even sit in the same slot with different meanings.

親(おや)にとって、子(こ)どもの進路(しんろ)は口(くち)を出(だ)しにくい問題(もんだい)だ。
For a parent, a child's career choice is a hard thing to weigh in on.

親(おや)として、一度(いちど)は本音(ほんね)を伝(つた)えておきたい。
As a parent, I'd like to say what I really think at least once.

～にとって · ～にとって vs ～として (cont.)

In the first, parents are the group whose viewpoint we are borrowing; the speaker need not be one. In the second, the speaker is a parent and is acting in that capacity. Notice also what follows each: にとって is followed by an evaluation, として by an action.

～にとって · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 私(わたし)にとって毎朝(まいあさ)コーヒーを飲(の)みます。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は毎朝(まいあさ)コーヒーを飲(の)みます。
    にとって frames a judgement, so it needs an evaluative predicate. A plain report of what you do takes an ordinary topic は.

  • ❌ この計画(けいかく)にとって意見(いけん)を聞(き)かせてください。 → ✅ この計画(けいかく)について意見(いけん)を聞(き)かせてください。
    にとって cannot mean "about". A plan is not a viewpoint-holder; it is a topic.

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)にとって失礼(しつれい)なことを言(い)ってしまった。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)に対(たい)して失礼(しつれい)なことを言(い)ってしまった。
    Rudeness is aimed at someone. That is に対(たい)して; にとって would put the teacher in the judge's seat instead of the victim's.

  • ❌ 彼(かれ)にとって感謝(かんしゃ)しています。 → ✅ 彼(かれ)に感謝(かんしゃ)しています。
    Gratitude has a recipient, not a standpoint. Plain に is what 感謝(かんしゃ)する takes.

～にとって · Practice 1

1. 受験生(じゅけんせい)____、冬(ふゆ)は一年(いちねん)で一番(いちばん)つらい季節(きせつ)だろう。

受験生(じゅけんせい)にとって、冬(ふゆ)は一年(いちねん)で一番(いちばん)つらい季節(きせつ)だろう。

The sentence rates winter from the exam candidates' position - for everyone else winter is just winter. つらい is an evaluation, which is exactly what にとって requires.

～にとって · Practice 2

2. Choose: 外国(がいこく)のお客様(きゃくさま)（にとって・に対(たい)して）は、英語(えいご)のメニューを用意(ようい)しています。

The restaurant is doing something for - that is, aimed at - those customers, so they are a target, not a viewpoint. にとって would need a judgement to follow, such as 外国(がいこく)のお客様(きゃくさま)にとって注文(ちゅうもん)は難(むずか)しい.

The whole point of the sentence is that the verdict is personal - someone else might well call the same failure a disaster. That is precisely the work にとって does. 私(わたし)には would also be natural in speech.

～にとって · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 会社(かいしゃ)にとって、社員(しゃいん)にとって新(あたら)しい制度(せいど)を説明(せつめい)する義務(ぎむ)がある。

✅ 会社(かいしゃ)には、社員(しゃいん)に対(たい)して新(あたら)しい制度(せいど)を説明(せつめい)する義務(ぎむ)がある。

The second にとって is wrong: the employees are who the explanation is delivered to, so they need に対(たい)して. The first is better replaced by plain には, since 義務(ぎむ)がある states a fact about the company rather than judging something from its viewpoint.

～にとって · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 田中(たなか)さんにとってこの仕事(しごと)は簡単(かんたん)だ。 (b) この仕事(しごと)は簡単(かんたん)だ。

(b) states the job's difficulty as a plain fact about the job - anyone would find it easy.

(a) attaches the verdict to one person. It says the job is easy as Tanaka experiences it, and it quietly implies that this is not everybody's experience: someone else might struggle. That built-in "…though not necessarily for others" is the whole reason the pattern exists, and it is why にとって is useful for softening an opinion you do not want to present as objective truth.

～として

Meaning · as; in the capacity of; in the role of - the hat somebody or something is wearing while it does what it does

として assigns a role. A person acts as a representative, a building is used as a gallery, a sum of money is handed over as a gift. The thing itself does not change; what として supplies is the capacity in which it is being treated.

Two extensions follow naturally. としては sets a standard of judgement - "measured as an X" - and, with a person, "speaking for myself". And a handful of fixed adverbs like 原則(げんそく)として and 参考(さんこう)として are simply this pattern frozen solid.

～として · Formation

Attaches to a bare noun. No な, no の before it.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (role), modifying a verb Noun + として 医者(いしゃ) → 医者(いしゃ)として働(はたら)く
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + としての + Noun 親(おや) → 親(おや)としての責任(せきにん)
Noun, as standard or standpoint Noun + としては 新人(しんじん) → 新人(しんじん)としてはよくやっている
Noun, as addition Noun + としても 趣味(しゅみ) → 趣味(しゅみ)としても楽(たの)しめる
Noun, ruling one out Noun + としてではなく 仕事(しごと) → 仕事(しごと)としてではなく
Fixed adverbs 原則(げんそく)／例外(れいがい)／参考(さんこう) + として 原則(げんそく) → 原則(げんそく)として禁止(きんし)です

～として · Formation (cont.)

Note - beyond N3 you will meet 一人(ひとり)として…ない, "not a single person", where として combines with a counter and a negative. It is the same word doing an emphatic job, and it is worth recognising even though you do not need to produce it yet.

～として · Examples

兄(あに)は医者(いしゃ)として働(はたら)いています。
My older brother works as a doctor.

趣味(しゅみ)として絵(え)を描(か)いています。
I paint as a hobby.

会社(かいしゃ)の代表(だいひょう)として式(しき)に出席(しゅっせき)しました。
I attended the ceremony as the company's representative.

この建物(たてもの)は今(いま)、美術館(びじゅつかん)として使(つか)われている。
This building is now used as an art museum.

参考(さんこう)として、去年(きょねん)の資料(しりょう)もお送(おく)りしておきます。
I'll send last year's materials along as well, for reference.

～として · Examples (cont.)

入(はい)ったばかりの人(ひと)としては、よくやっているほうだと思(おも)う。
For someone who's only just joined, I'd say he's doing well.

彼女(かのじょ)は友(とも)だちとしては最高(さいこう)だが、仕事(しごと)のパートナーとしては少(すこ)し心配(しんぱい)だ。
She's wonderful as a friend, but as a working partner I have my doubts.

留学(りゅうがく)は語学(ごがく)の勉強(べんきょう)としてだけでなく、人生(じんせい)の経験(けいけん)としても大(おお)きな意味(いみ)がある。
Studying abroad matters not only as language study but as a life experience too.

～として · Nuance & register

として is register-neutral. It appears in casual conversation, in job interviews, in newspapers and in contracts without changing shape or feeling. Nothing about it is stiff.

What is register-marked is the company it keeps. The fixed adverbs - 原則(げんそく)として ("as a rule"), 例外(れいがい)として ("by way of exception"), 結果(けっか)として ("as a result") - belong to written and formal speech. A friend says だいたいダメだよ; a notice says 原則(げんそく)としてお断(ことわ)りしております.

としては deserves its own line, because it does two different things and speakers rely on both.

～として · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • As a category standard: 中古(ちゅうこ)としては高(たか)い -
    "expensive for a second-hand one." The judgement is made against what is normal for the category.
  • As a personal position: 私(わたし)としては反対(はんたい)です -
    "my own position is that I'm against it." This is a politeness device. It marks the opinion as yours alone and leaves room for the group to decide otherwise, which is why it is everywhere in meetings.

The second of those is worth practising. Where English says "personally, I…", Japanese very often says 私(わたし)としては, and a learner who reaches for 私(わたし)は instead sounds more insistent than they meant to.

～として · ～として vs ～にとって

Rule of thumb - として puts the subject into the role; にとって looks out from the noun's viewpoint. "As a teacher, I…" is として; "for a teacher, this is…" is にとって.

教師(きょうし)として、生徒(せいと)の話(はなし)はきちんと聞(き)きたい。
As a teacher, I want to listen properly to what my students say.

教師(きょうし)にとって、生徒(せいと)に信頼(しんらい)されることほどうれしいことはない。
For a teacher, nothing is more gratifying than being trusted by the students.

In the first sentence the speaker is a teacher and is acting in that capacity. In the second the speaker is standing outside, describing how the world looks to teachers - and need not be one. The predicate gives it away too: として is followed by an action or an intention, にとって by an evaluation.

～として · ～として vs ～で

Rule of thumb - で can also mark a capacity, but only loosely and only in casual speech. として states the role explicitly and works at any register.

去年(きょねん)はアルバイトで入(はい)っていました。
I was there on a part-time basis last year.

去年(きょねん)はアルバイト職員(しょくいん)として採用(さいよう)されました。
Last year I was taken on as a part-time member of staff.

Both are natural; the second is what you would write on a form. The gap widens when the role is formal or contested - ❌代表(だいひょう)で意見(いけん)を述(の)べた is at best vague about whether you were the representative, while 代表(だいひょう)として意見(いけん)を述(の)べた states it plainly. When the role is what matters, use として.

～として · ～としては vs ～にしては

Rule of thumb - としては measures something within its category; にしては says the result is surprising for that category. Add "considering" and a raised eyebrow, and you have にしては.

子(こ)どもとしてはよく考(かんが)えているほうだ。
As children go, he thinks things through well.

子(こ)どもにしてはよく考(かんが)えている。
He thinks things through well, for a child.

～として · ～としては vs ～にしては (cont.)

The first ranks him among children - a neutral placement inside the category. The second is mildly astonished: given that he is a child, this is more than expected. Where they part company completely is the personal-position use. 私(わたし)としては賛成(さんせい)です means "my position is that I'm in favour"; ❌私(わたし)にしては賛成(さんせい)です would have to mean "surprisingly for me, I'm in favour", which is rarely what anyone intends.

～として · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 通訳(つうやく)になって会議(かいぎ)に出(で)ました。（intending "attended as an interpreter"） → ✅ 通訳(つうやく)として会議(かいぎ)に出(で)ました。
    になる means become: the wrong version says you turned into an interpreter and then went. として names the capacity you attended in.

  • ❌ この部屋(へや)は倉庫(そうこ)にして使(つか)っています。 → ✅ この部屋(へや)は倉庫(そうこ)として使(つか)っています。
    にする is "turn it into"; として使(つか)う is "use it as". Only the second describes an ongoing role.

  • ❌ 先生(せんせい)としての働(はたら)いています。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)として働(はたら)いています。
    としての exists only in front of a noun (先生(せんせい)としての経験(けいけん)). Before a verb, drop the の.

～として · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 私(わたし)として、この写真(しゃしん)はとても大切(たいせつ)です。 → ✅ 私(わたし)にとって、この写真(しゃしん)はとても大切(たいせつ)です。
    This is a judgement made from your side, not a role you are performing. 私(わたし)としては would be possible only if you were stating a position in a discussion.

～として · Practice 1

1. その画家(がか)は長(なが)い間(あいだ)、教師(きょうし)____生活(せいかつ)していた。

The painter was living in the capacity of a teacher - a role, held over a long period. 教師(きょうし)になって would say he became one at a particular moment, which is a different claim.

The sentence is expressing surprise: given that it was a first attempt, the result beat expectations. That is にしては. としては also attaches to nouns rather than clauses, so 初(はじ)めて作(つく)ったとしては would not work here anyway.

～として · Practice 3

Both slots are judging him within a role, so both need として - and は on each, because the whole sentence is built on setting one role against the other. にとって would make the leader the one doing the judging.

～として · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 私(わたし)はこの案(あん)に反対(はんたい)です。 (b) 私(わたし)としてはこの案(あん)に反対(はんたい)です。

(a) is a flat statement of opposition. In a meeting it lands as a personal stand and can sound confrontational, especially if others have already spoken in favour.

(b) frames the same opposition as your position within the discussion - "my own view is against it" - which implicitly grants that the group may decide otherwise. It is softer and more collegial, and it is the standard shape for disagreeing in a Japanese meeting. The propositional content is identical; only the social move differs.

～向き - facing

Meaning · facing such-and-such a way - the direction a thing is turned toward（むき）

向(む)き is the noun form of 向(む)く, "to face". Stuck onto a direction word it gives you the orientation of something that is standing still: a south-facing room, an arrow pointing down, an envelope placed face up.

There is no movement in it. A south-facing window is not going anywhere; it is simply turned that way. That single fact separates this pattern from に向(む)かって, and it is where learners slip.

～向き - facing · Overview

The same word has a second, unrelated-looking life meaning "suited to", as in 子(こ)ども向(む)きの本(ほん). That sense is treated separately; this page is only about direction.

When you rent a flat, it's worth checking which way the windows face, not just the rent and the floor space.

～向き - facing · Nuance & register

This is an ordinary, register-neutral noun. Nothing about it is formal or casual; it turns up in estate agents' listings, in assembly instructions, in weather reports and in conversation, all in the same shape.

Two of its habitats are worth knowing about, because they are where you will actually meet it.

Property. 南(みなみ)向(む)き is a selling point in Japan, and listings advertise it the way an English listing advertises a garden. The reason is practical - sun for the laundry and warmth in winter - and the vocabulary is fixed: 南(みなみ)向(む)きバルコニー, 東(ひがし)向(む)きの窓(まど).

Attitude. 前(まえ)向(む)き and 後(うし)ろ向(む)き have drifted from literal facing to mean "positive / constructive" and "negative / defeatist". These are extremely common in work Japanese: 前(まえ)向(む)きに検討(けんとう)します is what a company says when it wants to sound willing.

～向き - facing · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - 前(まえ)向(む)きに検討(けんとう)します is a famous soft refusal. Literally "we will consider it positively", it very often means the matter will quietly go nowhere. Take it as encouragement, not as a promise.

The bare noun 向(む)き also appears in a fixed phrase you will hear in shops and offices: 向(む)きを変(か)える, "turn it round". And 風(かざ)向(む)き has its own figurative use - 風(かざ)向(む)きが変(か)わった, "the wind has changed", said of a situation turning in someone's favour.

～向き - facing · ～向(む)き vs ～に向(む)かって

Rule of thumb - 向(む)き is a static orientation; に向(む)かって is movement or action toward something. If anything is travelling, you need に向(む)かって.

その像(ぞう)は海(うみ)のほうを向(む)いて立(た)っている。
The statue stands facing the sea.

船(ふね)は島(しま)に向(む)かって進(すす)んでいる。
The ship is heading toward the island.

The statue is not going to the sea; it is merely turned that way, and 海(うみ)向(む)き would describe it. The ship is under way, so に向(む)かって is the only option: ❌島(しま)向(む)きに進(すす)む does not work. Note also the grammatical shape - 向(む)き attaches directly to the noun with no particle, while 向(む)かって demands に.

～向き - facing · 向(む)き vs 方向(ほうこう)

Rule of thumb - 向(む)き is which way a thing is turned; 方向(ほうこう) is a direction out in the world, the way you go. A chair has a 向(む)き; a road has a 方向(ほうこう).

テーブルの向(む)きを少(すこ)し右(みぎ)に回(まわ)した。
I turned the table a little to the right.

駅(えき)はこの**方向(ほうこう)**でいいですか。
Is the station this way?

Swapping them produces sentences that sound off rather than wrong. ❌テーブルの方向(ほうこう)を変(か)える suggests moving the table somewhere else, not rotating it; ❌駅(えき)はこの向(む)きですか sounds as though the station is facing you. The pair 向(む)きを変(か)える (rotate) versus 方向(ほうこう)を変(か)える (change course) is the cleanest way to hold them apart.

～向き - facing · ～向(む)き vs ～向(む)け

Rule of thumb - only 向(む)き does direction. 向(む)け means "made for, aimed at an audience" and never means "facing".

北(きた)向(む)きの窓(まど)からは一日中(いちにちじゅう)日(ひ)が入(はい)らない。
A north-facing window gets no sun all day.

これは海外(かいがい)向(む)けに作(つく)られたモデルです。
This is a model built for overseas markets.

❌北(きた)向(む)けの窓(まど) is not Japanese. The two suffixes do overlap in the other sense of 向(む)き - "suitable for" - where 子(こ)ども向(む)き and 子(こ)ども向(む)け are both possible and differ subtly. In the direction sense there is no overlap at all, so here at least the choice is easy.

～向き - facing · Common mistakes

  • ❌ この部屋(へや)は南(みなみ)に向(む)きです。 → ✅ この部屋(へや)は南(みなみ)向(む)きです。
    The direction word joins directly to 向(む)き as a compound noun. There is no particle in between. With に you would need the verb: 南(みなみ)に向(む)いています.

  • ❌ 北(きた)向(む)けのベランダは洗濯物(せんたくもの)が乾(かわ)きにくい。 → ✅ 北(きた)向(む)きのベランダは洗濯物(せんたくもの)が乾(かわ)きにくい。
    向(む)け means "intended for" and cannot express facing. Only 向(む)き takes a compass point.

  • ❌ 子(こ)どもたちは公園(こうえん)向(む)きに走(はし)っていった。 → ✅ 子(こ)どもたちは公園(こうえん)に向(む)かって走(はし)っていった。
    Running is movement, and 向(む)き has no movement in it. Anything that travels takes に向(む)かって.

～向き - facing · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ カメラの方向(ほうこう)を上(うえ)にしてください。 → ✅ カメラの向(む)きを上(うわ)向(む)きにしてください。
    Tilting the camera changes how it is turned, not where it is going. 方向(ほうこう) would suggest carrying it somewhere.

～向き - facing · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) いすを窓(まど)のほうに向(む)けた。 (b) いすの向(む)きを変(か)えた。

(a) uses the transitive verb 向(む)ける and names the destination of the turning: the chair now faces the window specifically. The window is the point of the sentence.

(b) uses the noun and says only that the orientation is now different from what it was. Where it ended up is left open. In practice (b) is what you say while doing it and (a) is what you say when the direction matters - for instance because you wanted to look outside.

～向き - suited to

Meaning · suitable for; a good fit for - it happens to suit that person, group or purpose, whether or not anyone planned it that way（むき）

This is the same 向(む)き that gives you 南(みなみ)向(む)きの部屋(へや), but with the direction sense worn away. Here it says: this thing and that audience match. A book that suits beginners, a dish that suits summer, a job that suits people who like talking.

The key word is happens to. 向(む)き is the speaker's assessment of a fit. It makes no claim that anybody designed the thing for that audience - and that is exactly what separates it from 向(む)け, which does.

～向き - suited to · Formation

このアプリは文字(もじ)が大(おお)きくて操作(そうさ)も少(すく)ないので、スマホに慣(な)れていない人(ひと)向(む)きだ。
This app has big text and few controls, so it suits people who aren't used to smartphones.

～向き - suited to · Nuance & register

Register-neutral and fully at home in speech. You can say これ、子(こ)ども向(む)きだね to a friend in a shop without sounding formal, and the same phrase can appear in a product review.

What the word carries is a judgement, and judgements belong to somebody. When you say 初心者(しょしんしゃ)向(む)きだ you are giving your reading of how well the thing fits - you are not reporting a fact about the publisher's intentions. This is why 向(む)き works so naturally in reviews, recommendations and warnings, and why it so often appears with hedges: 向(む)きかもしれない, 向(む)きだと思(おも)う, 向(む)きとは言(い)えない.

The negative 不向(ふむ)き is a single word, not 向(む)き plus a prefix you can apply freely, and it is used above all of people and jobs: 彼(かれ)は接客(せっきゃく)には不向(ふむ)きだ. It is blunter than 向(む)きではない and can sting, so in careful speech people soften it: あまり向(む)いていないかもしれませんね.

～向き - suited to · Nuance & register (cont.)

向(む)き不向(ふむ)き as a pair is a small fixed noun meaning "aptitude" - literally the suits and the does-not-suits. 人(ひと)には向(む)き不向(ふむ)きがある is a stock consoling phrase: "people are cut out for different things."

～向き - suited to · ～向(む)き vs ～向(む)け

Rule of thumb - 向(む)き = it happens to suit them. 向(む)け = it was made for them. Fit versus intention.

This is the pair the whole page exists for, and the difference is real even though many sentences accept both.

この辞書(じしょ)は字(じ)が大(おお)きいので高齢者(こうれいしゃ)向(む)きだ。
This dictionary has large print, so it works well for older readers.

この辞書(じしょ)は高齢者(こうれいしゃ)向(む)けに作(つく)られている。
This dictionary was made for older readers.

～向き - suited to · ～向(む)き vs ～向(む)け (cont.)

The first is a reader's verdict: whatever the publisher had in mind, the big print suits them. The second is a statement about the design brief. Consequently 向(む)け is what appears in business and marketing language - 海外(かいがい)向(む)け, 法人(ほうじん)向(む)け - while 向(む)き is what appears in reviews and advice.

One useful consequence: 向(む)け takes に before a verb naturally (向(む)けに作(つく)る, 向(む)けに開(ひら)く) because intention modifies an action. 向(む)き mostly stays a predicate or a noun modifier, which is why ❌子(こ)ども向(む)きに作(つく)る sounds wrong to a native ear.

～向き - suited to · ～向(む)き vs ～用(よう)

Rule of thumb - 用(よう) states a designated use: this item is for that and not for anything else. 向(む)き only rates a fit.

子(こ)ども**用(よう)**のいすはあちらにございます。
The children's chairs are over there.

この映画(えいが)は子(こ)ども向(む)きだと思(おも)う。
I think this film is one for children.

子(こ)ども用(よう) is a category label - the chair is a child's chair, and an adult using it is misusing it. 子(こ)ども向(む)き is a recommendation; an adult can watch the film perfectly well, it just fits children better. Labels on shelves and menus overwhelmingly use 用(よう): 業務用(ぎょうむよう), 洗濯用(せんたくよう), 家庭用(かていよう) - it is the suffix of designated purpose.

～向き - suited to · ～向(む)きだ vs ～に向(む)いている

Rule of thumb - the two say the same thing from opposite ends. A は B 向(む)きだ: the thing is described, B is the audience. B は A に向(む)いている: the person is described, A is the activity.

この仕事(しごと)は細(こま)かい作業(さぎょう)が好(す)きな人(ひと)向(む)きだ。
This job suits people who like detailed work.

妹(いもうと)は細(こま)かい作業(さぎょう)に向(む)いている。
My younger sister is well suited to detailed work.

Getting these crossed is the commonest error with the pattern. Remember that 向(む)いている is a verb and needs に, while 向(む)き is a suffix and needs nothing at all. Also note that with a named individual, に向(む)いている is far more natural: 妹(いもうと)向(む)きだ would be odd, because 向(む)き prefers a type of person rather than a particular one.

～向き - suited to · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 子(こ)ども向(む)きに作(つく)られた番組(ばんぐみ)です。 → ✅ 子(こ)ども向(む)けに作(つく)られた番組(ばんぐみ)です。
    Being made for an audience is intention, and intention is 向(む)け. 向(む)き would only rate the result.

  • ❌ 彼(かれ)はこの仕事(しごと)向(む)きです。 → ✅ 彼(かれ)はこの仕事(しごと)に向(む)いています。
    The two ends are reversed. 向(む)き attaches to the audience, so the correct form with this word order would be この仕事(しごと)は彼(かれ)のような人(ひと)向(む)きです.

～向き - suited to · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この雑誌(ざっし)は若(わか)い女性(じょせい)向(む)きだ。 (b) この雑誌(ざっし)は若(わか)い女性(じょせい)向(む)けだ。

It is the sentence an editor or a bookshop clerk would use, and it says nothing about whether it actually suits them. ～向け

Meaning · intended for; aimed at - made, written, priced or organised with that audience or market in mind（むけ）

向(む)け comes from 向(む)ける, "to point something at". Somebody has aimed this thing at somebody: a programme at children, a car at the export market, a seminar at second-year students. The word carries intention, and that is its whole meaning.

It says nothing about whether the aim succeeded. A textbook can be 初心者(しょしんしゃ)向(む)け and still be far too hard for a beginner - the publisher aimed, and missed. Judging the fit is the job of 向(む)き.

～向け · Formation

Attaches to a bare noun naming an audience, a market, a region or a customer type.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (audience), as predicate Noun + 向(む)けだ 海外(かいがい) → この商品(しょうひん)は海外(かいがい)向(む)けだ
Noun, modifying a noun Noun + 向(む)けの + Noun 子(こ)ども → 子(こ)ども向(む)けの番組(ばんぐみ)
Noun, modifying a verb Noun + 向(む)けに + verb 学生(がくせい) → 学生(がくせい)向(む)けに作(つく)る
Market or destination 輸出(ゆしゅつ)・国内(こくない) + 向(む)け 輸出(ゆしゅつ) → 輸出(ゆしゅつ)向(む)けの車(くるま)

～向け · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Contrasted pair A向(む)けとB向(む)け 法人(ほうじん) → 法人(ほうじん)向(む)けと個人(こじん)向(む)け

Rule of thumb - 向(む)けに before a verb, 向(む)けの before a noun, 向(む)けだ at the end of the sentence. There is no 向(む)けな.

～向け · Examples

これは子(こ)ども向(む)けの番組(ばんぐみ)です。
This is a programme for children.

この商品(しょうひん)は海外(かいがい)向(む)けです。
This product is for overseas markets.

会社(かいしゃ)が学生(がくせい)向(む)けの説明会(せつめいかい)を開(ひら)いた。
The company held a briefing session for students.

この教材(きょうざい)は日本語(にほんご)を学(まな)び始(はじ)めたばかりの人(ひと)向(む)けに作(つく)られている。
These materials were made for people who have only just started learning Japanese.

輸出(ゆしゅつ)向(む)けの車(くるま)は、ハンドルが左(ひだり)についている。
Cars built for export have the steering wheel on the left.

～向け · Examples (cont.)

来月(らいげつ)から、高校生(こうこうせい)向(む)けのオンライン講座(こうざ)が始(はじ)まります。
An online course aimed at high-school students starts next month.

この雑誌(ざっし)はもともと専門家(せんもんか)向(む)けに作(つく)られたが、今(いま)では一般(いっぱん)の読者(どくしゃ)のほうが多(おお)い。
This magazine was originally produced for specialists, but these days most of its readers are ordinary members of the public.

国内(こくない)向(む)けと海外(かいがい)向(む)けでは、同(おな)じ製品(せいひん)でも説明書(せつめいしょ)の厚(あつ)さがまったく違(ちが)う。
For the domestic market and for overseas, the manual that comes with the same product is a completely different thickness.

～向け · Nuance & register

The word itself is register-neutral, but its habitat is not. You will meet it in product descriptions, publishers' catalogues, course listings, TV schedules, press releases and company presentations, far more often than in casual chat. That is not because it sounds formal - it is because talking about target audiences is mostly a business activity.

In conversation it turns up whenever the topic is products or content: これ、大人(おとな)向(む)けだよね is perfectly ordinary spoken Japanese. What you would not do is use it about your own private arrangements - see the contrast with のために below.

～向け · Nuance & register (cont.)

A few collocations are worth having ready-made, because they are how the pattern actually appears in the wild: 海外(かいがい)向(む)け, 国内(こくない)向(む)け, 輸出(ゆしゅつ)向(む)け, 法人(ほうじん)向(む)け ("for businesses"), 個人(こじん)向(む)け ("for individuals"), 初心者(しょしんしゃ)向(む)け, 女性(じょせい)向(む)け. The last of these has become slightly loaded in recent years - marketing categories drawn along gender lines attract comment - so it is worth noticing that it describes a marketing decision and not a claim about who may buy the thing.

Note - 向(む)け wants a group or market, not a named individual. 部長(ぶちょう)向(む)けの資料(しりょう) is odd unless you mean managers as a category. For one person, use 部長(ぶちょう)に見(み)せる資料(しりょう) or 部長(ぶちょう)のための資料(しりょう).

～向け · ～向(む)け vs ～向(む)き

Rule of thumb - 向(む)け = somebody aimed it there. 向(む)き = it turns out to suit them. Intention versus fit.

この問題集(もんだいしゅう)は受験生(じゅけんせい)向(む)けに作(つく)られた。
This question book was produced for exam candidates.

この問題集(もんだいしゅう)は説明(せつめい)が丁寧(ていねい)なので独学(どくがく)の人(ひと)向(む)きだ。
The explanations are thorough, so this question book is good for people studying alone.

The first reports a decision made by the publisher. The second is the speaker's verdict, and it could easily be about an audience the publisher never considered. This is why a shop sign says 子(こ)ども向(む)けコーナー - the shop has designated it - while a review says 子(こ)ども向(む)きだと思(おも)う.

～向け · ～向(む)け vs ～向(む)き (cont.)

Only 向(む)け combines comfortably with に plus a verb of making, writing, opening or selling, precisely because those verbs describe the act of aiming. And only 向(む)き can be preceded by a compass point: 南(みなみ)向(む)き is a direction, and ❌南(みなみ)向(む)け is nothing at all.

～向け · ～向(む)け vs ～用(よう)

Rule of thumb - 向(む)け names the audience something was aimed at; 用(よう) names the use something is for. Who versus what-for.

自宅(じたく)**用(よう)**ですか、贈(おく)り物(もの)**用(よう)**ですか。
Is it for yourself, or is it a gift?

会社(かいしゃ)向(む)けのサービスも始(はじ)めました。
We've launched a service for businesses as well.

The shop assistant's question is about the purpose the item will serve, so 用(よう). The second sentence names the customer segment, so 向(む)け. They can even stack in the same phrase without redundancy: 学生(がくせい)向(む)けの試験(しけん)用(よう)アプリ - an exam-taking app aimed at students.

～向け · ～向(む)け vs ～用(よう) (cont.)

Note that 用(よう) attaches to activities and occasions as readily as to people (旅行用(りょこうよう), 非常用(ひじょうよう)), whereas 向(む)け is essentially restricted to audiences and markets.

～向け · ～向(む)けに vs ～のために

Rule of thumb - のために is for someone's sake, personal and warm. 向(む)けに is targeted at a market, impersonal and commercial.

母(はは)のために大(おお)きい字(じ)の本(ほん)を買(か)った。
I bought a large-print book for my mother.

目(め)が悪(わる)い人(ひと)向(む)けに大(おお)きい字(じ)の本(ほん)が出(だ)されている。
Large-print books are published for people with poor eyesight.

Swapping them is a tonal mistake rather than a grammatical one, and it is a memorable one. ❌母(はは)向(む)けに本(ほん)を買(か)った makes your mother sound like a demographic segment. Conversely, a publisher's catalogue would not write 目(め)が悪(わる)い人(ひと)のために unless it wanted to sound charitable rather than commercial - which, occasionally, it does.

～向け · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 南(みなみ)向(む)けのベランダがある部屋(へや)を探(さが)しています。 → ✅ 南(みなみ)向(む)きのベランダがある部屋(へや)を探(さが)しています。
    Compass points take 向(む)き. A balcony faces south; nobody aimed it at south as a market.

  • ❌ 初心者(しょしんしゃ)向(む)けな教科書(きょうかしょ)を使(つか)っています。 → ✅ 初心者(しょしんしゃ)向(む)けの教科書(きょうかしょ)を使(つか)っています。
    向(む)け is a noun and joins the next noun with の. There is no な-adjective form.

  • ❌ 今夜(こんや)は家族(かぞく)向(む)けにカレーを作(つく)りました。 → ✅ 今夜(こんや)は家族(かぞく)のためにカレーを作(つく)りました。
    Cooking dinner at home is done for people's sake, not aimed at a market segment. 向(む)けに here sounds as though you are running a canteen.

～向け · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ この靴(くつ)は私(わたし)向(む)けです。 → ✅ この靴(くつ)は私(わたし)に合(あ)っています。
    向(む)け takes a group or a market, never a single named person. For an individual fit, use 合(あ)う, or 向(む)いている for an aptitude.

～向け · Practice 1

1. この会社(かいしゃ)は数年前(すうねんまえ)から高齢者(こうれいしゃ)____のスマートフォンを作(つく)っている。

高齢者(こうれいしゃ)向(む)けのスマートフォンを作(つく)っている。

The company deliberately designs the phones for that market, so the sentence is about intention. 高齢者(こうれいしゃ)向(む)き would be a reviewer's verdict on the result, which is not what a manufacturer's own activity is described with.

～向け · Practice 2

2. Choose: この部屋(へや)は東(ひがし)（向(む)き・向(む)け）なので、朝(あさ)は明(あか)るいです。

この部屋(へや)は東(ひがし)向(む)きなので、朝(あさ)は明(あか)るいです。

A compass direction is pure orientation, which only 向(む)き expresses. 向(む)け requires an audience or a market, and "east" is neither.

～向け · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The museum has started an English-language guided tour for foreign visitors."

美術館(びじゅつかん)が外国人(がいこくじん)観光客(かんこうきゃく)向(む)けの英語(えいご)のツアーを始(はじ)めました。

The museum has aimed a service at a defined group of visitors - a planning decision, so 向(む)け. You could equally write 外国人(がいこくじん)観光客(かんこうきゃく)向(む)けに英語(えいご)のツアーを始(はじ)めました, using the に form before the verb.

～向け · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: この辞書(じしょ)は小学生(しょうがくせい)向(む)けに作(つく)られているが、説明(せつめい)が短(みじか)くて分(わ)かりやすいので、大人(おとな)向(む)けにもいい。

✅ …説明(せつめい)が短(みじか)くて分(わ)かりやすいので、大人(おとな)向(む)きでもある。

The first 向(む)け is right - the publisher aimed it at primary school pupils. The second is not, because nobody aimed it at adults; the speaker is judging that it happens to suit them. That is exactly the 向(む)き job.

～向け · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 子(こ)ども向(む)けに書(か)かれた説明(せつめい) (b) 子(こ)どものために書(か)かれた説明(せつめい)

(a) is a statement about the target readership: the writer pitched the level, vocabulary and length at children. It is neutral and professional, the kind of thing said about published material, and it implies nothing about the writer's feelings.

(b) is about benefit. Somebody wrote it so that children would be helped, and the phrase carries warmth - it would suit a hospital leaflet or a note written by a teacher for their own class. In practice (b) also allows the reader and the beneficiary to differ: an explanation written for the sake of children might in fact be addressed to their parents, which (a) rules out.
