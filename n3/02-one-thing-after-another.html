～たとたん

Meaning · the instant X happened, Y happened - and Y was not expected

たとたん welds two past events together with no gap at all. What separates it from every other "as soon as" pattern is the second half: the thing that happens is a surprise, something the speaker noticed rather than arranged. If you planned it, たとたん is the wrong word.

～たとたん · Formation

とたん is really the noun 途端(とたん), "the very edge of the moment", so it takes a plain past verb in front of it, exactly the way a noun takes a modifying clause.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (た-form) た-form + とたん 着(つ)く → 着(つ)いたとたん
Verb (た-form) た-form + とたんに 開(あ)ける → 開(あ)けたとたんに
As a bare noun その + 途端(とたん) その途端(とたん)、電気(でんき)が消(き)えた
❌ Dictionary form ❌着(つ)くとたん ✅着(つ)いたとたん
❌ Non-past main clause ❌…とたん、驚(おどろ)く ✅…とたん、驚(おどろ)いた
❌ Request or invitation ❌…とたん、電話(でんわ)してください ✅…たら、電話(でんわ)してください

～たとたん · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - three things must all be true: the verb in front is た, the main clause is past, and nobody planned the result. Break any one of them and you want a different pattern.

～たとたん · Examples

ベッドに入(はい)ったとたん、眠(ねむ)ってしまった。
The moment I got into bed, I was asleep.

窓(まど)を開(あ)けたとたん、冷(つめ)たい風(かぜ)が入(はい)ってきた。
The instant I opened the window, cold air poured in.

立(た)ち上(あ)がったとたんに、めまいがした。
I stood up and the room span, just like that.

先生(せんせい)の顔(かお)を見(み)たとたん、弟(おとうと)は泣(な)き出(だ)した。
The second he saw the teacher's face, my little brother burst into tears.

試合(しあい)が終(お)わったとたん、雨(あめ)が降(ふ)り出(だ)した。
No sooner had the match finished than it started to rain.

～たとたん · Examples (cont.)

ドアを閉(し)めたとたんに、鍵(かぎ)を中(なか)に置(お)いてきたことに気(き)づいた。
The instant the door shut behind me, I realised I'd left the key inside.

部長(ぶちょう)は部屋(へや)に入(はい)ってきたとたん、理由(りゆう)も言(い)わずに大(おお)きな声(こえ)で怒(おこ)り出(だ)した。
The manager walked into the room and immediately started shouting, without saying why.

十年(じゅうねん)働(はたら)いた会社(かいしゃ)を辞(や)めたとたん、毎日(まいにち)が静(しず)かになりすぎて、かえって落(お)ち着(つ)かなかった。
The day I left the company I'd worked at for ten years, my life went so quiet it actually unsettled me.

～たとたん · Nuance & register

たとたん is neutral in politeness - the pattern sits in the middle of the sentence, so the ending decides the register: 降(ふ)り出(だ)した or 降(ふ)り出(だ)しました, either is fine. It is slightly more common in narration and in written accounts of events than in moment-to-moment chat, because it is a storyteller's device: it sets up a punchline.

The に is optional and carries almost no meaning. とたんに is a touch more deliberate, a beat of extra weight before the surprise; とたん alone is quicker. Most speakers use both without thinking about it.

The word is usually written in kana as とたん, though 途端(とたん) in kanji turns up in novels and newspapers. As a standalone noun it survives in その途端(とたん) - "at that very instant" - which is worth knowing because it lets you split the two events into separate sentences.

Three neighbours are worth naming, because JLPT questions live on the difference:

～たとたん · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ～次第(しだい) points forwards, not back. It is formal, it takes the
    verb stem rather than た, and the second clause is the speaker's own planned action: 会議(かいぎ)が終(お)わり次第(しだい)、ご連絡(れんらく)します。 Everything たとたん forbids, 次第(しだい) requires.
  • ～や否(いな)や means the same as たとたん but is literary. You will meet
    it in print and essentially never in conversation.
  • ～かと思(おも)うと also carries surprise, but it specialises in things that
    flip: 泣(な)いていたかと思(おも)うと、もう笑(わら)っている。

～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～たら

Rule of thumb - たら lets you put a request, an instruction, an invitation or a plan in the main clause. たとたん allows none of those. If the second half is something you want to happen, it has to be たら.

駅(えき)に着(つ)いたら、電話(でんわ)してください。
Call me when you get to the station.

Swap in とたん and the sentence collapses, because you cannot ask somebody to be surprised on cue. With a past clause and an unplanned result, the same setup is fine:

駅(えき)に着(つ)いたとたん、財布(さいふ)がないことに気(き)づいた。
The moment I reached the station, I realised my wallet was gone.

～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～たら (cont.)

たら is also happy with a gap in time - 大学(だいがく)を出(で)たら働(はたら)きます covers years. たとたん covers a fraction of a second.

～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～てすぐ(に)

Rule of thumb - てすぐ means "very soon after", and lets you do the second thing on purpose. たとたん means "in the same instant", and the second thing happens to you.

家(いえ)に帰(かえ)ってすぐ、シャワーを浴(あ)びた。
I had a shower as soon as I got home.

That is a decision, so たとたん would be odd. Compare:

家(いえ)に帰(かえ)ったとたん、猫(ねこ)が足(あし)に飛(と)びついてきた。
The second I got home, the cat launched itself at my legs.

Nobody arranged that, which is exactly why たとたん fits. The same test explains why a diary entry uses てすぐ for a routine and たとたん for the day something went wrong.

～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～と

Rule of thumb - と states a rule that holds every time. たとたん reports one particular moment that has already gone past.

このボタンを押(お)すと、お湯(ゆ)が出(で)ます。
Press this button and hot water comes out.

That is how the machine works, always - たとたん cannot express it, because it is anchored to a single past event. But a one-off goes the other way:

ボタンを押(お)したとたん、機械(きかい)から変(へん)な音(おと)がした。
The instant I pressed the button, the machine made a strange noise.

～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～と (cont.)

と can also carry discovery in the past - ドアを開(あ)けると、誰(だれ)もいなかった - and there it comes close to たとたん. The difference is speed and shock: と narrates smoothly, たとたん snaps.

～たとたん · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 座(すわ)るとたん、電話(でんわ)が鳴(な)った。 → ✅ 座(すわ)ったとたん、電話(でんわ)が鳴(な)った。
    The verb in front of とたん is always た-form, even when the sentence is about the past. Dictionary form is simply ungrammatical here.

  • ❌ ベルが鳴(な)ったとたん、教室(きょうしつ)を出(で)ましょう。 → ✅ ベルが鳴(な)ったら、教室(きょうしつ)を出(で)ましょう。
    An invitation is a plan, and とたん cannot host a plan. たら can.

  • ❌ 毎朝(まいあさ)、起(お)きたとたん水(みず)を飲(の)む。 → ✅ 毎朝(まいあさ)、起(お)きたらすぐ水(みず)を飲(の)む。
    A daily habit is neither a single moment nor a surprise. とたん reports one event, once.

  • ❌ 外(そと)に出(で)たとたん、雨(あめ)が降(ふ)り出(だ)すでしょう。 → ✅ 外(そと)に出(で)たとたん、雨(あめ)が降(ふ)り出(だ)した。
    The main clause has to be past. There is no way to predict a とたん; by the time you use it, it has happened.

～たとたん · Practice 1

1. 電車(でんしゃ)に乗(の)っ____、ドアが閉(し)まった。

電車(でんしゃ)に乗(の)ったとたん、ドアが閉(し)まった。

The verb takes the た-form before とたん, and the main clause is past. The doors closing is outside the speaker's control, which is what licenses the pattern.

～たとたん · Practice 2

2. Choose: 荷物(にもつ)が届(とど)い（たとたん・たら）、中身(なかみ)を確認(かくにん)してください。

荷物(にもつ)が届(とど)いたら、中身(なかみ)を確認(かくにん)してください。

The main clause is a request, and とたん refuses requests, commands and invitations. Anything you are asking the listener to do belongs with たら.

～たとたん · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The moment I sat down on the bench, it started to rain."

ベンチに座(すわ)ったとたん、雨(あめ)が降(ふ)り出(だ)した。

Both halves are past, the rain is unplanned, and the two events share a single instant - the ideal conditions for たとたん. Adding the optional に after とたん changes nothing.

～たとたん · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 春(はる)になるとたん、桜(さくら)が咲(さ)きます。

✅ 春(はる)になると、桜(さくら)が咲(さ)きます。

Two problems at once: とたん needs a た-form, and more importantly this sentence states what happens every year. A general rule takes と, never とたん.

～たとたん · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼(かれ)が部屋(へや)を出(で)たとたん、みんなが話(はな)し始(はじ)めた。 (b) 彼(かれ)が部屋(へや)を出(で)てから、みんなが話(はな)し始(はじ)めた。

(a) puts the two events in the same instant and frames the outburst of talk as something the speaker witnessed and did not expect - the room had clearly been holding its tongue. There is an edge of "the second he was gone".

(b) simply orders them: first he left, then people talked. It is neutral about how long the gap was and implies nothing about surprise, so it works equally well in a dry report of a meeting.

～上で

Meaning · after doing X - and on the strength of what X told you - do Y

上(うえ)で (read うえで) is not a plain "after". It says the first action was done in order to find something out, and the second action is the decision you reached with that information in hand. It is a formal word: paperwork, meetings, notices.

～上で · Formation

The same word covers two jobs, and the verb form in front tells you which. た-form means "after"; dictionary form means "in the course of".

～上で · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (た-form) - after た-form + 上(うえ)で 調(しら)べる → 調(しら)べた上(うえ)で
Action noun - after Noun + の + 上(うえ)で 相談(そうだん) → 相談(そうだん)の上(うえ)で
Verb (dictionary) - in the course of dictionary + 上(うえ)で 暮(く)らす → 暮(く)らす上(うえ)で
Noun - in the course of Noun + の + 上(うえ)で 仕事(しごと) → 仕事(しごと)の上(うえ)で
Before a noun ～上(うえ)での + Noun 検討(けんとう)した上(うえ)での判断(はんだん)
Formal notice style ～の上(うえ)、… ご記入(きにゅう)の上(うえ)、お出(だ)しください
❌ て-form ❌調(しら)べて上(うえ)で ✅調(しら)べた上(うえ)で

～上で · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - for the "after" sense, ask whether the first action produces information the second action needs. 見(み)た上(うえ)で決(き)める works because looking is what tells you how to decide. 歯(は)をみがいた上(うえ)で寝(ね)る does not, because brushing your teeth informs nothing.

～上で · Examples

よく考(かんが)えた上(うえ)で、お返事(へんじ)します。
I'll give you my answer once I've thought it through.

両親(りょうしん)と相談(そうだん)した上(うえ)で決(き)めます。
I'll decide after talking it over with my parents.

部屋(へや)を見(み)た上(うえ)で、借(か)りるかどうか決(き)めたいです。
I'd like to see the flat before deciding whether to rent it.

外国(がいこく)で暮(く)らす上(うえ)で、一番(いちばん)苦労(くろう)するのは言葉(ことば)です。
The hardest part of living abroad is the language.

この仕事(しごと)を続(つづ)ける上(うえ)で、体力(たいりょく)は欠(か)かせません。
Stamina is essential if you're going to keep doing this job.

～上で · Examples (cont.)

資料(しりょう)をお読(よ)みになった上(うえ)で、ご質問(しつもん)をお寄(よ)せください。
Please read the materials and then send us your questions.

工事(こうじ)の費用(ひよう)を正確(せいかく)に計算(けいさん)した上(うえ)で、あらためてお見積(みつ)もりをお送(おく)りいたします。
Once we have costed the work precisely, we'll send you a revised quotation.

皆(みな)さんのご意見(いけん)をうかがった上(うえ)で、来週(らいしゅう)の会議(かいぎ)で最終(さいしゅう)案(あん)を決(き)めたいと思(おも)います。
I'd like to hear everyone's views first, and then settle the final proposal at next week's meeting.

～上で · Nuance & register

This is a formal pattern. It belongs to written Japanese, business speech and public notices, and it sounds stiff in a chat with a friend - where you would simply say 見(み)てから決(き)める. Using it well is one of the fastest ways to make a work email sound like it was written by an adult.

In notices and forms the で is often dropped, leaving ～の上(うえ)、 with a comma: ご確認(かくにん)の上(うえ)、送信(そうしん)してください。 That clipped form is almost a fixed register marker; it appears on websites, application forms and station signs, and never in speech.

Two particles slot in usefully. 上(うえ)では narrows the frame to one domain - 法律(ほうりつ)の上(うえ)では問題(もんだい)ありません, "legally speaking there's no problem". 上(うえ)でも adds "for that purpose too".

～上で · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - do not confuse this with ～上(うえ)に, which is a completely different pattern meaning "and what's more": この店(みせ)は安(やす)い上(うえ)においしい。 Same kanji, に instead of で, and it stacks reasons rather than ordering events. There is also ～上(うえ)は, "now that (you have done X)", which is a step above N3.

～上で · ～た上(うえ)で vs ～てから

Rule of thumb - てから only says later. 上(うえ)で says later, and because of what the first step revealed. Every 上(うえ)で sentence could be てから; the reverse is not true.

手(て)を洗(あら)ってから、ケーキを作(つく)り始(はじ)めた。
I washed my hands and then started making the cake.

Washing your hands tells you nothing, so 洗(あら)った上(うえ)で would be strange. Now a case where the first step really does feed the second:

アンケートの結果(けっか)を分析(ぶんせき)した上(うえ)で、新(あたら)しい企画(きかく)を立(た)てます。
We'll draw up the new plan after analysing the survey results.

～上で · ～た上(うえ)で vs ～てから (cont.)

Register separates them too. てから is neutral and used at every level of politeness; 上(うえ)で raises the whole sentence a notch.

～上で · ～た上(うえ)で vs ～た後(あと)で

Rule of thumb - 後(あと)で measures time: X finished, then Y. 上(うえ)で measures dependence: Y is shaped by X.

食事(しょくじ)の後(あと)で、散歩(さんぽ)に行(い)きました。
After the meal we went for a walk.

Nothing about the meal determined the walk, so this is 後(あと)で and only 後(あと)で. Compare:

医者(いしゃ)の話(はなし)を聞(き)いた上(うえ)で、手術(しゅじゅつ)を受(う)けるかどうか考(かんが)えます。
I'll think about whether to have the operation after hearing what the doctor says.

～上で · ～た上(うえ)で vs ～た後(あと)で (cont.)

Here 後(あと)で would be grammatical but flat; it would lose the point, which is that the doctor's explanation is the basis of the decision. A second, harder difference: 後(あと)で accepts events nobody controls - 台風(たいふう)が過(す)ぎた後(あと)で - while 上(うえ)で needs a deliberate first action, so ❌台風(たいふう)が過(す)ぎた上(うえ)で is wrong.

～上で · ～る上(うえ)で vs ～た上(うえ)で

Rule of thumb - dictionary form + 上(うえ)で means "in doing X / when it comes to X" and points at a whole activity. た-form + 上(うえ)で means "after X" and points at one completed step.

日本語(にほんご)を勉強(べんきょう)する上(うえ)で、辞書(じしょ)の使(つか)い方(かた)はとても大切(たいせつ)です。
When it comes to studying Japanese, knowing how to use a dictionary matters a great deal.

日本語(にほんご)を勉強(べんきょう)した上(うえ)で、留学(りゅうがく)するかどうか決(き)めます。
I'll study some Japanese first, and then decide whether to go abroad.

The first sentence never happened and never will "happen" - it describes the activity in general. The second describes a step you take and then finish. Getting the two mixed up is the single most common error with this pattern, and the fix is always the verb form.

～上で · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 歯(は)をみがいた上(うえ)で寝(ね)ます。 → ✅ 歯(は)をみがいてから寝(ね)ます。
    A routine sequence with no decision in it takes てから. Brushing your teeth does not give you information to act on.

  • ❌ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)った上(うえ)で、試合(しあい)が中止(ちゅうし)になった。 → ✅ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ったので、試合(しあい)が中止(ちゅうし)になった。
    The first clause must be something somebody chose to do. Weather is not a deliberate act, and here the relation is cause, not preparation.

  • ❌ 見積(みつ)もりを見(み)て上(うえ)で返事(へんじ)します。 → ✅ 見積(みつ)もりを見(み)た上(うえ)で返事(へんじ)します。
    上(うえ) is a noun, so the clause in front of it ends in a plain form - た or dictionary - never て.

～上で · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 会社(かいしゃ)で働(はたら)いた上(うえ)で必要(ひつよう)な知識(ちしき) → ✅ 会社(かいしゃ)で働(はたら)く上(うえ)で必要(ひつよう)な知識(ちしき)
    "Knowledge you need in order to work at a company" is the in the course of sense, which takes the dictionary form.

～上で · Practice 1

1. 条件(じょうけん)をよく確(たし)かめ____、申(もう)し込(こ)んでください。

条件(じょうけん)をよく確(たし)かめた上(うえ)で、申(もう)し込(こ)んでください。

Checking the conditions is exactly the kind of deliberate, informing step this pattern wants, and the application is the decision that follows from it. Note the た-form: the checking has to be finished.

～上で · Practice 2

2. Choose: 安全(あんぜん)に運転(うんてん)（する・した）上(うえ)で、気(き)をつけることは何(なん)ですか。

安全(あんぜん)に運転(うんてん)する上(うえ)で、気(き)をつけることは何(なん)ですか。

The question is about driving safely as an ongoing activity, not about something finished, so it needs the dictionary form and the "in the course of" reading.

～上で · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "We would like to make a decision after hearing the customer's opinion."

お客様(きゃくさま)のご意見(いけん)をうかがった上(うえ)で、決(き)めたいと思(おも)います。

A business setting, a deliberate first step, and a decision that depends on it - all three signals point at 上(うえ)で rather than a plain てから.

～上で · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 地震(じしん)が起(お)きた上(うえ)で、電車(でんしゃ)が止(と)まりました。

✅ 地震(じしん)が起(お)きたため、電車(でんしゃ)が止(と)まりました。

An earthquake is not an action anyone performed to gather information, and the trains stopping is a consequence rather than a considered decision. This sentence needs a cause marker such as ため or ので.

～上で · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 全員(ぜんいん)の意見(いけん)を聞(き)いた上(うえ)で決(き)めます。 (b) 全員(ぜんいん)の意見(いけん)を聞(き)いてから決(き)めます。

(a) commits the speaker to letting those opinions shape the decision. It is the sentence a manager uses to promise that the consultation is real, and its formal tone suits a meeting or an email.

(b) only promises an order of events: opinions first, decision second. It is perfectly polite but leaves open the possibility that the decision was made already and the listening is a formality.

～て初めて

Meaning · not until X did Y become clear - it took X for the penny to drop

て初(はじ)めて is the grammar of hindsight. You did something, or something happened to you, and only then did you understand, notice or become able to do the thing in the second clause. The English that fits it best is almost always "only after…" or "it wasn't until…".

～て初めて · Formation

The pattern is simply the て-form followed by the adverb 初(はじ)めて. Word order is doing all the work here, so guard it: the て-form comes first.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (て-form) て-form + 初(はじ)めて 失(うしな)う → 失(うしな)って初(はじ)めて
Verb + てみる てみて + 初(はじ)めて 使(つか)う → 使(つか)ってみて初(はじ)めて
Noun (a new status) Noun + になって + 初(はじ)めて 親(おや) → 親(おや)になって初(はじ)めて
Often rounded off with のだ ～て初(はじ)めて…のだ 読(よ)んで初(はじ)めてわかったのだ
❌ た-form ❌失(うしな)った初(はじ)めて ✅失(うしな)って初(はじ)めて
❌ Adverb before the verb ❌初(はじ)めて失(うしな)って ✅失(うしな)って初(はじ)めて

～て初めて · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the main clause has to be a discovery: わかる、気(き)づく、 知(し)る、できるようになる、見(み)えてくる. If the second half is something you decided to do, you want てから, not て初(はじ)めて.

～て初めて · Examples

使(つか)ってみて初(はじ)めて、この辞書(じしょ)のよさが分(わ)かった。
It wasn't until I used it that I saw how good this dictionary is.

病気(びょうき)になって初(はじ)めて、健康(けんこう)のありがたさが分(わ)かった。
Only when I got ill did I appreciate being healthy.

一人(ひとり)で暮(く)らして初(はじ)めて、家事(かじ)の大変(たいへん)さに気(き)づいた。
It took living on my own for me to realise how much work housework is.

説明(せつめい)を聞(き)いて初(はじ)めて、この機械(きかい)の使(つか)い方(かた)が分(わ)かりました。
I only understood how to use this machine after hearing the explanation.

人(ひと)は失(うしな)って初(はじ)めて、その大切(たいせつ)さに気(き)づくものだ。
People never see the value of something until they've lost it.

～て初めて · Examples (cont.)

地図(ちず)を見(み)て初(はじ)めて、自分(じぶん)がどれだけ遠(とお)くまで歩(ある)いてきたのか知(し)った。
Not until I looked at the map did I realise how far I'd walked.

外国(がいこく)で生活(せいかつ)してみて初(はじ)めて、自分(じぶん)の国(くに)のことを何(なに)も知(し)らなかったのだと気(き)づかされた。
Living abroad was what made me realise I knew nothing about my own country.

親(おや)になって初(はじ)めて、父(ちち)がどうしてあんなに厳(きび)しかったのか理解(りかい)できるようになった。
It was only once I became a parent that I could understand why my father was so strict.

～て初めて · Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral in form - the ending decides politeness - but it is tonally marked. It draws a lesson, so it turns up in essays, in speeches, in the reflective part of a conversation, and in the kind of sentence that gets quoted. Using it for something trivial is faintly comic: 冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)を開(あ)けて初(はじ)めて、牛乳(ぎゅうにゅう)がないことに気(き)づいた is grammatical but sounds as though you are narrating your own life as a novel.

Three companions show up constantly. てみて in front, because trying something is the classic route to a discovery. のだ/んです at the end, because the sentence is explaining a state of affairs rather than reporting an event. And 本当(ほんとう)の or 本当(ほんとう)に inside the main clause - 本当(ほんとう)の意味(いみ)が分(わ)かった - because the whole point is that a shallower version of the understanding existed before.

～て初めて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Written Japanese sometimes reverses the halves for effect: 人(ひと)は気(き)づくのだ、失(うしな)って初(はじ)めて。 That is a stylistic inversion, not a second pattern, and you will not need to produce it.

～て初めて · ～て初(はじ)めて vs ～てから

Rule of thumb - てから tells you the order. て初(はじ)めて adds a claim about everything before that point: until then, it had not happened and could not have.

大学(だいがく)を出(で)てから、一人(ひとり)で住(す)み始(はじ)めました。
I started living alone after I finished university.

That is a timeline. Now the same shape with the "not until" claim added:

大学(だいがく)を出(で)て初(はじ)めて、親(おや)に感謝(かんしゃ)する気持(きも)ちが生(う)まれた。
It was leaving university that first gave me any sense of gratitude towards my parents.

The practical test: can you add "and not before"? If yes, て初(はじ)めて. If the sentence is just two things in order - and especially if the second one is planned - it is てから.

～て初めて · ～て初(はじ)めて vs 初(はじ)めて (the adverb)

Rule of thumb - 初(はじ)めて standing in front of a verb means "for the first time" and describes the verb. て初(はじ)めて sits between two clauses and says the first one unlocked the second.

去年(きょねん)初(はじ)めて京都(きょうと)へ行(い)きました。
I went to Kyoto for the first time last year.

京都(きょうと)へ行(い)って初(はじ)めて、日本(にほん)の庭(にわ)の美(うつく)しさが分(わ)かりました。
It was going to Kyoto that showed me how beautiful Japanese gardens are.

Both sentences contain the same three words; only the position of 初(はじ)めて differs, and it changes what is being claimed. In the first, the trip was a first. In the second, the trip is the cause and the understanding is the first.

～て初めて · ～て初(はじ)めて vs ～ないと分(わ)からない

Rule of thumb - て初(はじ)めて reports something that actually happened. ないと／なければ makes a hypothetical claim about what would be true in general.

やってみないと、向(む)いているかどうか分(わ)かりませんよ。
You won't know if it suits you unless you give it a go.

That is advice, aimed at the future, and nothing has been experienced yet. Compare the report of an experience already had:

やってみて初(はじ)めて、自分(じぶん)に向(む)いていないと分(わ)かりました。
Only after trying it did I work out that it wasn't for me.

Because of that difference, て初(はじ)めて almost never appears with a future-tense main clause, while ないと分(わ)からない almost always does.

～て初めて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 読(よ)んだ初(はじ)めて、作者(さくしゃ)の気持(きも)ちが分(わ)かった。 → ✅ 読(よ)んで初(はじ)めて、作者(さくしゃ)の気持(きも)ちが分(わ)かった。
    The verb takes the て-form, not た. The past meaning is already carried by the main clause.

  • ❌ 日本(にほん)に初(はじ)めて来(き)て、文化(ぶんか)の違(ちが)いが分(わ)かった。 → ✅ 日本(にほん)に来(き)て初(はじ)めて、文化(ぶんか)の違(ちが)いが分(わ)かった。
    Put 初(はじ)めて before the verb and you have said "I came to Japan for the first time"; the "not until" reading is gone.

  • ❌ 卒業(そつぎょう)して初(はじ)めて、働(はたら)こうと思(おも)います。 → ✅ 卒業(そつぎょう)してから、働(はたら)こうと思(おも)います。
    The main clause is a plan, and this pattern needs a realisation. A plan takes てから.

～て初めて · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 話(はな)して初(はじ)めて、彼(かれ)に電話(でんわ)してください。 → ✅ 話(はな)してから、彼(かれ)に電話(でんわ)してください。
    Requests and instructions are impossible here for the same reason: you cannot ask somebody to have a realisation.

～て初めて · Practice 1

1. 自分(じぶん)で料理(りょうり)を作(つく)っ____、母(はは)の大変(たいへん)さが分(わ)かった。

自分(じぶん)で料理(りょうり)を作(つく)って初(はじ)めて、母(はは)の大変(たいへん)さが分(わ)かった。

The second clause is a realisation, and the first is what triggered it - the signature shape of this pattern. The verb sits in the て-form.

～て初めて · Practice 2

2. Choose: 入院(にゅういん)し（て初(はじ)めて・てから）、家族(かぞく)のありがたさが分(わ)かった。

入院(にゅういん)して初(はじ)めて、家族(かぞく)のありがたさが分(わ)かった。

てから would say only that the gratitude came later in time. て初(はじ)めて adds the point of the sentence: before the hospital stay, the speaker had never felt it at all.

～て初めて · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "It wasn't until I lost my phone that I realised how much I depended on it."

携帯(けいたい)をなくして初(はじ)めて、どれだけそれに頼(たよ)っていたかが分(わ)かった。

"It wasn't until X" maps almost mechanically onto Xて初(はじ)めて, and the realisation verb goes in the main clause in the past tense.

～て初めて · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 初(はじ)めて海外(かいがい)で働(はたら)いて、言葉(ことば)の壁(かべ)の高(たか)さを知(し)りました。（intended: "only after working abroad did I learn how high the language barrier is"）

✅ 海外(かいがい)で働(はたら)いて初(はじ)めて、言葉(ことば)の壁(かべ)の高(たか)さを知(し)りました。

As written, the sentence says the speaker worked abroad for the first time and then learned something. Moving 初(はじ)めて behind the て-form restores the intended "not until" meaning.

～て初めて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 大(おお)ぜいの前(まえ)で話(はな)して初(はじ)めて、自分(じぶん)の発音(はつおん)の問題(もんだい)に気(き)づいた。 (b) 社会(しゃかい)に出(で)てから、自分(じぶん)の発音(はつおん)の問題(もんだい)に気(き)づいた。

(a) makes that one occasion of speaking in front of a crowd the trigger: nothing before it had revealed the problem, and the discovery is presented as sudden and slightly humbling.

(b) simply places the discovery somewhere after the speaker entered working life. It could have taken years, it could have come from anywhere, and no single event is being credited.

～てからでないと

Meaning · unless X is done first, Y is impossible - X is a precondition, not just an earlier step

てからでないと sets a gate. It names something that has to be finished before the rest becomes possible, and then says what cannot happen until it is. The second half of the sentence is always negative in meaning; that is the single fact that makes this pattern behave differently from every other "after".

～てからでないと · Formation

Take the て-form, add からでないと, and finish the sentence with something that cannot happen. Several endings are interchangeable and differ only in register.

～てからでないと · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (て-form) て-form + からでないと 調(しら)べる → 調(しら)べてからでないと
Verb (て-form), formal て-form + からでなければ 確認(かくにん)する → 確認(かくにん)してからでなければ
Verb (て-form), stiff/written て-form + からでなくては 届(とど)く → 届(とど)いてからでなくては
Verb (て-form), casual て-form + からじゃないと 食(た)べる → 食(た)べてからじゃないと
Main clause must be negative or "impossible" …答(こた)えられません／無理(むり)です／だめです
❌ Dictionary form ❌調(しら)べるからでないと ✅調(しら)べてからでないと
❌ Positive main clause ❌…てからでないと、できます ✅…てからでないと、できません

～てからでないと · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - write the ending first. If you cannot finish the sentence with できない、無理(むり)だ、いけない、わからない or another negative, you do not want this pattern; you want plain てから.

～てからでないと · Examples

手(て)を洗(あら)ってからでないと、食(た)べてはいけません。
You mustn't eat until you've washed your hands.

予約(よやく)してからでないと、入(はい)れませんよ。
You can't get in unless you book first.

説明書(せつめいしょ)を読(よ)んでからでないと、この機械(きかい)は使(つか)えない。
You can't use this machine without reading the manual first.

部長(ぶちょう)に聞(き)いてからでないと、お返事(へんじ)できません。
I can't give you an answer until I've asked the manager.

値段(ねだん)を調(しら)べてからじゃないと、注文(ちゅうもん)できないな。
I can't order it till I've checked the price.

～てからでないと · Examples (cont.)

準備(じゅんび)運動(うんどう)をしてからでないと、プールに入(はい)ってはいけないことになっている。
The rule is that you may not enter the pool without doing warm-up exercises first.

契約(けいやく)の内容(ないよう)をすべて確認(かくにん)してからでなければ、サインすることはできません。
I cannot sign until I have checked every part of the contract.

子(こ)どもが寝(ね)てからでないと、自分(じぶん)の時間(じかん)なんてまったく取(と)れないんです。
Honestly, I get no time to myself at all until the children are asleep.

～てからでないと · Nuance & register

The pattern is long, and that length is part of its job: it is a polite way of saying no. A shop assistant, a receptionist or a colleague who cannot commit reaches for it constantly, because it refuses while pointing at the step that would unblock things - 上司(じょうし)に確認(かくにん)してからでないと、お約束(やくそく)できかねます. Learners who only know できません sound much blunter than they intend.

The four endings form a rough ladder. からじゃないと is casual speech and very common among friends; てからでないと is the neutral all-purpose form; からでなければ and からでなくては lean formal and written, the sort of thing you meet in rules, contracts and announcements. Nothing else changes.

～てからでないと · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - do not confuse this with ～てからでは, which looks almost identical and means the opposite kind of thing: "once X has happened, it's too late." 事故(じこ)が起(お)きてからでは遅(おそ)い。 There, X is something you want to avoid; in てからでないと, X is something you want done.

Because the main clause is negative, the pattern often ends up describing rules, and it is very common in the passive-ish ～ことになっている frame that states how things are done at a company, a school or a pool.

～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～てから

Rule of thumb - てから puts two events in order. てからでないと claims the second one is blocked until the first is finished.

シャワーを浴(あ)びてから、出(で)かけます。
I'll have a shower and then go out.

That is a plan, and nothing is impossible about going out first. Now a genuine gate:

チケットを買(か)ってからでないと、中(なか)に入(はい)れません。
You can't go inside until you've bought a ticket.

The two are not interchangeable in either direction. Say ❌チケットを買(か)ってからでないと、中(なか)に入(はい)れます and the sentence falls apart, because the pattern demands a negative. Say 買(か)ってから入(はい)ります and you have merely described your afternoon.

～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～ないと

Rule of thumb - ないと negates the action: if you don't do X, no Y. てからでないと negates the timing: X has to be finished before Y is even possible.

めがねをかけないと、字(じ)が読(よ)めません。
I can't read the writing without my glasses.

Nothing here is about completion - the glasses are a condition that holds throughout. Compare a case where finishing is the whole point:

試験(しけん)の結果(けっか)が出(で)てからでないと、次(つぎ)の予定(よてい)が立(た)てられない。
I can't make any further plans until the exam results are out.

～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～ないと (cont.)

In practice, when either would work, てからでないと sounds more considered and more formal, and it stresses order; ないと is shorter and states a bare condition. A useful test: if you can insert "first" into the English, the longer pattern is available.

～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～た上(うえ)で

Rule of thumb - both are formal and both put a considered step first, but た上(うえ)で leads to a decision that will happen, while てからでないと leads to a refusal.

見積(みつ)もりを確認(かくにん)した上(うえ)で、ご連絡(れんらく)いたします。
I'll be in touch once I've gone over the quotation.

見積(みつ)もりを確認(かくにん)してからでないと、ご連絡(れんらく)いたしかねます。
I'm afraid I can't get back to you until I've gone over the quotation.

Same first step, opposite framing. The first sentence promises; the second declines for now. In a business email that difference is very visible, so choose by what you want the reader to take away.

～てからでないと · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 予約(よやく)してからでないと、入(はい)れます。 → ✅ 予約(よやく)してからでないと、入(はい)れません。
    The main clause must be negative. With a positive ending, the sentence contradicts itself.

  • ❌ 注文(ちゅうもん)するからでないと、作(つく)れません。 → ✅ 注文(ちゅうもん)してからでないと、作(つく)れません。
    It is built on the て-form. The dictionary form cannot take からでないと.

  • ❌ 病気(びょうき)になってからでないと遅(おそ)いですよ。 → ✅ 病気(びょうき)になってからでは遅(おそ)いですよ。
    "Once you're ill it's too late" is the てからでは pattern. でないと would make falling ill a precondition for something, which is not the intent.

  • ❌ 春(はる)になってからでないと、桜(さくら)が咲(さ)きます。 → ✅ 春(はる)にならないと、桜(さくら)は咲(さ)きません。
    Two faults: the ending has to be negative, and a natural process that nobody performs sits better with plain ないと than with the deliberate, step-completing てからでないと.

～てからでないと · Practice 1

1. パスポートを見(み)せ____、受(う)け取(と)れません。

Showing the passport is the step that unblocks collection, and the ending is already negative, which is exactly the environment this pattern needs.

～てからでないと · Practice 2

2. Choose: 宿題(しゅくだい)をし（てから・てからでないと）、テレビを見(み)てはいけません。

The ending 見(み)てはいけません is a prohibition, so the sentence is setting a condition rather than describing an order of events. Plain てから with a prohibition would be odd, since it would forbid the very sequence it just described.

～てからでないと · Practice 3

"Until I've…, I can't…" is the pattern's English signature: a completed first step plus an impossibility. The casual version would be 話(はな)してからじゃないと決(き)められない.

The main clause was positive. Turning 書(か)ける into 書(か)けない restores the required negative, and moving は onto the report makes the contrast sit naturally.

～てからでないと · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 社長(しゃちょう)に相談(そうだん)してからでないと、お答(こた)えできません。 (b) 社長(しゃちょう)に相談(そうだん)してから、お答(こた)えします。

(a) is a refusal, softened by naming the obstacle. Right now the speaker will not answer, and the sentence puts the reason in the listener's hands: nothing moves until the president has been consulted.

(b) is a promise. The speaker commits to answering and merely tells you what has to happen first, so the listener leaves the conversation expecting a reply.

～ついでに

Meaning · while you're doing X anyway, do Y as well - Y gets a free ride

ついでに is the grammar of efficiency. X is the real reason you are going, doing or moving; Y is a smaller job you slip in because the opportunity is already there. If Y is the reason and X is the excuse, you have the two the wrong way round.

～ついでに · Formation

ついで is a noun - "an occasion that happens to arise" - so a clause modifies it in the plain form, and a noun attaches with の.

～ついでに · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary) - not yet done dictionary + ついでに 出(で)かける → 出(で)かけるついでに
Verb (た-form) - already under way た-form + ついでに 行(い)く → 行(い)ったついでに
Action noun Noun + の + ついでに 散歩(さんぽ) → 散歩(さんぽ)のついでに
On its own, at the head of a sentence ついでに、… ついでに、これもお願(ねが)い
As a plain noun ついでが+ある／ついでで ついでがあったら寄(よ)ります
❌ の after a verb ❌行(い)くのついでに ✅行(い)くついでに
❌ Y bigger than X ❌切手(きって)を買(か)うついでに引(ひ)っ越(こ)した ✅引(ひ)っ越(こ)すついでに切手(きって)を買(か)った

～ついでに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ついでに needs a main errand and a minor extra, in that order, and they must share one outing, one trip or one stretch of activity. The dictionary form means the errand is still ahead of you; the た-form means you are already out.

～ついでに · Examples

買(か)い物(もの)のついでに、郵便局(ゆうびんきょく)にも寄(よ)った。
While I was out shopping I stopped by the post office too.

コンビニに行(い)くついでに、飲(の)み物(もの)を買(か)ってきて。
Grab me a drink while you're at the convenience store.

散歩(さんぽ)のついでに、公園(こうえん)のごみを拾(ひろ)うようにしている。
I make a point of picking up litter in the park while I'm out walking.

窓(まど)をふくついでに、カーテンも洗(あら)ってしまおう。
Since I'm cleaning the windows anyway, I may as well wash the curtains.

大阪(おおさか)に出張(しゅっちょう)したついでに、昔(むかし)の友(とも)だちに会(あ)ってきた。
I was in Osaka on business, so I met up with an old friend while I was there.

～ついでに · Examples (cont.)

病院(びょういん)に行(い)ったついでに、薬局(やっきょく)で風邪薬(かぜぐすり)も買(か)っておいた。
I picked up some cold medicine at the pharmacy while I was at the hospital anyway.

会議(かいぎ)で東京(とうきょう)まで来(き)たついでに、前(まえ)から気(き)になっていた美術館(びじゅつかん)をのぞいてきました。
Since the meeting brought me all the way to Tokyo, I looked in on a museum I'd been curious about.

駅(えき)まで送(おく)っていくついでに、この荷物(にもつ)を宅配便(たくはいびん)で出(だ)してきてもらえると助(たす)かります。
It'd be a great help if you could drop this parcel off at the courier while you're running me to the station.

～ついでに · Nuance & register

ついでに is register-neutral and enormously common in everyday speech, but it carries a social charge that learners should feel. Attached to a request, it shrinks the favour: コンビニに行(い)くついでに means "this costs you nothing extra", and that is why it makes an imposition easy to accept. Attached to your own offer, it does the same work in reverse - 「ついでですから、気(き)にしないでください」 is the standard way to wave off thanks for a lift or a delivery.

The flip side is that calling something a ついで demotes it. Say 結婚式(けっこんしき)のついでに and you have announced that the wedding was a convenient excuse. Around anything the other person takes seriously - a funeral, a hospital visit, a formal invitation - reach for a neutral phrase such as 帰(かえ)りに or 行(い)った時(とき)に instead. Japanese speakers also avoid ついでに when talking about something they did for a superior, since it implies the superior's business was the side job.

～ついでに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The choice between dictionary and た-form is aspect, not politeness. The dictionary form plans: 買(か)い物(もの)に行(い)くついでに寄(よ)ろう. The た-form reports from inside the trip: 買(か)い物(もの)に行(い)ったついでに寄(よ)った. Both are common, and native speakers slide between them without ceremony.

～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～ながら

Rule of thumb - ながら means the two actions happen at the same time, both by the same person. ついでに means one occasion covers two separate jobs; they can be minutes apart.

音楽(おんがく)を聞(き)きながら皿(さら)を洗(あら)っています。
I'm doing the washing-up while listening to music.

You genuinely do both at once, so this cannot be ついでに. Now something you cannot do simultaneously:

ゴミを出(だ)しに行(い)くついでに、ポストに手紙(てがみ)を入(い)れてきた。
I put the letter in the postbox while I was out taking the rubbish down.

Note also which clause is the main one. With ながら the main action is the second clause; with ついでに the main errand is the first. Swap them and you change what the sentence is about.

～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～途中(とちゅう)で

Rule of thumb - 途中(とちゅう)で is about position on a route: somewhere between start and finish. ついでに is about taking advantage of an occasion, and the extra job does not have to lie on the way.

帰(かえ)る途中(とちゅう)で本屋(ほんや)に寄(よ)りました。
I dropped into a bookshop on the way home.

実家(じっか)に帰(かえ)るついでに、祖母(そぼ)の家(いえ)にも顔(かお)を出(だ)した。
Since I was going back to my parents' anyway, I looked in on my grandmother too.

The grandmother's house may be a detour of an hour; ついでに does not care, as long as the one trip made it worth doing. 途中(とちゅう)で would be false if she lived in the opposite direction. Conversely, 帰(かえ)る途中(とちゅう)で雨(あめ)が降(ふ)ってきた is fine and ついでに is impossible there, because rain is not an errand you slip in.

～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～際(さい)に

Rule of thumb - 際(さい)に is a formal "on the occasion of" and says nothing about efficiency. ついでに always implies the second job was tacked on to save a trip - which is why it is out of place in a notice or a polite request to a customer.

ご来店(らいてん)の際(さい)に、こちらのカードをお見(み)せください。
Please show this card when you visit the shop.

Replacing that with ご来店(らいてん)のついでに would tell customers their visit is a side errand - polite in form, insulting in content. Among friends the same information is perfectly friendly:

店(みせ)に行(い)くついでに、このカード見(み)せてきてよ。
Show them this card while you're at the shop.

～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～際(さい)に (cont.)

So the split is two-way: register (formal versus everyday) and meaning (occasion versus opportunity taken).

～ついでに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 散歩(さんぽ)するのついでに、パンを買(か)った。 → ✅ 散歩(さんぽ)するついでに、パンを買(か)った。
    A verb modifies ついで directly, with no の. The の appears only after a noun: 散歩(さんぽ)のついでに.

  • ❌ 切手(きって)を買(か)うついでに、引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの手続(てつづ)きをした。 → ✅ 引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの手続(てつづ)きのついでに、切手(きって)を買(か)った。
    The bigger errand has to come first. As written, the sentence claims a house move was a minor extra attached to buying stamps.

  • ❌ お見舞(みま)いのついでに、買(か)い物(もの)をしてきました。 → ✅ お見舞(みま)いの帰(かえ)りに、買(か)い物(もの)をしてきました。
    Grammatically fine, socially cold: it says visiting the patient was the convenient excuse. Use 帰(かえ)りに when the first event matters to someone.

～ついでに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 勉強(べんきょう)するついでに音楽(おんがく)を聞(き)いています。 → ✅ 勉強(べんきょう)しながら音楽(おんがく)を聞(き)いています。
    Two activities that overlap continuously need ながら. ついでに wants two distinct jobs sharing one occasion.

～際に

Meaning · at the time of; on the occasion of - the formal word for "when"

際(さい)に (read さいに) does the job of 時(とき)に, but it belongs to a different world: notices, manuals, contracts, announcements and careful business speech. It also asks for a real occasion - something that takes place - rather than any old state of affairs.

～際に · Formation

際(さい) is a noun, so the clause in front of it takes a plain form, and a noun attaches with の.

～際に · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary) dictionary + 際(さい)に 利用(りよう)する → 利用(りよう)する際(さい)に
Verb (た-form) た-form + 際(さい)に 申(もう)し込(こ)む → 申(もう)し込(こ)んだ際(さい)に
Event noun Noun + の + 際(さい)に 入学(にゅうがく) → 入学(にゅうがく)の際(さい)に
With は - "in the event of" ～際(さい)は お帰(かえ)りの際(さい)は
Before a noun ～際(さい)の + Noun 使用(しよう)の際(さい)の注意(ちゅうい)
に dropped, notice style ～際(さい)、… ご入場(にゅうじょう)の際(さい)、…
❌ い-adjective ❌忙(いそが)しい際(さい)に ✅忙(いそが)しい時(とき)に
❌ Ongoing state ❌子(こ)どもの際(さい)に ✅子(こ)どもの時(とき)に

～際に · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - two tests. Is the sentence formal enough that 時(とき) would sound too casual? And is the clause an event rather than a state? Both must be yes. Adjectives never pass the second test.

～際に · Examples

ご利用(りよう)の際(さい)は、こちらのボタンをお押(お)しください。
Please press this button when using the machine.

お降(お)りの際(さい)は、足元(あしもと)にご注意(ちゅうい)ください。
Please watch your step as you get off.

申(もう)し込(こ)みの際(さい)に、身分証明書(みぶんしょうめいしょ)が必要(ひつよう)です。
You will need identification when you apply.

面接(めんせつ)を受(う)けた際(さい)に、給料(きゅうりょう)のことも聞(き)いておきました。
I asked about the salary as well when I had the interview.

海外(かいがい)へ出張(しゅっちょう)する際(さい)には、保険(ほけん)に入(はい)っておいたほうがいい。
When you travel abroad on business, you'd do well to take out insurance.

～際に · Examples (cont.)

非常(ひじょう)の際(さい)は、エレベーターを使(つか)わずに階段(かいだん)をご利用(りよう)ください。
In an emergency, please use the stairs rather than the lift.

契約(けいやく)を更新(こうしん)する際(さい)には、三(さん)か月(げつ)前(まえ)までにご連絡(れんらく)いただく必要(ひつよう)がございます。
If you wish to renew the contract, you must notify us at least three months in advance.

退職(たいしょく)の際(さい)にお世話(せわ)になった方々(かたがた)に、一人(ひとり)ずつ手紙(てがみ)を書(か)きました。
I wrote individually to everyone who helped me when I left the company.

～際に · Nuance & register

This is a formal word and it never stops being one. Put it in a conversation about breakfast and it sounds like a train announcement. The natural homes for it are: station and shop announcements, instruction manuals, application forms, company rules, and speech where you are addressing a customer or a superior.

It pairs constantly with honorific prefixes - ご利用(りよう)の際(さい)、 お帰(かえ)りの際(さい)、ご来館(らいかん)の際(さい) - because the same register that wants 際(さい) also wants お／ご. If you are writing a notice for customers, that combination is the default.

The particle after it shifts the feel slightly. 際(さい)に points at a moment. 際(さい)は sets up a condition and is the form you see in warnings - "should this happen, do that". 際(さい)には is both at once and is very common in careful writing. In printed notices the particle often disappears altogether, leaving a comma: ご記入(きにゅう)の際(さい)、 黒(くろ)のペンをお使(つか)いください。

～際に · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - a close relative, ～に際(さい)して, means "on the occasion of" in an even weightier way and belongs to N2: 開会(かいかい)に際(さい)して一言(ひとこと)ご挨拶(あいさつ)申(もう)し上(あ)げます。 It marks ceremonies and turning points, not everyday procedures.

～際に · ～際(さい)に vs ～時(とき)に

Rule of thumb - 時(とき) takes anything: verbs, adjectives, nouns, states, trivia. 際(さい) takes only events, and only in formal contexts. Everywhere 際(さい) works, 時(とき) also works; the reverse fails constantly.

小(ちい)さい時(とき)、よく祖父(そふ)の家(いえ)に泊(と)まった。
When I was small I often stayed at my grandfather's house.

An adjective and a childhood state - 際(さい) is impossible here on two counts. Compare a procedure described to a customer:

商品(しょうひん)を返品(へんぴん)される際(さい)は、レシートをお持(も)ちください。
When returning an item, please bring your receipt.

～際に · ～際(さい)に vs ～時(とき)に (cont.)

There 時(とき) would be grammatical but would flatten the register; the notice would read as if a friend had scribbled it. The tense rule you learned for 時(とき) carries over unchanged: dictionary form for "before/as you do it", た-form for "after you have done it".

～際に · ～際(さい)に vs ～場合(ばあい)は

Rule of thumb - 際(さい) assumes the event will happen and tells you what to do at that moment. 場合(ばあい) says "if it should turn out that…" and branches between possibilities.

雨(あめ)の場合(ばあい)は、体育館(たいいくかん)で行(おこな)います。
If it rains, it will be held in the gym.

Rain may or may not come, and the sentence exists to cover the branch - that is 場合(ばあい), and 際(さい) would be wrong because rain is not an occasion anyone attends. Now something that is definitely going to happen:

ご入場(にゅうじょう)の際(さい)に、チケットを拝見(はいけん)いたします。
We will check your ticket as you enter.

～際に · ～際(さい)に vs ～場合(ばあい)は (cont.)

Everyone entering will enter, so there is no branch to state. A quick diagnostic: if the English wants "if", think 場合(ばあい); if it wants "when", think 際(さい). Both are formal, so register does not separate them.

～際に · ～際(さい)に vs ～た上(うえ)で

Rule of thumb - both live in the same formal register, but 際(さい)に locates something at the time of an event, while た上(うえ)で puts it after the event and makes it depend on the result.

お申(もう)し込(こ)みの際(さい)に、ご希望(きぼう)の日(ひ)をお書(か)きください。
Please write your preferred date when you apply.

お申(もう)し込(こ)み内容(ないよう)を確認(かくにん)した上(うえ)で、ご連絡(れんらく)いたします。
We will contact you once we have checked the details of your application.

In the first, the writing happens during the application. In the second, the contact happens afterwards because of what the check revealed. Swapping them produces sentences that are still grammatical but say something the writer did not mean, which is why forms and letters keep the two carefully apart.

～際に · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 学生(がくせい)の際(さい)に、アルバイトをたくさんしました。 → ✅ 学生(がくせい)の時(とき)に、アルバイトをたくさんしました。
    "Being a student" is a state that lasted for years, not an occasion. 際(さい) needs a bounded event.

  • ❌ 眠(ねむ)い際(さい)には、コーヒーを飲(の)みます。 → ✅ 眠(ねむ)い時(とき)には、コーヒーを飲(の)みます。
    Adjectives cannot modify 際(さい) at all. This is the most reliable formal test of the two words.

  • ❌ ゲームをする際(さい)に、いつも夜更(よふ)かししてしまう。 → ✅ ゲームをする時(とき)に、いつも夜更(よふ)かししてしまう。
    Grammatical, but the register clashes: a casual admission in the vocabulary of a safety notice. Keep 際(さい) for formal writing and speech.

～際に · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 台風(たいふう)が来(く)る際(さい)は、授業(じゅぎょう)は休(やす)みになります。 → ✅ 台風(たいふう)が来(く)た場合(ばあい)は、授業(じゅぎょう)は休(やす)みになります。
    The sentence sets out a contingency, so it needs 場合(ばあい). 際(さい) would presuppose that the typhoon is coming.

An event noun with an honorific prefix, addressed to a customer - the natural home of 際(さい). は suits it because the sentence states a standing policy rather than one moment.

～たびに

Meaning · every time X happens, Y happens - without exception, as far as the speaker has seen

たびに counts occasions, not intervals. Each time the first event comes round, the second one comes with it. It is the pattern you reach for when a pairing has become reliable enough to comment on: every time I see him, every time it rains, every time that song comes on.

～たびに · Formation

たび is the noun 度(たび), "an occurrence", so a clause modifies it in the plain form and a noun attaches with の. The verb is in the dictionary form no matter when the events happened.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary) dictionary + たびに 会(あ)う → 会(あ)うたびに
Action noun Noun + の + たびに 試験(しけん) → 試験(しけん)のたびに
Written style ～度(たび)に 見(み)る度(たび)に
As a connector そのたびに …。そのたびに注意(ちゅうい)している
❌ た-form ❌会(あ)ったたびに ✅会(あ)うたびに
❌ Noun without の ❌試験(しけん)たびに ✅試験(しけん)のたびに
❌ A measured interval ❌三日(みっか)たびに ✅三日(みっか)ごとに

～たびに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - if you can put "every time" in front of an English clause, you want たびに. If what follows "every" is a number plus a unit - every three days, every two rows - you want ごとに instead.

～たびに · Examples

彼(かれ)に会(あ)うたびに、元気(げんき)をもらう。
Every time I see him I come away with more energy.

この歌(うた)を聞(き)くたびに、高校(こうこう)時代(じだい)を思(おも)い出(だ)す。
Every time I hear this song I think of my high-school days.

旅行(りょこう)のたびに、同(おな)じかばんを使(つか)っています。
I use the same bag every time I travel.

台風(たいふう)が来(く)るたびに、この辺(へん)は停電(ていでん)する。
Every time a typhoon comes through, this area loses power.

甥(おい)は会(あ)うたびに背(せ)が伸(の)びていて、おどろかされる。
My nephew is taller every time I see him - it's startling.

～たびに · Examples (cont.)

引(ひ)っ越(こ)しのたびに、本(ほん)を何(なん)箱(ばこ)も捨(す)てることになる。
Every move ends with me throwing out several boxes of books.

祖母(そぼ)は私(わたし)が訪(たず)ねるたびに、冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)の中(なか)のものを全部(ぜんぶ)持(も)たせようとする。
Every time I visit, my grandmother tries to send me home with the entire contents of her fridge.

担当者(たんとうしゃ)が変(か)わるたびに同(おな)じ説明(せつめい)をやり直(なお)さなければならず、正直(しょうじき)うんざりしています。
Having to start the whole explanation again every time they change our contact person is, frankly, wearing me down.

～たびに · Nuance & register

たびに is register-neutral: it appears in casual speech, in polite speech and in writing without changing shape, and the kanji spelling 度(たび) is simply a written-style choice. What it does carry is a mild emotional charge. The speaker has noticed the pattern and usually has a feeling about it - affection, irritation, nostalgia, surprise. Sentences with たびに very often end in 思(おも)い出(だ)す, うれしくなる, 困(こま)る or a passive of surprise.

That is also why it loves change over time. With a comparative or a verb of growth, たびに produces the "more and more each time" reading: 読(よ)むたびに新(あたら)しい発見(はっけん)がある。 Nothing extra is added to the grammar; the repetition does the work.

そのたびに is worth learning as a fixed connector for narrating: you describe a recurring situation in one sentence, then open the next with そのたびに - "and each time that happened…". It saves you repeating the whole clause.

～たびに · Nuance & register (cont.)

One limit: because the pattern generalises, it does not fit a single future occasion. 来週(らいしゅう)会(あ)うたびに makes no sense, because you are meeting once.

～たびに · ～たびに vs ～ごとに

Rule of thumb - たびに counts events; ごとに counts units. If a number and a counter come before it, use ごとに; if a clause comes before it, use たびに.

バスは十五分(じゅうごふん)ごとに出(で)ています。
The buses run every fifteen minutes.

You cannot say ❌十五分(じゅうごふん)たびに, because fifteen minutes is a measured interval rather than an occasion. The other direction fails just as hard:

母(はは)は電話(でんわ)するたびに、同(おな)じ話(はなし)をする。
Every time I ring, my mother tells me the same story.

～たびに · ～たびに vs ～ごとに (cont.)

Here the phone calls happen when they happen - there is no fixed interval - and ごとに would be wrong. There is a small overlap with nouns: 試合(しあい)のたびに and 試合(しあい)ごとに are both possible, but the first means "each time there is a match" and the second is closer to "match by match", tracking a change across a series.

～たびに · ～たびに vs ～と

Rule of thumb - と states an automatic consequence: this causes that, every time, as a matter of how the world works. たびに merely reports that the two keep coming together, and it can carry the speaker's reaction.

春(はる)になると、この川(かわ)の水(みず)が増(ふ)えます。
When spring comes, the water in this river rises.

That is nature, not commentary. Compare an observation with a person in it:

父(ちち)は酒(さけ)を飲(の)むたびに、若(わか)いころの話(はなし)を始(はじ)める。
Every time my father drinks, he starts on stories about his youth.

～たびに · ～たびに vs ～と (cont.)

と would also be grammatical there, but it would present the storytelling as a mechanical reflex. たびに keeps the speaker in the sentence - you can hear the raised eyebrow. Conversely, と accepts states and conditions (安(やす)いと売(う)れる) where たびに needs a repeatable event.

～たびに · ～たびに vs ～時(とき)に

Rule of thumb - 時(とき)に picks out one occasion, or is neutral about how many. たびに insists on all of them.

京都(きょうと)へ行(い)った時(とき)に、この茶碗(ちゃわん)を買(か)いました。
I bought this teacup when I went to Kyoto.

One trip, one purchase. Now the generalising version:

京都(きょうと)へ行(い)くたびに、同(おな)じ店(みせ)で器(うつわ)を買(か)ってしまう。
Every time I go to Kyoto I end up buying pottery at the same shop.

The tense rule also differs. 時(とき) chooses between dictionary and た-form to show whether the main clause happens before or after it; たびに has no such choice, because it is always the dictionary form.

～たびに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 日本(にほん)に行(い)ったたびに、温泉(おんせん)に入(はい)ります。 → ✅ 日本(にほん)に行(い)くたびに、温泉(おんせん)に入(はい)ります。
    たびに always follows the dictionary form. The past reading, if any, comes from the main clause.

  • ❌ 会議(かいぎ)たびに、資料(しりょう)を作(つく)り直(なお)す。 → ✅ 会議(かいぎ)のたびに、資料(しりょう)を作(つく)り直(なお)す。
    たび is a noun, so another noun has to be linked with の.

  • ❌ 二年(にねん)たびに、車(くるま)を買(か)い替(か)えている。 → ✅ 二年(にねん)ごとに、車(くるま)を買(か)い替(か)えている。
    A number plus a unit of time is an interval, and intervals take ごとに or おきに, never たびに.

  • ❌ 明日(あした)先生(せんせい)に会(あ)うたびに、この話(はなし)をします。 → ✅ 明日(あした)先生(せんせい)に会(あ)った時(とき)に、この話(はなし)をします。
    One scheduled meeting is not a repeated occasion, so there is nothing for たびに to generalise over.

～ごとに

Meaning · each; every; at intervals of - one unit at a time, with none skipped

ごとに divides something into equal units and says that whatever follows applies to every one of them. The units can be measured - three days, five metres - or they can be categories: each country, each department, each class. Either way, nothing is left out and nothing is skipped over.

～ごとに · Formation

ごと is a suffix. It attaches straight to a quantity or to a noun, with no particle in between.

～ごとに · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Number + counter (time) Quantity + ごとに 三日(みっか) → 三日(みっか)ごとに
Number + counter (distance, things) Quantity + ごとに 五(ご)メートル → 五(ご)メートルごとに
Noun as a category Noun + ごとに 国(くに) → 国(くに)ごとに
Verb (dictionary), literary dictionary + ごとに 年(とし)を取(と)る → 年(とし)を取(と)るごとに
Before a noun ～ごとの + Noun 部署(ぶしょ)ごとの予算(よさん)
❌ の between noun and ごと ❌国(くに)のごとに ✅国(くに)ごとに
❌ な before a noun ❌一年(いちねん)ごとな検査(けんさ) ✅一年(いちねん)ごとの検査(けんさ)
❌ Doubling with 毎(まい)～ ❌毎日(まいにち)ごとに ✅毎日(まいにち) / 一日(いちにち)ごとに

Rule of thumb - ごとに states the length of the cycle, counting the unit itself. 一日(いちにち)ごとに is therefore every day, and 二日(ふつか)ごとに is every second day. Do not translate ごと as "other".

～ごとに · Examples

水(みず)は二日(ふつか)ごとにやってください。
Please water it every two days.

国(くに)ごとに習慣(しゅうかん)が違(ちが)います。
Customs differ from country to country.

クラスごとに写真(しゃしん)を撮(と)ります。
We'll take the photographs class by class.

この検査(けんさ)は半年(はんとし)ごとに受(う)けることになっている。
You're supposed to have this check-up every six months.

道(みち)の両側(りょうがわ)に、五(ご)メートルごとに木(き)が植(う)えてある。
Trees have been planted every five metres along both sides of the road.

～ごとに · Examples (cont.)

部署(ぶしょ)ごとに予算(よさん)が決(き)められているので、勝手(かって)には使(つか)えません。
The budget is set department by department, so I can't just spend it as I like.

年(とし)を取(と)るごとに、時間(じかん)が過(す)ぎるのが速(はや)く感(かん)じられるようになった。
With each passing year, time seems to move faster.

この会社(かいしゃ)ではプロジェクトごとにチームを組(く)み直(なお)すので、同(おな)じ顔(かお)ぶれで働(はたら)くことは少(すく)ない。
This company rebuilds its teams for each project, so you rarely work with the same faces twice.

～ごとに · Nuance & register

ごとに is neutral and slightly businesslike. It is the word of timetables, manuals, survey results and internal rules - anything that has to state a rate or a breakdown precisely - and it sounds perfectly normal in speech too. It is not formal in the way 際(さい)に is; it is simply exact.

The two jobs it does feel quite different in use. With a number it gives a rate, and English usually says "every X". With a category noun it gives a breakdown, and English switches to "by" or "each": 地域(ちいき)ごとに → "region by region"; 家庭(かてい)ごとに → "in each household". That second use is extremely common in reports, almost always paired with 違(ちが)う, 異(こと)なる or 決(き)める.

The verb form - 見(み)るごとに、会(あ)うごとに - is real but literary, and in ordinary speech たびに has taken the job over. You can safely read it and avoid writing it.

～ごとに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - a different ごと exists, with no に, meaning "together with the whole of it": りんごを皮(かわ)ごと食(た)べる, "eat the apple skin and all"; 丸(まる)ごと, "the whole thing". It attaches to a noun exactly like ours, so read the sentence, not the suffix - if there is no に and no counting, it is this other word.

～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～おきに

Rule of thumb - ごとに measures the cycle; おきに measures the gap left empty between one occurrence and the next. With days and countable things the two therefore differ by one.

一日(いちにち)ごとに記録(きろく)をつけています。
I keep a record every day.

一日(いちにち)おきに記録(きろく)をつけています。
I keep a record every other day.

Same number, different arithmetic: the first is Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday; the second is Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Rewritten with ごとに, the second sentence becomes 二日(ふつか)ごとに.

～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～おきに (cont.)

With units of duration or length the two happen to coincide, because there is no "container" to leave empty: 十分(じゅっぷん)ごとに and 十分(じゅっぷん)おきに both describe something happening every ten minutes. That coincidence is exactly why learners generalise from minutes to days and get it wrong. When the unit is a day, a week, a seat or an item, stop and check which one you mean.

ごとに also has a whole second life that おきに does not: the category use. 学年(がくねん)ごとに時間割(じかんわり)が違(ちが)う is fine, and ❌学年(がくねん)おきに would mean skipping alternate year groups.

～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～たびに

Rule of thumb - ごとに follows a unit; たびに follows a clause describing an event. "Every three days" is ごとに; "every time I go" is たびに.

試験(しけん)のたびに胃(い)が痛(いた)くなる。
My stomach hurts every time there's an exam.

試験(しけん)ごとに成績(せいせき)を記録(きろく)しています。
We record the results for each exam.

Both are grammatical, and the pair shows the difference cleanly. The first counts occasions and reports the speaker's reaction to all of them. The second treats the exams as a series of units and does something per unit - a breakdown, not a reaction. That is why ごとに is at home in a spreadsheet and たびに is at home in a diary.

～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～ずつ

Rule of thumb - ずつ distributes an amount: how much each one gets. ごとに marks the unit itself: for each of these, such-and-such is true.

一人(ひとり)に二(ふた)つずつ配(くば)ってください。
Please hand out two to each person.

The number that matters there is the two. Now a sentence where the number that matters is the unit:

三人(さんにん)ごとにグループを作(つく)ってください。
Please form groups of three.

A useful diagnostic: ずつ answers "how many each?", ごとに answers "per what?". They can appear together - 一人(ひとり)ごとに一(ひと)つずつ渡(わた)す - and when they do, each is doing its own half of the job.

～ごとに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 毎週(まいしゅう)ごとに会議(かいぎ)があります。 → ✅ 毎週(まいしゅう)会議(かいぎ)があります。
    毎(まい)～ already means "every", so adding ごとに says it twice. Use one or the other.

  • ❌ 一日(いちにち)ごとに薬(くすり)を飲(の)みます。（intending every other day） → ✅ 一日(いちにち)おきに薬(くすり)を飲(の)みます。
    ごとに counts the cycle, so 一日(いちにち)ごとに is every day. "Every other" needs おきに, or 二日(ふつか)ごとに.

  • ❌ 三年(さんねん)ごとな点検(てんけん)が必要(ひつよう)です。 → ✅ 三年(さんねん)ごとの点検(てんけん)が必要(ひつよう)です。
    Before a noun the linker is の. ごと is not a な-adjective.

  • ❌ プリントを一(いち)まいごとに配(くば)ってください。 → ✅ プリントを一(いち)まいずつ配(くば)ってください。
    The sentence is about how many each person receives, which is ずつ. ごとに would make each sheet a unit that something is done per.

～から～にかけて

Meaning · from roughly A through to roughly B - one continuous stretch, with soft edges

から～にかけて draws a band across time or across a map. Something goes on right the way through it, and the two ends are deliberately approximate: not "from exactly here to exactly there", but "somewhere around here to somewhere around there". Weather forecasts and history books run on it.

～から～にかけて · Formation

Both halves take nouns. The pattern is a pair, and neither half works alone.

～から～にかけて · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Time expressions Noun + から + Noun + にかけて 夕方(ゆうがた) → 夕方(ゆうがた)から夜(よる)にかけて
Places Noun + から + Noun + にかけて 関東(かんとう) → 関東(かんとう)から東北(とうほく)にかけて
Dates, months, seasons Noun + から + Noun + にかけて 三月(さんがつ)から四月(しがつ)にかけて
Eras Noun + から + Noun + にかけて 昭和(しょうわ)から平成(へいせい)にかけて
Before a noun ～から～にかけての + Noun 春(はる)から夏(なつ)にかけての天気(てんき)
❌ Exact clock times ❌九時(くじ)から五時(ごじ)にかけて働(はたら)く ✅九時(くじ)から五時(ごじ)まで働(はたら)く

～から～にかけて · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
❌ にかけて alone in this sense ❌夜(よる)にかけて雨(あめ)が降(ふ)った ✅夕方(ゆうがた)から夜(よる)にかけて雨(あめ)が降(ふ)った

Rule of thumb - the thing you describe must spread across the whole band, not fill it exactly. If you could put a stopwatch on it, use から～まで.

～から～にかけて · Examples

夕方(ゆうがた)から夜(よる)にかけて、雨(あめ)が強(つよ)くなるでしょう。
The rain will get heavier from evening into the night.

三月(さんがつ)から四月(しがつ)にかけて、引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの予約(よやく)が取(と)りにくくなる。
Removal firms get hard to book anywhere from March through April.

関東(かんとう)から東北(とうほく)にかけて、大雪(おおゆき)が降(ふ)っています。
Heavy snow is falling across the Kanto and Tohoku regions.

去年(きょねん)の冬(ふゆ)から今年(ことし)の春(はる)にかけて、かぜが流行(はや)った。
Colds went round from last winter into this spring.

駅(えき)の北口(きたぐち)から公園(こうえん)にかけて、桜(さくら)の木(き)が続(つづ)いている。
Cherry trees run all the way from the station's north exit to the park.

～から～にかけて · Examples (cont.)

昭和(しょうわ)の終(お)わりから平成(へいせい)の初(はじ)めにかけて、この町(まち)の人口(じんこう)は大(おお)きく減(へ)った。
This town's population fell sharply from the end of the Showa era into the early Heisei years.

今週(こんしゅう)の水曜日(すいようび)から金曜日(きんようび)にかけて、工事(こうじ)のため水(みず)が止(と)まる時間(じかん)があります。
There will be periods without water from Wednesday through Friday this week owing to building work.

明(あ)け方(がた)から昼前(ひるまえ)にかけての風(かぜ)が強(つよ)く、電車(でんしゃ)が何度(なんど)か止(と)まった。
The wind between dawn and late morning was strong enough to stop the trains several times.

～から～にかけて · Nuance & register

The register is neutral to slightly formal. You will hear it every evening on the weather forecast, read it in newspapers and see it in official notices, and it is perfectly natural in careful conversation. It is not a stiff pattern; it is a precise-sounding one, which is why announcements like it.

What it will not tolerate is a sharp edge. から～まで says "these are the boundaries"; から～にかけて says "roughly this stretch". That is why clock times are wrong with it and vaguer time words - 夕方(ゆうがた)、夜中(よなか)、 週末(しゅうまつ)、春(はる) - are right. For the same reason the main clause usually describes something spread out or repeated: rain falling, prices rising, trees continuing, colds going round. A single completed action inside the band does not fit.

～から～にかけて · Nuance & register (cont.)

The の form is worth practising, because it is how the pattern gets in front of a noun: 夏(なつ)から秋(あき)にかけての台風(たいふう)、 夕方(ゆうがた)から夜(よる)にかけての混雑(こんざつ). Note that no な is possible.

Note - the same にかけて turns up in a completely unrelated sense, "when it comes to", where it normally carries は and praises somebody's ability: 計算(けいさん)にかけては誰(だれ)にも負(ま)けない。 The giveaway is the missing から: no から, no span.

～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～から～まで

Rule of thumb - まで gives a boundary; にかけて gives a band. まで can be measured and can end an action; にかけて is vague at both ends and describes what is going on across the middle.

銀行(ぎんこう)は九時(くじ)から三時(さんじ)まで開(あ)いています。
The bank is open from nine until three.

Those are exact, and the sentence would be nonsense with にかけて. Now something whose edges nobody can pin down:

夜中(よなか)から朝(あさ)にかけて、気温(きおん)が下(さ)がり続(つづ)けた。
The temperature kept falling from the small hours through to morning.

～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～から～まで (cont.)

There is a second, quieter difference. まで takes an endpoint that the action reaches and stops at - 駅(えき)まで歩(ある)く. にかけて never means "as far as and then stop"; it means "and everywhere in between", which is why 東京(とうきょう)から大阪(おおさか)にかけて describes a region, not a journey.

～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～の間(あいだ)に

Rule of thumb - 間(あいだ)に puts a single event somewhere inside a period. にかけて says the event occupies the period.

夏休(なつやす)みの間(あいだ)に、運転免許(うんてんめんきょ)を取(と)りました。
I got my driving licence during the summer holidays.

One licence, obtained at one point inside the holiday. にかけて would be wrong, because you cannot spread a single acquisition across the whole span. The pattern wants something continuous or repeated:

七月(しちがつ)から九月(くがつ)にかけて、台風(たいふう)が三(みっ)つも上陸(じょうりく)した。
Three separate typhoons made landfall between July and September.

～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～の間(あいだ)に (cont.)

Three events distributed across the band is exactly right; one event at a point inside it is 間(あいだ)に.

～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～にわたって

Rule of thumb - にわたって emphasises how much was covered - the scale of the span. にかけて emphasises where the span runs, from about here to about there.

工事(こうじ)は三年(さんねん)にわたって続(つづ)きました。
The work went on for three whole years.

That names a duration, not two endpoints, and it sounds impressed by the length. Compare:

去年(きょねん)の秋(あき)から今年(ことし)の春(はる)にかけて、工事(こうじ)が続(つづ)きました。
The work went on from last autumn through to this spring.

にわたって is also the more formal of the two and is common in written reports. When a sentence gives you both a start and an end, にかけて is usually the natural choice; when it gives you a total, reach for にわたって.

～から～にかけて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 九時(くじ)から五時(ごじ)にかけて働(はたら)いています。 → ✅ 九時(くじ)から五時(ごじ)まで働(はたら)いています。
    Clock times are exact boundaries, and this pattern needs vague ones. Working hours take まで.

  • ❌ 夜(よる)にかけて雨(あめ)が降(ふ)りました。 → ✅ 夕方(ゆうがた)から夜(よる)にかけて雨(あめ)が降(ふ)りました。
    The から half is not optional in this sense. Without it, にかけて is read as the unrelated "when it comes to" pattern, or simply fails.

  • ❌ 七月(しちがつ)から八月(はちがつ)にかけて北海道(ほっかいどう)へ旅行(りょこう)しました。 → ✅ 七月(しちがつ)から八月(はちがつ)にかけて北海道(ほっかいどう)を回(まわ)りました。
    A single trip taken at some point in the band needs の間(あいだ)に. If the travelling itself filled the two months, a verb like 回(まわ)る makes that continuous sense explicit.

～から～にかけて · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 春(はる)から夏(なつ)にかけてな天気(てんき)は変(か)わりやすい。 → ✅ 春(はる)から夏(なつ)にかけての天気(てんき)は変(か)わりやすい。
    Before a noun the linker is の. There is no な form.

The から half was missing, leaving no band for the wind to blow across. Adding a starting point restores the pattern.

～から～にかけて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 六月(ろくがつ)から七月(しちがつ)にかけて、雨(あめ)の日(ひ)が多(おお)かった。 (b) 六月(ろくがつ)と七月(しちがつ)は雨(あめ)の日(ひ)が多(おお)かった。

(a) treats the two months as one unbroken wet stretch, with the rain running through the join rather than stopping at the end of June. The edges are fuzzy on purpose.

(b) reports two separate months, each of which happened to be wet. It leaves open that the pattern of weather in the two was quite different, and it draws no line between them.

～にかけて

Meaning · when it comes to X - narrow the field to this one thing, and the claim that follows is absolute

Used on its own, にかけて - nearly always with は - picks out a single area of skill or quality and then makes a strong claim inside it: nobody better, no competition, complete confidence. The same string also closes the paired span pattern から～にかけて, and telling the two apart is half of what this page is for.

～にかけて · Formation

にかけて follows a noun. A verb has to be turned into a noun first, normally with こと.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (a field or skill) Noun + にかけては 料理(りょうり) → 料理(りょうり)にかけては
Verb, nominalised dictionary + こと + にかけては 走(はし)る → 走(はし)ることにかけては
Fixed superlative frame ～にかけては…の右(みぎ)に出(で)る者(もの)はいない 父(ちち)の右(みぎ)に出(で)る者(もの)はいない
Swearing on something Noun + にかけて + 誓(ちか)う／保証(ほしょう)する 名誉(めいよ)にかけて誓(ちか)う
Span (needs から) Noun + から + Noun + にかけて 朝(あさ)から昼(ひる)にかけて
❌ Verb without こと ❌泳(およ)ぐにかけては ✅泳(およ)ぐことにかけては

～にかけて · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
❌ Span without から ❌夜(よる)にかけて降(ふ)った ✅夕方(ゆうがた)から夜(よる)にかけて降(ふ)った

Rule of thumb - look for から. If there is a から earlier in the sentence, にかけて is closing a span. If there is a は attached to it and no から, it means "when it comes to".

～にかけて · Examples

走(はし)ることにかけては、クラスで一番(いちばん)だ。
When it comes to running, he's the best in the class.

記憶力(きおくりょく)にかけては、誰(だれ)にも負(ま)けない。
When it comes to memory, I'm second to none.

あの店(みせ)は、値段(ねだん)の安(やす)さにかけては町(まち)で一番(いちばん)だ。
For sheer cheapness, that shop beats anything in town.

魚(さかな)をさばくことにかけては、父(ちち)の右(みぎ)に出(で)る者(もの)はいない。
Nobody can touch my father when it comes to filleting fish.

技術(ぎじゅつ)にかけては自信(じしん)がありますが、営業(えいぎょう)はまったく苦手(にがて)です。
I'm confident about the technical side, but I'm hopeless at sales.

～にかけて · Examples (cont.)

彼女(かのじょ)は数字(すうじ)にかけては本当(ほんとう)に強(つよ)く、会議(かいぎ)でも即座(そくざ)に答(こた)えられる。
She is genuinely formidable with numbers and can answer on the spot even in meetings.

この品質(ひんしつ)については、会社(かいしゃ)の名(な)にかけて保証(ほしょう)いたします。
We guarantee this quality on the company's name.

昼(ひる)すぎから夕方(ゆうがた)にかけて、会場(かいじょう)の前(まえ)に長(なが)い列(れつ)ができていた。
From early afternoon into the evening there was a long queue outside the venue.

～にかけて · Nuance & register

The "when it comes to" use is emphatic by nature. It exists to set up a superlative, so the main clause almost always contains something like 一番(いちばん)、誰(だれ)にも負(ま)けない、右(みぎ)に出(で)る者(もの)はいない or 自信(じしん)がある. A flat, neutral statement after にかけては sounds unfinished.

It is normally praise, and praising yourself with it is fine in Japanese in a way that a bare boast would not be, because the pattern explicitly limits the claim to one narrow area - the implication is "only here". That is why 技術(ぎじゅつ)にかけては自信(じしん)があります works well in a job interview. Speakers do turn it ironic - 忘(わす)れることにかけては天才(てんさい)だ, "a genius at forgetting things" - and the joke works precisely because the frame is built for compliments.

～にかけて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral, leaning a little literary; you will meet it in profiles, introductions and reviews more than in idle chat. The に is fixed and the は is close to obligatory in this sense - にかけて without は is usually either the span pattern or the "swearing on" one.

Note - the oath use is a small, fixed set: 命(いのち)にかけて、名誉(めいよ)にかけて、社(しゃ)の信用(しんよう)にかけて. It is dramatic and formal, at home in speeches, courtroom scenes and corporate apologies, and it always attaches to a verb of promising or guaranteeing.

～にかけて · ～にかけては vs ～について・～に関(かん)して

Rule of thumb - について introduces a subject matter and is neutral about what comes next. にかけては introduces an arena of ability and commits you to a claim of superiority inside it.

地震(じしん)について、専門家(せんもんか)が説明(せつめい)しました。
An expert gave an explanation about earthquakes.

Nothing there is being ranked, and にかけては would be nonsense. Compare a sentence built to rank:

地震(じしん)の研究(けんきゅう)にかけては、彼(かれ)は国内(こくない)で屈指(くっし)の存在(そんざい)だ。
When it comes to earthquake research, he is one of the leading figures in the country.

～にかけて · ～にかけては vs ～について・～に関(かん)して (cont.)

A quick test: if you could continue with 説明(せつめい)します or 話(はな)します, use について. If you want to continue with "he's the best", use にかけては.

～にかけて · ～にかけては vs ～は・～では

Rule of thumb - plain は or では also narrows the frame, but softly. にかけては narrows it hard and signals that a strong claim is coming.

漢字(かんじ)は得意(とくい)です。
I'm good at kanji.

漢字(かんじ)にかけては、クラスの誰(だれ)にも負(ま)けません。
When it comes to kanji, I won't lose to anyone in the class.

Both restrict the topic; only the second promises a superlative. The consequence is practical: にかけては cannot be followed by a modest or a negative evaluation. ❌漢字(かんじ)にかけては苦手(にがて)です is wrong, and the fix is simply 漢字(かんじ)は苦手(にがて)です. If what you want to say is "in this area I'm weak", the plain topic particle is the whole answer.

～にかけて · ～にかけて（span）vs ～にかけては（ability）

Rule of thumb - the span sense needs a partner から and two nouns of time or place. The ability sense needs one noun and は. They never overlap in a real sentence.

水曜日(すいようび)から木曜日(もくようび)にかけて、雨(あめ)が続(つづ)くそうだ。
They say the rain will continue from Wednesday into Thursday.

天気(てんき)の予想(よそう)にかけては、祖父(そふ)はテレビの予報(よほう)より当(あ)たる。
When it comes to predicting the weather, my grandfather beats the television.

Two nouns and a から in the first; one noun and a は in the second. If you ever meet a bare にかけて with neither marker, it is almost certainly the oath use, and the verb at the end of the sentence will tell you so.

～にかけて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 泳(およ)ぐにかけては、兄(あに)が一番(いちばん)だ。 → ✅ 泳(およ)ぐことにかけては、兄(あに)が一番(いちばん)だ。
    にかけて follows a noun. A verb needs こと to become one.

  • ❌ 料理(りょうり)にかけては下手(へた)です。 → ✅ 料理(りょうり)は下手(へた)です。
    The pattern sets up a boast. Following it with an admission of weakness contradicts the frame it just built.

  • ❌ 日本(にほん)の歴史(れきし)にかけて研究(けんきゅう)しています。 → ✅ 日本(にほん)の歴史(れきし)について研究(けんきゅう)しています。
    "Researching about X" is subject matter, not ranked ability, so it needs について.

  • ❌ 夜(よる)にかけて勉強(べんきょう)しました。 → ✅ 夕方(ゆうがた)から夜(よる)にかけて勉強(べんきょう)しました。
    The span sense is a two-part pattern. Without から there is no span, and the sentence reads as a broken version of the ability use.

～にかけて · Practice 1

1. 話(はな)すのは苦手(にがて)ですが、書(か)くこと____自信(じしん)があります。

話(はな)すのは苦手(にがて)ですが、書(か)くことにかけては自信(じしん)があります。

The sentence contrasts a weakness with a strength, and the strength is being claimed emphatically inside one narrow area - exactly the environment for にかけては. Note the こと turning the verb into a noun.

～にかけて · Practice 2

2. Choose: 環境問題(かんきょうもんだい)（について・にかけては）レポートを書(か)きました。

環境問題(かんきょうもんだい)についてレポートを書(か)きました。

Writing a report names a subject matter and ranks nobody. にかけては would demand a superlative claim, which a report cannot supply.

～にかけて · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "When it comes to remembering people's names, she's better than anyone in the office."

人(ひと)の名前(なまえ)を覚(おぼ)えることにかけては、彼女(かのじょ)は社内(しゃない)の誰(だれ)よりも優(すぐ)れている。

"When it comes to" plus a comparison against everyone else is the pattern's signature. The verb becomes a noun with こと, and the main clause carries the superlative.

～にかけて · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 朝(あさ)にかけて強(つよ)い風(かぜ)が吹(ふ)くでしょう。

✅ 夜中(よなか)から朝(あさ)にかけて強(つよ)い風(かぜ)が吹(ふ)くでしょう。

In its span sense, にかけて must be paired with から. One noun on its own gives the reader no band, and a forecast needs both ends.

～にかけて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼(かれ)は運転(うんてん)にかけてはプロ並(な)みだ。 (b) 彼(かれ)は運転(うんてん)が上手(じょうず)だ。

(a) fences off driving as one specific arena and then makes a strong claim inside it. The fencing carries an implication: in other areas he may be quite ordinary, and the speaker is not commenting on those.

(b) is a plain compliment with no such implication and no superlative force. It would suit small talk, whereas (a) sounds like a considered verdict.

～ぶりに

Meaning · for the first time in - that much time has passed since it last happened, and now it has happened again

ぶりに measures a gap. You attach it to a length of time, and the sentence says two things at once: the last time was that long ago, and it has just happened again. Both halves matter - if it has not happened again, ぶりに is the wrong word.

～ぶりに · Formation

ぶり is a suffix. It attaches directly to a quantity of time, with no particle in between, and takes に when it modifies a verb.

～ぶりに · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Length of time + verb Quantity + ぶりに 三年(さんねん) → 三年(さんねん)ぶりに会(あ)う
As a predicate Quantity + ぶりだ／です 十年(じゅうねん)ぶりです
Before a noun Quantity + ぶりの + Noun 五年(ごねん)ぶりの優勝(ゆうしょう)
Vague length 久(ひさ)し + ぶりに 久(ひさ)しぶりに話(はな)した
As a greeting お久(ひさ)しぶりです -
❌ な before a noun ❌三年(さんねん)ぶりな再会(さいかい) ✅三年(さんねん)ぶりの再会(さいかい)
❌ Event noun ❌試験(しけん)ぶりに ✅試験(しけん)の日(ひ)以来(いらい)

～ぶりに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - what goes in front of ぶり is a stretch of time, never an event. 三年(さんねん)ぶり and 久(ひさ)しぶり are fine; ❌卒業(そつぎょう)ぶり is not. For an event, use ～以来(いらい).

～ぶりに · Examples

三年(さんねん)ぶりに国(くに)へ帰(かえ)った。
I went home for the first time in three years.

久(ひさ)しぶりに映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)った。
I went to the cinema for the first time in ages.

一週間(いっしゅうかん)ぶりに晴(は)れましたね。
It's the first clear day in a week, isn't it?

十年(じゅうねん)ぶりに中学(ちゅうがく)の友(とも)だちから連絡(れんらく)が来(き)た。
A friend from junior high got in touch after ten years.

うちのチームは五年(ごねん)ぶりの優勝(ゆうしょう)を果(は)たした。
Our team took the title for the first time in five years.

～ぶりに · Examples (cont.)

今日(きょう)は三十五度(さんじゅうごど)を超(こ)えて、八年(はちねん)ぶりの暑(あつ)さだそうだ。
It's over thirty-five degrees today - apparently the hottest in eight years.

忙(いそが)しくて、半年(はんとし)ぶりに美容院(びよういん)へ行(い)ったら、担当(たんとう)の人(ひと)が変(か)わっていた。
Work kept me away, and when I finally got to the hairdresser after six months my usual stylist had gone.

退院(たいいん)して、二(に)か月(げつ)ぶりに自分(じぶん)のベッドで眠(ねむ)ることができた。
I came out of hospital and slept in my own bed for the first time in two months.

～ぶりに · Nuance & register

ぶりに is register-neutral and extremely common in ordinary speech. It carries a small lift of feeling - pleasure, relief, or simply the sense that a long stretch has been broken. Sports reporting loves it (三十年(さんじゅうねん)ぶりの優勝(ゆうしょう)), weather reports love it (十年(じゅうねん)ぶりの大雪(おおゆき)), and so does anyone greeting an old friend.

The greeting forms are worth learning as fixed items. 久(ひさ)しぶり! is casual, お久(ひさ)しぶりです is the everyday polite version, and in business writing the formal equivalent is ご無沙汰(ぶさた)しております, which does not use ぶり at all. Note the shape: the greeting has no に, because it is a predicate - お久(ひさ)しぶりです, not ❌お久(ひさ)しぶりにです. The に appears only when ぶり is modifying a verb.

～ぶりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Speakers happily stretch the pattern for comic effect over absurdly short intervals - 五分(ごふん)ぶりだね, "long time no see (five minutes)". That is a joke, and it works because the pattern normally implies a genuinely long gap.

Note - a different ぶり means "the manner of doing something": 仕事(しごと)ぶり, 話(はな)しぶり, 飲(の)みっぷり. It attaches to a verb stem or an activity noun rather than to a length of time, and it never takes the "for the first time in" reading. 彼(かれ)の仕事(しごと)ぶりには感心(かんしん)した is about how he works, not about how long it had been.

～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～後(ご)に

Rule of thumb - 後(ご)に counts forward from a moment already in the story. ぶりに counts backward to the last time the same thing happened.

卒業(そつぎょう)の三年(さんねん)後(ご)に、彼(かれ)は会社(かいしゃ)を作(つく)った。
Three years after graduating, he started a company.

The three years run from graduation to the founding - two different events. Now the same number doing the other job:

三年(さんねん)ぶりに同(おな)じ店(みせ)で待(ま)ち合(あ)わせた。
We met up at the same place for the first time in three years.

～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～後(ご)に (cont.)

Here the three years run from the previous meeting to this one - the same event twice. That is the whole distinction, and it is why 三年(さんねん)後(ご)に会(あ)った and 三年(さんねん)ぶりに会(あ)った describe quite different situations. Note the reading too: in this counting sense, 後(ご) is read ご, not あと.

～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs 久(ひさ)しぶりに

Rule of thumb - same pattern, different precision. 久(ひさ)しぶりに is the version for when you do not want to name a number, and it is far more frequent in conversation.

久(ひさ)しぶりに母(はは)の声(こえ)を聞(き)いて、ほっとした。
Hearing my mother's voice after so long was a relief.

四年(よねん)ぶりに母(はは)に会(あ)い、思(おも)っていたより元気(げんき)そうで安心(あんしん)した。
I saw my mother for the first time in four years, and she looked better than I'd expected.

Naming the number makes the sentence a small piece of news, which is why headlines and announcements prefer it. Leaving it vague makes the sentence about the feeling. The one place they are not interchangeable is the greeting: only 久(ひさ)しぶり works there, since ❌四年(よねん)ぶりです! is a statement of fact rather than a hello.

～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～以来(いらい)

Rule of thumb - ぶり takes a length of time; 以来(いらい) takes an event or a point in time, and it does not require the thing to have happened again.

去年(きょねん)の夏(なつ)以来(いらい)、彼(かれ)とは会(あ)っていない。
I haven't seen him since last summer.

Notice the negative: nothing has happened again, and ぶり could not be used at all. Compare a sentence whose whole point is that the streak has just ended:

去年(きょねん)の夏(なつ)以来(いらい)、半年(はんとし)ぶりに彼(かれ)と会(あ)った。
I saw him for the first time in six months, the first time since last summer.

～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～以来(いらい) (cont.)

The two happily sit in one sentence, each doing its own job: 以来(いらい) names the starting event, ぶりに gives the length of the gap. This also explains the commonest error with ぶり - attaching it to an event, as in ❌入学(にゅうがく)ぶりに. Swap in 入学(にゅうがく)以来(いらい) and the sentence works.

～ぶりに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 卒業(そつぎょう)ぶりに先生(せんせい)に会(あ)いました。 → ✅ 卒業(そつぎょう)以来(いらい)、初(はじ)めて先生(せんせい)に会(あ)いました。
    ぶり needs a length of time in front of it. Graduation is an event, which takes 以来(いらい).

  • ❌ 十年(じゅうねん)ぶりな再会(さいかい)でした。 → ✅ 十年(じゅうねん)ぶりの再会(さいかい)でした。
    Before a noun the linker is の. ぶり behaves like a noun, not a な-adjective.

  • ❌ お久(ひさ)しぶりにですね。 → ✅ お久(ひさ)しぶりですね。
    The に belongs only when ぶり modifies a verb. As a greeting it is a predicate and stands alone.

  • ❌ 二年(にねん)後(ご)に温泉(おんせん)に行(い)った。（intending "went to a hot spring for the first time in two years"） → ✅ 二年(にねん)ぶりに温泉(おんせん)に行(い)った。
    後(ご)に would mean two years after some other event. To measure back to the previous visit, you need ぶりに.

～ぶりに · Practice 1

1. 台風(たいふう)が過(す)ぎて、三日(みっか)____青(あお)い空(そら)が見(み)えた。

台風(たいふう)が過(す)ぎて、三日(みっか)ぶりに青(あお)い空(そら)が見(み)えた。

Three days had passed since the sky was last blue, and now it is blue again - both halves of what ぶりに claims. The に is there because it modifies a verb.

～ぶりに · Practice 2

2. Choose: 入学(にゅうがく)して一年(いちねん)（後(ご)に・ぶりに）、初(はじ)めて寮(りょう)を出(で)た。

入学(にゅうがく)して一年(いちねん)後(ご)に、初(はじ)めて寮(りょう)を出(で)た。

The year is counted forward from enrolment, and leaving the dormitory is happening for the very first time - so there is no previous occasion for ぶり to measure back to.

～ぶりに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I got a letter from my grandfather for the first time in two years."

二年(にねん)ぶりに祖父(そふ)から手紙(てがみ)が届(とど)いた。

"For the first time in two years" maps straight onto 二年(にねん)ぶりに: a named gap, and the event recurring at the end of it.

～ぶりに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 五年(ごねん)ぶりな日本(にほん)へ行(い)ってきました。

✅ 五年(ごねん)ぶりに日本(にほん)へ行(い)ってきました。

ぶり takes に in front of a verb and の in front of a noun; な is never an option. Here the phrase modifies 行(い)く, so に is correct.

～ぶりに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 五年(ごねん)ぶりにピアノを弾(ひ)いた。 (b) 五年(ごねん)間(かん)、ピアノを弾(ひ)いていなかった。

(a) puts the emphasis on the moment the gap ended: the playing has just happened, and the five years are there to tell you how remarkable that is. There is usually a flicker of feeling attached.

(b) describes the gap itself and says nothing about whether it has ended. It is the sentence you use to explain why your fingers are stiff, not to announce that you have started again.
