～たとたん

Meaning · the instant X happened, Y happened - and Y was not expected

たとたん welds two past events together with no gap at all. What separates it from every other "as soon as" pattern is the second half: the thing that happens is a surprise, something the speaker noticed rather than arranged. If you planned it, たとたん is the wrong word.

～たとたん · Formation

とたん is really the noun 途端とたん, "the very edge of the moment", so it takes a plain past verb in front of it, exactly the way a noun takes a modifying clause.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (た-form) た-form + とたん 着つく → 着ついたとたん
Verb (た-form) た-form + とたんに 開あける → 開あけたとたんに
As a bare noun その + 途端とたん その途端とたん、電気でんきが消きえた
❌ Dictionary form ❌着つくとたん ✅着ついたとたん
❌ Non-past main clause ❌…とたん、驚おどろく ✅…とたん、驚おどろいた
❌ Request or invitation ❌…とたん、電話でんわしてください ✅…たら、電話でんわしてください

～たとたん · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - three things must all be true: the verb in front is た, the main clause is past, and nobody planned the result. Break any one of them and you want a different pattern.

～たとたん · Examples

ベッドに入はいったとたん、眠ねむってしまった。
The moment I got into bed, I was asleep.

窓まどを開あけたとたん、冷つめたい風かぜが入はいってきた。
The instant I opened the window, cold air poured in.

立たち上あがったとたんに、めまいがした。
I stood up and the room span, just like that.

先生せんせいの顔かおを見みたとたん、弟おとうとは泣なき出だした。
The second he saw the teacher's face, my little brother burst into tears.

～たとたん · Examples (cont.)

試合しあいが終おわったとたん、雨あめが降ふり出だした。
No sooner had the match finished than it started to rain.

ドアを閉しめたとたんに、鍵かぎを中なかに置おいてきたことに気きづいた。
The instant the door shut behind me, I realised I'd left the key inside.

部長ぶちょうは部屋へやに入はいってきたとたん、理由りゆうも言いわずに大おおきな声こえで怒おこり出だした。
The manager walked into the room and immediately started shouting, without saying why.

十年じゅうねん働はたらいた会社かいしゃを辞やめたとたん、毎日まいにちが静しずかになりすぎて、かえって落おち着つかなかった。
The day I left the company I'd worked at for ten years, my life went so quiet it actually unsettled me.

～たとたん · Nuance & register

たとたん is neutral in politeness - the pattern sits in the middle of the sentence, so the ending decides the register: 降ふり出だした or 降ふり出だしました, either is fine. It is slightly more common in narration and in written accounts of events than in moment-to-moment chat, because it is a storyteller's device: it sets up a punchline.

The に is optional and carries almost no meaning. とたんに is a touch more deliberate, a beat of extra weight before the surprise; とたん alone is quicker. Most speakers use both without thinking about it.

～たとたん · Nuance & register (cont.)

The word is usually written in kana as とたん, though 途端とたん in kanji turns up in novels and newspapers. As a standalone noun it survives in その途端とたん - "at that very instant" - which is worth knowing because it lets you split the two events into separate sentences.

Three neighbours are worth naming, because JLPT questions live on the difference:

～たとたん · Nuance & register (cont.)

  • ～次第しだい points forwards, not back. It is formal, it takes the
    verb stem rather than た, and the second clause is the speaker's own planned action: 会議かいぎが終おわり次第しだい、ご連絡れんらくします。 Everything たとたん forbids, 次第しだい requires.
  • ～や否いなや means the same as たとたん but is literary. You will meet
    it in print and essentially never in conversation.
  • ～かと思おもうと also carries surprise, but it specialises in things that
    flip: 泣ないていたかと思おもうと、もう笑わらっている。

～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～たら

Rule of thumb - たら lets you put a request, an instruction, an invitation or a plan in the main clause. たとたん allows none of those. If the second half is something you want to happen, it has to be たら.

駅えきに着ついたら、電話でんわしてください。
Call me when you get to the station.

Swap in とたん and the sentence collapses, because you cannot ask somebody to be surprised on cue. With a past clause and an unplanned result, the same setup is fine:

駅えきに着ついたとたん、財布さいふがないことに気きづいた。
The moment I reached the station, I realised my wallet was gone.

～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～たら (cont.)

たら is also happy with a gap in time - 大学だいがくを出でたら働はたらきます covers years. たとたん covers a fraction of a second.

～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～てすぐ(に)

Rule of thumb - てすぐ means "very soon after", and lets you do the second thing on purpose. たとたん means "in the same instant", and the second thing happens to you.

家いえに帰かえってすぐ、シャワーを浴あびた。
I had a shower as soon as I got home.

That is a decision, so たとたん would be odd. Compare:

家いえに帰かえったとたん、猫ねこが足あしに飛とびついてきた。
The second I got home, the cat launched itself at my legs.

Nobody arranged that, which is exactly why たとたん fits. The same test explains why a diary entry uses てすぐ for a routine and たとたん for the day something went wrong.

～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～と

Rule of thumb - と states a rule that holds every time. たとたん reports one particular moment that has already gone past.

このボタンを押おすと、お湯ゆが出でます。
Press this button and hot water comes out.

That is how the machine works, always - たとたん cannot express it, because it is anchored to a single past event. But a one-off goes the other way:

ボタンを押おしたとたん、機械きかいから変へんな音おとがした。
The instant I pressed the button, the machine made a strange noise.

～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～と (cont.)

と can also carry discovery in the past - ドアを開あけると、誰だれもいなかった - and there it comes close to たとたん. The difference is speed and shock: と narrates smoothly, たとたん snaps.

～たとたん · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 座すわるとたん、電話でんわが鳴なった。 → ✅ 座すわったとたん、電話でんわが鳴なった。
    The verb in front of とたん is always た-form, even when the sentence is about the past. Dictionary form is simply ungrammatical here.

  • ❌ ベルが鳴なったとたん、教室きょうしつを出でましょう。 → ✅ ベルが鳴なったら、教室きょうしつを出でましょう。
    An invitation is a plan, and とたん cannot host a plan. たら can.

  • ❌ 毎朝まいあさ、起おきたとたん水みずを飲のむ。 → ✅ 毎朝まいあさ、起おきたらすぐ水みずを飲のむ。
    A daily habit is neither a single moment nor a surprise. とたん reports one event, once.

～たとたん · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 外そとに出でたとたん、雨あめが降ふり出だすでしょう。 → ✅ 外そとに出でたとたん、雨あめが降ふり出だした。
    The main clause has to be past. There is no way to predict a とたん; by the time you use it, it has happened.

～たとたん · Practice 1

1. 電車でんしゃに乗のっ____、ドアが閉しまった。

電車でんしゃに乗のったとたん、ドアが閉しまった。

The verb takes the た-form before とたん, and the main clause is past. The doors closing is outside the speaker's control, which is what licenses the pattern.

～たとたん · Practice 2

2. Choose: 荷物にもつが届とどい（たとたん・たら）、中身なかみを確認かくにんしてください。

荷物にもつが届とどいたら、中身なかみを確認かくにんしてください。

The main clause is a request, and とたん refuses requests, commands and invitations. Anything you are asking the listener to do belongs with たら.

～たとたん · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "The moment I sat down on the bench, it started to rain."

ベンチに座すわったとたん、雨あめが降ふり出だした。

Both halves are past, the rain is unplanned, and the two events share a single instant - the ideal conditions for たとたん. Adding the optional に after とたん changes nothing.

～たとたん · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 春はるになるとたん、桜さくらが咲さきます。

✅ 春はるになると、桜さくらが咲さきます。

Two problems at once: とたん needs a た-form, and more importantly this sentence states what happens every year. A general rule takes と, never とたん.

～たとたん · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれが部屋へやを出でたとたん、みんなが話はなし始はじめた。 (b) 彼かれが部屋へやを出でてから、みんなが話はなし始はじめた。

(a) puts the two events in the same instant and frames the outburst of talk as something the speaker witnessed and did not expect - the room had clearly been holding its tongue. There is an edge of "the second he was gone".

(b) simply orders them: first he left, then people talked. It is neutral about how long the gap was and implies nothing about surprise, so it works equally well in a dry report of a meeting.

～上で

Meaning · after doing X - and on the strength of what X told you - do Y

上うえで (read うえで) is not a plain "after". It says the first action was done in order to find something out, and the second action is the decision you reached with that information in hand. It is a formal word: paperwork, meetings, notices.

～上で · Formation

The same word covers two jobs, and the verb form in front tells you which. た-form means "after"; dictionary form means "in the course of".

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (た-form) - after た-form + 上うえで 調しらべる → 調しらべた上うえで
Action noun - after Noun + の + 上うえで 相談そうだん → 相談そうだんの上うえで
Verb (dictionary) - in the course of dictionary + 上うえで 暮くらす → 暮くらす上うえで
Noun - in the course of Noun + の + 上うえで 仕事しごと → 仕事しごとの上うえで
Before a noun ～上うえでの + Noun 検討けんとうした上うえでの判断はんだん
Formal notice style ～の上うえ、… ご記入きにゅうの上うえ、お出だしください
❌ て-form ❌調しらべて上うえで ✅調しらべた上うえで

～上で · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - for the "after" sense, ask whether the first action produces information the second action needs. 見みた上うえで決きめる works because looking is what tells you how to decide. 歯はをみがいた上うえで寝ねる does not, because brushing your teeth informs nothing.

～上で · Examples

よく考かんがえた上うえで、お返事へんじします。
I'll give you my answer once I've thought it through.

両親りょうしんと相談そうだんした上うえで決きめます。
I'll decide after talking it over with my parents.

部屋へやを見みた上うえで、借かりるかどうか決きめたいです。
I'd like to see the flat before deciding whether to rent it.

外国がいこくで暮くらす上うえで、一番いちばん苦労くろうするのは言葉ことばです。
The hardest part of living abroad is the language.

～上で · Examples (cont.)

この仕事しごとを続つづける上うえで、体力たいりょくは欠かかせません。
Stamina is essential if you're going to keep doing this job.

資料しりょうをお読よみになった上うえで、ご質問しつもんをお寄よせください。
Please read the materials and then send us your questions.

工事こうじの費用ひようを正確せいかくに計算けいさんした上うえで、あらためてお見積みつもりをお送おくりいたします。
Once we have costed the work precisely, we'll send you a revised quotation.

皆みなさんのご意見いけんをうかがった上うえで、来週らいしゅうの会議かいぎで最終さいしゅう案あんを決きめたいと思おもいます。
I'd like to hear everyone's views first, and then settle the final proposal at next week's meeting.

～上で · Nuance & register

This is a formal pattern. It belongs to written Japanese, business speech and public notices, and it sounds stiff in a chat with a friend - where you would simply say 見みてから決きめる. Using it well is one of the fastest ways to make a work email sound like it was written by an adult.

In notices and forms the で is often dropped, leaving ～の上うえ、 with a comma: ご確認かくにんの上うえ、送信そうしんしてください。 That clipped form is almost a fixed register marker; it appears on websites, application forms and station signs, and never in speech.

Two particles slot in usefully. 上うえでは narrows the frame to one domain - 法律ほうりつの上うえでは問題もんだいありません, "legally speaking there's no problem". 上うえでも adds "for that purpose too".

～上で · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - do not confuse this with ～上うえに, which is a completely different pattern meaning "and what's more": この店みせは安やすい上うえにおいしい。 Same kanji, に instead of で, and it stacks reasons rather than ordering events. There is also ～上うえは, "now that (you have done X)", which is a step above N3.

～上で · ～た上で vs ～てから

Rule of thumb - てから only says later. 上うえで says later, and because of what the first step revealed. Every 上うえで sentence could be てから; the reverse is not true.

手てを洗あらってから、ケーキを作つくり始はじめた。
I washed my hands and then started making the cake.

Washing your hands tells you nothing, so 洗あらった上うえで would be strange. Now a case where the first step really does feed the second:

アンケートの結果けっかを分析ぶんせきした上うえで、新あたらしい企画きかくを立たてます。
We'll draw up the new plan after analysing the survey results.

～上で · ～た上で vs ～てから (cont.)

Register separates them too. てから is neutral and used at every level of politeness; 上うえで raises the whole sentence a notch.

～上で · ～た上で vs ～た後で

Rule of thumb - 後あとで measures time: X finished, then Y. 上うえで measures dependence: Y is shaped by X.

食事しょくじの後あとで、散歩さんぽに行いきました。
After the meal we went for a walk.

Nothing about the meal determined the walk, so this is 後あとで and only 後あとで. Compare:

医者いしゃの話はなしを聞きいた上うえで、手術しゅじゅつを受うけるかどうか考かんがえます。
I'll think about whether to have the operation after hearing what the doctor says.

～上で · ～た上で vs ～た後で (cont.)

Here 後あとで would be grammatical but flat; it would lose the point, which is that the doctor's explanation is the basis of the decision. A second, harder difference: 後あとで accepts events nobody controls - 台風たいふうが過すぎた後あとで - while 上うえで needs a deliberate first action, so ❌台風たいふうが過すぎた上うえで is wrong.

～上で · ～る上で vs ～た上で

Rule of thumb - dictionary form + 上うえで means "in doing X / when it comes to X" and points at a whole activity. た-form + 上うえで means "after X" and points at one completed step.

日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうする上うえで、辞書じしょの使つかい方かたはとても大切たいせつです。
When it comes to studying Japanese, knowing how to use a dictionary matters a great deal.

日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうした上うえで、留学りゅうがくするかどうか決きめます。
I'll study some Japanese first, and then decide whether to go abroad.

The first sentence never happened and never will "happen" - it describes the activity in general. The second describes a step you take and then finish. Getting the two mixed up is the single most common error with this pattern, and the fix is always the verb form.

～上で · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 歯はをみがいた上うえで寝ねます。 → ✅ 歯はをみがいてから寝ねます。
    A routine sequence with no decision in it takes てから. Brushing your teeth does not give you information to act on.

  • ❌ 雨あめが降ふった上うえで、試合しあいが中止ちゅうしになった。 → ✅ 雨あめが降ふったので、試合しあいが中止ちゅうしになった。
    The first clause must be something somebody chose to do. Weather is not a deliberate act, and here the relation is cause, not preparation.

  • ❌ 見積みつもりを見みて上うえで返事へんじします。 → ✅ 見積みつもりを見みた上うえで返事へんじします。
    上うえ is a noun, so the clause in front of it ends in a plain form - た or dictionary - never て.

～上で · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 会社かいしゃで働はたらいた上うえで必要ひつような知識ちしき → ✅ 会社かいしゃで働はたらく上うえで必要ひつような知識ちしき
    "Knowledge you need in order to work at a company" is the in the course of sense, which takes the dictionary form.

～上で · Practice 1

1. 条件じょうけんをよく確たしかめ____、申もうし込こんでください。

条件じょうけんをよく確たしかめた上うえで、申もうし込こんでください。

Checking the conditions is exactly the kind of deliberate, informing step this pattern wants, and the application is the decision that follows from it. Note the た-form: the checking has to be finished.

～上で · Practice 2

2. Choose: 安全あんぜんに運転うんてん（する・した）上うえで、気きをつけることは何なんですか。

安全あんぜんに運転うんてんする上うえで、気きをつけることは何なんですか。

The question is about driving safely as an ongoing activity, not about something finished, so it needs the dictionary form and the "in the course of" reading.

～上で · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "We would like to make a decision after hearing the customer's opinion."

お客様きゃくさまのご意見いけんをうかがった上うえで、決きめたいと思おもいます。

A business setting, a deliberate first step, and a decision that depends on it - all three signals point at 上うえで rather than a plain てから.

～上で · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 地震じしんが起おきた上うえで、電車でんしゃが止とまりました。

✅ 地震じしんが起おきたため、電車でんしゃが止とまりました。

An earthquake is not an action anyone performed to gather information, and the trains stopping is a consequence rather than a considered decision. This sentence needs a cause marker such as ため or ので.

～上で · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 全員ぜんいんの意見いけんを聞きいた上うえで決きめます。 (b) 全員ぜんいんの意見いけんを聞きいてから決きめます。

(a) commits the speaker to letting those opinions shape the decision. It is the sentence a manager uses to promise that the consultation is real, and its formal tone suits a meeting or an email.

(b) only promises an order of events: opinions first, decision second. It is perfectly polite but leaves open the possibility that the decision was made already and the listening is a formality.

～て初めて

Meaning · not until X did Y become clear - it took X for the penny to drop

て初はじめて is the grammar of hindsight. You did something, or something happened to you, and only then did you understand, notice or become able to do the thing in the second clause. The English that fits it best is almost always "only after…" or "it wasn't until…".

～て初めて · Formation

The pattern is simply the て-form followed by the adverb 初はじめて. Word order is doing all the work here, so guard it: the て-form comes first.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (て-form) て-form + 初はじめて 失うしなう → 失うしなって初はじめて
Verb + てみる てみて + 初はじめて 使つかう → 使つかってみて初はじめて
Noun (a new status) Noun + になって + 初はじめて 親おや → 親おやになって初はじめて
Often rounded off with のだ ～て初はじめて…のだ 読よんで初はじめてわかったのだ
❌ た-form ❌失うしなった初はじめて ✅失うしなって初はじめて
❌ Adverb before the verb ❌初はじめて失うしなって ✅失うしなって初はじめて

～て初めて · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the main clause has to be a discovery: わかる、気きづく、 知しる、できるようになる、見みえてくる. If the second half is something you decided to do, you want てから, not て初はじめて.

～て初めて · Examples

使つかってみて初はじめて、この辞書じしょのよさが分わかった。
It wasn't until I used it that I saw how good this dictionary is.

病気びょうきになって初はじめて、健康けんこうのありがたさが分わかった。
Only when I got ill did I appreciate being healthy.

一人ひとりで暮くらして初はじめて、家事かじの大変たいへんさに気きづいた。
It took living on my own for me to realise how much work housework is.

説明せつめいを聞きいて初はじめて、この機械きかいの使つかい方かたが分わかりました。
I only understood how to use this machine after hearing the explanation.

～て初めて · Examples (cont.)

人ひとは失うしなって初はじめて、その大切たいせつさに気きづくものだ。
People never see the value of something until they've lost it.

地図ちずを見みて初はじめて、自分じぶんがどれだけ遠とおくまで歩あるいてきたのか知しった。
Not until I looked at the map did I realise how far I'd walked.

外国がいこくで生活せいかつしてみて初はじめて、自分じぶんの国くにのことを何なにも知しらなかったのだと気きづかされた。
Living abroad was what made me realise I knew nothing about my own country.

親おやになって初はじめて、父ちちがどうしてあんなに厳きびしかったのか理解りかいできるようになった。
It was only once I became a parent that I could understand why my father was so strict.

～て初めて · Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral in form - the ending decides politeness - but it is tonally marked. It draws a lesson, so it turns up in essays, in speeches, in the reflective part of a conversation, and in the kind of sentence that gets quoted. Using it for something trivial is faintly comic: 冷蔵庫れいぞうこを開あけて初はじめて、牛乳ぎゅうにゅうがないことに気きづいた is grammatical but sounds as though you are narrating your own life as a novel.

～て初めて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Three companions show up constantly. てみて in front, because trying something is the classic route to a discovery. のだ/んです at the end, because the sentence is explaining a state of affairs rather than reporting an event. And 本当ほんとうの or 本当ほんとうに inside the main clause - 本当ほんとうの意味いみが分わかった - because the whole point is that a shallower version of the understanding existed before.

Written Japanese sometimes reverses the halves for effect: 人ひとは気きづくのだ、失うしなって初はじめて。 That is a stylistic inversion, not a second pattern, and you will not need to produce it.

～て初めて · ～て初めて vs ～てから

Rule of thumb - てから tells you the order. て初はじめて adds a claim about everything before that point: until then, it had not happened and could not have.

大学だいがくを出でてから、一人ひとりで住すみ始はじめました。
I started living alone after I finished university.

That is a timeline. Now the same shape with the "not until" claim added:

大学だいがくを出でて初はじめて、親おやに感謝かんしゃする気持きもちが生うまれた。
It was leaving university that first gave me any sense of gratitude towards my parents.

～て初めて · ～て初めて vs ～てから (cont.)

The practical test: can you add "and not before"? If yes, て初はじめて. If the sentence is just two things in order - and especially if the second one is planned - it is てから.

～て初めて · ～て初めて vs 初めて (the adverb)

Rule of thumb - 初はじめて standing in front of a verb means "for the first time" and describes the verb. て初はじめて sits between two clauses and says the first one unlocked the second.

去年きょねん初はじめて京都きょうとへ行いきました。
I went to Kyoto for the first time last year.

京都きょうとへ行いって初はじめて、日本にほんの庭にわの美うつくしさが分わかりました。
It was going to Kyoto that showed me how beautiful Japanese gardens are.

～て初めて · ～て初めて vs 初めて (the adverb) (cont.)

Both sentences contain the same three words; only the position of 初はじめて differs, and it changes what is being claimed. In the first, the trip was a first. In the second, the trip is the cause and the understanding is the first.

～て初めて · ～て初めて vs ～ないと分からない

Rule of thumb - て初はじめて reports something that actually happened. ないと／なければ makes a hypothetical claim about what would be true in general.

やってみないと、向むいているかどうか分わかりませんよ。
You won't know if it suits you unless you give it a go.

That is advice, aimed at the future, and nothing has been experienced yet. Compare the report of an experience already had:

やってみて初はじめて、自分じぶんに向むいていないと分わかりました。
Only after trying it did I work out that it wasn't for me.

～て初めて · ～て初めて vs ～ないと分からない (cont.)

Because of that difference, て初はじめて almost never appears with a future-tense main clause, while ないと分わからない almost always does.

～て初めて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 読よんだ初はじめて、作者さくしゃの気持きもちが分わかった。 → ✅ 読よんで初はじめて、作者さくしゃの気持きもちが分わかった。
    The verb takes the て-form, not た. The past meaning is already carried by the main clause.

  • ❌ 日本にほんに初はじめて来きて、文化ぶんかの違ちがいが分わかった。 → ✅ 日本にほんに来きて初はじめて、文化ぶんかの違ちがいが分わかった。
    Put 初はじめて before the verb and you have said "I came to Japan for the first time"; the "not until" reading is gone.

  • ❌ 卒業そつぎょうして初はじめて、働はたらこうと思おもいます。 → ✅ 卒業そつぎょうしてから、働はたらこうと思おもいます。
    The main clause is a plan, and this pattern needs a realisation. A plan takes てから.

～て初めて · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 話はなして初はじめて、彼かれに電話でんわしてください。 → ✅ 話はなしてから、彼かれに電話でんわしてください。
    Requests and instructions are impossible here for the same reason: you cannot ask somebody to have a realisation.

～て初めて · Practice 1

1. 自分じぶんで料理りょうりを作つくっ____、母ははの大変たいへんさが分わかった。

自分じぶんで料理りょうりを作つくって初はじめて、母ははの大変たいへんさが分わかった。

The second clause is a realisation, and the first is what triggered it - the signature shape of this pattern. The verb sits in the て-form.

～て初めて · Practice 2

2. Choose: 入院にゅういんし（て初はじめて・てから）、家族かぞくのありがたさが分わかった。

入院にゅういんして初はじめて、家族かぞくのありがたさが分わかった。

てから would say only that the gratitude came later in time. て初はじめて adds the point of the sentence: before the hospital stay, the speaker had never felt it at all.

～て初めて · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "It wasn't until I lost my phone that I realised how much I depended on it."

携帯けいたいをなくして初はじめて、どれだけそれに頼たよっていたかが分わかった。

"It wasn't until X" maps almost mechanically onto Xて初はじめて, and the realisation verb goes in the main clause in the past tense.

～て初めて · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 初はじめて海外かいがいで働はたらいて、言葉ことばの壁かべの高たかさを知しりました。（intended: "only after working abroad did I learn how high the language barrier is"）

✅ 海外かいがいで働はたらいて**初はじめて**、言葉ことばの壁かべの高たかさを知しりました。

As written, the sentence says the speaker worked abroad for the first time and then learned something. Moving 初はじめて behind the て-form restores the intended "not until" meaning.

～て初めて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 大おおぜいの前まえで話はなして初はじめて、自分じぶんの発音はつおんの問題もんだいに気きづいた。 (b) 社会しゃかいに出でてから、自分じぶんの発音はつおんの問題もんだいに気きづいた。

(a) makes that one occasion of speaking in front of a crowd the trigger: nothing before it had revealed the problem, and the discovery is presented as sudden and slightly humbling.

(b) simply places the discovery somewhere after the speaker entered working life. It could have taken years, it could have come from anywhere, and no single event is being credited.

～てからでないと

Meaning · unless X is done first, Y is impossible - X is a precondition, not just an earlier step

てからでないと sets a gate. It names something that has to be finished before the rest becomes possible, and then says what cannot happen until it is. The second half of the sentence is always negative in meaning; that is the single fact that makes this pattern behave differently from every other "after".

～てからでないと · Formation

Take the て-form, add からでないと, and finish the sentence with something that cannot happen. Several endings are interchangeable and differ only in register.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (て-form) て-form + からでないと 調しらべる → 調しらべてからでないと
Verb (て-form), formal て-form + からでなければ 確認かくにんする → 確認かくにんしてからでなければ
Verb (て-form), stiff/written て-form + からでなくては 届とどく → 届とどいてからでなくては
Verb (て-form), casual て-form + からじゃないと 食たべる → 食たべてからじゃないと
Main clause must be negative or "impossible" …答こたえられません／無理むりです／だめです
❌ Dictionary form ❌調しらべるからでないと ✅調しらべてからでないと
❌ Positive main clause ❌…てからでないと、できます ✅…てからでないと、できません

～てからでないと · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - write the ending first. If you cannot finish the sentence with できない、無理むりだ、いけない、わからない or another negative, you do not want this pattern; you want plain てから.

～てからでないと · Examples

手てを洗あらってからでないと、食たべてはいけません。
You mustn't eat until you've washed your hands.

予約よやくしてからでないと、入はいれませんよ。
You can't get in unless you book first.

説明書せつめいしょを読よんでからでないと、この機械きかいは使つかえない。
You can't use this machine without reading the manual first.

部長ぶちょうに聞きいてからでないと、お返事へんじできません。
I can't give you an answer until I've asked the manager.

～てからでないと · Examples (cont.)

値段ねだんを調しらべてからじゃないと、注文ちゅうもんできないな。
I can't order it till I've checked the price.

準備じゅんび運動うんどうをしてからでないと、プールに入はいってはいけないことになっている。
The rule is that you may not enter the pool without doing warm-up exercises first.

契約けいやくの内容ないようをすべて確認かくにんしてからでなければ、サインすることはできません。
I cannot sign until I have checked every part of the contract.

子こどもが寝ねてからでないと、自分じぶんの時間じかんなんてまったく取とれないんです。
Honestly, I get no time to myself at all until the children are asleep.

～てからでないと · Nuance & register

The pattern is long, and that length is part of its job: it is a polite way of saying no. A shop assistant, a receptionist or a colleague who cannot commit reaches for it constantly, because it refuses while pointing at the step that would unblock things - 上司じょうしに確認かくにんしてからでないと、お約束やくそくできかねます. Learners who only know できません sound much blunter than they intend.

The four endings form a rough ladder. からじゃないと is casual speech and very common among friends; てからでないと is the neutral all-purpose form; からでなければ and からでなくては lean formal and written, the sort of thing you meet in rules, contracts and announcements. Nothing else changes.

～てからでないと · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note - do not confuse this with ～てからでは, which looks almost identical and means the opposite kind of thing: "once X has happened, it's too late." 事故じこが起おきてからでは遅おそい。 There, X is something you want to avoid; in てからでないと, X is something you want done.

Because the main clause is negative, the pattern often ends up describing rules, and it is very common in the passive-ish ～ことになっている frame that states how things are done at a company, a school or a pool.

～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～てから

Rule of thumb - てから puts two events in order. てからでないと claims the second one is blocked until the first is finished.

シャワーを浴あびてから、出でかけます。
I'll have a shower and then go out.

That is a plan, and nothing is impossible about going out first. Now a genuine gate:

チケットを買かってからでないと、中なかに入はいれません。
You can't go inside until you've bought a ticket.

～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～てから (cont.)

The two are not interchangeable in either direction. Say ❌チケットを買かってからでないと、中なかに入はいれます and the sentence falls apart, because the pattern demands a negative. Say 買かってから入はいります and you have merely described your afternoon.

～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～ないと

Rule of thumb - ないと negates the action: if you don't do X, no Y. てからでないと negates the timing: X has to be finished before Y is even possible.

めがねをかけないと、字じが読よめません。
I can't read the writing without my glasses.

Nothing here is about completion - the glasses are a condition that holds throughout. Compare a case where finishing is the whole point:

試験しけんの結果けっかが出でてからでないと、次つぎの予定よていが立たてられない。
I can't make any further plans until the exam results are out.

～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～ないと (cont.)

In practice, when either would work, てからでないと sounds more considered and more formal, and it stresses order; ないと is shorter and states a bare condition. A useful test: if you can insert "first" into the English, the longer pattern is available.

～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～た上で

Rule of thumb - both are formal and both put a considered step first, but た上うえで leads to a decision that will happen, while てからでないと leads to a refusal.

見積みつもりを確認かくにんした上うえで、ご連絡れんらくいたします。
I'll be in touch once I've gone over the quotation.

見積みつもりを確認かくにんしてからでないと、ご連絡れんらくいたしかねます。
I'm afraid I can't get back to you until I've gone over the quotation.

Same first step, opposite framing. The first sentence promises; the second declines for now. In a business email that difference is very visible, so choose by what you want the reader to take away.

～てからでないと · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 予約よやくしてからでないと、入はいれます。 → ✅ 予約よやくしてからでないと、入はいれません。
    The main clause must be negative. With a positive ending, the sentence contradicts itself.

  • ❌ 注文ちゅうもんするからでないと、作つくれません。 → ✅ 注文ちゅうもんしてからでないと、作つくれません。
    It is built on the て-form. The dictionary form cannot take からでないと.

  • ❌ 病気びょうきになってからでないと遅おそいですよ。 → ✅ 病気びょうきになってからでは遅おそいですよ。
    "Once you're ill it's too late" is the てからでは pattern. でないと would make falling ill a precondition for something, which is not the intent.

～てからでないと · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 春はるになってからでないと、桜さくらが咲さきます。 → ✅ 春はるにならないと、桜さくらは咲さきません。
    Two faults: the ending has to be negative, and a natural process that nobody performs sits better with plain ないと than with the deliberate, step-completing てからでないと.

～てからでないと · Practice 1

1. パスポートを見みせ____、受うけ取とれません。

パスポートを見みせてからでないと、受うけ取とれません。

Showing the passport is the step that unblocks collection, and the ending is already negative, which is exactly the environment this pattern needs.

～てからでないと · Practice 2

2. Choose: 宿題しゅくだいをし（てから・てからでないと）、テレビを見みてはいけません。

宿題しゅくだいをしてからでないと、テレビを見みてはいけません。

The ending 見みてはいけません is a prohibition, so the sentence is setting a condition rather than describing an order of events. Plain てから with a prohibition would be odd, since it would forbid the very sequence it just described.

～てからでないと · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I can't decide until I've talked to my family."

家族かぞくと話はなしてからでないと、決きめられません。

"Until I've…, I can't…" is the pattern's English signature: a completed first step plus an impossibility. The casual version would be 話はなしてからじゃないと決きめられない.

～てからでないと · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 資料しりょうを集あつめてからでないと、レポートが書かけるでしょう。

✅ 資料しりょうを集あつめてからでないと、レポートは書かけないでしょう。

The main clause was positive. Turning 書かける into 書かけない restores the required negative, and moving は onto the report makes the contrast sit naturally.

～てからでないと · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 社長しゃちょうに相談そうだんしてからでないと、お答こたえできません。 (b) 社長しゃちょうに相談そうだんしてから、お答こたえします。

(a) is a refusal, softened by naming the obstacle. Right now the speaker will not answer, and the sentence puts the reason in the listener's hands: nothing moves until the president has been consulted.

(b) is a promise. The speaker commits to answering and merely tells you what has to happen first, so the listener leaves the conversation expecting a reply.

～ついでに

Meaning · while you're doing X anyway, do Y as well - Y gets a free ride

ついでに is the grammar of efficiency. X is the real reason you are going, doing or moving; Y is a smaller job you slip in because the opportunity is already there. If Y is the reason and X is the excuse, you have the two the wrong way round.

～ついでに · Formation

ついで is a noun - "an occasion that happens to arise" - so a clause modifies it in the plain form, and a noun attaches with の.

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary) - not yet done dictionary + ついでに 出でかける → 出でかけるついでに
Verb (た-form) - already under way た-form + ついでに 行いく → 行いったついでに
Action noun Noun + の + ついでに 散歩さんぽ → 散歩さんぽのついでに
On its own, at the head of a sentence ついでに、… ついでに、これもお願ねがい
As a plain noun ついでが+ある／ついでで ついでがあったら寄よります
❌ の after a verb ❌行いくのついでに ✅行いくついでに
❌ Y bigger than X ❌切手きってを買かうついでに引ひっ越こした ✅引ひっ越こすついでに切手きってを買かった

～ついでに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ついでに needs a main errand and a minor extra, in that order, and they must share one outing, one trip or one stretch of activity. The dictionary form means the errand is still ahead of you; the た-form means you are already out.

～ついでに · Examples

買かい物もののついでに、郵便局ゆうびんきょくにも寄よった。
While I was out shopping I stopped by the post office too.

コンビニに行いくついでに、飲のみ物ものを買かってきて。
Grab me a drink while you're at the convenience store.

散歩さんぽのついでに、公園こうえんのごみを拾ひろうようにしている。
I make a point of picking up litter in the park while I'm out walking.

窓まどをふくついでに、カーテンも洗あらってしまおう。
Since I'm cleaning the windows anyway, I may as well wash the curtains.

～ついでに · Examples (cont.)

大阪おおさかに出張しゅっちょうしたついでに、昔むかしの友ともだちに会あってきた。
I was in Osaka on business, so I met up with an old friend while I was there.

病院びょういんに行いったついでに、薬局やっきょくで風邪薬かぜぐすりも買かっておいた。
I picked up some cold medicine at the pharmacy while I was at the hospital anyway.

会議かいぎで東京とうきょうまで来きたついでに、前まえから気きになっていた美術館びじゅつかんをのぞいてきました。
Since the meeting brought me all the way to Tokyo, I looked in on a museum I'd been curious about.

駅えきまで送おくっていくついでに、この荷物にもつを宅配便たくはいびんで出だしてきてもらえると助たすかります。
It'd be a great help if you could drop this parcel off at the courier while you're running me to the station.

～ついでに · Nuance & register

ついでに is register-neutral and enormously common in everyday speech, but it carries a social charge that learners should feel. Attached to a request, it shrinks the favour: コンビニに行いくついでに means "this costs you nothing extra", and that is why it makes an imposition easy to accept. Attached to your own offer, it does the same work in reverse - 「ついでですから、気きにしないでください」 is the standard way to wave off thanks for a lift or a delivery.

～ついでに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The flip side is that calling something a ついで demotes it. Say 結婚式けっこんしきのついでに and you have announced that the wedding was a convenient excuse. Around anything the other person takes seriously - a funeral, a hospital visit, a formal invitation - reach for a neutral phrase such as 帰かえりに or 行いった時ときに instead. Japanese speakers also avoid ついでに when talking about something they did for a superior, since it implies the superior's business was the side job.

The choice between dictionary and た-form is aspect, not politeness. The dictionary form plans: 買かい物ものに行いくついでに寄よろう. The た-form reports from inside the trip: 買かい物ものに行いったついでに寄よった. Both are common, and native speakers slide between them without ceremony.

～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～ながら

Rule of thumb - ながら means the two actions happen at the same time, both by the same person. ついでに means one occasion covers two separate jobs; they can be minutes apart.

音楽おんがくを聞ききながら皿さらを洗あらっています。
I'm doing the washing-up while listening to music.

You genuinely do both at once, so this cannot be ついでに. Now something you cannot do simultaneously:

ゴミを出だしに行いくついでに、ポストに手紙てがみを入いれてきた。
I put the letter in the postbox while I was out taking the rubbish down.

～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～ながら (cont.)

Note also which clause is the main one. With ながら the main action is the second clause; with ついでに the main errand is the first. Swap them and you change what the sentence is about.

～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～途中で

Rule of thumb - 途中とちゅうで is about position on a route: somewhere between start and finish. ついでに is about taking advantage of an occasion, and the extra job does not have to lie on the way.

帰かえる途中とちゅうで本屋ほんやに寄よりました。
I dropped into a bookshop on the way home.

実家じっかに帰かえるついでに、祖母そぼの家いえにも顔かおを出だした。
Since I was going back to my parents' anyway, I looked in on my grandmother too.

～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～途中で (cont.)

The grandmother's house may be a detour of an hour; ついでに does not care, as long as the one trip made it worth doing. 途中とちゅうで would be false if she lived in the opposite direction. Conversely, 帰かえる途中とちゅうで雨あめが降ふってきた is fine and ついでに is impossible there, because rain is not an errand you slip in.

～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～際に

Rule of thumb - 際さいに is a formal "on the occasion of" and says nothing about efficiency. ついでに always implies the second job was tacked on to save a trip - which is why it is out of place in a notice or a polite request to a customer.

ご来店らいてんの**際さいに**、こちらのカードをお見みせください。
Please show this card when you visit the shop.

Replacing that with ご来店らいてんのついでに would tell customers their visit is a side errand - polite in form, insulting in content. Among friends the same information is perfectly friendly:

～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～際に (cont.)

店みせに行いくついでに、このカード見みせてきてよ。
Show them this card while you're at the shop.

So the split is two-way: register (formal versus everyday) and meaning (occasion versus opportunity taken).

～ついでに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 散歩さんぽするのついでに、パンを買かった。 → ✅ 散歩さんぽするついでに、パンを買かった。
    A verb modifies ついで directly, with no の. The の appears only after a noun: 散歩さんぽのついでに.

  • ❌ 切手きってを買かうついでに、引ひっ越こしの手続てつづきをした。 → ✅ 引ひっ越こしの手続てつづきのついでに、切手きってを買かった。
    The bigger errand has to come first. As written, the sentence claims a house move was a minor extra attached to buying stamps.

  • ❌ お見舞みまいのついでに、買かい物ものをしてきました。 → ✅ お見舞みまいの帰かえりに、買かい物ものをしてきました。
    Grammatically fine, socially cold: it says visiting the patient was the convenient excuse. Use 帰かえりに when the first event matters to someone.

～ついでに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 勉強べんきょうするついでに音楽おんがくを聞きいています。 → ✅ 勉強べんきょうしながら音楽おんがくを聞きいています。
    Two activities that overlap continuously need ながら. ついでに wants two distinct jobs sharing one occasion.

～ついでに · Practice 1

1. 銀行ぎんこうに行いく____、この手紙てがみも出だしてきてくれない?

銀行ぎんこうに行いくついでに、この手紙てがみも出だしてきてくれない?

The bank trip is already planned and posting a letter is the small extra, so ついでに frames the request as almost no trouble at all. The verb takes the dictionary form because the errand has not started yet.

～ついでに · Practice 2

2. Choose: 新聞しんぶんを読よみ（ながら・ついでに）コーヒーを飲のんでいた。

新聞しんぶんを読よみながらコーヒーを飲のんでいた。

Reading and drinking happen at the same moment, which is what ながら is for. ついでに would need two separable jobs and would also require the plain form, not the stem.

～ついでに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "Since I was at the library anyway, I returned the book my sister had borrowed."

図書館としょかんに行いったついでに、妹いもうとが借かりていた本ほんも返かえしてきた。

"Since I was there anyway" is the English signature of ついでに. The た-form fits because the speaker is reporting from a trip that already happened.

～ついでに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 結婚式けっこんしきのついでに、京都きょうとを案内あんないしてもらいました。

✅ 結婚式けっこんしきで京都きょうとに行いったついでに、町まちを案内あんないしてもらいました。

The original makes the wedding itself the throwaway errand, which is rude to whoever was getting married. Anchoring ついでに to the trip rather than the ceremony keeps the efficiency meaning without demoting the event.

～ついでに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 東京とうきょうに行いくついでに、叔父おじに会あいます。 (b) 東京とうきょうに行いった時ときに、叔父おじに会あいます。

(a) makes clear that something else is taking the speaker to Tokyo and the uncle is being fitted in around it. Said to the uncle himself, it would be a little tactless.

(b) simply locates the meeting in time - when I'm in Tokyo, I'll see him - and says nothing about which was the reason for the trip. It is the safer sentence in almost any company.

～際に

Meaning · at the time of; on the occasion of - the formal word for "when"

際さいに (read さいに) does the job of 時ときに, but it belongs to a different world: notices, manuals, contracts, announcements and careful business speech. It also asks for a real occasion - something that takes place - rather than any old state of affairs.

～際に · Formation

際さい is a noun, so the clause in front of it takes a plain form, and a noun attaches with の.

～際に · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example

Rule of thumb - two tests. Is the sentence formal enough that 時とき would sound too casual? And is the clause an event rather than a state? Both must be yes. Adjectives never pass the second test.

～際に · Examples

Please press this button when using the machine.

Please watch your step as you get off.

You will need identification when you apply.

面接めんせつを受うけた**際さいに**、給料きゅうりょうのことも聞きいておきました。
I asked about the salary as well when I had the interview.

～際に · Examples (cont.)

海外かいがいへ出張しゅっちょうする**際さいには**、保険ほけんに入はいっておいたほうがいい。
When you travel abroad on business, you'd do well to take out insurance.

契約けいやくを更新こうしんする**際さいには**、三さんか月げつ前まえまでにご連絡れんらくいただく必要ひつようがございます。
If you wish to renew the contract, you must notify us at least three months in advance.

退職たいしょくの**際さいに**お世話せわになった方々かたがたに、一人ひとりずつ手紙てがみを書かきました。
I wrote individually to everyone who helped me when I left the company.

～際に · Nuance & register

This is a formal word and it never stops being one. Put it in a conversation about breakfast and it sounds like a train announcement. The natural homes for it are: station and shop announcements, instruction manuals, application forms, company rules, and speech where you are addressing a customer or a superior.

It pairs constantly with honorific prefixes - ご利用りようの際さい、 お帰かえりの際さい、ご来館らいかんの際さい - because the same register that wants 際さい also wants お／ご. If you are writing a notice for customers, that combination is the default.

～際に · Nuance & register (cont.)

The particle after it shifts the feel slightly. 際さいに points at a moment. 際さいは sets up a condition and is the form you see in warnings - "should this happen, do that". 際さいには is both at once and is very common in careful writing. In printed notices the particle often disappears altogether, leaving a comma: ご記入きにゅうの際さい、 黒くろのペンをお使つかいください。

Note - a close relative, ～に際さいして, means "on the occasion of" in an even weightier way and belongs to N2: 開会かいかいに際さいして一言ひとことご挨拶あいさつ申もうし上あげます。 It marks ceremonies and turning points, not everyday procedures.

～際に · ～際に vs ～時に

Rule of thumb - 時とき takes anything: verbs, adjectives, nouns, states, trivia. 際さい takes only events, and only in formal contexts. Everywhere 際さい works, 時とき also works; the reverse fails constantly.

小ちいさい時とき、よく祖父そふの家いえに泊とまった。
When I was small I often stayed at my grandfather's house.

An adjective and a childhood state - 際さい is impossible here on two counts. Compare a procedure described to a customer:

～際に · ～際に vs ～時に (cont.)

商品しょうひんを返品へんぴんされる**際さいは**、レシートをお持もちください。
When returning an item, please bring your receipt.

There 時とき would be grammatical but would flatten the register; the notice would read as if a friend had scribbled it. The tense rule you learned for 時とき carries over unchanged: dictionary form for "before/as you do it", た-form for "after you have done it".

～際に · ～際に vs ～場合は

Rule of thumb - 際さい assumes the event will happen and tells you what to do at that moment. 場合ばあい says "if it should turn out that…" and branches between possibilities.

雨あめの場合ばあいは、体育館たいいくかんで行おこないます。
If it rains, it will be held in the gym.

Rain may or may not come, and the sentence exists to cover the branch - that is 場合ばあい, and 際さい would be wrong because rain is not an occasion anyone attends. Now something that is definitely going to happen:

～際に · ～際に vs ～場合は (cont.)

ご入場にゅうじょうの**際さいに**、チケットを拝見はいけんいたします。
We will check your ticket as you enter.

Everyone entering will enter, so there is no branch to state. A quick diagnostic: if the English wants "if", think 場合ばあい; if it wants "when", think 際さい. Both are formal, so register does not separate them.

～際に · ～際に vs ～た上で

Rule of thumb - both live in the same formal register, but 際さいに locates something at the time of an event, while た上うえで puts it after the event and makes it depend on the result.

お申もうし込こみの**際さいに**、ご希望きぼうの日ひをお書かきください。
Please write your preferred date when you apply.

お申もうし込こみ内容ないようを確認かくにんした上うえで、ご連絡れんらくいたします。
We will contact you once we have checked the details of your application.

～際に · ～際に vs ～た上で (cont.)

In the first, the writing happens during the application. In the second, the contact happens afterwards because of what the check revealed. Swapping them produces sentences that are still grammatical but say something the writer did not mean, which is why forms and letters keep the two carefully apart.

～際に · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 学生がくせいの際さいに、アルバイトをたくさんしました。 → ✅ 学生がくせいの時ときに、アルバイトをたくさんしました。
    "Being a student" is a state that lasted for years, not an occasion. 際さい needs a bounded event.

  • ❌ 眠ねむい際さいには、コーヒーを飲のみます。 → ✅ 眠ねむい時ときには、コーヒーを飲のみます。
    Adjectives cannot modify 際さい at all. This is the most reliable formal test of the two words.

  • ❌ ゲームをする際さいに、いつも夜更よふかししてしまう。 → ✅ ゲームをする時ときに、いつも夜更よふかししてしまう。
    Grammatical, but the register clashes: a casual admission in the vocabulary of a safety notice. Keep 際さい for formal writing and speech.

～際に · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 台風たいふうが来くる際さいは、授業じゅぎょうは休やすみになります。 → ✅ 台風たいふうが来くた場合ばあいは、授業じゅぎょうは休やすみになります。
    The sentence sets out a contingency, so it needs 場合ばあい. 際さい would presuppose that the typhoon is coming.

～たびに

Meaning · every time X happens, Y happens - without exception, as far as the speaker has seen

たびに counts occasions, not intervals. Each time the first event comes round, the second one comes with it. It is the pattern you reach for when a pairing has become reliable enough to comment on: every time I see him, every time it rains, every time that song comes on.

～たびに · Formation

たび is the noun 度たび, "an occurrence", so a clause modifies it in the plain form and a noun attaches with の. The verb is in the dictionary form no matter when the events happened.

～たびに · Formation (cont.)

Attaches to Form Example
Verb (dictionary) dictionary + たびに 会あう → 会あうたびに
Action noun Noun + の + たびに 試験しけん → 試験しけんのたびに
Written style ～度たびに 見みる度たびに
As a connector そのたびに …。そのたびに注意ちゅういしている
❌ た-form ❌会あったたびに ✅会あうたびに
❌ Noun without の ❌試験しけんたびに ✅試験しけんのたびに
❌ A measured interval ❌三日みっかたびに ✅三日みっかごとに

～たびに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - if you can put "every time" in front of an English clause, you want たびに. If what follows "every" is a number plus a unit - every three days, every two rows - you want ごとに instead.

～たびに · Examples

彼かれに会あうたびに、元気げんきをもらう。
Every time I see him I come away with more energy.

この歌うたを聞きくたびに、高校こうこう時代じだいを思おもい出だす。
Every time I hear this song I think of my high-school days.

旅行りょこうのたびに、同おなじかばんを使つかっています。
I use the same bag every time I travel.

台風たいふうが来くるたびに、この辺へんは停電ていでんする。
Every time a typhoon comes through, this area loses power.

～たびに · Examples (cont.)

甥おいは会あうたびに背せが伸のびていて、おどろかされる。
My nephew is taller every time I see him - it's startling.

引ひっ越こしのたびに、本ほんを何なん箱ばこも捨すてることになる。
Every move ends with me throwing out several boxes of books.

祖母そぼは私わたしが訪たずねるたびに、冷蔵庫れいぞうこの中なかのものを全部ぜんぶ持もたせようとする。
Every time I visit, my grandmother tries to send me home with the entire contents of her fridge.

担当者たんとうしゃが変かわるたびに同おなじ説明せつめいをやり直なおさなければならず、正直しょうじきうんざりしています。
Having to start the whole explanation again every time they change our contact person is, frankly, wearing me down.

～たびに · Nuance & register

たびに is register-neutral: it appears in casual speech, in polite speech and in writing without changing shape, and the kanji spelling 度たび is simply a written-style choice. What it does carry is a mild emotional charge. The speaker has noticed the pattern and usually has a feeling about it - affection, irritation, nostalgia, surprise. Sentences with たびに very often end in 思おもい出だす, うれしくなる, 困こまる or a passive of surprise.

That is also why it loves change over time. With a comparative or a verb of growth, たびに produces the "more and more each time" reading: 読よむたびに新あたらしい発見はっけんがある。 Nothing extra is added to the grammar; the repetition does the work.

～たびに · Nuance & register (cont.)

そのたびに is worth learning as a fixed connector for narrating: you describe a recurring situation in one sentence, then open the next with そのたびに - "and each time that happened…". It saves you repeating the whole clause.

One limit: because the pattern generalises, it does not fit a single future occasion. 来週らいしゅう会あうたびに makes no sense, because you are meeting once.

～たびに · ～たびに vs ～ごとに

Rule of thumb - たびに counts events; ごとに counts units. If a number and a counter come before it, use ごとに; if a clause comes before it, use たびに.

バスは十五分じゅうごふんごとに出でています。
The buses run every fifteen minutes.

You cannot say ❌十五分じゅうごふんたびに, because fifteen minutes is a measured interval rather than an occasion. The other direction fails just as hard:

母ははは電話でんわするたびに、同おなじ話はなしをする。
Every time I ring, my mother tells me the same story.

～たびに · ～たびに vs ～ごとに (cont.)

Here the phone calls happen when they happen - there is no fixed interval - and ごとに would be wrong. There is a small overlap with nouns: 試合しあいのたびに and 試合しあいごとに are both possible, but the first means "each time there is a match" and the second is closer to "match by match", tracking a change across a series.

～たびに · ～たびに vs ～と

Rule of thumb - と states an automatic consequence: this causes that, every time, as a matter of how the world works. たびに merely reports that the two keep coming together, and it can carry the speaker's reaction.

春はるになると、この川かわの水みずが増ふえます。
When spring comes, the water in this river rises.

That is nature, not commentary. Compare an observation with a person in it:

父ちちは酒さけを飲のむたびに、若わかいころの話はなしを始はじめる。
Every time my father drinks, he starts on stories about his youth.

～たびに · ～たびに vs ～と (cont.)

と would also be grammatical there, but it would present the storytelling as a mechanical reflex. たびに keeps the speaker in the sentence - you can hear the raised eyebrow. Conversely, と accepts states and conditions (安やすいと売うれる) where たびに needs a repeatable event.

～たびに · ～たびに vs ～時に

Rule of thumb - 時ときに picks out one occasion, or is neutral about how many. たびに insists on all of them.

京都きょうとへ行いった時ときに、この茶碗ちゃわんを買かいました。
I bought this teacup when I went to Kyoto.

One trip, one purchase. Now the generalising version:

京都きょうとへ行いくたびに、同おなじ店みせで器うつわを買かってしまう。
Every time I go to Kyoto I end up buying pottery at the same shop.

～たびに · ～たびに vs ～時に (cont.)

The tense rule also differs. 時とき chooses between dictionary and た-form to show whether the main clause happens before or after it; たびに has no such choice, because it is always the dictionary form.

～たびに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 日本にほんに行いったたびに、温泉おんせんに入はいります。 → ✅ 日本にほんに行いくたびに、温泉おんせんに入はいります。
    たびに always follows the dictionary form. The past reading, if any, comes from the main clause.

  • ❌ 会議かいぎたびに、資料しりょうを作つくり直なおす。 → ✅ 会議かいぎのたびに、資料しりょうを作つくり直なおす。
    たび is a noun, so another noun has to be linked with の.

  • ❌ 二年にねんたびに、車くるまを買かい替かえている。 → ✅ 二年にねんごとに、車くるまを買かい替かえている。
    A number plus a unit of time is an interval, and intervals take ごとに or おきに, never たびに.

  • ❌ 明日あした先生せんせいに会あうたびに、この話はなしをします。 → ✅ 明日あした先生せんせいに会あった時ときに、この話はなしをします。
    One scheduled meeting is not a repeated occasion, so there is nothing for たびに to generalise over.

～たびに · Practice 5

～ごとに

Meaning · each; every; at intervals of - one unit at a time, with none skipped

ごとに divides something into equal units and says that whatever follows applies to every one of them. The units can be measured - three days, five metres - or they can be categories: each country, each department, each class. Either way, nothing is left out and nothing is skipped over.

～ごとに · Formation

ごと is a suffix. It attaches straight to a quantity or to a noun, with no particle in between.

Attaches to Form Example
Number + counter (time) Quantity + ごとに 三日みっか → 三日みっかごとに
Number + counter (distance, things) Quantity + ごとに 五ごメートル → 五ごメートルごとに
Noun as a category Noun + ごとに 国くに → 国くにごとに
Verb (dictionary), literary dictionary + ごとに 年としを取とる → 年としを取とるごとに
Before a noun ～ごとの + Noun 部署ぶしょごとの予算よさん
❌ の between noun and ごと ❌国くにのごとに ✅国くにごとに
❌ な before a noun ❌一年いちねんごとな検査けんさ ✅一年いちねんごとの検査けんさ
❌ Doubling with 毎まい～ ❌毎日まいにちごとに ✅毎日まいにち / 一日いちにちごとに

～ごとに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - ごとに states the length of the cycle, counting the unit itself. 一日いちにちごとに is therefore every day, and 二日ふつかごとに is every second day. Do not translate ごと as "other".

～ごとに · Examples

水みずは二日ふつかごとにやってください。
Please water it every two days.

国くにごとに習慣しゅうかんが違ちがいます。
Customs differ from country to country.

クラスごとに写真しゃしんを撮とります。
We'll take the photographs class by class.

この検査けんさは半年はんとしごとに受うけることになっている。
You're supposed to have this check-up every six months.

～ごとに · Examples (cont.)

道みちの両側りょうがわに、五ごメートルごとに木きが植うえてある。
Trees have been planted every five metres along both sides of the road.

部署ぶしょごとに予算よさんが決きめられているので、勝手かってには使つかえません。
The budget is set department by department, so I can't just spend it as I like.

年としを取とるごとに、時間じかんが過すぎるのが速はやく感かんじられるようになった。
With each passing year, time seems to move faster.

この会社かいしゃではプロジェクトごとにチームを組くみ直なおすので、同おなじ顔かおぶれで働はたらくことは少すくない。
This company rebuilds its teams for each project, so you rarely work with the same faces twice.

～ごとに · Nuance & register

ごとに is neutral and slightly businesslike. It is the word of timetables, manuals, survey results and internal rules - anything that has to state a rate or a breakdown precisely - and it sounds perfectly normal in speech too. It is not formal in the way 際さいに is; it is simply exact.

The two jobs it does feel quite different in use. With a number it gives a rate, and English usually says "every X". With a category noun it gives a breakdown, and English switches to "by" or "each": 地域ちいきごとに → "region by region"; 家庭かていごとに → "in each household". That second use is extremely common in reports, almost always paired with 違ちがう, 異ことなる or 決きめる.

～ごとに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The verb form - 見みるごとに、会あうごとに - is real but literary, and in ordinary speech たびに has taken the job over. You can safely read it and avoid writing it.

Note - a different ごと exists, with no に, meaning "together with the whole of it": りんごを皮かわごと食たべる, "eat the apple skin and all"; 丸まるごと, "the whole thing". It attaches to a noun exactly like ours, so read the sentence, not the suffix - if there is no に and no counting, it is this other word.

～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～おきに

Rule of thumb - ごとに measures the cycle; おきに measures the gap left empty between one occurrence and the next. With days and countable things the two therefore differ by one.

一日いちにちごとに記録きろくをつけています。
I keep a record every day.

一日いちにちおきに記録きろくをつけています。
I keep a record every other day.

Same number, different arithmetic: the first is Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday; the second is Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Rewritten with ごとに, the second sentence becomes 二日ふつかごとに.

～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～おきに (cont.)

With units of duration or length the two happen to coincide, because there is no "container" to leave empty: 十分じゅっぷんごとに and 十分じゅっぷんおきに both describe something happening every ten minutes. That coincidence is exactly why learners generalise from minutes to days and get it wrong. When the unit is a day, a week, a seat or an item, stop and check which one you mean.

ごとに also has a whole second life that おきに does not: the category use. 学年がくねんごとに時間割じかんわりが違ちがう is fine, and ❌学年がくねんおきに would mean skipping alternate year groups.

～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～たびに

Rule of thumb - ごとに follows a unit; たびに follows a clause describing an event. "Every three days" is ごとに; "every time I go" is たびに.

試験しけんのたびに胃いが痛いたくなる。
My stomach hurts every time there's an exam.

試験しけんごとに成績せいせきを記録きろくしています。
We record the results for each exam.

Both are grammatical, and the pair shows the difference cleanly. The first counts occasions and reports the speaker's reaction to all of them. The second treats the exams as a series of units and does something per unit - a breakdown, not a reaction. That is why ごとに is at home in a spreadsheet and たびに is at home in a diary.

～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～ずつ

Rule of thumb - ずつ distributes an amount: how much each one gets. ごとに marks the unit itself: for each of these, such-and-such is true.

一人ひとりに二ふたつずつ配くばってください。
Please hand out two to each person.

The number that matters there is the two. Now a sentence where the number that matters is the unit:

三人さんにんごとにグループを作つくってください。
Please form groups of three.

A useful diagnostic: ずつ answers "how many each?", ごとに answers "per what?". They can appear together - 一人ひとりごとに一ひとつずつ渡わたす - and when they do, each is doing its own half of the job.

～ごとに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 毎週まいしゅうごとに会議かいぎがあります。 → ✅ 毎週まいしゅう会議かいぎがあります。
    毎まい～ already means "every", so adding ごとに says it twice. Use one or the other.

  • ❌ 一日いちにちごとに薬くすりを飲のみます。（intending every other day） → ✅ 一日いちにちおきに薬くすりを飲のみます。
    ごとに counts the cycle, so 一日いちにちごとに is every day. "Every other" needs おきに, or 二日ふつかごとに.

  • ❌ 三年さんねんごとな点検てんけんが必要ひつようです。 → ✅ 三年さんねんごとの点検てんけんが必要ひつようです。
    Before a noun the linker is の. ごと is not a な-adjective.

～ごとに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ プリントを一いちまいごとに配くばってください。 → ✅ プリントを一いちまいずつ配くばってください。
    The sentence is about how many each person receives, which is ずつ. ごとに would make each sheet a unit that something is done per.

～ごとに · Practice 1

1. この町まちでは、三十分さんじゅっぷん____バスが来きます。

A number plus a unit of time gives a rate, which is what ごとに is for. With minutes, おきに would mean the same thing, since there is no whole unit being skipped.

～ごとに · Practice 2

2. Choose. 練習れんしゅうは月げつ・水すい・金きんにあります。つまり、二日ふつか（ごとに・おきに）練習れんしゅうがあります。

つまり、二日ふつかごとに練習れんしゅうがあります。

Monday, Wednesday and Friday leave one day empty, which makes the cycle two days long. 二日ふつかおきに would leave two days empty and give Monday, Thursday, Sunday; the equivalent of the correct answer is 一日いちにちおきに.

This is the category use: sizes are the units, and the statement holds for each of them. 違ちがう and 異ことなる are the verbs this use attracts.

～から～にかけて

Meaning · from roughly A through to roughly B - one continuous stretch, with soft edges

から～にかけて draws a band across time or across a map. Something goes on right the way through it, and the two ends are deliberately approximate: not "from exactly here to exactly there", but "somewhere around here to somewhere around there". Weather forecasts and history books run on it.

～から～にかけて · Formation

Both halves take nouns. The pattern is a pair, and neither half works alone.

Attaches to Form Example
Time expressions Noun + から + Noun + にかけて 夕方ゆうがた → 夕方ゆうがたから夜よるにかけて
Places Noun + から + Noun + にかけて 関東かんとう → 関東かんとうから東北とうほくにかけて
Dates, months, seasons Noun + から + Noun + にかけて 三月さんがつから四月しがつにかけて
Eras Noun + から + Noun + にかけて 昭和しょうわから平成へいせいにかけて
Before a noun ～から～にかけての + Noun 春はるから夏なつにかけての天気てんき
❌ Exact clock times ❌九時くじから五時ごじにかけて働はたらく ✅九時くじから五時ごじまで働はたらく
❌ にかけて alone in this sense ❌夜よるにかけて雨あめが降ふった ✅夕方ゆうがたから夜よるにかけて雨あめが降ふった

～から～にかけて · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - the thing you describe must spread across the whole band, not fill it exactly. If you could put a stopwatch on it, use から～まで.

～から～にかけて · Examples

夕方ゆうがたから夜よるにかけて、雨あめが強つよくなるでしょう。
The rain will get heavier from evening into the night.

三月さんがつから四月しがつにかけて、引ひっ越こしの予約よやくが取とりにくくなる。
Removal firms get hard to book anywhere from March through April.

関東かんとうから東北とうほくにかけて、大雪おおゆきが降ふっています。
Heavy snow is falling across the Kanto and Tohoku regions.

去年きょねんの冬ふゆから今年ことしの春はるにかけて、かぜが流行はやった。
Colds went round from last winter into this spring.

～から～にかけて · Examples (cont.)

駅えきの北口きたぐちから公園こうえんにかけて、桜さくらの木きが続つづいている。
Cherry trees run all the way from the station's north exit to the park.

昭和しょうわの終おわりから平成へいせいの初はじめにかけて、この町まちの人口じんこうは大おおきく減へった。
This town's population fell sharply from the end of the Showa era into the early Heisei years.

今週こんしゅうの水曜日すいようびから金曜日きんようびにかけて、工事こうじのため水みずが止とまる時間じかんがあります。
There will be periods without water from Wednesday through Friday this week owing to building work.

明あけ方がたから昼前ひるまえにかけての風かぜが強つよく、電車でんしゃが何度なんどか止とまった。
The wind between dawn and late morning was strong enough to stop the trains several times.

～から～にかけて · Nuance & register

The register is neutral to slightly formal. You will hear it every evening on the weather forecast, read it in newspapers and see it in official notices, and it is perfectly natural in careful conversation. It is not a stiff pattern; it is a precise-sounding one, which is why announcements like it.

What it will not tolerate is a sharp edge. から～まで says "these are the boundaries"; から～にかけて says "roughly this stretch". That is why clock times are wrong with it and vaguer time words - 夕方ゆうがた、夜中よなか、 週末しゅうまつ、春はる - are right. For the same reason the main clause usually describes something spread out or repeated: rain falling, prices rising, trees continuing, colds going round. A single completed action inside the band does not fit.

～から～にかけて · Nuance & register (cont.)

The の form is worth practising, because it is how the pattern gets in front of a noun: 夏なつから秋あきにかけての台風たいふう、 夕方ゆうがたから夜よるにかけての混雑こんざつ. Note that no な is possible.

Note - the same にかけて turns up in a completely unrelated sense, "when it comes to", where it normally carries は and praises somebody's ability: 計算けいさんにかけては誰だれにも負まけない。 The giveaway is the missing から: no から, no span.

～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～から～まで

Rule of thumb - まで gives a boundary; にかけて gives a band. まで can be measured and can end an action; にかけて is vague at both ends and describes what is going on across the middle.

銀行ぎんこうは九時くじから三時さんじまで開あいています。
The bank is open from nine until three.

Those are exact, and the sentence would be nonsense with にかけて. Now something whose edges nobody can pin down:

夜中よなかから朝あさにかけて、気温きおんが下さがり続つづけた。
The temperature kept falling from the small hours through to morning.

～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～から～まで (cont.)

There is a second, quieter difference. まで takes an endpoint that the action reaches and stops at - 駅えきまで歩あるく. にかけて never means "as far as and then stop"; it means "and everywhere in between", which is why 東京とうきょうから大阪おおさかにかけて describes a region, not a journey.

～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～の間に

Rule of thumb - 間あいだに puts a single event somewhere inside a period. にかけて says the event occupies the period.

夏休なつやすみの間あいだに、運転免許うんてんめんきょを取とりました。
I got my driving licence during the summer holidays.

One licence, obtained at one point inside the holiday. にかけて would be wrong, because you cannot spread a single acquisition across the whole span. The pattern wants something continuous or repeated:

七月しちがつから九月くがつにかけて、台風たいふうが三みっつも上陸じょうりくした。
Three separate typhoons made landfall between July and September.

～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～の間に (cont.)

Three events distributed across the band is exactly right; one event at a point inside it is 間あいだに.

～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～にわたって

Rule of thumb - にわたって emphasises how much was covered - the scale of the span. にかけて emphasises where the span runs, from about here to about there.

工事こうじは三年さんねんにわたって続つづきました。
The work went on for three whole years.

That names a duration, not two endpoints, and it sounds impressed by the length. Compare:

去年きょねんの秋あきから今年ことしの春はるにかけて、工事こうじが続つづきました。
The work went on from last autumn through to this spring.

～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～にわたって (cont.)

にわたって is also the more formal of the two and is common in written reports. When a sentence gives you both a start and an end, にかけて is usually the natural choice; when it gives you a total, reach for にわたって.

～から～にかけて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 九時くじから五時ごじにかけて働はたらいています。 → ✅ 九時くじから五時ごじまで働はたらいています。
    Clock times are exact boundaries, and this pattern needs vague ones. Working hours take まで.

  • ❌ 夜よるにかけて雨あめが降ふりました。 → ✅ 夕方ゆうがたから夜よるにかけて雨あめが降ふりました。
    The から half is not optional in this sense. Without it, にかけて is read as the unrelated "when it comes to" pattern, or simply fails.

  • ❌ 七月しちがつから八月はちがつにかけて北海道ほっかいどうへ旅行りょこうしました。 → ✅ 七月しちがつから八月はちがつにかけて北海道ほっかいどうを回まわりました。
    A single trip taken at some point in the band needs の間あいだに. If the travelling itself filled the two months, a verb like 回まわる makes that continuous sense explicit.

～から～にかけて · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 春はるから夏なつにかけてな天気てんきは変かわりやすい。 → ✅ 春はるから夏なつにかけての天気てんきは変かわりやすい。
    Before a noun the linker is の. There is no な form.

～から～にかけて · Practice 1

1. 今夜こんやから明日あすの朝あさ____、雪ゆきが積つもるおそれがあります。

A weather warning across a stretch with soft edges is the pattern's home ground, and snow accumulating is a continuous process rather than a single act.

Opening hours are exact boundaries, and にかけて cannot take them. Vagueness is not a stylistic preference here - it is a requirement of the pattern.

～から～にかけて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 六月ろくがつから七月しちがつにかけて、雨あめの日ひが多おおかった。 (b) 六月ろくがつと七月しちがつは雨あめの日ひが多おおかった。

(a) treats the two months as one unbroken wet stretch, with the rain running through the join rather than stopping at the end of June. The edges are fuzzy on purpose.

(b) reports two separate months, each of which happened to be wet. It leaves open that the pattern of weather in the two was quite different, and it draws no line between them.

～にかけて

Meaning · when it comes to X - narrow the field to this one thing, and the claim that follows is absolute

Used on its own, にかけて - nearly always with は - picks out a single area of skill or quality and then makes a strong claim inside it: nobody better, no competition, complete confidence. The same string also closes the paired span pattern から～にかけて, and telling the two apart is half of what this page is for.

～にかけて · Formation

にかけて follows a noun. A verb has to be turned into a noun first, normally with こと.

Attaches to Form Example
Noun (a field or skill) Noun + にかけては 料理りょうり → 料理りょうりにかけては
Verb, nominalised dictionary + こと + にかけては 走はしる → 走はしることにかけては
Fixed superlative frame ～にかけては…の右みぎに出でる者ものはいない 父ちちの右みぎに出でる者ものはいない
Swearing on something Noun + にかけて + 誓ちかう／保証ほしょうする 名誉めいよにかけて誓ちかう
Span (needs から) Noun + から + Noun + にかけて 朝あさから昼ひるにかけて
❌ Verb without こと ❌泳およぐにかけては ✅泳およぐことにかけては
❌ Span without から ❌夜よるにかけて降ふった ✅夕方ゆうがたから夜よるにかけて降ふった

～にかけて · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - look for から. If there is a から earlier in the sentence, にかけて is closing a span. If there is a は attached to it and no から, it means "when it comes to".

～にかけて · Examples

走はしることにかけては、クラスで一番いちばんだ。
When it comes to running, he's the best in the class.

記憶力きおくりょくにかけては、誰だれにも負まけない。
When it comes to memory, I'm second to none.

あの店みせは、値段ねだんの安やすさにかけては町まちで一番いちばんだ。
For sheer cheapness, that shop beats anything in town.

魚さかなをさばくことにかけては、父ちちの右みぎに出でる者ものはいない。
Nobody can touch my father when it comes to filleting fish.

～にかけて · Examples (cont.)

技術ぎじゅつにかけては自信じしんがありますが、営業えいぎょうはまったく苦手にがてです。
I'm confident about the technical side, but I'm hopeless at sales.

彼女かのじょは数字すうじにかけては本当ほんとうに強つよく、会議かいぎでも即座そくざに答こたえられる。
She is genuinely formidable with numbers and can answer on the spot even in meetings.

この品質ひんしつについては、会社かいしゃの名なにかけて保証ほしょういたします。
We guarantee this quality on the company's name.

昼ひるすぎから夕方ゆうがたにかけて、会場かいじょうの前まえに長ながい列れつができていた。
From early afternoon into the evening there was a long queue outside the venue.

～にかけて · Nuance & register

The "when it comes to" use is emphatic by nature. It exists to set up a superlative, so the main clause almost always contains something like 一番いちばん、誰だれにも負まけない、右みぎに出でる者ものはいない or 自信じしんがある. A flat, neutral statement after にかけては sounds unfinished.

It is normally praise, and praising yourself with it is fine in Japanese in a way that a bare boast would not be, because the pattern explicitly limits the claim to one narrow area - the implication is "only here". That is why 技術ぎじゅつにかけては自信じしんがあります works well in a job interview. Speakers do turn it ironic - 忘わすれることにかけては天才てんさいだ, "a genius at forgetting things" - and the joke works precisely because the frame is built for compliments.

～にかけて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral, leaning a little literary; you will meet it in profiles, introductions and reviews more than in idle chat. The に is fixed and the は is close to obligatory in this sense - にかけて without は is usually either the span pattern or the "swearing on" one.

Note - the oath use is a small, fixed set: 命いのちにかけて、名誉めいよにかけて、社しゃの信用しんようにかけて. It is dramatic and formal, at home in speeches, courtroom scenes and corporate apologies, and it always attaches to a verb of promising or guaranteeing.

～にかけて · ～にかけては vs ～について・～に関して

Rule of thumb - について introduces a subject matter and is neutral about what comes next. にかけては introduces an arena of ability and commits you to a claim of superiority inside it.

地震じしんについて、専門家せんもんかが説明せつめいしました。
An expert gave an explanation about earthquakes.

Nothing there is being ranked, and にかけては would be nonsense. Compare a sentence built to rank:

地震じしんの研究けんきゅうにかけては、彼かれは国内こくないで屈指くっしの存在そんざいだ。
When it comes to earthquake research, he is one of the leading figures in the country.

～にかけて · ～にかけては vs ～について・～に関して (cont.)

A quick test: if you could continue with 説明せつめいします or 話はなします, use について. If you want to continue with "he's the best", use にかけては.

～にかけて · ～にかけては vs ～は・～では

Rule of thumb - plain は or では also narrows the frame, but softly. にかけては narrows it hard and signals that a strong claim is coming.

漢字かんじは得意とくいです。
I'm good at kanji.

漢字かんじにかけては、クラスの誰だれにも負まけません。
When it comes to kanji, I won't lose to anyone in the class.

～にかけて · ～にかけては vs ～は・～では (cont.)

Both restrict the topic; only the second promises a superlative. The consequence is practical: にかけては cannot be followed by a modest or a negative evaluation. ❌漢字かんじにかけては苦手にがてです is wrong, and the fix is simply 漢字かんじは苦手にがてです. If what you want to say is "in this area I'm weak", the plain topic particle is the whole answer.

～にかけて · ～にかけて（span）vs ～にかけては（ability）

Rule of thumb - the span sense needs a partner から and two nouns of time or place. The ability sense needs one noun and は. They never overlap in a real sentence.

水曜日すいようびから木曜日もくようびにかけて、雨あめが続つづくそうだ。
They say the rain will continue from Wednesday into Thursday.

天気てんきの予想よそうにかけては、祖父そふはテレビの予報よほうより当あたる。
When it comes to predicting the weather, my grandfather beats the television.

Two nouns and a から in the first; one noun and a は in the second. If you ever meet a bare にかけて with neither marker, it is almost certainly the oath use, and the verb at the end of the sentence will tell you so.

～にかけて · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 泳およぐにかけては、兄あにが一番いちばんだ。 → ✅ 泳およぐことにかけては、兄あにが一番いちばんだ。
    にかけて follows a noun. A verb needs こと to become one.

  • ❌ 料理りょうりにかけては下手へたです。 → ✅ 料理りょうりは下手へたです。
    The pattern sets up a boast. Following it with an admission of weakness contradicts the frame it just built.

  • ❌ 日本にほんの歴史れきしにかけて研究けんきゅうしています。 → ✅ 日本にほんの歴史れきしについて研究けんきゅうしています。
    "Researching about X" is subject matter, not ranked ability, so it needs について.

  • ❌ 夜よるにかけて勉強べんきょうしました。 → ✅ 夕方ゆうがたから夜よるにかけて勉強べんきょうしました。
    The span sense is a two-part pattern. Without から there is no span, and the sentence reads as a broken version of the ability use.

The sentence contrasts a weakness with a strength, and the strength is being claimed emphatically inside one narrow area - exactly the environment for にかけては. Note the こと turning the verb into a noun.

"When it comes to" plus a comparison against everyone else is the pattern's signature. The verb becomes a noun with こと, and the main clause carries the superlative.

～にかけて · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 朝あさにかけて強つよい風かぜが吹ふくでしょう。

✅ 夜中よなかから朝あさにかけて強つよい風かぜが吹ふくでしょう。

In its span sense, にかけて must be paired with から. One noun on its own gives the reader no band, and a forecast needs both ends.

～にかけて · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 彼かれは運転うんてんにかけてはプロ並なみだ。 (b) 彼かれは運転うんてんが上手じょうずだ。

(a) fences off driving as one specific arena and then makes a strong claim inside it. The fencing carries an implication: in other areas he may be quite ordinary, and the speaker is not commenting on those.

(b) is a plain compliment with no such implication and no superlative force. It would suit small talk, whereas (a) sounds like a considered verdict.

～ぶりに

Meaning · for the first time in - that much time has passed since it last happened, and now it has happened again

ぶりに measures a gap. You attach it to a length of time, and the sentence says two things at once: the last time was that long ago, and it has just happened again. Both halves matter - if it has not happened again, ぶりに is the wrong word.

～ぶりに · Formation

ぶり is a suffix. It attaches directly to a quantity of time, with no particle in between, and takes に when it modifies a verb.

Attaches to Form Example
Length of time + verb Quantity + ぶりに 三年さんねん → 三年さんねんぶりに会あう
As a predicate Quantity + ぶりだ／です 十年じゅうねんぶりです
Before a noun Quantity + ぶりの + Noun 五年ごねんぶりの優勝ゆうしょう
Vague length 久ひさし + ぶりに 久ひさしぶりに話はなした
As a greeting お久ひさしぶりです -
❌ な before a noun ❌三年さんねんぶりな再会さいかい ✅三年さんねんぶりの再会さいかい
❌ Event noun ❌試験しけんぶりに ✅試験しけんの日ひ以来いらい

～ぶりに · Formation (cont.)

Rule of thumb - what goes in front of ぶり is a stretch of time, never an event. 三年さんねんぶり and 久ひさしぶり are fine; ❌卒業そつぎょうぶり is not. For an event, use ～以来いらい.

～ぶりに · Examples

三年さんねんぶりに国くにへ帰かえった。
I went home for the first time in three years.

久ひさしぶりに映画えいがを見みに行いった。
I went to the cinema for the first time in ages.

一週間いっしゅうかんぶりに晴はれましたね。
It's the first clear day in a week, isn't it?

十年じゅうねんぶりに中学ちゅうがくの友ともだちから連絡れんらくが来きた。
A friend from junior high got in touch after ten years.

～ぶりに · Examples (cont.)

うちのチームは五年ごねんぶりの優勝ゆうしょうを果はたした。
Our team took the title for the first time in five years.

今日きょうは三十五度さんじゅうごどを超こえて、八年はちねんぶりの暑あつさだそうだ。
It's over thirty-five degrees today - apparently the hottest in eight years.

忙いそがしくて、半年はんとしぶりに美容院びよういんへ行いったら、担当たんとうの人ひとが変かわっていた。
Work kept me away, and when I finally got to the hairdresser after six months my usual stylist had gone.

退院たいいんして、二にか月げつぶりに自分じぶんのベッドで眠ねむることができた。
I came out of hospital and slept in my own bed for the first time in two months.

～ぶりに · Nuance & register

ぶりに is register-neutral and extremely common in ordinary speech. It carries a small lift of feeling - pleasure, relief, or simply the sense that a long stretch has been broken. Sports reporting loves it (三十年さんじゅうねんぶりの優勝ゆうしょう), weather reports love it (十年じゅうねんぶりの大雪おおゆき), and so does anyone greeting an old friend.

The greeting forms are worth learning as fixed items. 久ひさしぶり! is casual, お久ひさしぶりです is the everyday polite version, and in business writing the formal equivalent is ご無沙汰ぶさたしております, which does not use ぶり at all. Note the shape: the greeting has no に, because it is a predicate - お久ひさしぶりです, not ❌お久ひさしぶりにです. The に appears only when ぶり is modifying a verb.

～ぶりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Speakers happily stretch the pattern for comic effect over absurdly short intervals - 五分ごふんぶりだね, "long time no see (five minutes)". That is a joke, and it works because the pattern normally implies a genuinely long gap.

Note - a different ぶり means "the manner of doing something": 仕事しごとぶり, 話はなしぶり, 飲のみっぷり. It attaches to a verb stem or an activity noun rather than to a length of time, and it never takes the "for the first time in" reading. 彼かれの仕事しごとぶりには感心かんしんした is about how he works, not about how long it had been.

～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～後に

Rule of thumb - 後ごに counts forward from a moment already in the story. ぶりに counts backward to the last time the same thing happened.

卒業そつぎょうの三年さんねん後ごに、彼かれは会社かいしゃを作つくった。
Three years after graduating, he started a company.

The three years run from graduation to the founding - two different events. Now the same number doing the other job:

三年さんねんぶりに同おなじ店みせで待まち合あわせた。
We met up at the same place for the first time in three years.

～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～後に (cont.)

Here the three years run from the previous meeting to this one - the same event twice. That is the whole distinction, and it is why 三年さんねん後ごに会あった and 三年さんねんぶりに会あった describe quite different situations. Note the reading too: in this counting sense, 後ご is read ご, not あと.

～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs 久しぶりに

Rule of thumb - same pattern, different precision. 久ひさしぶりに is the version for when you do not want to name a number, and it is far more frequent in conversation.

久ひさしぶりに母ははの声こえを聞きいて、ほっとした。
Hearing my mother's voice after so long was a relief.

四年よねんぶりに母ははに会あい、思おもっていたより元気げんきそうで安心あんしんした。
I saw my mother for the first time in four years, and she looked better than I'd expected.

～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs 久しぶりに (cont.)

Naming the number makes the sentence a small piece of news, which is why headlines and announcements prefer it. Leaving it vague makes the sentence about the feeling. The one place they are not interchangeable is the greeting: only 久ひさしぶり works there, since ❌四年よねんぶりです! is a statement of fact rather than a hello.

～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～以来

Rule of thumb - ぶり takes a length of time; 以来いらい takes an event or a point in time, and it does not require the thing to have happened again.

去年きょねんの夏なつ以来いらい、彼かれとは会あっていない。
I haven't seen him since last summer.

Notice the negative: nothing has happened again, and ぶり could not be used at all. Compare a sentence whose whole point is that the streak has just ended:

去年きょねんの夏なつ以来いらい、半年はんとしぶりに彼かれと会あった。
I saw him for the first time in six months, the first time since last summer.

～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～以来 (cont.)

The two happily sit in one sentence, each doing its own job: 以来いらい names the starting event, ぶりに gives the length of the gap. This also explains the commonest error with ぶり - attaching it to an event, as in ❌入学にゅうがくぶりに. Swap in 入学にゅうがく以来いらい and the sentence works.

～ぶりに · Common mistakes

  • ❌ 卒業そつぎょうぶりに先生せんせいに会あいました。 → ✅ 卒業そつぎょう以来いらい、初はじめて先生せんせいに会あいました。
    ぶり needs a length of time in front of it. Graduation is an event, which takes 以来いらい.

  • ❌ 十年じゅうねんぶりな再会さいかいでした。 → ✅ 十年じゅうねんぶりの再会さいかいでした。
    Before a noun the linker is の. ぶり behaves like a noun, not a な-adjective.

  • ❌ お久ひさしぶりにですね。 → ✅ お久ひさしぶりですね。
    The に belongs only when ぶり modifies a verb. As a greeting it is a predicate and stands alone.

～ぶりに · Common mistakes (cont.)

  • ❌ 二年にねん後ごに温泉おんせんに行いった。（intending "went to a hot spring for the first time in two years"） → ✅ 二年にねんぶりに温泉おんせんに行いった。
    後ごに would mean two years after some other event. To measure back to the previous visit, you need ぶりに.

～ぶりに · Practice 1

1. 台風たいふうが過すぎて、三日みっか____青あおい空そらが見みえた。

台風たいふうが過すぎて、三日みっかぶりに青あおい空そらが見みえた。

Three days had passed since the sky was last blue, and now it is blue again - both halves of what ぶりに claims. The に is there because it modifies a verb.

～ぶりに · Practice 2

2. Choose: 入学にゅうがくして一年いちねん（後ごに・ぶりに）、初はじめて寮りょうを出でた。

入学にゅうがくして一年いちねん後ごに、初はじめて寮りょうを出でた。

The year is counted forward from enrolment, and leaving the dormitory is happening for the very first time - so there is no previous occasion for ぶり to measure back to.

～ぶりに · Practice 3

3. Translate into Japanese: "I got a letter from my grandfather for the first time in two years."

二年にねんぶりに祖父そふから手紙てがみが届とどいた。

"For the first time in two years" maps straight onto 二年にねんぶりに: a named gap, and the event recurring at the end of it.

～ぶりに · Practice 4

4. Find and fix the error: 五年ごねんぶりな日本にほんへ行いってきました。

✅ 五年ごねんぶりに日本にほんへ行いってきました。

ぶり takes に in front of a verb and の in front of a noun; な is never an option. Here the phrase modifies 行いく, so に is correct.

～ぶりに · Practice 5

5. Explain the difference in nuance: (a) 五年ごねんぶりにピアノを弾ひいた。 (b) 五年ごねん間かん、ピアノを弾ひいていなかった。

(a) puts the emphasis on the moment the gap ended: the playing has just happened, and the five years are there to tell you how remarkable that is. There is usually a flicker of feeling attached.

(b) describes the gap itself and says nothing about whether it has ended. It is the sentence you use to explain why your fingers are stiff, not to announce that you have started again.
